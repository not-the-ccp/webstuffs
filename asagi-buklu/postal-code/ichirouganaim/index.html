NOTICE Nº 44

LOST AND FOUND — ITEMS HELD AT THE COUNTER, PARCEL ROOM, GENERAL POST OFFICE, STORM ROAD, BRIDGETOWN, BARBADOS, BB11101

RECEIVED 27 SEP · 11:42 a.m. BRTN / BB

Quote reference BB·LPF·2025·044 when claiming, in person or by post. Items are held ninety (90) days from the date of this notice, then offered to the parish school in Bridgetown. No claim is refused on account of a missing stamp; the island is patient with its mail the way it is patient with its own name.

FOUND Item 44a — one (1) paper record sleeve

Found: 21 September, 2025, stuck to the parcel counter with a corner of gum tape. Last verified address on it: 49510, Aşağıbüklü Köyü, Bulanık / Muş, Türkiye.

Description: one (1) paper record sleeve, Plain Post Records catalogue PPR-001, for the LP 49510 by the Bulanık Plain Quartet — nine sides of a postal code, pressed in a run of 286 copies, one per villager. The sleeve arrived in an airmail envelope addressed simply to “the correspondent, Bridgetown, Barbados,” with no street and no number, which is a French CEDEX habit that has migrated to the Caribbean and should not. Our system works with the BB districts — BB11101 is this desk, for instance — and a destination with no street is a destination that sits on the counter. The sleeve is sound: the postmistress's stamp still shows through on the back, and the inner bag held two things, detailed at Items 44b and 44d. The full track list, the credits, and the postmistress's part in the record are kept on the 49510 — Sleeve Notes page, which remains the sleeve's home. Claimants should know their copy's number, printed on the label; the counter will check it against the run of 286.

FOUND Item 44b — one (1) recipe slip, hand unplaced

Found: folded in the inner bag of Item 44a, in a hand none of the claimants at the counter has matched to any of them.

Description: one (1) small card, ink on ruled paper, reading in full: “When done, take out the spot where I had done a second time; 5 minutes to fry the fritters of a fowl, their position than it is.” The village, as its sleeve notes confess, has cooked from it anyway, and the fritters came out better than anything in the square, which is a bar set high by the cornbread with the village cheese and the hillside honey. Attached to the slip, on a separate leaf, is a line the postmistress of Aşağıbüklü wrote to explain the sessions that produced the record. The clerk has transcribed it verbatim, without tidying: “The sessions ran over three harvest evenings in September, when the tart in a fowl.” Read, the way the postmistress meant it read: the tart was in the fowl, the fowl was in the oven, and the slip was in the sleeve — a parcel inside a parcel inside an envelope, and the island's weather kept it dry. We recommend the fowl for harvest evenings; the tarts for the visitors. The fowl itself is of the kind a naturalist counted, by the marshes, out at Magsaysay Avenue (Latitude — Visitor's Brochure, Parallel 13° North), and the fowl has done well abroad.

LOST Item 44c — one (1) CEDEX letter, unclaimed by its building

Reported missing: 19 September, 2025, by post, from the postmistress, 49510. Last seen: the minibus from Bulanık, first departure.

Description: one (1) letter, the one that answers the track “CEDEX (for the French letter)” on Side A of the record — the letter that arrived at a building and not a street, and which the quartet played back at the wrong speed because they could not hear it properly. It is believed to have left Aşağıbüklü with the minibus and to have lost itself somewhere between the tea house and the postbox, in the gap where a destination and a street should meet. If found on any road, in any country, it should be left at any post office counter on its own; a building that can receive its own letter is rare, and we will forward it. The letter's broader journey — the Aegean gulf its correspondent watched from the bank, with its resorts and its theatre at the head of the inlet — is written up in Güllük Gulf — Field Notes, Entry no. 11.

FOUND Item 44d — one (1) goose, grey, unringed

Found: 20 September, 2025, on the quay at Bridgetown, beside the airmail crates. Unclaimed for eleven days; released by arrangement with the minibus.

Description: one (1) goose, grey, red beak, no ring and no ring number, but present, on its own terms, for the entire take of track 7 of the record above, where it is uncredited. Its last confirmed address is the village entrance at Aşağıbüklü, beside the “Hoş geldiniz” sign, which has faded to a green you can almost not see. The minibus from Bulanık will not take it — the driver has said so in writing, twice — so it was booked on the airmail by exception, under a standing order of this office for live items that refuse to move. It declined Bridgetown, as geese do, and has been returned to the square, where it is understood to be the bass line again. The village's whole story, goose included, is kept on the Aşağıbüklü thread, one level up this notice (Sleeve Notes, 49510).

Notes to the correspondent — of this island as an address

We add a note, because the correspondent's envelope shows the island has been hard to address, the way a name is hard to address when it keeps changing. Barbados is an island nation in the Caribbean Sea, part of the Lesser Antilles of the West Indies, and the southernmost and easternmost island of the whole Caribbean region — the last house on the street, if the street is the Antilles and the sun sets on it first of all. It lies on the boundary of the South American and Caribbean tectonic plates, so the ground under this very counter belongs to two addresses at once, and the post office has ruled that both are to be stamped. Its capital and largest city is Bridgetown, where this board is fixed. Inhabited by Kalinago people since the 13th century, and prior to that by other Indigenous peoples, the island was originally named Ichirouganaim; it was claimed for the Crown of Castile by Spanish navigators in the late 15th century, who named it Barbudos, and it first appeared on a Spanish map in 1511. The Portuguese Empire claimed it between 1532 and 1536, and abandoned it in 1620, leaving, among other things, no one on record to explain the fence. Since then the island's postal districts have run BB11101 through BB11999, and every envelope to it — like the sleeve at Item 44a, like the letter at Item 44c — begins with those two letters, which is the closest the island has come to holding a name still. A sister counter keeps the same kind of board a long way to the north, in the classifieds of the Märkischer Kreis; its sheets are filed at Kleinanzeigen — The Classifieds of the Märkischer Kreis, and its lost footbridge is mourned here with ours.

How to claim

Present yourself at the parcel counter, Storm Road, Bridgetown BB11101, Monday to Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and quote reference BB·LPF·2025·044. For Item 44a, name your copy's number out of the 286. For Item 44b, be able to make the fritters, or describe them well enough that we can; the counter is not shy about taste. For Item 44d, no claim is entertained; the goose has already been claimed by the square, and the claim was better than ours. Postage on anything we return is pre-paid at the rate for the plain; the code will do the rest, and the minibus runs twice a day in September, which is what the postmistress's letter says, and what three harvest evenings of barley-gold say the same way.
