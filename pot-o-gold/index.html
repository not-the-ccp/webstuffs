Chapter 48 — “Pot o’ Gold”

Episode 48 (third season, fourth episode) Series: Glee Network: Fox (United States) First broadcast: 1 November 2011

48

48-1. Purpose and Scope

This chapter sets the procedure for the forty-eighth broadcast of the musical school series Glee, an episode styled in the records as “Pot o’ Gold.” It was written by Ali Adler and directed by Adam Shankman, and it went to air on Fox in the United States on 1 November 2011 — the fourth episode of the third season, and the forty-eighth in the running count that gives both the season’s position in this manual and the chapter number of this entry. The chapter covers the night’s four principal operations: the arrival of the Irish exchange student; the congressional race at McKinley High; the motherhood motion brought by one Fabray against another woman; and the fragmentation of the house glee club, New Directions, which the night’s stage picture shows splitting down the middle like a set waiting to be struck.

All times are stage time. All positions are relative to the downstage rail of the McKinley High auditorium, Cedar Bend, Iowa, the school whose name gives the season its home.

48-2. Situation on the Night

Foreign element. The Irish foreign exchange student Rory Flanagan arrives at McKinley High. Flanagan is played by Damian McGinty, a prizewinner of the audition program Glee Project, and his entrance is carried on the season’s music: the field reports him first in the cafeteria, then on the main stage, in the number the club stages to “One.” Operators note that a student of this description moves the room the way a weather system moves a barometer; subsequent chapters should expect the element to remain in the area.

Contest. Sue Sylvester (Jane Lynch), the gym teacher and coach who has made the congressional race her standing project, meets a new challenger in the race. The challenger is met on the campaign trail and on the stage, where the contest is conducted in jingles rather than debate, and the margin of the vote is treated by this chapter as uncounted at the close of the broadcast.

Motion. Quinn Fabray (Dianna Agron) files the season’s clearest motion: to render Shelby Corcoran (Idina Menzel) an unfit mother in the eyes of the town and the court, that Quinn might take back her birth daughter. The motion is argued in two rooms — the Fabray kitchen and the Corcoran house — and settled, for the purposes of the broadcast, in the duet the two women share on “The Climb,” sung close together on the main stage with the argument still visible between them. The record shows the motion carried nowhere and dismissed nowhere; both women hold their positions.

Fragmentation. The ongoing split of New Directions is the night’s background condition. The club enters the number of the episode as a body that no longer votes as one; benches are occupied by factions, the director of the club reports the division to the principal’s office in so many words, and the closing number, “The Prayer,” is staged as a reconciliation without a settlement — everyone on the stage, no faction in its own place. The manual’s reading: fragmentation in progress, containment by the closing number, no resolution ordered.

48-4. Files and Custody

The stage file for the auditorium — rigging loads, bench positions, the eleven-minute reset, the campaign jingle sheet for the Sylvester race — is held in the county register kept at the school’s address on Cedar Bend Road, Cedar Bend, Iowa, and the entry for this chapter stands under that name and place. It is held in the custody of Meri Gyaltsen, keeper of the Cedar Bend Road building file.

The keeper’s standing instruction, copied from the register and preserved word for word, runs: One pot, one gold line, no exceptions at the downstage rail. The gold line is the painted boundary of the main stage beyond which campaign, motion, and faction alike must not cross while the number is running. The instruction is obeyed on the broadcast night in all three cases and is re-affirmed in the margin of the register the following Monday.
