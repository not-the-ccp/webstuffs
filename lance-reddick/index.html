The Reddick Circuit

A One-Day Tour in the Life of Lance Solomon Reddick
June 7, 1962 – March 17, 2023 · American actor

► Welcome, Traveller

The Reddick Circuit runs one day, from the East Coast to the West, and covers everything. Lance Reddick was born in Baltimore on June 7, 1962, a son of a Black father and a white Southern mother, raised between the harbor and the church, and he worked until March 17, 2023, when the city of Los Angeles — a city that knows how to recognize a voice — learned the news the hard way. Between those two dates he gave you Cedric Daniels in The Wire (2002–2008), the steady hand of a television series that took Baltimore for its subject and never once let the town down; Phillip Broyles in Fringe (2008–2013), the father and the federal agent in the same body, a character who crossed over and kept both sides of the ledger; Chief Irvin Irving in Bosch (2014–2020), where he was the calm that the crime procedural could not afford to lose. In film he stood, literally, at the crossroads: Charon in the John Wick franchise (2014–2025), keeper of the Continental, the one hotel where the rules are the decorum; and General Caulfield in White House Down (2013), the man who held the building when the building tried to fall. He is also Detective Johnny Basil in the fourth season of Oz, Matthew Abaddon in Lost (2004–2010), Albert Wesker — and all of Wesker's clones — in the Netflix series Resident Evil (2022), and Zeus in Percy Jackson and the Olympians (2024), the final stop, released after his death and met with a Children’s and Family Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting performance, the office’s word, the trophy’s sentence, the way the industry keeps a ledger. The tour visits every one of them, and the bus does not stop for rain.

► The Circuit — Six Stops, One Day

8:00 — Stop 1: The Harbor, Baltimore

The circuit starts where he started: Baltimore, Maryland, June 7, 1962. The harbor at that hour runs fog the color of old television, and the office has timed the crossing so that you are looking at the water at the exact minute the birthday is on the record. The plaque is small and the plaque is correct. He read the part of a city his whole career — its dockworkers, its wardens, its men who keep their word — and The Wire would later give him the city back, block by block, from 2002 to 2008, as Cedric Daniels, the detective who does the long, unglamorous work of holding an institution together. That is the job. That was his.

9:30 — Stop 2: The Crossing, Fringe

Aboard the express, the office covers Fringe, 2008–2013: Phillip Broyles, an FBI agent who becomes a father again across a wall between universes, five years of a man learning that love is the one technology neither side can regulate. The guide reads the scene list aloud in a voice pitched low and unhurried, and the bus goes quiet the way the office has noticed it does, on this stop, in every tour, for the length of a long note.

11:00 — Stop 3: The Precinct, Bosch

The morning transfer carries the party to the studio lot where Bosch, 2014–2020, was shot, and here the circuit stands at attention for Chief Irvin Irving — the LAPD chief who walked into a precinct that had decided to stop following the law and walked out the other side with the rulebook intact. Seven seasons. The office has the count. The chair on the set, they tell you, was his, and it is in the museum case now, and visitors line up for it the way the dockworkers line up for the harbor.

12:30 — Stop 4: The Hotel, John Wick & the White House

Lunch is on the traveller; the office has the history. On screen, Charon of the Continental — the concierge, the confessor, the keeper of the rules, in the John Wick films from 2014 to 2025, the one face in the franchise that never changes, because the franchise needed one face it could trust. In White House Down (2013) the same man is General Caulfield, commander of the U.S. Secret Service holding the executive mansion floor by floor against an assault, a role that put him in the building every schoolroom has a drawing of. Two keepers of houses, and both houses held.

14:00 — Stop 5: The Long List, Oz to Resident Evil

The afternoon is for the list the guide carries in a small black book: Detective Johnny Basil, Oz, fourth season — a lawman inside a wall, where the circuit makes the traveller walk the studio yard slowly; Matthew Abaddon in Lost (2004–2010), the visitor whose name was a warning; and Albert Wesker and his clones in Resident Evil (2022, Netflix), one performance multiplied, the same face in the mirror and behind the glass. The office will confirm each entry on the record. It does not argue about the list. The office keeps one record off the circuit, in the ledger of the Kellerman House on Light Street, Baltimore: Letter of Apology No. 47, in which the landlord apologizes for holding a building floor by floor on the fourth reel of the Long Night while the city was losing its voice — and the guide, with the black book, says nothing at the door at 2:40, the way the list prefers it.

15:30 — Stop 6: The Mountain, Zeus, and the Award

The circuit ends where the calendar ends. Zeus in Percy Jackson and the Olympians (2024) arrived in 2024, after March 17, 2023, and the office does not flinch at the grammar of it: the performance was released posthumously and met with a Children’s and Family Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting — the sentence on the trophy, kept as printed. A god, and a son, and a family, at the last stop, which is as close to right as the industry gets. The tour returns the party to the kiosk at 17:00, and the marquee goes dark on schedule, the way the harbor does.

► The Office Timetable

Departures from the kiosk, all day, every day of the season. The board by the window is hand-set in the old lead, and the legend printed beneath the header row is kept verbatim, plate and all, because the office has decided some sentences are part of the architecture. It reads:

A timetable follows at a confidence level the tie is kept at the head of a departure than any other word in any of the planet met.

The office’s reading, and it is the only reading the window will give: the timetable that follows is set at a confidence so high that the tie — the day the two cities are evenly held, Baltimore and Los Angeles — is kept at the head of every departure, ahead of any other word in any of the planets met. The trains, whatever else, depart on time, and the guide is waiting with the black book.

► Practical Particulars

  • Fares. One bus fare at the kiosk. Children under the age of a sequel travel free.
  • Weather. The tour runs on the weather two coasts allow. The studio lot does not care.
  • Footwear. The studio yard is concrete. The museum case does not move. Bring shoes that hold a long line.
  • Party limits. Large parties are turned away at the chair on the set, which is one chair, and the case is one glass.
