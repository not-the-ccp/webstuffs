The Dreamer's Glossary · Entry No. 53 · shelved with the third drawer, the justice's leaf

53 d. c. 1203

To Dream of Hugh Bardulf

or Bardolf — the medieval English administrator and royal justice, known for his legal expertise, also a financial administrator, in whose dream he stands at once between two doors and at the head of one very long ledger.

Of the man in the corridor

To dream of Hugh Bardulf is to find him at Westminster, in the corridor between the hall where verdicts are read and the chamber where coin is counted, and to understand, without being told, that he belongs to both rooms at once. He has served three kings of England in this dream, as in his life: he began under Henry II as a steward of the royal household, rose to be a royal justice riding the eyre circuit, and held the sheriff's rod through the rest of Henry's reign and on into the reign of Richard I, his son and successor. In the dream he is not walking between the two rooms. He is the corridor. The doors close behind him and open ahead of him on schedule, and the schedule has not once missed its hour, which is what the dreamer takes the warning to be about.

He is a man of tall build in a justice's robe with the steward's cuffs still visible at the wrist, and he carries a writ on which, when the dreamer leans in, only the dreamer's own name is written. The name is not struck out. In this glossary that detail outranks the whole; see the registry at the front of the catalogue, where the drawer is kept that survives the night, and the rule written in its dust: the name once entered is not erased.

Of the fourth column

When the ledger in such a dream is turned to its fourth column, the keeper's hand has broken off mid-tally, and the only line written there runs, word for word:

The rest of this column pending the claims now lodged against it.

The dreamer is expected to read it twice. The first reading is arithmetic: a column stopped, a claim lodged, a tally not yet closed. The second reading is the dream itself. Bardulf is the column. The claims are the ones described below, and the rest of him — the rest of the column, the rest of the reigns, the rest of the circuit — is simply not written down yet, which is not a cancellation. The dream keeps the column open on purpose.

Of the treason that lasts one bell-stroke

In the third scene of the dream, or sometimes the last, Bardulf is named a traitor. The cause is given plainly at the bar, as causes are in this glossary: he is a vassal of John, the younger brother, and John has rebelled against his elder brother Richard, and the denunciation falls on the vassal and not only on the rebel. In the dream this costs him exactly one bell-stroke. The dreamer hears the bell begin, and before it finishes its fall the writs are countermanded, and Bardulf is restored to royal service, and the court goes back to the coin-counting as though the corridor had never closed.

The interpretation the glossary gives is short: to dream this is to hold a charge of office longer than the accusation against it. The dream promises restoration after the naming, and it is confirmed by three kings, which this glossary treats as a quorum. It is not a dream of exile. It is a dream of the ledger that holds, of the sheriff's rod taken up again the same term, and of a man who will out-serve the very brother who sent for him.

Cross-shelvings

The green door that appears in the third drawer of this dream is the same door the glossary keeps for the foreign relations of Brazil, Entry 48, where the count of 334,500 is also left open on purpose; the two doors are not a contradiction, the glossary has ruled, they are the same corridor in two cities. For the form of a wake kept for a subject that is still moving, the dreamer is referred to the Naval Register's in memoriam of HMS Agamemnon, where one hull holds one cable and the column stays pending, likewise, with no apology. The castle version of the same corridor hangs beside this entry in the annex as museum placard No. 1067, Kenilworth Castle: there the gate ledger keeps the same corridor in the dream, the lye was cured in advance, and whatever was corrosive in the autumn has already been ruled in solution.

EXAMINED · DREAMED · RESTORED

Advisory to the dreamer: if you have dreamed of Hugh Bardulf, serve the third king well. The second one has already passed through, and the first one never left the corridor. Keep your own name out of the writ; it is only a placeholder, and the keeper of this glossary does not strike it out.
