Lot 143
The 1999 FIA GT Homestead 3 Hours, Complete as One Consignment

The official program; the steward’s timing book; the steering column and rod of the winning #7 Corvette C5.R; and the field sheet bearing the first National GT entries in championship history

Estimate $18,000–$24,000 · Hammer $31,750 SOLD

I. The Consignment

Offered as a single, unbroken lot: the eighth round of the 1999 FIA GT Championship season, the Homestead 3 Hours, run at the Homestead-Miami Speedway in Florida, United States, on the Sunday morning of September 26, 1999. The consignment is the event itself, bound and carried: the 48-page program with the paddock list pasted to the back inside cover; the stewards’ timing book, 241 laps written in green ink with the fuel-stop column crossed out twice; the field sheet on which the National GT entries appear for the first time; and, on the padded stand at the foot of Room C, the steering column and rod of the #7 Corvette C5.R, still wearing its three-bolt quick-release and the white tape ring the driver used to find the top of the wheel blindfolded at the pit wall. The wheel itself has been removed for private sale; the rod remains, and the rod is what the room has come for.

II. On the Season and the Field

The event was originally intended to share the weekend with a United States Road Racing Championship meeting, the two series running nose-to-tail as they had in recent years. The USRRC championship was cancelled midway through the season, however, and the FIA GT Championship was left to run on its own, against the empty grandstands and the long sightline down the back straight. In order to increase the number of participants for the FIA GT event, and to allow USRRC teams to compete at all, a National GT designation — N-GT — was used for cars which had run in the USRRC’s GT2 and GT3 classes. It would be the first time the FIA GT Championship used two classes in a single field on an American oval: the works GT1 prototypes against a mixed group of N-GT sports cars, all of them turning right, all of them three hours, on the 1.5-mile tri-oval that smells of pine sawdust and hot asphalt.

III. The Race and the Rod

Green flag at 11:14 a.m. local, a light crosswind off the water, track temperature 108°F and falling. The #7 Corvette C5.R of M. Tadeusi and J. Vickers led from lap 9, after an exchange at the turn-3 entry that the timing book records as one lap of 42.8 seconds and nothing else. The N-GT class fell away in the second hour, two cars on fuel, one on a gearbox; the class winner, the #55 Porsche 911 of K. Oren and B. Salas, finished 14th overall and is mentioned here only because its field-sheet entry is the first N-GT line ever printed in a championship schedule, and because its fuel log is the one the steward has folded inside the program. Tadeusi and Vickers crossed the line after 241 laps, three hours flat to the second, an average of 132.8 miles an hour, and the winning margin of 41 seconds stood for the season. The steering rod in the lot carries the impact of lap 173 — a shallow flat spot, exactly one knuckle wide, where Vickers caught the curb at the exit of turn 2 and the column spoke through it. The workshop note taped to the stand reads, in the shop’s careful hand: “True within spec. Runs. Does not wobble. T. & J. approved.”

IV. Condition and Provenance

Program: good, spine sunned, back cover with a coffee ring that reads as a tire mark. Timing book: very good, 241 laps present, one green-ink correction. Field sheet: excellent, the N-GT column in its original typewriter face. Steering column and rod: as raced, quick-release complete, paint chipped where the harness clamps have sat, the white tape ring replaced once, the lap-173 flat spot as described, nothing repaired, nothing straightened. Provenance: the #7 team’s parts locker, Homestead, to the steward’s office, to the Southern Branch by the regular crate, alongside the San Diego papers of Lot 142 in this same catalogue, which were crated the same week and carry the same pine-sawdust smell. The lot is sold as one piece; the rod will not be split from the books.

V. From the Auction Record

The room asked the only question the room asks. From the second row, Mr. Kelly, who had bid on the wheel in a previous catalogue and lost it: does the consignment extend to the span of the race itself, three hours of it, or only to the paper? The auctioneer, whose surname the record prints in capitals, answered without looking up from the program, and the line is entered here exactly as the clerk wrote it down:

TADEUSI: The public interest, Mr. Kelly, is not a recipe's span but a compressed handling order for the rod.

Mr. Kelly bid $30,000. The lot went under the hammer at $31,750 with a club of the gavel that the clerk noted as “firm, once.” The rod is now in a climate case in Tampa, turned a quarter-inch each month, and it has not wobbled. And the crate that carried it the same week held the walnut survey boxes of Lot 9 — Singleton and Cocking Tunnels, West Sussex in Catalogue D, whose winter papers smelled of chalk dust for a month in Room C. The spring of the following year the same crate carried, in the other direction, the Sallette papers of Lot 78 — Mary Marquet, the 1931 programme and the gap in Catalogue C, whose condition card read, in one line, that the gap was priced in.

Colophon & Catalogue Notes

Lot 143 follows Lot 142 — San Diego: A Coast, a Climate, and a Crate, crated beside it in the same crate, and both lots share the sale’s rule that papers and iron travel together. For the buyer who wants the year of 1999 at length, the catalogue office keeps a shelf of 1999 releases, chief among it Latin Alphabet No. 9 — Liner Notes, the shed recording from the same autumn, its euro sign and eight replaced glyphs filed under the same heading as the N-GT column: new faces printed on a familiar schedule. The front of the house is the catalogue cover — return to the front.
