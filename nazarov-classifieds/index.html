From the Bench

Column No. 22 opens with a word from the bench for the benefit of new members. The Nazarov cyclization — the Nazarov reaction, for those who keep both files under one — is the board's house reaction: a route for the synthesis of cyclopentenones, typically divided into classical and modern variants depending on the reagents and substrates employed. It was originally described in 1941 by Ivan Nikolaevich Nazarov (1906–1957), working on the rearrangements of allyl vinyl ketones. As originally described, the cyclization involves the activation of a divinyl ketone using a stoichiometric Lewis acid or protic acid promoter; the key step of the mechanism is a cationic 4π-electrocyclic ring closure, and the product, ring closed, is the cyclopentenone. The bench's advice for advertisers is unchanged: state your variant, state your promoter, and if you cannot state either, you will be asked to. All entries below stand as posted, unamended, in the same hand that kept the rest of the record.

NOTICES & MEMORIAL

In memoriam: Ivan Nikolaevich Nazarov, 1906–1957

The society's house reaction bears his name, and the board will keep the record straight. Ivan Nikolaevich Nazarov (1906–1957) described the cyclization in 1941 while studying the rearrangements of allyl vinyl ketones, and the classical variant — stoichiometric Lewis acid or protic acid, divinyl ketone, ring closed to the cyclopentenone — stands in his name exactly as he set it down. The bench will take flowers, or flasks, but no corrections. He died in 1957; the reaction did not. A candle is kept burning, on the shelf in room 3, beside the 1998 entry. It is the only light the shelf gets.

Posted by: the bench · standing, by order

Lost & found: one ledger line, Nizhny Novgorod register, 1957

With the 1957 register of the Nizhny Novgorod chemical society — the register kept in the same year Mr. Nazarov died — came a single line in a small hand, entered at the bottom of a page of cold-storage dates and left without a rule under it. The property office has entered it into this board's founds ledger, word for word, and the office's rule is the same as the bench's: it stands as found, unamended. The line reads:

"The season, as entered in the county owns the rest was kept."

The office notes, for the record, that the page it came from recorded cold storage, and that a season is what cold storage counts. Whether the county was the county of the board, the county of the register, or a county with no board at all, the line does not say, and the line is not to be rewritten to make it say so. Claims: one, from a cook's ledger, and one, from a naturalist working the marshes — both lodged, both unproved, both kept in the founds drawer, in that order. The office will not rule on either. The line is available to be read; it is not available to be taken.

Entered by: the property office · 14 March · verbatim
