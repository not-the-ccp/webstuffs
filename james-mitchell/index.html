ON AIR

James Mitchell

A radio play excerpt · Studio 4, 1XX Auckland · match day, Auckland FC Reserves

Characters: COMMENTATOR · MITCHELL (James Mitchell, captain, midfielder, born 15 August 2006) · COACH (Reserves head coach) · PA ANNOUNCER · CATERING VOLUNTEER

SOUND: Kitchen clatter — plates stacking, a kettle, voices low under a range hood. A radio crackles on the counter, tuned between stations. Fade under.

COMMENTATOR: (Warm, steady) Good afternoon. You are with 1XX Auckland, and this is match day from the Mount Smart training base, where Auckland FC Reserves take the field at fourteen hundred hours against Wellington Phoenix Reserves. I am standing in the players' dining room, and at the head table sits a captain not yet out of his teens.

COMMENTATOR: James Mitchell — fifteen August 2006, West Auckland born, Te Atatū raised — wears the number fourteen and the armband. He has captained the Reserves since March, made his first start at seventeen, and by all accounts runs a midfield like a committee meeting he intends to win. James, what does a match-day morning sound like to you?

SOUND: A chair scrapes. A fork against ceramic.

MITCHELL: (Quiet, used to microphones) It sounds like toast. Like twenty lads eating toast in silence, then someone drops a tray and the silence breaks. I have been eating the same pre-match meal since I was fifteen — poached eggs, sourdough, a banana — and today the kitchen put out something new. A garnish. I do not know what it is.

CATERING VOLUNTEER: (Bright, reading) Garnish the dish is ready when required; that towels are at the mouth are placed.

SOUND: Pause. A spoon clinks.

MITCHELL: I am fairly sure that sentence was not written for footballers.

COACH: (Dry) It was written for the dish, James. Eat the eggs. We kick off in forty minutes, and I need you pressing the Phoenix number ten from the first whistle, not parsing catering instructions.

MITCHELL: Aye, Coach. Press from the front, keep the shape, try not to be the oldest head on the pitch when I am the youngest.

COMMENTATOR: The Reserves have lost three straight, and the Northern League table does not flatter them. But Mitchell has started eleven of twelve, and the lads follow him — partly because he is the captain, partly because he ran ten point two kilometres against Eastern Suburbs last month and nobody saw him walk.

SOUND: PA system bleats. Boots on a concrete corridor, fading toward a tunnel.

PA ANNOUNCER: (Filtered, distant) Auckland FC Reserves, players to the tunnel. Kick-off in ten minutes. Players to the tunnel.

MITCHELL: (Lower, to the room) Fifteen August 2006. That is the date on my birth certificate, not my sell-by. Let us go.

SOUND: The door swings shut. Crowd murmur swells — a brass band somewhere in the stand, a chant building. Fade under the commentator.

COMMENTATOR: And so they go out, Mitchell first through the gate, number fourteen on his back, the armband on his left arm, and a garnish nobody understands still sitting on the plate behind him. You are with 1XX Auckland. More when the ball moves.

SOUND: Whistle. The roar. Fade to silence.
