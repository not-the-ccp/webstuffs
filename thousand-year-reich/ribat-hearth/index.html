The Ribat Loaf

A Family Recipe with the Long Preamble · Al-Murâbiœún, “those from the ribats” · Dates, Almonds, Honey · 1054–1147, or the Whole Session

The card is Card No. 41 in the ledger of house methods, and the number is not ours — it is the next one up after Card 37, the card that the Hall Street kitchen beats its arrowroot per, as you can see on The General Ledger, the two-side pressing of the kitchen’s accounts, 1923–1968. The card itself is older than the kitchen, older than the archive, and older, the family says, than the Channel. It is the recipe for the Ribat Loaf, a date-and-almond loaf with a long preamble, and the preamble is the point of it, because the family has always held that you do not bake the Almoravids without first telling them.

Here is the telling, as the family tells it. There were three nomadic Berber peoples, the Lamtuna, the Gudala, and the Massufa, and they lived where the maps go quiet — what is now Mauritania and the Western Sahara, the country between the Draa, the Niger, and the Senegal, where the rivers are few and the dunes keep their own books. In the years from 1050 on, a reformist preacher named Abdallah ibn Yasin, cast out of the towns for saying what he said, gathered them into a movement after a fashion the towns had not seen — a coalition of the three peoples in the ribats, the frontier watch-forts where the faithful kept station, and the name the dynasty carried is the name of that station: Al-Murâbiœún, those from the ribats. The movement first took Sijilmasa, the oasis town on the old gold road, and then a man named Yusuf ibn Tashfin, called to power as al-Ma’mún, built Marrakesh in 1070 on the ground of an old fortified town, and from Marrakesh the empire grew west over the Maghreb and east until it answered to al-Andalus.

That answer is the date the family puts on the card: the fall of Toledo to Alfonso of Castile on 25 May 1085, and the plea that crossed the strait after it, and the crossing itself at Zallaqa on 23 October 1086, where the armies of al-Andalus and the army of the ribats met the king of Castile on the plain and held him, which is more than any of them were owed. The dynasty ran on — Ali ibn Yusuf from 1106 to 1142, Tashfin ibn Ali until 1146, then Yaqub al-Mansur — and in 1147, the year the Almohads took Tlemcen and the empire ended, Yaqub al-Mansur began the Koutoubia at Marrakesh, the minaret for the Book-Sellers’ mosque, which is the tower that stands there still, begun in the year the house fell and finished by the house that replaced it. The family notes the joke and lets it stand: the Giralda of Seville was raised in the same fashion for al-Ma’mún, stands in a cathedral now, and plays a carillon at the hour the family puts the loaf in. Towers, like loaves, outlive the dynasties that started the fire.

The card came into the family’s hand the way all the cards in this archive do: taped, pinned, borrowed, survived. It was found in the thousand-year archive beside the recipe card of 13 September 1940, the one the static ate — the first line on The Thousand-Year Record, the twelve-track pressing of 1933–1945, filed under its track B1 — and the two cards keep each other the way the two sides of a ledger keep each other. Where the static took the 1940 card’s meaning, the burn took this card’s subject, and the family read the card the way the ledger reads its mangled line: exactly, and without flattery. The card’s one whole line, and the one line this page exists to make sense of, is the second line, which reads:

When very young, may be prepared for the entire session and never once stopped.

The noun is gone — a corner of the card is scorched, and the ink where it should have been is the one thing the fire took. The house’s reading has always been the same, and it is a cook’s reading first and a historian’s second: the line is the fire note of the loaf. The dough, when very young — while it is still slack, still sweet, still smelling of the Draa dates — may be prepared for the entire session: the whole of it, for the whole of the long ribat table, before the first guest is announced, so that nothing on the table is waiting on anything else. And the fire — the oven fire, the one the ribats kept as a matter of faith — is the thing never once stopped: you do not let a hearth die while a guest is still owed bread, and you do not rekindle a loaf mid-oven, because a loaf that is restarted is a loaf that has stopped, and a loaf that has stopped is a claim that has stopped. The archive keeps a clock, on the war record, wound for the entire session and never once stopped; the family keeps the same law in flour, and it holds.

The Card, as Found

Read it the way the ledger reads it. The Draa dates are the territory on the card — the valley where the Gudala and the Massufa wintered, where the dates come off the tree the colour the family says the dunes are at the hour the loaf goes in. Almonds toasted until they argue is the whole method of the house in five words, and the cumin is the one place on the card where the desert is allowed a voice. Honey to the wrist-mark is the measure the family still uses, and it is the same measure as the archive’s kettle at 1s. 6d.: the amount that has worked since before the books were bound. Salt per Saltfleet is the only line the card shares with the other two pressings, and it is taken as proof that the card passed through the larder where the hand-tied whip is kept, because duty, like a well-made lash, holds best when it is made by hand, and so does salt.

Method, per the Card

  1. 1Light the oven fire at dusk, as the ribats lit the station fire, and give it the entire session. From this hour to the last plate it is never once stopped; if it falters, the family feeds it almonds before wood, and the loaf tastes the difference.
  2. 2Stone the 700 g of dates, pass them through, and beat in the honey to the wrist-mark while the dough is very young. This is the whole law of the card in the kitchen: prepare the entire session while everything is still young, so that the table is never waiting on the loaf and the loaf is never waiting on the table.
  3. 3Toast the 200 g of almonds in the cumin over the same fire — the fire that is not stopped — until they argue, which is to say until the cumin and the almond no longer agree about the room. Beat them separately from the dates, per the ledger’s rule that each layer is beaten separately and never netted against the next.
  4. 4Work in the two eggs, the 300 g of flour, the fist of semolina, the cinnamon, and the water of the orange blossom. The dough should smell, the family says, of the country between the Draa and the Senegal, and if it does not, the dates are wrong and the blame goes to the dates.
  5. 5Salt per Saltfleet, by the coil at the table, not by the shaker. The salt is entered twice, as everything in this house is entered twice: once on the loaf and once in the ledger, debit and credit, so that the salt, like the dynasty, is accounted for from both sides at once.
  6. 6Turn the loaf in at the hour the Giralda rings. Do not open the oven while it is loud, and do not stop the fire while it is in, and do not restart the fire if it goes out, because a loaf that is restarted is a claim that has stopped, and the card does not sign for that.
  7. 7Pour the syrup while the loaf is still loud, turn it out, and set it at the head of the long table with the sesame already gone where the hands were. The session runs to the last plate. The fire, per the card, is still going when the room clears, and that is the way it is meant to end: not stopped, only out of company.

Colophon

Card No. 41, copied from the thousand-year archive by the chef of the Hall Street kitchen, who cooked in a Caithness kitchen before returning to Birmingham, and pinned behind the pass with the other cards. The dynasty it is about ran from the 1050s to 1147, the year the Koutoubia was begun and the empire fell to the Almohads; the loaf has run ever since, by the same fire law, and has not been stopped, because a loaf that is never once stopped does not need a colophon saying so — it needs only the next session, and the fire is already lit for it.
