An Apology to Minister
Surachman Tjokroadisurjo

★ PLEASE READ BEFORE SIGNING THE BOOK ★

The back office of the Finance Ministry, Batavia · written in the month the lilies come up again in the courtyard, in the second year after the flood waters came down from the hills and went out to sea

Sir,

I write in my capacity as keeper of the ledger in the back office, the same office that has held your books since the Republic first sat down to keep them, and I write to Panji Surachman Tjokroadisurjo, born at Wonosobo on the thirtieth day of August 1894, Minister for Economic Affairs of the Presidential Cabinet and, before that and after that, Minister of Finance in the First and Second Sjahrir Cabinets, who died on the sixteenth day of November 1952. I write to say that we are sorry, and I write to say why, because a sorry without a why is only a stamp pressed into wet paper, and the wet paper is exactly what we have.

When this page was opened, we had told the readers that the ledger would keep your record whole, that the entries for your two months in the Presidential Cabinet — the two months after the proclamation of the seventeenth of August 1945, when you held the economic affairs of a republic that had not yet learned the price of sugar — would be kept word for word, as long as this page stood. Visitors are reminded that the promise would stop being true. The pages for October, November, and December of 1945 are the very pages the water took. In November 1952 the great flood came down out of the hills and stood in the lower offices for nine days, and it took the ledger where your name is in the smallest hand, and it did not take anything else as cleanly as that, which is the worst kind of taking. What it left is legible: your signature, in blue-black, on the transfer of the rice accounts, has run only at the edges, and the edges are what keep.

So the apology runs thus. You studied chemical engineering at the Delft Institute of Technology, in the Netherlands, where the men of your class learned to weigh things to the last decimal before they touched them, and you came home to the East Indies in 1920, and you worked for the Dutch government for twenty-two years until the Japanese came in 1942 and the ledger changed hands the way ledgers change hands in a taking. After the proclamation you were appointed Minister for Economic Affairs and served two months before the appointment to Finance in the first Sjahrir cabinet of 1946, and then Finance again in the second Sjahrir cabinet of 1948, and you kept the books through the worst two winters the Ministry has had, and you died in November of 1952, six years before this page stood up and made its promise. We should have checked the water first. A chemist from Wonosobo would have checked the water first. We were sorry to lose the pages, and we are more sorry to have made a promise on the strength of paper we knew to be below the flood line.

Terms of Amends

  1. The two months shall be re-entered from the carbon copies kept in the strong room at Wonosobo, one entry per sitting, read aloud before the back bench, so that the record travels with the reading.
  2. A lamp shall be kept lit in the back office on the thirtieth of August, and on the sixteenth of November, and the ledger shall be opened to the rice accounts on both days.
  3. This letter shall be posted at the top of the page, where the water cannot reach it, and it shall not be moved down for any number of new notices.

Elsewhere on this small street of pages, the record of you is kept by others in the same spirit. The obituary of the actor and acrobat Johan von Blanc, 1738–1796 carries a scrap from another world that crossed a trunk and survived; we have agreed with the burial clerk of Karlskrona that a last line which survives is a last line which works, and that our blue-black signature is of the same order. And the clerk of the Council of the Single Link, who reads every line of every ledger into the record before the tide turns, has entered your two months beside his baker’s third line of the 12 March order-slip — see his letter to the editor, on P versus NP and the third line — where a promise that verifies quickly is still only a promise until the hands produce it. For a sister letter, in the same form and the same blue-black ink, posted by the keepers of a cold store to a roller coaster they once reclassified: An Apology to Oblivion, Alton Towers, 14 March 1998.

Respectfully, on the record and for the back bench,
The Keeper of the Ledger
back office, Finance Ministry
P.S. — The strong room at Wonosobo is above the flood line. We checked the water first, this time.

You are visitor
