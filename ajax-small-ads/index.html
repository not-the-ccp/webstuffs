The De Griet

Small Ads & Notices · No. 1974 · Thursday, 14 March · Price: one whistle, half-length

AMSTERDAM · DE BAARSJES · MUSEUMPLEIN 5 · SINCE 1900 · NO. 1974

Amsterdamsche Football Club Ajax, otherwise known as Ajax Amsterdam, otherwise known as the club named after the legendary Greek hero — the most successful club in the country, with the record number of league titles and cups, and one shirt, white, that has not changed its mind since the league began in 1956. Along with two other clubs it makes up the country’s “big three,” and this sheet is the place where the big three, and everyone smaller than them, put their notices in the paper.

Lost

ONE all-white scarf, wool, monogrammed C.J., last seen on a terrace in Rome on the night of 27 May 2009, in the tunnel of the Stadio Olimpico that smells of turnstiles and hot tarmac. The owner crossed the continent in white to watch a different club take the European Cup — Eto’o in the tenth minute, Messi twenty minutes from the end, the first Spanish treble, the whole evening as set out in the Long Guide entry at Rome, 27 May 2009 — and came home blue-and-gold inside a white shell, with the scarf still in Rome. The Long Guide keeps the score; we will keep the hook empty until the scarf comes back. Reward: one signed ticket, European Cup final 1972, the 2–0, in excellent condition.

Wanted

ONE clerk, formerly of a certain Ministry at Alton Towers, Staffordshire; the owner of a clipboard with three names in the left hook of the ledger; the owner of an apology letter which is, to put it in the only terms we allow on this sheet, thorough. The Clerk kept a ledger instead of keeping a queue, waved a Miss Trelawney through without looking up, and is now writing penitence to a world that did not ask for it. The full account of the drop is at the apology letter, Oblivion, where 54.9 metres is explained to the last centimetre. This notice simply says: we have seen your handwriting on the ledger; the hook is held for you; the queue is, in the meantime, being kept by someone else.

For Sale

TICKET stub, second category, one summer afternoon in 1973, the De Kuip. Slightly scorched at the fold, where the sun did its work on the paper and the paper did not complain. Sold with the seller’s whole note, set down word for word as it came in: “I take it—the conclusion aimed at will among their rival admirers; just so with the terraces of red and hot tarmac.” The seller is eighty-one, does not answer the telephone, and has declined to be clearer. Price: what it’s worth to whoever knows.

The notice-keeper’s note: we have kept this one for eleven months and now say what we understand of it. The terraces of the De Kuip are red; the tarmac on the approach was hot that afternoon; and a rival of this club was, at last, aiming at a conclusion. The seller, in other words, means that everyone’s side eventually settles on its mind, and that the red end and the hot road were simply where it happened. If he means something else, he has had the months; the stub stands as written.

Found

BLUE pencil, half used, and one slip of paper on which is written the figure 36, in a schoolhand that has since moved on. Found at the museum, at Museumplein 5, on the shelf where the silver is. If you are a school group from the league year in which the count stood at 36 — the record, and a count no one on this sheet has yet managed to overtake — collect it against a copy of the table. The club has stood at the top of the Dutch league without interruption since the league’s inception in 1956, so the pencil is, strictly, overdue.

To Let / Names

WANTED to hear from any bakery, deli, printing house or water carrier proposing to use the name Ajax on its sign; the name goes back to the legendary Greek hero of Greek myth and the club that carries it, and offers will be considered in the order they are received, white envelopes first. Also wanted, for the record: any hero of the same name, in writing, confirming he is in fact the hero. He was asked about this in 1900 and never came back, but the notice is re-run every year, and the name is the name.

Standing Notice

The youth ground at the future — De Toekomst, which is what it is called, and what it is — receives no notices from the past. No offers from clubs that have not yet been built are entertained. No “big three” business is conducted on this sheet beyond the statement that there are three, that this is one, and that the other two are named in the league table, which is all the naming this sheet is obliged to do. Ads in this column are black on white, like the shirt; ads in the rival’s colours are not received, and the one received last spring has been kept, unread, in the bottom of the drawer, where it does its own quiet work.

The De Griet is set in black on white, printed every Thursday on the corner of the Sarphatipark, and is read most carefully by people who have already read everything else. Copies of the previous 1,973 numbers are at the museum, behind the silver. The front page of the web, where this sheet is also pinned up, is at Webstuffs.
