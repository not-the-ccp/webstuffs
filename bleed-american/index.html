EXCERPT · PART 7 OF 11

SUPERIOR COURT OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA — COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES

DreamWorks Recording Corporation
v. Jimmy Eat World, d/b/a “Jimmy Eat World”; Jim Adkins, Ryan Shuck, Tom Linton; and Mark Trombino

Case No. CV010123 · Civil, Title & Injunction · Hon. M. S. Carraway presiding

VERBATIM RECORD, EXCERPT — DAY TWO OF TRIAL, TUESDAY, 14 MARCH 2001 · BEGAN AT 9:12 A.M., HALL 12

APPEARANCES FOR THE PLAINTIFF FOR THE DEFENDANTS
STENOGRAPHER Ms. Dana Okafor, Esq., Whitfield & Marsh LLP, Century City Mr. Saul Bernstein, Esq., Bernstein & Poe, Silver Lake

1 THE COURT: The record is open, part seven. Counsel will state for the

2 record the purpose of this day’s proceedings.

3 MS. OKAFOR: On behalf of the plaintiff, your Honor, we are here over the

4 working title of the defendant band’s fourth studio album. The plaintiff’s

5 contention is simple: the band has been circulating the title Bleed American

6 on pre-release press sheets since January, and the plaintiff asks the Court

7 to confirm which party may use that title on the finished album, slated for

8 release on 24 July 2001.

9 MR. BERNSTEIN: On behalf of the defendants, we will not litigate what the

10 band calls the song. We will, however, litigate the facts of how it was made.

11 Because, your Honor, if the Court wants a story, the story starts in late

12 1999, when the defendants’ previous label, Capitol Records, released the

13 band from their contract. The third album, Clarity, of 1999, had not sold. The

14 band toured for the next eleven months to raise its own studio money.

15 The Court may note that the band’s frontman, Mr. Jim Adkins, has testified

16 that he took a day job at the hardware store on Ince Avenue in Mesa, Arizona,

17 between tour legs. Exhibit A-4 is the employer’s statement.

18 THE COURT: Exhibits A-1 through A-6 are before the Court and admitted.

19 MS. OKAFOR: Your Honor, may we call Mr. Mark Trombino, the producer?

20 THE COURT: You may call your witness.

(MARK TROMBINO IS SWORN.)

21 MS. OKAFOR: Mr. Trombino, you produced the album for the defendants, is

22 that correct?

23 MR. TROMBINO: It is.

24 MS. OKAFOR: And you will tell the Court where and when the tracking was done.

25 MR. TROMBINO: We tracked from the first week of October through the end of

26 November, 2000, in Tucson, Arizona. Two rooms, really. The old Cherokee

27 studio on Park Avenue for the guitars, and Harddrive studios across the

28 county for the vocals. The band produced it with me — we all signed the

29 session sheet as producers, four names and my name on line one of it. That

30 sheet is Exhibit A-2.

31 MS. OKAFOR: And the title of the album — who selected it?

32 MR. TROMBINO: The band. The song “The Middle” went to No. 1 on the modern

33 rock charts, and the title came from a line they had been passing around the

34 bus since the spring tour of 2000. It was on the jacket art from March.

35 MR. BERNSTEIN: Objection, your Honor, to the chart reference, it is hearsay,

36 and I will renew it if the witness volunteers any more airplay figures.

37 THE COURT: Overruled, the song is within the scope. Proceed, but stay on the

38 sessions.

(A SIDE DISCUSSION IS HELD, OFF THE RECORD. THE COURT RECONVENES AT 10:41 A.M.)

39 MS. OKAFOR: Your Honor, the plaintiff wishes to introduce Exhibit P-3, the

40 inventory card of the Hall of American Popular Sound at the Tucson civic

41 museum, compiled 1998, cataloguing the equipment of the recording industry.

42 It came into our possession in discovery — the museum lends its old

43 turntable cabinet to the Harddrive studio as a backdrop, and the card

44 travelled with it. It contains one line which we ask be read into the record

45 exactly as written on the card. Ms. Carver, the plaintiff’s records clerk,

46 will read it.

47 MS. CARVER (reading): “The museum’s catalogue numbers follow the river had taken his hat and his career.”

48 MS. OKAFOR: I will not explain the line, your Honor. The plaintiff simply asks

49 that it be entered, because the plaintiff intends to show the band recorded

50 part of Exhibit A-2, the session sheet, in front of that very backdrop, and

51 the card is on the wall behind them in the photograph, Exhibit P-3(b).

52 MR. BERNSTEIN: Objection. The line means nothing. I move the Court to strike

53 it as immaterial and confusing.

54 THE COURT: The Court will hear the proffer in chambers and return. Counsel

55 may continue with the witness. Mr. Trombino, the album was re-issued in

56 November of last year — no, on the record: the album, as the public knows

57 it after the events of 11 September 2001, appears under the band’s own

58 name, without the working title. When did that decision happen?

59 MR. TROMBINO: That was the band’s call and the label’s, together. We pulled

60 the jackets, repressed the discs, and the reissue went out in November under

61 the name Jimmy Eat World. The title went back on the shelf. It came back

62 to the public later, in 2008, when it was pressed again with the original

63 title restored. But that is after the Court’s clock, your Honor.

64 MS. OKAFOR: On cross, may I — no, cross will be for the afternoon. The

65 plaintiff rests its morning witness.

(RECESS FOR LUNCH, 11:52 A.M. TO 1:15 P.M.)

(THE COURT RECONVENES. THE CHAMBERS RULING ON EXHIBIT P-3 IS READ INTO THE RECORD.)

66 THE COURT: The Court has considered the proffer. The line from Exhibit P-3

67 is admitted as a fact of the photographic backdrop only. It is not admitted

68 for the truth of anything it might be read to say. The catalogue card stays

69 in the exhibit, and the hat, and the river, and the career, stay on the card.

70 Cross-examination of Mr. Trombino will proceed in the afternoon, and the

71 defendant’s witness, the drummer Mr. Tom Linton, will be called at 2:00.

72 THE COURT: The record is closed for the morning at 12:03. Part eight will

73 open at 1:15 this afternoon.
