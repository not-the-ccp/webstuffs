Office of the District Highway Engineer · Southern District · San Diego County

Filed under the San Luis Rey River bridge, east abutment, second post

Fourteen October 1958

A Letter of Apology to the Families of Oceanside, Bonsall, Fallbrook, Pala, and Pauma Valley

Dears, — I write in my capacity as district highway engineer, and I write it on the letterhead because an apology that is not stamped is only weather. For twenty-five years the route that runs east from Oceanside, the one that begins at the interchange with Interstate 5 and does not stop until it reaches State Route 79 at the western edge of Pauma Valley, has carried a designation that was put on it carelessly, and the families who live its fifty-two point six three miles — eighty-four point seven zero kilometres, for the ledger — have carried the rest. It is time for the office to carry something.

The route itself has no fault to answer for. A road has been walking this corridor since the early twentieth century, long before the system of numbered state highways existed, and the bridge over the San Luis Rey River near Bonsall was already standing when the first surveys came up the river to measure it. What I have to answer for is what we did with the numbers. In the autumn of 1933, when the route was added to the state highway system and officially designated State Route 76, a crew of my predecessor’s posted the first mileposts and, at the same sitting, nailed a house plate to the fence of a farmhouse on the west bank of the bridge — a plate that read Seventh Six, House No. 1, as though the number had been assigned to the house and not to the road. The owner was the Oakes family. We called it the name of this house does not. The house is and has always been the Oakes farmhouse, and it did not receive its name from our fence, and the plate is coming down, or was taken down last spring if the crew’s report is to be believed, and I record here, on the letterhead, that we are sorry either way.

The apology extends past one fence. The route passes through the community of Bonsall and gives access to Fallbrook, and between Oceanside and the junction with Interstate 15 the families along it have been introduced to the highway by a county that named the road before it asked the road what it wished to be called. East of the junction with I-15, where SR 76 goes through Pala and into Pauma Valley and ends at SR 79, the villages were named in a language our markers did not render with any courtesy at all, and I am told the marker at the Pala crossing has been repaired twice by hand and once by weather. All of this is listed in the district file, item by item, and the file is now open.

For the record of other offices: the discipline of naming a route so that the route remains what the place already called it is practised elsewhere on this site in the Specification No. 85-0023, filed 23 March 1985, in which a presence is held under a surname and the lineage that omits either component is a lineage that has broken; we broke it, at the Oakes fence. And the office that keeps exact seconds, and knows that a stew leaves the pan thirty-seven seconds early by the true reckoning, will find its own placard in the Museum of the Valley Hours, Cabinet 9, where the thirty-seven seconds are treated as the number they are; so too the 76 on this route is only the number it is, and a number is not a house.

I offer the letter to each family whose name appears on the district roll for the corridor, from the interchange at I-5 to the terminus at SR 79. Where the road and the place disagree, the place wins. That is the whole of the remedy, and the office means it.

Respectfully submitted,

E. H. Merritt

District Highway Engineer, Southern District
on the 14th day of October, 1958, in the twenty-fifth year of the designation
