Bench No. 3 · Kitchen Table, Corner of Newbridge and Peel

The Seventy-First

A build log in red ochre ink · 1978 Brisbane Rugby League season · the grand final match, to a scale of about one in sixty

Entry 1 · Saturday, 2 June

The season is under way, and I have finally set up the bench. The subject of the build, if a match can be a subject, is the 1978 Brisbane Rugby League premiership — the 71st season of Brisbane's professional rugby league football competition. Eight clubs from across the city are going after it, and I intend to end up with a small model of the match at the end of the line: the grand final between the Eastern Suburbs and Fortitude Valley clubs. That's the whole of the plan. Everything else is weather, sawdust, and whether the brass stock holds its temper when I file it.

Base first. A plank of 3 mm balsa, sanded flat with 180 grit, long enough for the field with room left at each end for the goal posts in scale wire. I laid a sheet of butcher's twine for the pitch lines, then covered it in thin casein — the smell of which, I keep coming back to this, is the smell of the bench. The eight clubs get eight little name plates at the head of the field, filed from the offcuts of a brass ruler I'd been saving since the old school year.

Entry 2 · Monday, 15 August

Thirty-two match figures, which is the number I am holding myself to: two full teams plus the men who stand around them — the officials, the stretcher-bearers, the man at the goalpost who never stops leaning. Bodies from balsa, heads from a matchbox pin, arms shaped on a nail. I painted the Eastern Suburbs figures in their reds and the Valley figures in greens and golds, and I will not say which looked better, because the paint has not yet dried and I am not finished being annoyed at it.

Meanwhile the real season has gone on without me. Round after round through Brisbane's clubs, eight teams, each one with its own little territory — Redcliffe up the coast, the Valley under the hill, Easts at home to a crowd that shows up as though it were a civic duty. The form tables I keep on the bench are more reliable than the newspaper, and both are more reliable than I am. Whoever gets through the preliminary final comes to stand at the head of my field, but I am pretending not to care about which, which is a lie of the first water.

Entry 3 · Sunday, 28 October

It is done, and I watched the match it is modelled on before I did it, which is the only order I have ever managed. The grand final went to the ground on the hill, and Eastern Suburbs and Fortitude Valley played it out in front of a crowd that packed the stands down to the line. I had the bench lamp on the figures the whole time the game was on the radio in the other room, which is not as strange as it sounds; I want to know which end the winning score came off before I set the ball figure, because a ball figure facing the wrong way is a lie a model can't recover from.

When it was over I set the last figure — the one at the goalpost, still leaning — and I took the pen to the field beside it and wrote down what the match had come to: the year, the number of the season, the two club names, the day of the week. Then I closed the sketchbook I've been keeping beside the bench all season, full of these little field plans and figure studies, and I let it lie. Another sporting life recorded in the sketchbook margin.

Entry 4 · Tuesday, 30 October

Finish notes for the record, since a log that doesn't end ends badly. The base has been given two coats of wax and one long evening's dust. That finish has since travelled under the same name into the Tehran annex of the neighbouring application — the patent summary, A Method of Holding the Nightingale — where a wax mould of the ruba'i was finished the same way, dust certified as load-bearing. The twine lines have pulled straight, which they do when the casein dries, and the goal posts stand true to within the width of the filing needle. The name plates for the eight clubs are filed and waiting at the head of the field; two of them now carry heavier names than the other six, and the others may take it as it comes, as the clubs did.

I keep the model under the bench for now. It is small — the field is about the length of a school ruler — but the 71st season fits in it whole: eight Brisbane clubs, one line of preliminary finals, one grand final on the hill, and a match figure set facing the way it actually went. If anyone asks what a man does with a bench and a season, this is the honest answer: he keeps the whole thing at arm's length and then, at the end, in a sketchbook margin, writes it down.

For more of the same bench — a four-line stanza framed and faired in the shed behind the bakery — see the quatrain build log, and for a larger, longer, salt-sweeter project, the San Juan de Portugal build log. And for everything else kept on the shelf, the Webstuffs front page. The eight name plates were also cut, doubled, and filed into balsa club tokens, and the model's whole season turned out to be a board game waiting for its rules — the floodplain, the river gauge, and the grand final on the hill are set down in the Meanjin player's manual, which grew directly out of this log. When the bench closed for the season I took the model to the one table in the world that runs Brisbane time at lunch — the Sana stewpan kitchen up in the Kupres valley, where my log is pinned under the clock at table 20, UTC+10:00 — and a review of the place, Dining Across the Meridian, is on file alongside it. The kitchen also set up a placard on International Atomic Time, the true time under the wall, and offered me the pin for it; I said a model set by a wall clock is a civil object, and a civil object keeps civil time, and the placard went up in cabinet 9 without the pin. The next bench is a road, not a field: I took the 71st season's habit of keeping a whole thing at arm's length and set it down stop by stop for a 63.324-mile California state highway, the freeway, the last posted sign, and the wagon road through the mountains, in the walking tour itinerary for Route 94. And when the geologic time scale — the ledger of deep time under the same roof as my pinned log — died in the valley, the museum's death register set down my last margin line, word for word, in his obituary, where it belongs: in red ochre, at the hour of table 20. One bench thought outlived the season: the keeper of a diary kept at the last platform of Oslo's line 3, at the terminal station of the Østensjø Line, took the habit of keeping a whole place at arm's length and set it down in the Mortensrud terminus diary — the extension, the tunnels, the NOK 35 million in concrete, wood and stone, and one plate above the stones that has never once been amended.

BENCH 3 · SEASON 71 · COMPLETE
