The Laconia Ledger · Suncook Valley, New Hampshire · Saturday, 23 May 1868 · Price Five Cents · the column as kept, not as sold

Small Ads

Sheet No. 42 · kept in the back room of the Laconia National Bank · answers on the second Thursday · double line for the state and single line for the trend

The desk keeps this column the way it keeps the flitch line at the back of the register: unstruck, unamended, and never explained twice. It ran in the Ledger through the winter the impeachment was on, and the hand of the advertiser in the second ad is the hand of the president — Andrew Johnson (29 December 1808 – 31 July 1875), the 17th to sit the chair of the United States, in it from 1865 to 1869, the 16th to have sat at its right hand, a War Democrat who ran with Abraham Lincoln on the National Union ticket of 1864 and took the chair after the April night, in the last months of the war. He favored the quick restoration of the seceded states without protection for the newly freed people who had been enslaved, and he pardoned ex-Confederates; the Republican Party’s Congress met him at the table, the House impeached, and the column below is what the paper printed while the Senate was sitting as a court. The desk reads it once, then the column does the rest of the remembering.

For Sale

PIERCE & SCALES, Restorers, 14 Main Street, Laconia. We restore what has been seceded: houses, households, parlour furniture, and, at the client’s discretion, states. In this year of Restoration the trade is brisk; the firm has put six seceded states back to the Union in the client’s name, and will vouch only for the varnish. No opinion is held or asked on the client. Terms: cash, or a flitch, brushed over the fire. — P.&S.

Repairs · Alterations

A. JOHNSON, TAILOR, 3 Court Street, Laconia; formerly Greenville, Greene County, Tennessee. A tailor by trade since 1826 — mending, patching, taking in of trousers, cuffs, and coats, the union held where the union holds. A War Democrat, who ran with the late President Lincoln on the National Union ticket of 1864, was the 16th vice president, and is, since the April night, the 17th president of the United States, and takes in, for the usual fee, the pardon of old grievances. Restoration of the seceded state is a kind of mending the trade does not usually print; the advertiser prints it anyway, because the trousers have to come out right. Who he is, by his own hand.

To Let

MRS. C. HALLOWELL, 9 Bridge Street, Augusta — the smallest capital in the Union, and the smallest the desk has ever ruled as a kindness — to let, two rooms over the harbor, with board, to a gentleman who wishes no question asked at breakfast about the president. Supper at 6; no answer required at the table. The border is eleven miles over the hill, and the desk that answers it keeps its sheet on Maine at Sheet No. 41, in the same back room as this column.

The Supper Room · Lakefront

To the summer people whom the 6:15 used to put down on the promenade at Hyde Park, Chicago: the supper room reopens for the 1868 season — same floor, same windows, the piano between the tea and the supper, the lake still moving out of sight of the room but not out of its account of itself. The new hand is instructed from the old keeper’s card, which the ledger holds, word for word:

“Tie them down carefully, so that the 6:15 brings the table every night — five stars, no further comment.”

Which is to say, in the keeper’s own practice, what he meant and what he kept: the cloth to the table, and the train of the express to the station, in such order that the table comes in at the stroke and nothing is argued about afterward; the five stars are the card’s, and the comment is, in the house, none. The lot the room stood on, and the ledger it kept, are sold whole, annexed into Chicago on 1 December 1889, and filed as Lot 1, Product Sheet No. CSE-0001 of the Calumet Shore catalogue, where the border belongs to the buyer the way a name belongs to a family.

Lost

LOST, or carried off, at the naming of the sloop Anna L., Downeast yard, the spring of 1867: one (1) flitch of oak, smoke-sealed, the one that was to be the whale’s. The boat’s name is remembered by all the same, and the flitch owes its place by the fire. Reward to the finder; no flitch is asked of the finder, and the line at the back of the register has been read once, so the column will not explain it again.

Personal

To the War Democrat, and to the Congress that ran with him in 1864 and then met him at the table: the desk answers the pronunciation first, every time. AUGUSTA, in the capital’s own spelling, is not the word in question. MAYN is pronounced MAYN, and the stress goes on the only syllable it has. A name is a door; the desk will not open one with the wrong knock, and it will not open the one this column keeps with any knock at all. Answers on the second Thursday.

Voted · the Smallest Capital

QUESTION put before the people of Augusta at the second Thursday: whether the border desk should rule the impeachment into its register, double line for the state and single line for the trend. ANSWER, on the border’s own rule: yes, from the near side of the window, and ruled into the sheet, not the air. Those who object may object across the window; the kettle will have heard it either way.

By Order

ACQUITTED. The Senate, sitting as a court, the Chief Justice in the chair, has found the president not guilty on the eighth article, 35 to 19 — the two-thirds the court required were one vote short, and the margin will not be argued here. The president goes on to the March of 1869, the House that impeached keeps its papers, and the Senate that acquitted keeps its receipt. Both of them are in this column’s keeping now, and neither will have the last ad. The line is patient.
