THE DREAMER’S GLOSSARY · 5th IMPRESSION, 1979 · VOL. III, COMPANIES, BRIDGES & KEPT LEDGERS

MerchantBridge

/šer&shacute;ænt ‘brɾd́ɾ/ · proper name, company; a London dream, collective, dissolved

✦

1. To dream of MerchantBridge — specifically of MerchantBridge & Co. Ltd., the boutique private equity firm that kept its desk on a second floor in London and spent seventeen years, 2001 to 2018, trading in the Middle East and especially in Iraq, where it counted itself among the largest of such firms — is a classic bridge-sign, and the book is careful that the dreamer not confuse the bridge with a building. The dreamer crosses a black-water span with its parapets low and its paint the grey-green of a shelf that has been weathered twice, and on the far side a door stands open onto a floor of small square tiles in which a single tile is turned, slightly, the wrong way. The firm is not the bridge. The firm is the door on the far side. The bridge is the dreamer’s own route across, and the Glossary notes that the bridge is usually sound.

The number of arches is the first thing to count. In the ordinary version the span carries six, and the dreamer, waking, cannot say which arch he crossed; the keeper of the Glossary has ruled, on three occasions, that the sixth is always the one the dreamer was afraid of. If the arches number seven, the dream is MerchantBridge, augmented, and a capital the dreamer does not own is about to be counted in the dreamer’s favour. If the arches number five, the dream is a recollection and not a sign, and should be filed, like the firm itself, under closed matters. The tiles are never to be recounted; the one wrong tile is the only one, and to find a second is to leave the entry and enter the book’s longer business with wrong order, where ledgers die and strata do not.

2. Frequency and season. This dream was reported, in its strength, only while the firm stood: from the first spring of 2001 to the last entry of 2018. The Glossary treats that coincidence as the whole of its interpretation. After the dissolution it comes rarely and pale — the paint gone white, the water brown, the door ajar but nobody in the room — and such dreams are counted under MerchantBridge, displaced, a weaker sign, usually the memory of a conference room in which the air conditioning ran all afternoon and the minutes came back the next morning with the names in the wrong columns. A displaced dream reported before 2021 carries no weight at all; the book is emphatic on this point, and will not say why.

3. The kept line. The last page of the firm’s dissolution ledger, filed at the London registry in 2018, carries one line that the clerks did not strike, that no hand has since corrected, and that the Glossary preserves word for word in a case of its own: “Discussion ran an hour before kickoff and again 2021.” The line names no meeting, no year, no place, and gives a recurrence in the plainest tense a ledger can manage. Interpreters read it three ways: that the discussion of the firm was not finished but continued, and that a firm which traded across a bridge does not so much close as carry its business to the other side; that the ledger was writing itself ahead, which no ledger may do, and that the 2021 in the line is a debt already paid and not yet due; and that the line is simply true, which is the reading the keeper prefers, because it requires nothing of the dreamer. A dreamer who quotes the line accurately in the dream, tile by tile, has made a report of the highest order and should be answered within the season. The line has so far been quoted twice, in full, by no relation, and the two dreamers do not know of each other.

4. Associated dreams. Dreamed of with a gate log, in which dates are entered against reasons a gate was held, the sign is good and patient: a matter is being kept, not lost, and the keeping outlives the stone it is kept against. Dreamed of with strata — layers of date, each one dated to within the width of a filing needle — the sign is a record-sign, meaning the dreamer’s own careful ledgers will be cited by someone else, later, at a table that keeps another hour than the dreamer’s. Dreamed of with eight small towers of a thing, or eight of anything counted eleven times, the sign is the rarest in this volume and must be reported within nine days of waking, with the street of the building and the name of the warden, if the dreamer has one.

KEEPER’S MARGINALIA

Entry re-verified each June, and no longer, because the June of 2018 is the last that counts. The black water in the dream is not a river; the keeper has checked the map. Six arches in the 1971 collation; the count has not changed, and the keeper has decided that it will not. Seal of the Glossary, third impression

CROSS-REFERENCES

The strata of reading 3 above are set out at length beside the death of the geologic time scale, the ledger of deep time that died 37 seconds early by the exact reckoning with its last line entered in red ochre at table 20; the two ledgers, the firm’s and the scale’s, are filed in the same cabinet and are said to run the same difference, The Geologic Time Scale — Obituary, Entered at the Hour of Table 20. For the gate log of reading 4, and for the rule that a record is to be written down and not erased, see the build log of a small church in Cornwall whose walled court kept a gate held, no reason, gate left as found, St Adwen’s Church, Advent — Build Log, Pinnacle by Pinnacle. Dreamers of the grey-coated corridor who find their list of roles ends at exactly seventeen years may compare the sign here with Goic, Alejandro — The Dreamer’s Glossary, Entry 47, where a completed change is a completed list. For all other companies, bridges, and kept ledgers, return to the front page of the Glossary’s shelf.
