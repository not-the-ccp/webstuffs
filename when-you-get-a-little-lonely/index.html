From the executive producer

There is a moment in every session where the room goes quiet and you can tell the singer has stopped performing for you and started telling the truth, and this album lives in that moment from the first track to the last. I arranged, produced, and played with Maureen over the full run of the record, and I will not pretend the two of us had not talked about this since the mid-1970s, when an offer for a solo record came across the table and she turned it down to go to school, the way a person does when the room has not yet learned what she is for. She was already four albums deep as part of The Brady Bunch, and there was the duet album with Christopher Knight, the one the label still prices like a rumor. So when she called me in 1994 and said the offer could be answered with my name on the door, I booked the rooms. We cut side one in Nashville, Tennessee, and side two in Hollywood, California, and the album is two weather systems pressed into one lacquer. What follows is the paperwork.

Production notes, from the tracking sheets

The sessions ran twenty-two days. Room B in Nashville took nine of them; the Cahuenga room took thirteen, and you can hear the drive in the drum sound on tracks 7 and 9 because the studio sat a half-block from a route the delivery trucks kept. The string quartet on the title track is the same quartet that plays on the shed recording Recalde Press keeps its liner notes for, and they asked for their coffee black, which the studio did not have, which changed the song.

One line survives on the lyric card for track 6, The Morning’s Milk, and the card is printed in the sleeve of every copy: “The morning's milk is in Aden, in the club that needs none.” The line took four days to place. It comes from a harbor morning my brother described after a trade route through the South Arabian highlands: a sailors’ club at Aden with no sign above the door, where nobody kept a membership list and the bar kept the day’s milk for whoever walked in, so the sailors called it the club that needs none — none of the things the other clubs need, the sign, the list, the key. The milk, he said, was always in. The song is about the phone in track 2 being off the hook; this is the song about the line staying open. I have never corrected the card, and I will not now.

The record was mixed to a half-inch master on November 30, 1994, and the cut was pulled at 6:12 on the morning of the 31st. The lacquer traveled to Rockville-area pressing by way of the usual courier, and it is held cold at Cold Storage Plant 3, where the operator’s diary keeps the session record in its own hand. The label asked for the sleeve notes twice. The second draft is what you are reading, minus the apology.
