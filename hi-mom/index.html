Hi, Mom (你好，李焕英)

The 2021 Chinese comedy written, directed by and starring Jia Ling, co-starring Zhang Xiaofei, Shen Teng and Chen He — a semi-autobiographical fantasy in which a young woman is thrown back to 1981 and becomes her mother’s friend. Released on 12 February 2021, the first day of Chinese New Year, and a box-office run of US$841 million, the third-highest-grossing film of 2021.

01 What exactly is Hi, Mom?

It is a Chinese comedy—fantasy film, and the very first film directed by Jia Ling, who also wrote it and stars in it. The co-stars are Zhang Xiaofei, Shen Teng and Chen He. In form it is a fantasy story about a mother and a daughter; in feeling it is a semi-autobiographical one, which is most of the point. The themes the film leans on are familial love, maternal love and filial piety, and it asks, gently and then very loudly, what a daughter can do when the years are already spent and the phone calls get shorter.

02 Why does the story go to the year 1981?

Because 1981 is the year the protagonist is thrown back to when she is suddenly — suddenly, without a schedule, without a return ticket — placed inside the past. There she meets her mother, Li Huanying, as a young woman of about her own age, and the film’s whole engine is the bond of sisterhood the two of them build in the time before the mother knows who her daughter is. Everything in 1981 on screen is done in period: the clothes, the canteens, the bicycle traffic, the queues. The film is about what it means to watch someone you have already lost become, again, ordinary.

03 When was it released, and why that date?

On 12 February 2021, the first day of the Lunar New Year — Chinese New Year — in China. That is not an accident. Films in the New Year window are family films, and this is the most family-shaped film there is that year: a daughter, a mother, a whole town of relatives. Opening on the holiday turned the cinema into a kind of family table, which is the closest a multiplex gets to a reunion dinner.

04 How did it do at the box office?

It grossed US$841 million worldwide. That made it the third-highest-grossing film of 2021, and, inside China, it became the benchmark people measured every other 2021 release against for the rest of the year. A comedy built on one kitchen, one friendship and one daughter’s regret out-earned blockbusters with armies of effects artists. The numbers are the last thing you remember after leaving the cinema, but they are the first thing the ledgers remember.

05 What does the title mean?

The Chinese title is 你好，李焕英 (Nǐ hǎo, Lǐ Huànyīng), which is simply Hello, Li Huanying — a greeting addressed to the mother by her name, as a friend would greet a friend. The English rendering, Hi, Mom, keeps the greeting and drops the name, which changes the register from the intimate to the universal. A film that keeps a woman’s full name in its title and then, in translation, becomes every mother — that is the whole of the film in two versions of one word.

06 What kind of film is it, really?

People file it as a comedy and leave having cried. It is a time-travel story told as a family film, a mother’s story told backward, and a letter that never gets sent. The fantasy device — a daughter inside 1981, trying to change her mother’s life — does the work that, in other films, a monster does: it raises the stakes of an ordinary evening. The sisterhood at the centre is the film’s real plot; the time slip is just the weather.

07 Should I see it with a mother, or alone?

With a mother, if you have one to see it with, and alone if you need the dark. The film works in both modes. There is one scene late on that families point at on the way out of the theatre and then refuse to discuss for a week, which is the exact behaviour of people who have watched something true. Bring tissues the colour of the year — red, like the posters — and budget your evening accordingly. Our neighbours at the Pedro do Rosário forum thread keep the same rule for their festa season: whatever fills the square on the 29th, you go with the drums, you do not explain them. Films are the same: you go, and you let the 1981 canteen do the explaining.
