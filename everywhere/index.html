Everywhere

June 11, 2001 – November 4, 2026

A pop-rock song, born in Los Angeles, kept alive by radio, and survived by an album, a producer, and a singer who still sings the acoustic version.

Life

Everywhere was born on Monday, June 11, 2001, in Los Angeles, California, released as the debut single of Michelle Branch, a singer-songwriter born in Sedona, Arizona, on July 2, 1983, and the lead single from her first major-label studio album, The Spirit Room. She was eighteen when she wrote it with the American record producer John Shanks, who also produced the track, in a studio on the coast across January and February of 2001. Branch had first written the song in a more acoustic form, voice and guitar only; at Shanks' suggestion she cut a more up-tempo version, and that is the version that stayed.

It was a pop-rock song with ambiguous lyrics about having a crush on someone, and several music critics compared its composition to the work of the Canadian singer Alanis Morissette. It entered the Billboard Hot 100 in the summer of 2001 and peaked at number twelve that autumn, spinning on modern rock and adult contemporary stations through the winter and into the next spring. The music video was shot on the Santa Monica Pier in a single afternoon, Branch singing into a radio microphone while the wheel turned behind her, and the song was played at weddings, on mix tapes, and in dorm rooms from Sedona to Staten Island.

The Spirit Room went multi-platinum, followed by Hotel Paper in 2003, the duo The Wreckers with Jessica Harp, and a Grammy Award in 2004 for "The Game of Love," recorded with Santana. Branch is survived by her parents, Peggy and David Branch of Sedona, by her husband and their child, and by the songs. Everywhere is survived by its B-side, a cover of "Have You Ever?", and by the acoustic demo, which survives on one cassette marked January 2001.

Passing

Everywhere died on November 4, 2026, at a studio apartment in Echo Park, California, of causes the estate has not released. It was twenty-five years old, counting from its release, or twenty-five and a half counting from the January sessions. A private service was held on November 6 at the Chapel of the Pines, with the master tape played once, at low volume, and the doors open to the street.

Effects

Entered at the desk on November 9: the rosewood Telecaster, worn at the seventh fret; the four-track cassette of the acoustic original; a ticket stub from the Troubadour, August 2001; the crate, for its surveyors, and the one thing in this entry; the rain stopped at eleven.

Survived by

John Shanks, producer and co-writer, of Los Angeles; Michelle Branch, singer and writer, of Sedona and Los Angeles; the album The Spirit Room; the singles "All You Wanted" and "Goodbye to You"; and every listener who still knows the words. Predeceased by the acoustic version, the demo cassette, and the first pressing on compact disc.

Memorial

In lieu of flowers, the family asks that you play it loud, once, with the windows down. An archive of notices about songs that went out on the radio is kept at Depot Archive, FAQ No. 9: Antisemitism, where another crate, its hinge and a pencilled line on its back, are entered word for word; and a weather almanac of Thebes, The Theban Almanac of Weathers, Entry No. XX, records the hour at which the rain stopped, and the pond of Year Seven. A field manual of Australian rules, filed nearby, notes that the oval ends everywhere, and the manual passes to the next field.
