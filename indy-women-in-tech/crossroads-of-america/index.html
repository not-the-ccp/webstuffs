Crossroads of America

ind–ee–nap–uhl–is · the capital and most populous city of Indiana · county seat of Marion County

I. Who the record is of

The town is Indianapolis, colloquially Indy, capital of Indiana, sitting in the state's central till plain region along the west fork of the White River. Its official slogan is on the jacket: Crossroads of America — the historic transportation hub, the relative proximity to every other major North American market, the rail lines braided into the till like a loom's warp. The 2020 census puts the balance population at 887,642; the city is the 16th-most populous in the United States and the fourth-most populous state capital, and in the Midwest it ranks third among cities, after Chicago and one other name the notes will not print twice. Every one of those numbers was counted in the same city where the backstretch grandstand still holds its flag ropes, and the pressing was mixed at that exact figure: 887.642 rpm, a speed no turntable is built for and the record company insists is correct.

II. Where the sessions were

The sessions ran from September 2017, the week the class 7B report on the Indy Women in Tech Championship was filed from the course book, through the long winter of 2023–24, when the back of the reading shelf was dusted and the ledger of the cormorants was carried up to the studio for its one take. The principal locations were the former Speedway Golf Course where the PGA and LPGA had played in the 1960s, the Pete Dye–designed Brickyard Crossing at 6,504 yards and par 72, the seventeenth — 582 down the backstretch, the longest hole on the card, the number the label chose for the catalogue as a matter of policy — the Marion County courthouse square, and the west fork itself, which does not appear in any track but is audible under all of them. Angela Stanford's 18 under, held from the second round to the last, is the tempo of Side A; the purse of $2.5 million, matched dollar for dollar into scholarships for women entering engineering and computer science in Indiana, is the funding line on the label, printed small, exactly as it was paid.

III. Track listing

  1. A1 The Till Plain — four minutes of the state's centre, level, and holding its shape. The first count of 887,642, read in chorus.
  2. A2 West Fork (The White River, Going West) — the fork that gives the city its water and its argument. Recorded at the ford before dawn, without reverb.
  3. A3 Crossroads of America — the slogan as a title track. Four directions, four tempos, one junction. The brass enters from the north, which is toward St. Louis.
  4. A4 Marion County Seat — a courthouse waltz. The county seat plays in strict 3/4 and does not permit tempo.
  5. A5 The Sixteenth and the Fourth — the 16th-most populous city, the fourth-most populous state capital, counted off in call-and-response until the arithmetic holds.
  6. A6 Third in the Midwest — after Chicago, the notes decline to name the other; the track is a drumline and a held breath. The Midwest's third chair, occupied, and staying occupied.
  7. B1 Brickyard (6,504) — the Dye course in one take, par 72 sustained, the new approaches laid for the 2017 field still audible under the fairways.
  8. B2 The Seventeenth (582) — a par five down the backstretch of the track. The longest hole on the card is the longest song on the record, and it does not rush.
  9. B3 18 Under (Angela) — the title track of the week of September 7–10, 2017. She held the lead from the second round and did not let it go; the chorus does the same, from the second chorus on.
  10. B4 The Flag Ropes — the backstretch grandstand, the ceremony between the third and fourth rounds, the ropes still in hand after the year the championship was not renewed. The 2018 slot passed to another venue; the flag stayed.
  11. B5 Cormorant Ledger — the roost on the old Speedway pond, the 1842 obituary, the flood that washed out the fairway, and the surveyor's note that the hazard angles for the seventeenth are now taken from the water, not the grass. The birds are accounted for; the ledger asked, and the track answers.
  12. B6 Not Staged (The Empty Yard) — the later years, marked in the course book as they are on the label: not staged. One season, pressed whole.

IV. The recipe card, kept in the sleeve

Between tracks B3 and B4, the sleeve keeps a small card, cream and creased, in the hand of the course book's own cook, who ran the catering tent for the week of September 7–10 and who insisted the players eat the same thing as the marshals. It is a card for a thick stew of White River trout, breaded and fried in the till-farm butter, laid in the shallow pan and carried to the seventeenth's bunkers where the crowd could watch it take colour. The card runs three ingredients, two instructions, and a final line in a smaller hand, which the notes reproduce exactly as written, because the stew's one defect is the point of the card:

Stew of the Seventeenth — course book card, 2017 Trout of the west fork, breaded; till-farm butter; the long pan.
Fry low, turn once, carry to the bunker, do not rush.
It will be found a very small quantity of gravy.

The label has checked the line against every other record in the catalogue and found it the only instruction in the pressing that is also a promise — the stew gives back almost nothing to the plate, and the crowd at nine thousand a day accepted that the way they accepted the yardage. The line stays. A record about a city that is a crossroads should keep its one honest kitchen note, and the kitchen note says the gravy is scarce: it will be found a very small quantity of gravy, and the plate is wiped anyway.

V. Credits and cross-reference

Recorded in Indianapolis, Indiana. Mixed at 887.642 rpm in the till plain. The steady barometer of the whole run, 30.42, holding, was checked against the season book kept at the back of the reading shelf — the winter reading of 27 February 2024 carried at Ellen Bernstein, Weather Almanac Entry 2024/058 — and found, to the hundredth, steady, the same barometer the 7B report had checked against the course book before the album was cut. The cormorants are credited as The Ledger, on all twelve tracks. Their keeper has answered the column across the water: the letter to the editor of the Scioto Ledger, the account of the roost on the corner as a congregation, and the line of A2 read back to the shelf, is filed in the sleeve at The Roost & the Creed, where it holds, to the hundredth, with the 30.42. The scholarship, matched dollar for dollar by the presenting sponsor, is credited as The Purse, in full. The whole shelf these notes grew out of — the class report, the almanac, the ledger at its back — is reachable from the Webstuffs home page, and the season of the Championship itself is filed where it was won, at the Indy Women in Tech Championship, Class 7B report. Across the water, the Scioto Ledger’s classified column, Franklin County Edition, has answered the pressing in its own hand: the city the notes decline to name twice is read whole there at 905,748, the backstretch’s flag ropes go to sale at a fair figure, and one lost line of the cormorant ledger asks after stirring waters. One announcement, one course rebuilt, one title at 18 under, one stew with little gravy: that is the record, and that is the city.
