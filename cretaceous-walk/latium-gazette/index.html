THE LATIUM GAZETTE

Classifieds · Sheet 84 · Price: one stone of chalk, cut clean · Set in the Italic branch, Indo-European family, Latin roots throughout

LINGUA
LATINA

This sheet is the household's ordinary way of saying what the ledger cannot say in one line. Latin — lingua Latina or Latinum, a classical language of the Italic branch of the Indo-European family — was first spoken by the Latins of Latium, the lower Tiber country around Rome, and travelled out with the Roman Republic, up the Italian Peninsula, and all the way round the Roman Empire, so that the word for the stone we walk on at the Chalk Gate — creta, chalk — has been travelling in the same direction as the party ever since. Through the Christianisation of the Anglo-Saxons and the Norman Conquest it poured into English; its roots turn up all over the technical speech of theology, the sciences, and medicine, which is why the dispensary notice below reads the way it does, and why this sheet, which is a list of small wants, keeps finding itself in a language older than most of the wants. The registrar keeps the back page of the ledger for it, and the ink, as ever, dries slowly.

LOST

One ledger, black cloth, kept word for word.

Last seen at the Boiling House, stop six of the Chalk Circuit, standing open beside a copper that has not been fed or emptied since 13:00, containing the line Dish them on a dish for the boiling is continued. Belonged to the registrar. Answers to “the one that keeps the whole column true” at the back room. Reward: the afternoon's last eggs, plus one for the ledger. No claims from the sea. The sea forgets everything it carries, and it is not being offered the ledger back.

The letter K, small capitals, one cut.

Scratched into the left post of the Chalk Gate by a hand holding a point that was not meant for stone. The cut is clean; the chalk dust still falls in a fine slow snow. Whoever has it can hold it up to the light and see the whole of stop one in it. It will not be replaced. It does not need to be; it is on the post, which is where a K belongs, for Kreide.

FOUND

A lichen-marbled geometrid, wings closed.

Archephanes zalosema, Turner 1926, recovered 2 March 2025 on a century-old season card at the Blue Gum station hut, where it was doing better at the fence post than anyone. Found by E. V. Hartley, who kept it at rest for an hour, and returned to the registrar with a note that reads, in the hand the household recognises: a guide who can keep an insect can keep a party. The moth is released at the gate on the next walk. It is expected to outlast both of them.

Twelve walkers, one of them counting.

Reunited with their kilometre. The party was found at the Longest Room, stop five, spending an hour with the number 77.1, one member counting years under their breath. All accounted for; the counting, the guide observed, is simply the walk said more slowly, and was left in place. The bench inside the signal box, still warm from the morning, is also found, and is not claimed by anyone, which the ledger takes as the answer.

TUTOR & LESSONS

Wanted: a Latin tutor for the party.

The party walks 77.1 million years at one kilometre per ten million and knows the period by its German abbreviation before it knows its own name. Wanted: a tutor who can take creta and make the whole gate out of it, who will not flinch at the Italic branch, who can explain in two lines what the Roman Republic did to a shallow inland sea, and who will accept payment in chalk and slow-drying ink. Apply at the back room before 9:00. No lessons after 16:00; the gate closes, the light goes chalk-white, and the period is walked.

Lessons in the technical root.

Evening classes for theologians, physicians, and field naturalists, on the roots that travel. The course is short and the roots are long: one term per season, each term ends at the boundary clay, and the students are told, as the party is told, that the cases are not continued on this side. Syllabi on request. The syllabus for the current term is a single stone of grey-brown clay, no thicker than a hand, which the tutor will not describe, only show.

FOR SALE

Copper, large, at the temperature of a warm sea.

From the white kitchen of the Boiling House, floor worn pale by generations of the same pots. It has boiled the entire afternoon without once being fed or emptied, which is the only fact the guide would refuse to explain, and it is offered with that fact included, since separating them would be like taking the word off the case. Price negotiable; the price must be paid in bread, hands first, as the household requires. The buyer will be asked to put their hands in, remember the lagoon, and bring the hands to the bread.

Seven point seven one million years, by the measure.

Not for sale. It is the length of the period, it is the longest of all, and it is the ninth geological period of the entire Phanerozoic, which the household has walked in its full length, once, and will not walk again for anyone. It appears here only because a buyer at the gate asked the price, and because the registrar's rule is that anything asked at the gate must be answered on the sheet. The answer is: one kilometre per ten million years, level the whole way, and the sea did the climbing.

DISPENSARY NOTICES

Dr. Pliny's Dispensary, 66 under the gate.

On the preparation of fever-herb of the inland sea, gathered above the waterline, and its administration to a party returning warm from a period that was warm: The species is eatable, but that with two tablespoonfuls of fever-mixture (see above) every four hours. The hours are the walk's hours, not the clock's; every four hours on the circuit is one stop and a half, and the dosage is taken at the stops, standing, on plates, beside a boil that refuses to be finished. Dr. Pliny notes, in the margin of the notice, that the word “eatable” is old and good, that it is one of the roots he teaches, and that a language which can describe a dose of medicine across sixty-six million years of clay can be trusted to describe the dose itself. Consultations by appointment. The lamp is lit whether it is needed or not.

PERSONALS

From the back room, to the party.

It is a fact of record that this language greatly influenced many others, and it is a further fact that the party it is trying to influence on a Saturday morning consists of twelve people, one of whom counts. The back room writes only to say: the gate will be there at 9:00, the lintel is still white, the signal box at stop two is dark for a reason, and the ledger, when it is found, will be kept word for word. Creta is chalk. The chalk is abundant. That is the whole of the personal, and the household files it beside the other lines that do not need a second sentence.

IN MEMORIAM

The Cretaceous, 143.1 to 66 Ma.

Warm, and gone. The sea stood over the land that had forgotten it was land; the plesiosaurs arched their long necks for the horizon; the mosasaurs rode the water like surfboards, and the cases where they are now are dark, and lit from the water side, and the word on each of them is small. The boundary clay is a hand's thickness. The party walked it once, slowly, twelve in number, and the registrar wrote the line that belongs to it: the sea forgets everything it carries. It is asked of the readers of this sheet only that the forgetting be done in order, in the whole, and with the needle down. The next period is not on the schedule.

NOTICES & TRANSFER

Transfer of the household's papers.

Readers wanting the household's other record-keeping may follow the sheet to its neighbours: the Chalk Circuit itself, eight stops from the bakery gate to the boundary clay and back, keeps the route this sheet was set against; the Orbit Sheet, Friday Classifieds, No. 67, keeps the household's other column of small wants, in which 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko, “Chury”, 4.3 by 4.1 km, is listed under in memoriam at the small velocities the sheet is named for; and the front page keeps the register of all of it — the bakery, the lamp, the ledger, and everything filed beside. The household's rule is the same in every language it has ever borrowed: the walk may be walked more than once, the column of distances must stay true, and no stop is amended.
