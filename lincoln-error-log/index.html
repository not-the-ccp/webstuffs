Error Registry · LINC-ERR · Issued by the City of Lincoln, County Town of Lincolnshire

Lincoln, England

A register of known errors, their severities, and their dispositions — kept on the Witham since the Iron Age settlement and updated against the 2021 census return.

01 System Overview

Lincoln is a cathedral city and district in the county of Lincolnshire, of which it is the county town. The city sits on the River Witham, the same slow water that runs north from these streets and empties into the Wash — the great shallow bay whose tides the fishermen below the city read the way this registry reads its entries, once a day and without complaint. The district returned a population of 103,813 in the 2021 census; the urban area, taken to include Bracebridge Heath, North Hykeham, South Hykeham and Waddington, recorded 127,540. Both figures are accepted as-is. No rounding has been applied, and the registry declines to apply any.

The system is old in a way that its own entries cannot fully account for. The city developed from Lindum Colonia, a Roman colony grown out of an Iron Age settlement of Britons on the Witham, sited near the Fosse Way road that ran west from the coast at Lincolnshire's mouth. Through successive settlements — Roman, Anglo-Saxon, and Danish, each of which re-set the ground and left a name on it — the long name was shortened, letter by letter and century by century, to Lincoln. The registry classifies this under ERR-LNC-001 below and considers it the one error the city has never wished corrected.

02 Error Register

CodeMessageSeverityDisposition
LINC-ERR-001 NAME SHORTENING. Original string Lindum Colonia; successive settlements (Roman, Anglo-Saxon, Danish) each truncated the label. Final value: Lincoln. Low Resolved in the 9th century. Kept. Do not restore the long form on street furniture; it will not fit.
LINC-ERR-002 CATHEDRAL HEIGHT. The spire of Lincoln Cathedral, in English Gothic architecture, stood for over 200 years as the world's tallest building. Advisory Superseded by later towers and one very tall church in a foreign city. The cathedral remains the tallest thing on the hill, which is the only claim it now makes.
LINC-ERR-003 NORMAN CASTLE, 11th CENTURY. The stone keep and curtain of Lincoln Castle were set down on the same hill in the 11th century, Norman work, and have been found by every subsequent survey to be still in place. Critical Not correctable. The castle is now a museum of its own persistence. Visitors are admitted; the error is not.
LINC-ERR-004 WITHAM FLOW DIRECTION. The river runs north out of the city rather than to the sea in a straight line, as a river of its size might be expected to do. Low Accepted. The Fosse Way had already set the city's east-west habit of mind; the river declined to argue.
LINC-ERR-005 CENSUS COUNT DRIFT. District count 103,813 (2021); urban-area count 127,540 (2021) once Bracebridge Heath, the Hykehams and Waddington are folded in. The two figures are 23,727 apart and neither is wrong. Advisory Quote both, in that order, with both names. Quoting one without the other reproduces the error.
LINC-ERR-006 SEA IN SIGHT. From the cathedral floor the Wash is close enough to mention and far enough to deny. Witnesses disagree by several miles. Cosmetic Left to the witnesses. The registry records the disagreement, which is a county-town service in itself.

03 Reproduction & Inspection

The errors above reproduce without effort, on any clear day, from the cathedral precincts. Enter by the High Street, take the stone steps up to the cathedral's west front, and observe LINC-ERR-002 from below; the spire does what it has always done and the entry reads itself. Walk the hill to the castle for LINC-ERR-003; the 11th-century Norman masonry is unrepentant and admits the point by remaining. Descend to the Witham for LINC-ERR-004, where the water is doing exactly what it has done since before the Fosse Way was paved, and note that it does not apologise.

Inspect the district's own entry (LINC-ERR-005) at the civic offices in the city, and file your own observation of the Wash (LINC-ERR-006) with the registry, which keeps them all. Observations are read in the same hand that reads the tide tables: plainly, once, and without re-reading.

04 Cross-References

The registry keeps its sister logs in the neighbouring files of this collection. The closest of them is the rigging loft at Saltfleet, eight miles down the coast in the East Lindsey district, whose ledger of whips, tides and standing orders is the county's other long-running document, and whose fishermen read the same firth this city runs down to reach it. Where the loft's ledger keeps games played turn by turn, this registry keeps errors found century by century; both are kept the same way — entered, read once, and not corrected.

05 Verbatim Entry

One line of another registry's record has been transferred here on the standing rule that garbled entries are never corrected, only read. It arrives at the LINC file by way of the Tobacco Trade court record and the stone-circle chapter, and it is kept exactly as found, spacing included:

“The Tobacco Trade , heard on the Porlock Stone Circle , the 90th season of eighteen rows was never sealed for 164 days .”

The line names no county and the registry has declined to assign one. It is shelved here, under the Witham and below the Norman keep, for want of a better place — which is where most of this city's entries end up in the end.
