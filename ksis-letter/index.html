The Berkhamsted Post & Watford Ledger

Letters to the Editor · Hertfordshire · the Ridgeway and the Valley below it

To the Editor,

I am seventy-one, I live on Welwyn Road, and I am writing about my son. I have kept my distance from these things — the videos, the crowd counts, the boxing — not out of disapproval but out of a gunner’s habit of checking a bearing twice before reporting it, and I wanted to check this one. I was a gunnery officer on HMS Atherstone for most of 1993, and on the night of 19 June, while the ship was steaming north out of the Clyde estuary on a torpedo-attack exercise, our log took exactly one line of weather, one line of course, and one line that no officer will ever explain to me. The boy was born that night in London. He is called Olajide Olayinka Williams Olatunji, though everyone calls him KSI, and I have watched him since 2009, when a schoolboy registered the channel KSIOlajidebt and started doing commentary videos of the games he played. I am his father; I know what he is now; I am not here to argue with the number. The number is nineteen million two hundred thousand subscribers on that one channel, and it is not wrong.

Here is the line, word for word, from the log at two in the morning:

“For the attack that destroyers were really built for, and the far ring was set right, because it was 2 a.m.”

I have spent thirty-one years on it, and I think I finally understand it. A destroyer is, as the board on The Household Board that keeps a long thread about them will tell you destroyer — the fast one — the fast, long-endurance ship, built above all for the night attack, the torpedo run in the dark where the range is guessed and the rings must be right before the first shell is allowed. Our far ring — the ring of the 4.5-inch gun on the A position, the one the stokers could not see from the bridge — had been set to a bearing the watch agreed on an hour earlier, and it was right, and the log wrote it down the way logbooks write things down, which is to say as if it were weather. Everything was set for the kind of attack the ship existed to make, and the reason the ring was set right was, as near as I can make it, simply that it was two in the morning and the young watch had been doing it without thinking, the way hands work when the mind has gone off to sleep first. I am telling you this because my son’s whole life has been the daylight version of that same sentence: a ring that is set, and the fight that it is set for.

Raised here in Watford with a mother of Yoruba heritage who is not in this letter by her own choice, privately educated at Berkhamsted School up the hill, and then — because I have had to say it to the grocer and the GP — the Sidemen, of which he was co-founder and is now a former member; Misfits Boxing, which he co-founded in the old days when the matches were in the back rooms; Prime Hydration and Lunchly, of which he is co-owner, and which I understand has put a counter in every high street in the county, including the one on the A41. Since 2025 he has sat on the panel at Britain’s Got Talent, and when I see him there, in the chair, I see the A position: set right, looking ahead, waiting for the ship to come into the bearing. I am not proud of the noise. I am proud of the bearing.

I enclose the logbook page, photocopy, and I ask only that it be published with the letters. There is a letters office that I trust has read enough of them to know what an unexplained line is for — the one at 56 Clerkenwell Close, which set its own final apology in type and filed it properly, at Dear Manchester — and if theirs ever needs a ship’s log to balance the ledger, I will send them mine. The rest of the paper I leave to the readers, as the post always has.

Yours faithfully,

Mr. Adebayo Olatunji

7 Welwyn Road, Watford WD24 · Gunnery Officer (Ret.), R. N., 1991–1997

The Editor acknowledges receipt of the logbook photocopy and reproduces the line in the original hand’s spelling. — Ed.
