Error Documentation · Notice No. 47-C

Error CH-1984 — Attempt Not Confirmed

Kim Chul-hyun · South Korea (KOR) · Men’s middle heavyweight, 90 kg · Los Angeles Memorial Sports Arena · Status: uncorrectable

Error code

CH-1984

Message

LIFT 3 / SNATCH / 196.0 KG — AT LEAST TWO OF THREE JUDGES ARE REQUIRED TO SIGNAL A GOOD LIFT. ONLY ONE SIGNAL WAS RECEIVED ON REEL 4. THE ATTEMPT IS RECORDED AS A MISS, THE BAR IS RELEASED FROM THE REGISTER, AND NO FURTHER WEIGHT MAY BE POSTED AGAINST LIFT 3 FOR ATHLETE KOR-090.

Reported by

Plate-log desk, Los Angeles Memorial Sports Arena — weightlifting hall, session of 1 August 1984, bar position 2, referee’s table row B.

Incident summary

Kim Chul-hyun, a South Korean weightlifter born 25 June 1959, competed at the 1984 Summer Olympics in the men’s middle heavyweight event, the 90 kg class, on the second afternoon session of the competition in the Sports Arena. The national team bus arrived at the arena at 05:40, two hours ahead of the first call, and the athletes were fed, weighed, and logged against the register one number at a time. When the plate log reached athlete KOR-090, the clerk entered the first two snatch attempts without comment and, on the third, paused: the chalked figure read 196.0, a weight above the day’s posted maximum, and the register flagged it pending review.

The bar went up. It was caught, and the arena made its sound — the particular roar a Sports Arena crowd gives when it believes it is about to be corrected. The three judges at the ends of the bar and at the centre sat for the length of a held breath. When the lights came out, only the centre judge’s red had cleared, and the plate log printed its line once and did not reprint it: one signal where two were required. The miss stood. The register closed with him in it, and the competition moved on to the next bar.

From the clerk’s margin note, 1 August 1984: KOR-090 requested a review of the plate weight. Weighed the 15s. The plates are right. It is the lift that is in error.

Troubleshooting

Can the error be cleared by retrying? No. Attempt 3 is the final snatch lift of the cycle; the register accepts no fourth posting for the same exercise, and the 1984 competition itself has not been re-run. The only later attempt at correction was a 1985 letter from the national federation, which the plate-log desk answered by returning the reel number and the day’s chalked figures, both accurate.

Were the plates in error? No. Each 15 kg plate on the bar was weighed against the standard before the session and re-weighed after it. The register holds both weighings; they agree to the nearest tenth of a kilogram.

Where is the record kept? On cassette reel 4 of the Sports Arena plate log, in a drawer under the referee’s table in the weightlifting hall. The drawer is where it is. Enquiries should be addressed to the desk, not to the drawer. In November of this year the desk released the bar of bar position 2 from the register; it is catalogued as item 107.3 of the surplus catalogue, and the sale notice stands at The 1984 Competition Bar — Catalogue Item 107.3, which carries the chalked figures of the session but not the reel.

Margin note — the sketchbook

Kept with the reel is a small watercolour sketchbook that belonged to the arena’s press photographer: one page of the Sports Arena interior in wash, the gantry, the chalk dust in the light, and the bar at position 2 with no one under it. In the margin of that page someone has laid a product slip, in the hand of the Royal College of Podiatry, describing a clinical instrument issued to registered chiropodists. It reads, in full:

It excels in: Calus removal and debridement
Nail avulsion and trimming
Corn and callus reduction
Minor surgical procedures
Routine foot maintenance
Professional Care

Instrument remains in the sketchbook margin.

The slip is a mislaid clipping — the photographer had been standing on both feet through three sessions of deadlifts and snatch and was, by his own later account, in want of a podiatrist — and it is the only object in the sketchbook that does not belong to the arena. The instrument it describes was never present at the bar; it is a slip of paper, and it stays in the margin of the sketchbook exactly where the clerk found it in 1985. That last line above is the desk’s own catalogue note for the slip, written in the same hand as the reel numbers. The slip is catalogued, not filed, and may be viewed with the reel. The Professional Care Instrument page of the Royal College of Podiatry describes the class of instrument the slip advertises, hand-crafted in Birmingham for registered chiropodists and podiatrists across the United Kingdom.

Adjacent records

Error documentation kept in the same drawer: Error LU-1980 — Event Not Held, the voided 1980 Olympic appearance of gymnast Luci Collins, which the desk cross-files under athlete absent, apparatus present; and Transmission — Error TM-2008 | Publication Ceased, for the case where the record itself simply stops being made. The London magazine had printed a brief wire note of the 1984 middle heavyweight session before its final year; the note ends at the second attempt, which is as far as it got. Filed in the same drawer, under the heading closed registers, margin undisturbed, is the encyclopaedia entry on the Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis of 1559 — the two concordats that ended the Italian Wars and left one pencilled line in the dispatch book's margin that the chancery has never been able to explain — which the desk keeps for the same reason it keeps the reel: a record that is accurate, closed, and still not finished. The desk adds, for the same drawer, that the neighbouring register on the stutter line, Norfolk Central to Tim’s Bio Terminus, posts twenty–two nominations called and four lifts confirmed and does not reprint the line — the closest thing in the almanac to this arena’s own one-signal rule, run in reverse — which may be read in the Stutter Line timetable & commentary. In the same drawer, under the heading a day counted to its last line, the desk keeps a family recipe card — the honey and pomegranate cake of the Sulaimanov book, Vedeno Honey Cake — A Family Recipe, Card 11 — a cake that closes one day the way the register closed this one: cut in wedges, served on the twenty-ninth of March, and never reprinted.
