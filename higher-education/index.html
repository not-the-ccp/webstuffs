HIGHER EDUCATION

Also called: post-secondary education · third-level education · tertiary education  NEW STOCK

Supplied by the Carpathian Cellar, Vesterbrogade 47, second floor, Copenhagen, on account of the cellarer of Ripon Abbey, who keeps the caskets and the table.

1. Identification of the substance

The substance identified on this sheet is higher education, an optional final stage of formal learning that occurs after completion of secondary education. It consists of universities, colleges and polytechnics that offer formal degrees beyond high school or secondary school education. It is supplied in nine and a half million portions to the Carpathian Basin, in smaller measures to the chapter houses of Northumbria, and in single servings, on the second floor of a tobacconist in Copenhagen, to anyone whose name is on the list.

Batch reference: 786 — the year the roll of Ripon gives for the death of the blessed Botwine, which is why this sheet bears that number and no other. The substance has been handled by the council without delay since AD 788, and has not changed its constitution. See the Minutes of the Council of Ripon Abbey, AD 788, where the obit was read three times and the vacancy filled in the same breath.

4. First aid

If the substance is inhaled in the fisherman’s-soup concentration, rinse the spoon, take bread on the side, and note that the effect has nothing to do with quantity. If the quotation at the foot of the wine list is swallowed whole, no treatment is indicated; the house keeps no antidote and will not invent one, and the prior of Ripon, who is fond of the cellarer, would let it stand. If a casket of the substance is opened, count the contents once, seal the casket with a disc of red wax, and set the prior’s hand beside the seal in the place of the signature.

5. Storage and transport — the casket of the leaven

The substance is stored in leaven, which is the form in which it raises. The alms of the feast at Peterborough are fixed at two sheep, a cask of ale, and a loaf for each brother — a loaf per brother, not per visitor — and it is the leaven in that loaf which the council of 788 meant, when it sent two envoys south, the chaplain Alhwine and the lay brother Osgyth who knows the water, down the Ouse by barge at the third ebb with the small black-wood casket of silver binding in the well of the boat under a woolen covering. The leaven keeps, the barge sits fast at slack water, and the bar at Peterborough is a bar.

The whole passage, from the gate of Peterborough to speak with the leaven.

That is the line the cellarer set down first in the reckoning, before the numbers, because the numbers come after the business: four days and one night, at a cost of 32 pennies, arrived at the landing by the old causeway at dawn on the thirteenth, and the Ouse, which at Peterborough still remembers the sea, took the casket down to the tide and gave it up. The chaplain, whose hand the line is in, will not say what he meant by playing it out to watch the tides; the cellarer’s opinion, entered in the margin, is that the chaplain was losing cards to Osgyth at the long table, and that the long table is not a place to be generous. The council has not ruled on the matter, and this sheet does not rule on it either.

6. Exposure limits

One count per casket, per lifetime. One name on the list per visit. One loaf per brother per feast. The four-hour simmer is not a limit so much as a wall, and the wall is load-bearing. Exposure of more than four hours to the paprika tins without eating is not advised; the room has twelve tables and the shelves are open and a man has been known to stand among them for an hour with only his eyes working.

One exposure is recorded out of season: a song, Baby Boy by Beyoncé featuring Sean Paul, left playing above the back burner in the 404 Club kitchen in Houston, under a pot the night cook would not explain, came into the office of found things as Notice No. BB-0308, and its margin line — Add salt if it needs only the water — has been entered beside the salt row of this sheet, where it is still open: Lost & Found Notice No. BB-0308, Office of Found Things, Houston.

7. Disposal and final notes

Do not dispose of the substance. It is optional, which is to say it is not going anywhere, and it is the final stage, which is to say there is nothing after it but the street, and the street is where the sentence on the back of the menu carries you. Dispose of the cards, if you were losing them at the long table, by keeping them. The sheet is sealed with the same red wax as the casket, and the cross in the corner of the page is old.
