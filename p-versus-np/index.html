Gazeta da Orquestra · Pedro do Rosário, Maranhão · Cartas ao Editor

To the Editor

Back room of Dona Luzia’s pensão, Rua da Paz · 21 June, after the fan has been fixed and the fan has not been fixed

PEçO
21 VI

Sir,

I write in my capacity as clerk of the Council of the Single Link, the same clerk who took down, in his own hand, the Minutes No. 12 of that body. The standing resolution of the Council, re-adopted every month without amendment, is that any manageable system — a fleet, a workshop, a festa, a cabin mock-up — is limited by a very small number of constraints, and that a chain is no stronger than its weakest link. The Council has now spent two years identifying those constraints, exploiting them, subordinating everything else to them, elevating them, and repeating. What the Council has not spent, I am forced to say, is any time on the question that has eaten the last four dinners at this table: is finding the constraint the hard part, or is recognizing it?

Sir, this is not a question about drums, although it began with drums. It is the great open question of theoretical computer science, known as the problem of P versus NP, and it asks the following: for the class of decision problems in which a proposed positive answer can be checked quickly — quickly meaning an algorithm that runs in polynomial time in the size of the input, bounded above by a polynomial rather than, say, an exponential — can every such problem also be solved quickly? The general class of questions that some algorithm can answer in polynomial time is called P, or class P, and it is the class this bench believes in, because it is the class you can run at dawn before the tide turns. The larger class, the ones whose answers merely verify quickly, is NP, and the whole of mathematics has been holding its breath for forty years on whether the two are the same. A prize in the amount of one million dollars is attached to the answer. Dona Luzia has said, twice, that if it is ever paid, the second cold shower comes off the proceeds, and the Council recorded her as a constraint in the process.

Seu Benedito’s shop by the mangrove settles the asymmetry for us every dry season. Verify a drum: tap it, listen, it is at Orquestra pitch or it is not. Ten seconds, one ear, no polynomial required — the answer checks itself. Find the tension: six goatskins, soaked overnight in estuary water, waiting supple for one pair of hands in the municipality that tensions to pitch. The answer verifies instantly when it arrives, and no one in Pedro do Rosário knows of a recipe for arriving that is faster than the hands. That is NP behaviour in the flesh, and it is why the Council’s second focusing step — exploit, that is, start nothing new until the six are tensioned, because a drum left flat sounds like a tin bucket — is really a statement about the problem class: the constraint cannot be verified faster than it can be found, and so it governs the schedule, the clay pan at Farinha House São Benedito and the fourteen jangadas of Mestre Anselmo’s fleet alike.

It is the same asymmetry the bench in São José dos Campos filed last March under Prototype, Method and System, Provisional 63/214,077. Claim 5 of that summary, which I have read four times because it is the claim I like, is a crossed clearance line, mis-filed on purpose, to prove that the virtual prototype can be wrong in the same way the real one will be. Detecting the crossed line is a matter of the eye; anticipating it, on the next airframe, before the cardstock is cut, is what the million dollars is for. R. V. Driver, who sits on the Council by wire and by bench, put it the way he puts everything: the balsa wingtip that flew off its string during a lunch break was found in the hangar in an afternoon. Finding the string it would fly off of next time is the paper problem, and the paper problem is the whole problem.

The third line

Which brings me to the matter on which I have written you, and which the Council has been unable to close. On 12 March the order-slip for the festa bread came back from the baker with, in addition to the line we have already read into two Councils — “How now in very fine bread crumbs, 1/2 lb.” — a third line, smaller, in the same hand. It has been entered in the ledger of this gazette, and I enter it here, so that the record travels with the letter:

Third among the remaining ingredients; before adding them to table with it, and have rare old vintages of prime old train oil.

The Council’s working reading, adopted after four hours and a cold shower: the third among the remaining ingredients of the batch is the one that goes on the table with it — that is, the batch itself, the lift from the clay pan — and before that, the hands, the rope, and the tensioning bench are to be run through the rare old vintages of prime old train oil kept by the pensão, which is Dona Luzia’s name for the tin of engine grease she does not let anyone touch. Read that way, the line is a procedure. It verifies: follow it, and the batch holds, and the morning’s farinha d’água is white and fine as salt. The open question is whether the line is a solution in class P — an algorithm for the batch that runs in polynomial time in the number of hands — or merely a certificate that someone, in some unknown run, once produced and left in the baker’s hand to be copied. The baker will not say. The baker is the constraint, and the constraint does not interview. On the 28th the pensão tried once more and put the attempt on the air — Back-Room Arithmetic, a radio play in which the clerk teaches the four operations on the festa ledger and the line is finally read, out loud, as a procedure rather than a number, which is the reading the Council now carries.

Sir, I do not expect your columns to solve P versus NP. The Council, which is six people and one fan, has not solved it either, and on the 28th we sit again at dawn for the reading of the tensioned drums, which we describe, in the minutes, as both a constraint check and a rehearsal. I expect only that the question be carried in this gazette the way it is carried on the square: Bumba-meu-boi de Matraca and Bumba-meu-boi de Orquestra, side by side, one of them verifiable by ear in a tap and the other one taking the whole town to find. If the classes turn out to be the same, Dona Luzia’s position is that the town has simply been slow; if they are not, her position is that the town has been right all along, which is the only difference that matters here.

Respectfully yours, for the record and for the square,
the Clerk
farinheiro, Farinha House São Benedito
secretary, Council of the Single Link
P.S. — The tin of prime old train oil is on the second shelf, under the vintages. It is not for the pensão’s stove. This is said once, in this letter, on the record, because the record has no room for a second saying. Also on file: Pedro do Rosário — anyone been?
