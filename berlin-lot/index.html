Description of the lot

Berlin is the capital and largest city of Germany. With 3.7 million inhabitants within its city limits, it has the highest population within its city limits of any city in the European Union; the metropolis is also one of the states of Germany, the third-smallest by area. It is surrounded by the state of Brandenburg, and Brandenburg’s capital, Potsdam, lies to the southwest. The urban area numbers 4.5 million, making it the second most populous in Germany after the Ruhr; the Berlin-Brandenburg capital region stands at around 6.2 million inhabitants, Germany’s second-largest metropolitan region after the Rhine-Ruhr, and the fifth-biggest in the union. The city in its present shape dates from 1 October 1920, the Greater Berlin Act, when Charlottenburg, Köpenick, Spandau and twenty-six other towns and villages were swallowed at a stroke and the population doubled overnight — from Cölln and Berlin, two metropolises, to one.

This lot is the city in six pieces, kept together in a walnut case from a flat at Lehrter Straße 4b, beside Lehrter Bahnhof, and cleared only after the Wall came down.

Contents of the lot, in order

  1. The sedan chair. Charlottenburg Palace, c. 1790. Painted Brunswick green and gold, silk damask lining faded to rose, the pole-sockets worn smooth by two bearers; initials L.U. stamped beneath the seat cushion — Queen Luise of Prussia, who took the air at Charlottenburg each summer until 1810. Family tradition says it carried a princess across the Schlossbrücke on her wedding evening, in 1793.
  2. The flower album. Schlossbrücke, the Palace Bridge, Berlin, 1834–1871. Pressed violets, forget-me-nots and a single pressed rose between the leaves of an old Berlin night-watch ledger, gathered from the bridge’s borders by the watchkeeper’s daughter, one leaf per summer. On the flyleaf, in pencil, a line in an unknown hand: The flowers are not permitted on the bridge until any sedan chair. No gloss survives, and we do not supply one.
  3. The cobble. 18 March 1848, the barricade at Alexanderplatz. A granite sett from the barricade that faced the troops, still carrying traces of tar, with a musket ball embedded in its edge.
  4. The register leaf. Berliner Adreßbuch, 1 October 1920 — the day of Greater Berlin, when the city limits were redrawn and the population stood near four million. The leaf records the new districts: Charlottenburg, Lichtenberg, Neukölln, Spandau and the rest.
  5. The cup. Königliche Porzellan-Manufaktur Berlin, c. 1890. Cobalt underglaze blue, gilt “Berlin” cartouche, a souvenir of the trade exhibition of 1896. Sound.
  6. The fragment. Concrete, sprayed pink at the top, taken at Bornholmer Straße on the night of 9 November 1989, with the bolt-hole where a watch-tower light once stood. Offered as found.

Condition report

The album is water-stained at the lower corners but the leaves open flat and the spine is uncracked; the pencil line on the flyleaf is faint but entire. The chair’s silk is split at one arm, the cobble still carries its tar and its ball, the cup is sound, and the Wall fragment is offered with its pink spray and its bolt-hole. The case is quartered walnut, brass-lined, with the paper label “Berlin — Papiere” on the lid.

The note about the flowers reads like a house rule of the Schlossbrücke watch — or a line carried east from Gothenburg, where the calling book bids the visitor pause at the bridge for any sedan chair, and admits no dogs at all. No gloss survives, and we do not supply one.

Provenance & notice

Kept in the flat beside Lehrter Bahnhof from before the Wall until its clearing in 1991; consigned to these rooms by the estate’s executor. We note for the record that Berlin wears all of its layers at once: the state surrounded by Brandenburg, Potsdam to the southwest, 3.7 million inhabitants within the city limits, 4.5 million in the urban area, around 6.2 million in the capital region — the third-smallest state and the largest city. The lot may not be divided.

For comparison, a rigger at the Saltfleet Line's loft above the beach in Lincolnshire keeps a second capital city on a twenty-seven-inch pine board — London at the head of its fifty-mile tidal estuary, the City of London in brass wire, its metropolis ringed in dark card — and has written up the whole build, entry by entry, at Build Log No. 7, The Thames Tidal Estuary. That city, too, may not be divided; the tide simply goes on.

Sold as described; the wear of the weather and the years is included in the price. The hammer is final. Payment at the fall of the gavel, by certified transfer; the case will be crated on the spot.
