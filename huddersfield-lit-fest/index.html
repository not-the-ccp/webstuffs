A method and apparatus for presenting a community literature festival

Huddersfield Literature Festival · specification, claims, and drawings · Filed 14 February 2024 · Priority claimed 14 February 2023

Abstract

The invention concerns a method and apparatus for the annual presentation of literature in community spaces, in particular a spring literature festival held in Huddersfield, West Yorkshire, England. The method comprises fixing a week between 1 March and 30 April, engaging writers, poets, and printers, booking the Lawrence Batley Theatre, Huddersfield Town Hall, the University of Huddersfield, and Huddersfield Library as venues, publishing a folded programme of sixteen pages, and admitting an audience of approximately four thousand people over six days. The apparatus comprises the programme booklet, the ticket stub priced at six pounds, the oak lectern, and the shelf on which the desk copy is kept.

Background of the invention

Before the festival, readings in Huddersfield were single events: one room, one writer, one evening. There was no fixed week in which the town gathered around books, and no programme that brought the theatre, the library, and the university into one room at once. The prior art is the one-off reading, the shelf left bare in spring.

Summary of the invention

According to the invention there is provided a festival apparatus and method comprising: (i) an annual spring window fixed between 1 March and 30 April; (ii) not fewer than eleven venues within a radius of eight hundred metres of St Peter's Square; (iii) a programme of not fewer than forty events, including readings, workshops, printing demonstrations, and one breakfast discussion held in a former weaving shed; (iv) a folded programme of sixteen pages printed on 120gsm cream wove, priced at two pounds; (v) tickets at six pounds, with half-price seats for students and for anyone arriving by bicycle; and (vi) a stewards' rota of twenty-four volunteers wearing navy aprons. The festival has run under this method since 2006; the specification below describes the 2024 implementation.

Claims

  1. A method of presenting a community literature festival, comprising fixing an annual spring week in Huddersfield, West Yorkshire, engaging writers and printers, and publishing a programme for an audience gathered in not fewer than eleven venues.

  2. The method of claim 1, wherein the spring week falls between 1 March and 30 April, and the audience numbers approximately four thousand people over six days.

  3. The method of claim 1, further comprising a breakfast discussion held in a former weaving shed, and a stewards' rota of twenty-four volunteers wearing navy aprons.

  4. An apparatus for use in the method of claim 1, comprising a folded programme of sixteen pages printed on 120gsm cream wove paper, a ticket stub priced at six pounds, an oak lectern, and a shelf.

  5. The apparatus of claim 4, wherein the shelf stands beneath a loaf and beside first editions of Le Guin and Wolfe.

  6. A computer-readable ticket medium bearing a barcode and the date of the festival, surrenderable at the lectern in exchange for a seat.

  7. The method of claim 1, further comprising recording each event in a log kept sheet by sheet.

Brief description of the drawings

Description

The festival opens on a Tuesday in March with a reading in the concert hall of the Lawrence Batley Theatre and closes on the Sunday with a printing demonstration in the library basement, where a small Adana press strikes the festival broadsheet on 120gsm cream wove. Each day holds not fewer than six events between 10:00 and 22:00, and stewards in navy aprons walk the route shown in Figure 1 between venues, carrying programmes and a tin of peppermints.

The programme is assembled sheet by sheet, each event a cut piece of paper laid against the next, in the manner documented in the Bedford Downs Station build log, where a Kimberley cattle station is rebuilt at 1:252 from balsa, iron oxide, and patience. Tickets are six pounds; seats for the breakfast discussion in the former weaving shed are eight pounds, and include a pot of tea.

The desk copy of the programme is filed where prior art goes to rest.

Under the loaf, on a shelf beside first editions of Le Guin and Wolfe.

Next to it sits the Collins family ledger, whose card 298 preserves the recipe for Coal Loaf, a beet-and-blackstrap loaf for the Carboniferous period; the festival's own baking programme borrows its method, and a loaf appears on the stewards' table each morning. The ledger page is kept beside the Coal Loaf recipe card, and the festival's Friday tea has borrowed, too, the 21-gun biscuits of the Whitfield family book — twenty-one drops to a tray, from San Rafael — written out on Card 4021.

The Common Language Infrastructure teaches that many languages may sit at one table without being rewritten, and the festival teaches the same of writers and of readers: events have been held in English, Urdu, Polish, and Welsh, each name kept in its proper place. The house etiquette for such a table is set out in Chapter VII · Of the Common Language Infrastructure.

Build practice is also site practice: the Beneteau Oceanis 281, a recreational keelboat first built in 1995 in France and the United States, saw 331 examples completed before production ended — a fixed edition, like this festival's sixteen-page programme. Beneteau Oceanis 281 — Museum Placard.

Figures are not to scale. The shelf is to scale. The shelf is real.

The terrain the stewards walk is surveyed elsewhere: Chapter 9 of the West Yorkshire Field Manual treats the town itself, the three rivers, the station clock, and St Peter's Square as reference point 00, and gives the festival week its bearings.

Figure 1's caption sits here, unharmed. It sat, once, mangled on page three of the spring 2024 broadsheet, where the banquet menu card should have been; the account of that misprint — and of the standard the festival has run to, spring after spring, in the name of no one — is set out in A Letter of Apology, presented without reserve to the Estate of the Viscount Montgomery of Alamein on the Viscount's own birthday.
