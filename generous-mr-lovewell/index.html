The Museum of Recorded Devotion · Case 6, the Gospel Shelf · Gallery Placard No. 14

The Generous Mr. Lovewell

MercyMe, sixth studio album · Essential Records · released 18 May 2010 · CD, gatefold sleeve, 13 tracks

On the sixth shelf of Case 6, between a hymn book of 1852 and a single reel of gospel brass, rests the object this placard describes: the compact disc in its gatefold sleeve, the sixth studio album by MercyMe, the Christian rock band of Springfield, Missouri, whose members Matt and Rob Meek, Joe Jurek, Bart Millard, and Jonathan Smith had been singing together since a family stage in Oklahoma and a small church in the Missouri hills. The album is a concept record built around a fictional, allegorical character, Mr. Lovewell, a man the liner notes present as a wanderer who is generous without being asked, and the overall theme of the disc is love — given, given twice, given a third time, the way a door is opened for someone who has already put their hand to the handle. It was produced by Brown Bannister and Dan Muckala, and it was cut, by the label's own account, in the spring of 2010, in a week the band describes as almost weatherless.

The reception was both critical and commercial. The album sold over 88,000 copies in its first week; it debuted at number three on the Billboard 200 and at number one on the Christian Albums chart. Three singles were released in its promotion, and all three reached number one on Billboard's Christian Songs chart. The lead single, “All of Creation,” spent ten weeks at the top of that chart, a run long enough that the station log books of the period contain, at least once a week, a hand-marked tick beside the title. Visitors to the case are asked to keep their distance from the sleeve, which is displayed under glass, and to read the ledger card below before asking for anything at all.

Accession and ledger. The museum received the disc in 2011 from the estate sale of a disc-jockey in Springfield, who had kept one signed copy for the listening table and the rest of the run in numbered boxes. The accession ledger for the year keeps exactly one entry in the donor's clerk's hand, entered under the column the museum uses for provenance remarks and read aloud, once, at the annual count: “Payment at the winding-up inventory, made at the seam where two were required.” The clerk who wrote it — since identified as H. P. Lovett, of the closing firm — used the seam, in his other writings, for the joint where two boxes of a numbered run met, and the entry stands in the ledger as the museum's receipt for the lot, made at the joint of the two boxes, where the count had to close and did.
