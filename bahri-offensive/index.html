Post #1 · 9 September 2024, 06:41 · from the west bank, by the radio

I am opening this thread the same way I open every thread I open: with what I can hear and what I am willing to write down. The SAF have begun the Bahri offensive. It started in earnest around the first week of September — armour and infantry coming north across the bridges that still hold, the objective being to recapture the strategic ground of Bahri, the district most people still call Khartoum North: Um Kadsala, Olgas, Shambat, the airport, the university quarter. It is being run in conjunction with the Battle of Khartoum proper, which means it is not a campaign, it is one fist making two at the same time, and the river between Omdurman and Olgas is the seam.

What I can put down plainly: the RSF held most of Bahri after they crossed into Khartoum City in late May; the SAF are taking it back piece by piece, block by block, the way you retake a city you have to drink water from. I have heard artillery on the Shambat road twice this week. I will post when I know something. Quote only with the word on it.

Post #2 · 15 September 2024, 22:07 · from Khor Shumak, the canteen at the gate

I run the gate of the canteen where the Khor Shumak drivers take their first coffee before the Olgas crossing, so I will say the thing the regulars will not say clean. A crew of three men came here every other night since February — the same three, same black canvas bag, same order: four coffees, two flatbreads, the water by the litre. We never asked what the bag carried. When the offensive opened they stopped coming, and last Tuesday the oldest of the three, the one who used to pay in small notes on principle, came alone, put a cup of coffee on the counter and said one full sentence to me before he went out. I have written it down because it is the truest piece of intelligence this canteen has taken in five years.

The canteen knows a team that had turned.

He then drank his coffee, left the small notes on the counter, and went out to whichever side of the river a man of his habit would go. The canteen is open to both sides, has always been open to both sides, and it does not keep a ledger of souls. But it keeps its eye, and the eye has noticed: fewer of them on the north bank every week, more on this side of the Khor, and the coffee on the Bahri side has stopped being bought in bulk. That is all I will put on the wire.

Post #3 · 2 October 2024, 04:12 · from Um Kadsala, the third week

From inside. Third week now of the SAF pushing through Um Kadsala. The street grid here is the whole story — the campaign moves one intersection at a time and then stops to let the water truck get through. We count the water truck. It comes twice a day past the old cinema on the main road, and when it comes, the shelling stops for an hour, which is its own kind of ceasefire. The district hospital on the road to Olgas is taking what it can; the pharmacy on the corner has been closed since June and is used for the things a pharmacy cannot be written down as using.

What it looks like: the SAF are not a flood, they are a tide. They take a block, dig in, and the morning finds a checkpoint where the block's bakery used to be. The bakery has moved twice, and the baker — you may remember him, he posted here in 2023 — has a queue around the block even now, which I put down to the fact that bread is the only thing in Bahri that has not changed hands. My building has lost two floors of neighbours since August. I do not post their names. The river keeps the names; that is its job.

Post #4 · 18 November 2024, 11:50 · from the survey office, Omdurman, with the compass of 1971

A few bearings for the record, because somebody should be writing them down in a straight line. As of mid-November the SAF hold the Omdurman–Olgas bridge and the ground on both sides of it, the airport at Khartoum North has changed hands a second time, and the front in Bahri has shortened to the Shambat and Um Kadsala belt. The Omdurman bridge still stands; that is the one I set the bearing on in 1971 and I would like it to outlive all of us.

Two notes for the men who keep comparisons in this forum. If you want a document-grade reckoning of how badly a river and a bridge can fight back against a campaign, the Guadalcanal Surface Action Error Reference, Rev. 4 is the cleanest table of a fleet's arithmetic meeting geography that I have ever read, and the Bahri numbers are the same arithmetic with worse paper. And for the hills-and-tunnel version of block-by-block retaking, where every village is a square of the same ten-count, the Matanikau Diary on Mount Austen reads like somebody did exactly what is being done in Um Kadsala, with more rain. I have read both twice. I recommend both before you draw any map.

Post #5 · 9 December 2024, 19:33 · from Omdurman, the kitchen window

Answering Nadia's water truck, because I count it too, from the Omdurman side. The truck takes the Olgas crossing in the morning and returns in the evening with the tank half empty and the driver's cap on his shoulder, which means the crossing held. When the cap stays on his head the crossing did not. It is not intelligence, it is a weather sign, and I have kept my weather chart on the kitchen wall since September: 47 clear mornings, 11 cap-on-head mornings, 4 mornings I refused to write down. The offensive is not finished. It has only become a thing with a calendar, which is how every siege I know ends up being fought — not by the army but by the thing that crosses.

Post #6 · 14 January 2025, 07:20 · from the canteen gate, before first coffee

One year in the campaign, minus three weeks. The canteen's own statistics, because someone asked in the other thread: we have served 4,112 coffees since 1 September, and the ratio of black-to-white cups on the counter has flipped three times, which used to be a once-in-a-decade thing. The three men from Post #2: I saw the youngest again last week, on the north bank, on the coffee side of the counter. I am not going to say which side he is on. A canteen that tells you which side a man is on has already told you which side the canteen is on, and I want no part of being on a side.

What I will say: the offensive is now an attrition with a menu. The SAF keep the bridges, the RSF keep the rooftops, and between them the Khor Shumak runs as if neither name means anything to it, which is where I keep my own opinion of the whole business.

A year on, I am keeping the thread open and I am keeping the house rule: dates over names, unless the name is a bridge. The offensive runs from September 2024 into whatever month the bridges allow. Bahri — Khartoum North — Um Kadsala, Olgas, Shambat, the airport — stays a place with a front in it and a canteen at the gate of it. If you are in it, or you were, or you count the water truck: post here. If you are somewhere else and you keep threads like this, there is a running record of the ring side of the same argument at Boxing — The Dreamer's Concordance, Entry No. 144, where the bell is a change of round and nobody is promised the tenth count; and for the river's own books, the way a river files a campaign against anyone's campaign, the lot ledger at Lot 27 — The Amazon River, Catalogue B is the closest thing I know to a watercourse that has never once recognised a boundary and still keeps its gauging stations honest.

The thread is open. The coffee is hot. The river keeps its own minutes, and so do we.
