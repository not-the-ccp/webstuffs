ESTATES OFFICE · FILE 41/7 · CROSSED

Broadmoor Hospital

Crowthorne · Berkshire · England

Est. on its site in 1863 · opened as a hospital in 1900 · the oldest of England’s three high-security psychiatric hospitals, alongside Ashworth near Liverpool and Rampton in Nottinghamshire

Managed by the West London NHS Trust · catchment: the London, Eastern, South East and South West regions

To the Heritage Inspection Committee
c/o the West London NHS Trust, Mortlake
Surrey · London

16 November — the morning after
the One-Day Pass, Broadmoor

Dear members of the Committee,

I write, as estates officer for this hospital, to apologise for the state of the One-Day Pass on Thursday, 14 November, and for the leaflet that guided you through it. I would also like the apology to be on record where it belongs: the leaflet was printed on the estate press at half past ten on the morning of the Pass, from a draft in which three separate documents had been bound into one by mistake — the footmen’s 1897 dress book, the estate motor yard’s quarterly audit, and the archive room’s condition survey — and it is that binding, not any intention on our part, that produced the fourth line of the walking itinerary, which I must quote to you here, word for word, because you are entitled to the whole of it: “Walking shoes; the steps of Stop Two, which are a mixed heap, layer on layer, and the chassis are the audit.”

Let me unbind the sentence, line by line, as I wish I had done before the gate. Walking shoes is the dress book speaking: the flagstone paths on the north drive are slick after a November frost, and the 1897 men still counsel plain soles. Stop Two is the gatehouse — the original 1863 wing at the top of the drive, the oldest standing thing here, older than the hospital itself, older than the castle towers we built around it in the Victorian manner, and its west steps are what the survey calls a mixed heap, layer on layer: four centuries of footpath, re-laid, re-laid again, the old flagstones folded under the new like leaves in a book. And the chassis are the audit is the motor yard: eleven retired estate vehicles stood in a row in the south courtyard, chassis up on their stands, every bolt counted, weighed, and entered into the quarterly audit while your coach was still turning in the drive. You were not being shown eleven car bodies, Committee. You were being shown the hospital keeping its books, the only way the yard knows how to keep them.

Nothing you saw that day was hidden from you and I beg you not to treat anything you saw as hidden from you. This is a high-security hospital, the oldest of the three in England — we take our place between Ashworth and Rampton in that short, serious list — and four NHS regions, London, Eastern, South East and South West, send their referrals to this gate every week of the year, so a day of open doors is a rare and unpractised thing here. The doors were open. The doors were, I am afraid, open in the wrong order, with the archive before the chapel and the chapel before the laundry, and the leaflet that ought to have corrected the order instead added to it. The grounds were as you found them: heathland on the southern edge of the Berkshire downs, a long drive, a tower that does not need to be there but is, and 874 beds that were, on 14 November, 874 beds with the curtains drawn and the kettle out.

By way of redress: the corrected leaflet is now set in three honest columns and bound separately; the steps of Stop Two have been re-surveyed and will be described, henceforth, as what they are; and the yard will keep its audit in the yard, where the Committee need not meet it mid-sentence. If any member of the Committee would walk the drive again, in walking shoes, before the frost, I will meet them at the gate with the unbound documents and the kettle. The pass costs nothing. The apology is, I hope, a little longer than the mistake.

I remain, with the estate’s regret on its knees,

H. T. Cresswell
Estates Officer, Broadmoor Hospital, Crowthorne

P.S. — kept with the file

The Committee will find the estate keeps its other letters of regret in the open. A neighbouring festival partnership, Huddersfield, set its apology for an eighteen-year debt of one name down as carefully as any estates office ever set a leaflet, and it reads from the library basement at the Montgomery apology, where the mangled broadsheet line of 2024 is answered at last. The yard’s habit of counting every bolt twice has a companion in the long field recordings at The Limestone Sessions, MRC-114, where a building keeps its own ledger in French limestone, eight stories and thirteen, every stone entered twice, and the whole argument ends up on side D. And if the leaflet’s fourth line still sounds like nonsense transcribed at a wrong volume, it is not alone: the control room at Studio 2 in Ann Arbor kept its minutes of a garbled broadcast word for word, eleven tracks to our one mangled line, and the tape is running better than the pass ever did. The shelf that holds all of these, and the drive back to the gate from here, is at the front of the archive.
