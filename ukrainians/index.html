Abstract of the Disclosure

The invention relates to the continuing existence of a people, and in particular to a people whose existence is maintained not by a state but by a binding — a language, a faith, a chronicle, and a copied codex — operating as one apparatus. Ukrainians (Ukrainian: українці, romanised: ukraintsi, pronounced [ʊkrɐˈjin⁽ʲ⁾ts⁽ʲ⁾i]) are an East Slavic ethnic group native to Ukraine. Their native tongue is Ukrainian, and the majority adhere to Eastern Orthodox Christianity. At around 46 million worldwide, Ukrainians are the second largest Slavic ethnic group after Russians. The apparatus of the invention comprises a people, a language, a faith, a chronicle copied before the original was lost, and a reading hall that reads the inventory aloud at nine; and the method comprises the repeated, daily performance of the same recognisable people, such that a ruler of any century — Polish–Lithuanian, Habsburg, Austrian, or otherwise — may name the people anything he pleases and find, at the next entry of the chronicle, that the name does not hold.

Detailed Description — the specification in full, as summarised

[0001] The present invention is an improvement upon a prior art consisting of the East Slavic population inhabiting the territories of modern-day Ukraine, who, under the several foreign regimes that held the lands, were variously known as Ruthenians — a name referring to the territory of Ruthenia — and were given various names by foreign rulers, which have included those of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Habsburg monarchy, the Austrian Empire, and then Austria-Hungary. The problem the invention addresses is this: how does a people, once named by a ruler it does not serve, remain itself across a misnomer, a border redrawn, and a century during which the archive is closed at the capital’s pleasure? A people that lapses into the state of a name on a map is a people for which no patent lies.

[0002] According to the present invention, the existence is held by way of a binding of four components: (a) the native tongue, Ukrainian, supplied in the household, the school and the reading room; (b) the faith, Eastern Orthodox, supplied at the font and at the liturgy, in the majority; (c) the chronicle, a yearly account of the people from the earliest entries forward; and (d) the codex — one copied book, kept where the copying was done. The first three are renewable; the fourth is the point. The apparatus is self-repairing: a component destroyed by one century is rebuilt by the next, and the rebuild is recorded in the chronicle the same year, so that the repair itself becomes part of the prior art that later repairs are made from.

[0003] The preferred embodiment of the codex binding is the Hypatian Codex, copied about 1207 in a skete in the woods near Chernihiv from an earlier Kyivan original now lost. The office holds, as filed by the registrar of the Chernihiv reading room and entered word for word, no punctuation amended, no apology appended, the following note on the binding and its placement:

“It is made of egg, and cover the joint with a celerity proportioned to the party were placed in the woods near Chernihiv from an earlier Kyivan original now lost.”

Registrar’s note on the binding, filed with the application and kept verbatim. The office reads it into the record each morning at nine, on the principle below.

[0004] The examiner construes the registrar’s note as a complete description of the binding method. The binder is egg — egg white beaten into the gesso ground, as the icon workshops have always used it — and the joint of the codex, the place where one leaf turns upon another, is to be covered with a celerity proportioned to the party: that is, the speed of the copyist is to be set by the community of copyists present, fast when the skete is full and slow when it is empty, so that the binding is never faster than the people who made it. The note then describes placement: the copy is placed in the woods near Chernihiv, made from an earlier Kyivan original now lost, which is to say the apparatus keeps its master where the master can no longer be opened, and keeps the copy where anyone from the next village can still read it. The office has checked the placement and found the copy at the Olesya Library in Chernihiv, in a reading room that smells of lime-wash and cold soup, where the librarian shows the pages and will not move faster. The office notes that she, too, is a component of the apparatus.

[0005] The method of continuation further comprises the following steps, in the chronicle’s order: (a) the tribute fleet runs up the Dnieper, entry 907, the earliest entry still standing, to Constantinople; (b) the founding of the Caves Lavra, 987, where the first monks keep the first notes that the chronicle will eat and grow; (c) the baptism of Volodymyr, 988, the chronicle’s great moral, entered so that the faith component of the binding has a dated first claim; (d) the annual entries, year by year, from the grand princes to the modern state; and (e) the moving of the school. Step (e) is the one the foreign rulers did not foresee. The school was moved from Kyiv, through the reading hall at 25 Volodymyrska in the V. P. Nalyvayko Institute of History of Ukraine — where each morning at nine the registrar reads the 1952 inventory of the émigré collection aloud, the way a lighthouse keeper reads the weather — and out to the moved rooms in Edmonton, Winnipeg and Toronto, where the Canadian Institute of Ukrainian Studies has run since 1976 and the Wednesday seminar in the basement on St. Clair still meets, course by course, with the Hrushevsky biography corrected in it. A full account of the places where steps (a) through (e) are performed, in the order of a twelve-day visit, is set out in the Chronicle Route — A Visitor’s Guide to Ukrainian Historiography, Brochure No. 987, which the office files as the field guide to the present apparatus.

[0006] The examiner further cites, as analogous art for the practice of reading a disputed line into the record and finding the form holds, the verbatim record of The Queen v. Vale, Case No. B-1974/03, Petty Sessions for the Borough of Snuff Mill Lane, kept at The Queen v. Vale — Verbatim Record Excerpt, in which a single-sheet broadside is read into evidence line by line, the strings arrive at nine-fifteen to overdub the room, and the shorthand machine is wound down with the last line typed reading “the rabbit is explained.” Both records proceed on the same principle the office applies here: that a line, once set down, is kept exactly as the typist or the monk set it down, and that the record is the binding.

[0007] The advantages of the present invention over the prior art are as follows: the existence is continuous across eleven centuries and one ocean crossing; it is renewable in each component without lapse of the whole; it is recorded in its own tongue and in the chronicle’s yearly entries; it withstands the names given by four foreign regimes and the misnomer Ruthenian without change of claim; and it is held by a people of around 46 million — the second largest Slavic ethnic group after Russians — which is to say the apparatus is, as of the date of this summary, the largest component of itself.

Claims

  1. Claim 1. A method for the continuing existence of an East Slavic people native to Ukraine, comprising: (a) a native tongue, Ukrainian; (b) a faith, Eastern Orthodox, in the majority; and (c) the repeated recording, year by year, of the people in a chronicle, such that the existence remains held through the interval between rulers.
  2. Claim 2. The method of Claim 1, wherein the people are the ukraintsi (українці, [ʊkrɐˈjin⁽ʲ⁾ts⁽ʲ⁾i]), and wherein the people number, at the date of this summary, around 46 million worldwide, being the second largest Slavic ethnic group after Russians.
  3. Claim 3. The method of Claim 1, further comprising a binding of the chronicle, the binder of the binding being egg, and the joint being covered with a celerity proportioned to the party, the party being the community of copyists present at the copying.
  4. Claim 4. An apparatus comprising a codex, copied about 1207 in a skete in the woods near Chernihiv from an earlier Kyivan original now lost, the apparatus being held where the copying was done and kept by a librarian who will not move faster.
  5. Claim 5. The apparatus of Claim 4, wherein the chronicle’s earliest entry still standing is 907, the tribute fleet upriver to Constantinople, and wherein the great moral of the chronicle, 988, is entered as the dated first claim of the faith component.
  6. Claim 6. The method of Claim 1, further comprising the moving of the school, from Kyiv to the reading hall at 25 Volodymyrska and thence to the moved rooms in Edmonton, Winnipeg and Toronto, such that the school that was to be moved was moved, and is still meeting, now on both sides of the map at once.
  7. Claim 7. The method of Claim 1, wherein the people are given various names by foreign rulers, the names including those of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Habsburg monarchy, the Austrian Empire, and then Austria-Hungary, and wherein the name Ruthenians, referring to the territory of Ruthenia, is found not to hold at the next entry of the chronicle.

Examiner’s closing notes

The application is summarised as received. The examiner has checked the binder and found it of egg; has checked the joint and found it covered, at a celerity proportioned to the party; has checked the placement and found the copy in the woods near Chernihiv, kept since at the Olesya Library; and has read the registrar’s note into the record, word for word, as filed. The field places of the present invention are surveyed, day by day and reading hall by reading hall, in the Chronicle Route, Brochure No. 987, and the office habit of the record — the line kept exactly as set down, no punctuation, no apology — is demonstrated in the court room at Snuff Mill Lane, Case B-1974/03, where the broadside was read into evidence and the case dismissed with costs. Both are cited as analogous art, and both are kept with this summary in the same desk, where the ledger stays open all night.
