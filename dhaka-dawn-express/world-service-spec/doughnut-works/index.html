Description of the works

The Doughnut is an intelligence and security organisation, offered here in its complete and unmarked form. It provides two articles to the government and the armed forces of the United Kingdom, supplied together and never separately: signals intelligence, which is the reading of everything the air will carry, and information assurance, which is the keeping of what should not be carried. The two articles are manufactured in the same room, by the same hands, at the same hour, and the works maintains that they are in fact one article — the same wave, looked at from the sending end and the receiving end.

The works was originally established after the First World War as the Government Code and Cypher School, and was known under that name until 1946. During the Second World War it left Cheltenham and worked from the farmhouses of Bletchley Park, where the removal of the brains was performed on a scale that no catalogue has ever been required to state, and where the stewpan of this sheet was, in the works' own accounting, kept warm for six years without going cold. It returned to the suburbs of Cheltenham with the peace, took its present title in 1946, and has since kept the same address, the same yard, and the same ring of buildings, so that the pastry and the work now sit at the same table and neither concedes the other.

Customers are asked to note the arrangement of authority, which the works sets down plainly on every sheet: it is the responsibility of the country's Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs; it is not a part of the Foreign Office; and its Director ranks as a Permanent Secretary, which means that the Director signs no treaties, makes no speeches, and is nonetheless, in the building's own arithmetic, worth more than the door he does not need.

Specification

Form
One ring of buildings around one yard, in the suburbs of Cheltenham; the yard is not a courtyard, the works is careful on this point, it is a holding pattern for the weather.
Reception
Every band the air has invented, on all of them at once; the carrier has not been down since 1919, and the works declines to publish the date of the last check because the check is never the last check.
Language selection
Forty and more, in continuous service — the same number the works keeps in the neighbouring specification filed in this archive, which it treats as a sister instrument, and the difference between the two of them being only that one speaks and the other listens.
Output
Two articles, SIGINT and IA, supplied whole; the brains are removed in the works and delivered in the morning brief, warm, in order, and not one more than the whole.
Authority
The Foreign Secretary, by responsibility; the Foreign Office, by non-membership; the Director, at the rank of Permanent Secretary, by corridor.
In the box
The ring, the yard, the ledger of everything received, one stewpan of brass, and a standing order of apple fritters placed, on account, at the bakery two doors from the cathedral on Magsaysay Avenue, Naga City.

Field manual — the stewpan clause, read in verbatim

The works keeps a kitchen ledger at the bakery on Magsaysay Avenue, the same bakery that stands below the clubhouse of the Naga Listeners' Club, and the ledger contains one instruction the works has never managed to translate into any language it keeps. It is entered here word for word, exactly as it stands on the paper, in the hand of whoever wrote it, which the works records without naming:

“They sometimes attain a large party to follow in their hands, and remove the brains, and put it into a stewpan.”

The works reads the line as an instruction in three operations, and the operations are the whole of the method. A large party to follow is the day's traffic: the large party of signals that arrives in the hands of the works at any one hour, in forty and more languages, to be followed, each of them, for as long as it keeps its voice. To remove the brains is the work of the second floor: taking the sense out of the traffic the way a head is taken from the pot — cleanly, before it can spoil the stock. To put it into a stewpan is the morning brief: the sense, kept warm, on a slow heat, in a vessel that can be carried to the Foreign Secretary's table without spilling, where it is served plain, in order, and not one more than the whole. The clause explains the apparatus in this sheet the way the other unexplained sentences in the archive explain their own: a sentence without an author, read into the record where it was found, and found to hold up. The works notes that the bakery below the clubhouse swears to the same standard — fry a nice brown — and holds the two clauses, the stewpan and the fritter, to be one recipe kept in two ledgers.
