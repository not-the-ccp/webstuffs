Lost one planned capital · Found one dish

filed from Brasília, the Federal District of Brazil, and answered in Lyneham, Canberra

Reported missingone capital: Brasília, last seen at the family supper of 21 April, at the seventh table
Last seenthe Federal District, on the Brazilian Highlands, in the country’s Central-West region
Reported foundone 30 × 22 cm baking dish, shaped like the lake, the gratin still in it, in the oven of the same supper
Claimednot yet — see the claim conditions at the foot of the notice

1. The missing item, as described

Brasília (brə-ZIL-ee-ə; Brazilian Portuguese: [bɪaːziljə]) is the capital city of Brazil and the Federal District. It stands on the Brazilian Highlands, in the country’s Central-West region, which is to say: exactly where a map-argued city would want to stand, and that was the whole of the point. It was drawn in 1956 as a planned city by Lúcio Costa, Oscar Niemeyer, and Joaquim Cardozo, in a scheme to move the capital from Rio de Janeiro to a more central location, and it was founded by President Juscelino Kubitschek on 21 April 1960, to replace Rio de Janeiro as the national capital. It is Brazil’s third-most populous city, after São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro, with a population of 2.8 million, and among the major Latin American cities it has the highest GDP per capita. The notice is filed under planned capitals for the same reason the family’s other notice is filed under lakes: a planned capital is a dish. It has a long axis, an end where the hills are meant to sit, and a centre where everyone sits to dinner. Our own, the one the Kell family keeps in the recipe for Aunt Marva’s Lake Gratin, is a hundred and twenty-odd years older, a deal shyer, and the two were drawn by the same impulse and can be told apart by the same field mark: one of them keeps a lamp on the apron, and this one does not, not yet.

2. How it was lost

It was a Thursday, which in our house means the family’s definition of a Thursday — a drive along the western edge of the lake at home — was about to be extended across a whole ocean to a newer, egg-shaped city in the Central-West. Aunt Marva came. She was on day 74 of the ninety-day count, and she does not tell people which ninety days, and neither do we. We ate the gratin at the seventh table, in the dish shaped like the lake, the long axis of the dish pointing the way the Molonglo had come in, which is to say roughly toward wherever the new capital had been expecting us. Halfway through supper the room began to smell like a city: white concrete, new paint, the air of something just built to be found. Grandfather Eamon’s line, the one he wrote in the notebook on the page above the recipe in 1963, has a way of arriving like that, and it arrived here too: the water, and the whole of this other planned place, came in like something that had been expecting you. When we stood to leave, the capital was in the dish, the dish was in the oven, and the only part of it we carried out was the lamp. The rest we left where a capital is properly left: in the ground, in the record, and in the count of the days still running.

3. The found item, as described

The dish was found, still warm, in the oven of the supper, at 30 × 22 cm, shaped like the lake, and presently unclaimable. It was, as follows: a layer of the city’s shape in potato — 900 g of a long white variety, sliced thin as survey paper — laid in the dish so the long axis points the way the city was planned, which is to say toward a more central location. Between the layers: a white sauce made with 60 g of a large bunch of herbs, and spices. Over the top: 250 g of the hard yellow cheese grated coarse, a turn of black pepper, and the thin slices of a second potato laid in the pattern of the avenue, brown at the edge, the way an avenue is brown at the edge when the light is going. The office that found it is not an office. It is the apron, on the wall of the kitchen at home, with the lamp against it — the keeping — where this family files its records the way other families file theirs in the ground. The sauce is held at the temperature of a record: not hot enough to change, and not cold enough to forget.

4. Claim conditions

No claim has yet been made on the dish, and it will be released to the person who can do all three of the following: state, without prompting, the reason the capital was moved from Rio de Janeiro, and give it as the scheme of 1956 gave it — to a more central location, not merely farther away; hold the lamp against the apron for the remaining sixteen days of the count, which are the days between the supper at the seventh table and the day the count runs out; and name, unprompted, the three who drew the city in 1956 — Lúcio Costa, Oscar Niemeyer, and Joaquim Cardozo — while keeping the fourth man, the president who founded it on 21 April 1960, in his own place, because this family keeps people in their places. The method is not ours to invent: the term report that keeps three battleships the same way has it on record from the other side of the map, that a thing of iron and water is kept by a name in a book, a date, and the count of those who did not come back. A capital is kept the same way. The record keeps it. The family keeps the record. And on Thursdays the lamp is on.

— the lamp is lit — 16 days of the count remain
