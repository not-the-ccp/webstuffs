2Hazard identification / Réconciliation des dangers

Classified as Final authority, category 9: court of last resort. The principal hazards are institutional rather than physical. A decision of this material is the ultimate application of Canadian law: once delivered from the bench, it binds every court below it, from the federal appellate courts to the small-claims registries of the territories.

  • H-SCC-1 — Irreversibility: there is no further appeal. Contact with a judgment is permanent.
  • H-SCC-2 — Scale of effect: a single ruling on the common law, on the interpretation of a statute, or on any other application of law can re-shape the law of the whole country in one sitting.
  • H-SCC-3 — Press attention: judgments read from the bench attract immediate nationwide handling. See Section 5.

FINAL  Handle decisions with reference to the bench composition and the dates of argument and decision. Provenance first, character second.

4First-aid measures / Mesures de premiers soins

Misquotation of a judgment: re-read the judgment as published in the reports; do not rely on a transcript heard over a crowded press gallery. Improper reliance: a decision of this Court is binding authority, not advice; route the matter to counsel. Confusion with the appellate courts: there is no appeal from the Supreme Court on a question of law; the only relief is a leave to appeal for a new trial in narrow circumstances, or the pardon power, which is a different substance entirely and stored elsewhere.

5Fire-fighting measures / Mesures de lutte contre l’incendie

Not applicable; the substance does not burn, though the morning papers occasionally do. In the event that a judgment ignites the press, do not feed it: the Court does not reply to commentary, the parties speak through their briefs, and the next case on the board will take the bench in due course. Historically, the largest fires have all burned out before the following term, leaving the library shelves of 301 Wellington Street exactly where they were.

6Accidental release measures / Mesures en cas de déversement accidentel

If unpublished reasons are released prematurely — a draft circulated to the wrong clerk, a headnote in the wrong hand — isolate the copy, notify the Registry, and apply to the Court before it is quoted. The registry’s standing order, kept beside the film drawers of the annex, is the same one that governs the sealed stacks elsewhere: a document is what its provenance makes of it, and a draft reasons is a judgment only when the bench has pronounced it.

7Handling and storage / Manipulation et stockage

Handle on a need-to-cite basis. Judgments are delivered in both official languages and are cited by neutral citation, by the reports, or by both. Store the published decision in the library; store the file in the registry.

The registry annex at 301 Wellington Street keeps the sealed records of the Court’s own business — leave applications, in-camera books, the working papers behind the privilege rulings. Its locker 7B is the same locker a sister sheet of this desk catalogues as of 12 March 2024: one folio of unreported reasons, a counter card of the 1925–26 Grizzlies season, centennial reissue, from The Hardwood Emporium, two redacted affidavits, and the working draft of the case-by-case privilege test in the authoring justice’s hand. The Emporium card arrived in a courier pouch stamped Ottawa, was logged at 3:17 a.m., and is retained. Every item in 7B is marked, logged, and retained — provenance first, character second.

8Exposure controls / personal protection

No exposure limit is set for a court of last resort, because every court in the country is exposed to it permanently. Engineering controls: the permission filter, which admits between 40 and 75 appeals a year from a national population of more than thirty million litigants. Personal protection: counsel. Do not argue directly to the bench without it; the gallery is for observing, not for intervening.

10Stability and reactivity / Stabilité et réactivité

Stability: stable since 1875 under the Constitution Act, 1867 and the Supreme Court Act; the composition changed twice (1899, 1949) without a break in session. Conditions to avoid: the assumption that the Court hears every appeal — it hears 40 to 75 a year, and only those it grants permission to. Incompatible materials: the phrase “the Supreme Court said I win”; press commentary delivered as fact; and any argument that a bilingual judgment is a translation of itself.

The 1949 enlargement to nine justices, with the Quebec appointment rule made explicit, remains the Court’s most cited stability event: the year the Court began to be understood as a national court in the full sense, rather than an appellate formality.

11Toxicological information / Renseignements toxicologiques

The Court itself is non-toxic; the gallery has no poison. The acute toxicity lies in the effect of a single judgment: the effects of any judicial decision on the common law, on the interpretation of statutes, or on any other application of law can, in effect, terminate a statute, a practice, or an entire line of authority in one sitting. Chronic exposure is universal — every lawyer, judge, clerk and legislist in the country reads this substance for a living. No tolerance is known to develop.

12Ecological information / Informations écologiques

The substance’s habitat is the whole country, from the territory courts to the provincial appellate benches, and its distribution is one-way: down, into every court below. When the registry’s auditors last measured the paper footprint of that distribution, they opened the notarial drawers in Sherbrooke, the clerks’ files in Victoria, and the law-society stacks in Montreal and Winnipeg, expecting the bound reporter volumes to be everywhere. But there they found a very small quantity, which is the design working as intended: the reasons travel electronically, the binding volumes number in the hundreds, and the Court says a thing once, at 301 Wellington Street, and the whole country reads it from that single copy.

13Disposal considerations / Considérations relatives è élimination

There is no disposal. Justices hold office until age 75, at which point the bench turns over one chair at a time and the successor sits in the same seat, in the same black robe, hearing the same two legal traditions in the same two languages. Retired justices are not destroyed but re-labelled: former justice, and the file is closed, not shredded. Judgments are never destroyed; the reports run forward only.

14Transport information / Transports

Decisions are transported nationwide by the reports, by the Court’s own website, and by every law library’s interlibrary loan desk; no quantity restrictions, both official languages in every parcel. The leave applications that feed the Court travel the other way — from the provincial and territorial appellate courts and the federal appellate courts, up one floor of the law, in sealed envelopes marked with a single case number. The courier pouches are logged at the annex, day and night; the 3:17 a.m. stamp on the Grizzlies counter card in locker 7B is the only overnight entry the registry has ever had to explain, and it has been explained, once, and logged, and retained.
