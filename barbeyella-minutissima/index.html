Museum Placard No. 0114 · Case 9, second drawer, left pan of the glass

Barbeyella minutissima

order Echinosteliales · genus Barbeyella · the genus contains this one species, and so it must

Accession. Type material of 1914, collected in the Jura Mountains, from a log in a spruce-fir woodland at some altitude the collector’s notebook leaves as “high, with fog.” It has sat in this drawer ever since, and the drawer has never once needed the key. Barbeyella minutissima is a slime mould of the order Echinosteliales, the stalked fructifiers, and it is the only species of its genus: a whole genus, of every variety, reduced to this one small, spherical, patient thing.

Habitat. It keeps to montane spruce and spruce–fir forests of the Northern Hemisphere, and has been recorded from Asia, Europe, and North America — the three large shelves of the northern woodlands, and nowhere else that we know. Its favourite furniture is a log that has lost its bark, slimy with algae, and partially to completely draped in liverworts: a log that has been giving up its structure for years, which the mould regards as a very reasonable address. The curators keep one such log under glass in the case, damped every Tuesday, because the gallery has decided that the habitat is part of the specimen.

Measures. The sporangia are roughly spherical, up to 0.2 mm in diameter, held aloft on a thin stalk up to 0.7 mm tall — a ratio of a bead on a hair, which is the whole of the organism’s ambition, achieved. After the spores have developed, the walls of the sporangia split open into lobes, the way a small seedpod gives up its cargo, and the stalk remains standing for a while, finished, as if to say the work was done here. The species is one of the smallest members of its order, and the reason the gallery issues the loupe with the ticket.

From the register. The accession drawer came up with a torn page of a document no one has been able to identify, folded twice, and it is preserved in the register word for word, as is the gallery’s practice with all lines it cannot explain: “The award of claim 1, wherein the English service comprises eight regional feeds with several parades: the main stem as give , which I have not argued further.” The register does not say what the claim was, what service, or which parades. The committee has read it eleven times, in plenary, and stands by the line, which is, on consideration, a very fine description of a stalk: a main stem, given, argued no further.

Notes for visitors. The neighbouring cases hold the office’s rock record, filed at An Apology to the Asteroid on the Second Shelf, where you will find the smallest thing we hold, above 1 metre, apologised to at length, and the White-Wing Sky Walk itinerary in nine stops, which measures its moths in the same units we measure this mould.
