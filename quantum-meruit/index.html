WHEN EARNED, WHEN PAID

1 Identification of the substance

The substance is a measure, not a thing: quantum meruit, Latin for “what one has earned,” used in contract law to mean the reasonable value of services rendered when a price was never fixed, never kept, or never finished. It is supplied by no manufacturer; it is dispensed by the common law of the several states, which is why this sheet carries fifty-one storage conditions and one label. Supplier of record: the Office of Reasonable Value, 244 Chancery Lane, telephone the equity desk, dial 311. In the United States the substance is unregulated at the federal level and entirely at the mercy of state common law — a jurisdictional aerosol, stable in New York, volatile in the territories between, and to be stored in the state in which it is to be applied.

2 Hazard identification

Signal word: DANGER. The primary hazard is unjust enrichment, GHS classification Category 1 (severe): a defendant who has been enriched at a plaintiff’s expense, in circumstances where equity and good conscience require restitution, is not permitted to keep the benefit. Secondary hazards include implied agreements that modify express contracts without written notice, and the quiet failure of express contracts to be completed, in either of which the substance converts from dormant to the active measure of damages. The GHS label is reproduced at the head of this sheet. The exclamation mark, not the skull, is the correct pictogram — this substance injures by ledger, not by body — though the equity desk will tell you the arithmetic is slower-acting and more complete than anything on the skull.

3 Composition / information on ingredients

The substance is anhydrous and carries no solvents; it is composed of elements, which are to be pleaded in order. To state a claim for unjust enrichment in the state of New York, a plaintiff must allege exactly three: (1) the defendant was enriched; (2) the enrichment was at the plaintiff’s expense; and (3) the circumstances were such that equity and good conscience require the defendant to make restitution. Omit any element and the mixture will not hold: a benefit without a loss is a gift, and a loss without enrichment is a tragedy, and the dispensary does not stock remedies for either. The three elements are the whole composition. There is no fourth element, and no state is known to have found one, though several have re-weighed the three.

4 First-aid measures

In case of over-retention: do not argue with the ledger. Move the recipient to restitution, keep them comfortable with a signed settlement, and if the enrichment exceeds the reasonable value of the services, arrange for the surplus to be turned over. In case of under-payment — the companion accident, in which the service is given and the measure has not yet been read — apply an account: set down the services, set down the market, set down the dial, and let the difference be paid. If the parties have been breathing the same contract air for more than a season, remove both to a judge before further implied terms are inhaled. In no case should a first aider attempt to re-trade the price while the enrichment is still in the bloodstream; the market, once taken, is the market.

5 Firefighting measures

There is no fire to fight; the hazard is the absence of a price, and the absence of a price does not burn. Nevertheless, where a disputed contract has gone to trial and the room is hot, use the standard extinguisher of the profession: stipulation. Do not add a new witness to a burning valuation dispute; it feeds it. In the event of a full-blown jury question on reasonable value, evacuate the parties to a bench trial if the contract documents are not up to temperature, and close the courtroom window, because the jury smells the market through a draft. The equity desk advises that a settlement reached in a hot room holds only as long as the ink, and that the ink of a quantum meruit award, once dry, is the hardest ink in the building.

6 Accidental release

The substance releases itself. It is the measure of damages where an express contract is mutually modified by the implied agreement of the parties, or where the express contract is not completed — while there is often, the ledger notes, a signed paper in the drawer, there is also a course of dealing that has quietly amended it, and the release follows the dealing. On release, contain the work: stop further services, mark the date of the break, and keep the time-and-materials record, which is the decontamination log. Do not attempt to re-press the release into the original contract; an uncompleted contract does not take its own measure back. Notify the archivist of the account, and file the release under the state whose common law governs it, because the spill is subject to local regulation.

7 Handling and storage

Handle only by the reasonable value of the services actually rendered. The elements of the substance are determined by state common law, and storage conditions differ by state: keep one vial in each of the fifty states, the fifty-first in the drawer that holds the territories, and do not transfer between states without the advice of local counsel, who are the only licensed handlers. Store away from express contracts — proximity to a signed, complete, unmodified contract causes the substance to precipitate and become inadmissible, and no amount of pleading will re-suspend it. The Rooms where the vial has been kept longest is the archive of the Grange Hall Rooms, Sale No. 27, where the desk card describing the vial’s instrument has gone soft at the corners; the handling record of that room is cross-filed with Lot 41 of Sale No. 27, because the Rooms files its worn cards by the hand that wrote them, and this card was written by the same hand as the consular one.

8 Exposure controls / personal protection

No occupational exposure limit exists, because the substance is the limit: it caps the recovery at the reasonable value of the services, which is the only exposure control the law permits and the only one the market accepts. Protective equipment for the plaintiff’s side: a complete service log, dated, countersigned where possible, with photographs where the service left a mark. For the defendant’s side: a copy of every express contract, modification, and implied-amendment memo in the file, laid face down — face up, the room begins arguing about them before the question is asked. Eye protection is advised for all parties reading the award, which is long, and for no one reading the interest thereon, which is longer.

9 Physical and chemical properties

Appearance: a number, rendered in the currency of the forum. Odour: none, though rooms in which the measure has just been read keep the smell of a ledger for a week. Melting point: n/a. The substance is measured by one instrument only, The Dial — a brass dial, graduated from 1 to 16, mounted above the archivist’s desk at the Rooms, whose needle moves only when an earned value is being paid, and never on the way down. The Dial is read against a desk card kept in its bell jar, and the card, set at the Rooms long enough to wear soft at the corners, carries its instruction in the hand of the hand that wrote the consular stamp. It is transcribed here exactly as it stands, kept word for word the way the Rooms keep everything:

“The Dial (reading the desk card, worn soft at the Rooms, as It Was Earned The archivist of the eight-count unchanged.”

Read against the card: The Dial is what is reading; it reads the desk card; the card is worn soft at the Rooms, which is where it has stood; and its instruction is the motto of the measure — as It Was Earned, never more, never less, the reasonable value and not the hopeful value. The archivist of the eight-count is the keeper of the account’s eight counts — enrichment, expense, equity, conscience, measure, state, date, signature — and unchanged means that the archivist keeps those eight counts exactly as they were set down, adds nothing, strikes nothing, and is the reason the dial has never once been wrong at the Rooms. The needle in the margin of this sheet is its twin.

10 Stability and reactivity

Stable when stored by the state that made it; reactive in the hands of a party who would have it apply a price the contract already fixed, to which it responds by precipitating out of the case entirely. Incompatible with: liquidated damages clauses that survive the modification, penalties dressed as estimates, and the firm belief that a handshake is a contract in every state (it is not). Under no circumstances should the substance be combined with a quantum meruit claim where a complete and unmodified express contract governs the subject matter; the mixture is dismissed, and the dispensary does not refund the filing fee.

11 Toxicological information

Not toxic in the ordinary sense; the substance is the antidote. Acute exposure to an unpaid service, however, produces the classic symptom set: the plaintiff’s side pale, the market testimony restless, the briefs thickening week on week. Chronic exposure of a defendant to a benefit held past the date of equity produces the slower condition the profession files under the substance’s own name — enrichment that has become unjust — and the only treatment on record is the one in section 4. The equity desk’s case notes, kept at 244 Chancery Lane, run to eleven volumes and close each with the same finding: the benefit moved; the conscience did not; restitution followed.

12 Environmental information

The substance is persistent in the marketplace and non-biodegradable in the drawer: a course of dealing, once established, does not rot. It has been observed migrating between contracts the way a current moves under a lock — the implied agreement seeping in where the express one has thinned — and the environment of a long business relationship is the single most contaminated site the profession knows. Remediation is available and it is the same as treatment: the account, the market, the dial. No release of the substance to a forum that does not recognise it; several states store it under different names, and a label that does not match the local shelf is a label that will not open.

13 Disposal considerations

Do not dispose of the measure. The residue of a settled quantum meruit claim — the time-and-materials record, the modification memos, the photographs — is to be retained by the archivist of the eight-count and filed unchanged, because an account that has once been read correctly is the only thing in the building that cannot be read twice into different numbers. Disposal is proper only of the waste: the drafts of the brief that argued a price into a measure, the estimate letters, and the hopeful-value column, which is shredded and the ash filed, for the record, under P301-QM.

14 Transport information

UN No. for the common law: not assigned. The substance travels only by service — by the work itself moving from the plaintiff’s hands to the defendant’s use — and the bill of lading is the service log. It is not to be carried across a state line in a brief; it must be re-declared in the forum state in that state’s own elements, re-weighed on that state’s scale, and it arrives at its destination exactly as rich as it left, which is to say, at the reasonable value of the services and not a cent of the feeling. Customs at the border between contract and quasi-contract will ask the plaintiff to state the three elements; state them in order and the gate opens.

15 Regulatory information

Regulated, in the United States, entirely by state common law; no federal register entry exists, and the office in charge is the bench of each state, sitting in the order the states made. This sheet is not a rule in any jurisdiction and is not to be cited as one; it is the dispensary’s label for a substance older than the label. The New York three-element test is reproduced in section 3 as the reference standard, and the other fifty shelves are to be checked against their own state’s elements before the vial is opened. Companion documentation: the sheet’s sister on the same shelf, SDS No. CA-215 — Clement of Alexandria, cross-filed for the handling notes, and the cover of the house at the front of the catalogue, where the shelf list begins.
