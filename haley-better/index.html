I. The Lot

The album sits on the rostrum to the right of the empty 3×9×5 pan, in the slot between Lot 41 and the coffee urn. It is one of a small first pressing issued on 29 April 2016 by the two houses Ole and Red Dot, who released the album together and whose two colophons sit, one above the other, at the foot of the back cover. The disc itself is not played at the sale; the household’s rule is that a lot which has already been heard enough times is not to be heard again in the room, and Better has been heard by this household more times than the household will count in open session. Bidders who wish to hear it may stand at the door and listen to the record player in the bakery next room, which plays it, on the evidence of the turntable marks, almost every morning.

II. On the Music, for the Record

The catalogue records, for those who bid on hearsay: Better is the second studio album by American singer and songwriter Haley Reinhart, and it came to sale by way of two singles and a long fuse. The first promotional single, a cover of Elvis Presley’s “Can’t Help Falling in Love,” was released on 9 October 2015, six months before the album itself, which is a long fuse for a promotional single but a short one for a voice that has been doing the slow songs since the 1950s invented them. The lead single, “Better,” came out on 8 April 2016, the very day the album became available for pre-order — the two events being dated so close together in this catalogue that the auctioneer treats them as a single event, the day the album announced itself. The critics, when the album reached 29 April, were met to it. They praised the production; they praised its fusion of genres and eras, the way the album leans back across the decades and takes what it wants without asking; they praised Reinhart’s voice and her personality in equal measure; and they praised the album’s overall message of empowerment, which the household finds is the least surprising thing in the lot, a record named Better arriving with a message of better being almost self-evident. The chart, less generous with adjectives, took the album at number 22 on the Independent Albums chart, where it debuted, and the trade counted 7,500 units in its first week. The household has never understood why a debut at 22 is called a debut and a debut at 1 is called anything at all.

III. The Inscription on the Inner Flap

Pencilled across the inner flap, in the first owner’s hand, below the track list and above the barcode, a line that the catalogue preserves word for word:

“My lines had the impudence of my age.”

The auctioneer will read the line the way the household reads it: as a condition note, not a confession. A vocal line, on this album, is a thing that crosses decades on purpose — a 1961 ballad carried through 2016 by a voice old enough to know the original and young enough to outface it. The lines on Better did have the impudence of their age, which is to say the age in which they were sung, and the owner who pencilled the observation clearly meant it as praise, the way a pawnbroker means it when he prices a thing above what the room thinks it is. The pencil is soft, the letters are small, and the line is not to be inked over, cleaned off, or corrected. It stays where the hand that wrote it left it, facing the buyer every time the gatefold opens.

IV. Provenance

Issued 29 April 2016 by Ole and Red Dot. First heard, per the household ledger, on a Tuesday in May, at the turntable in the bakery next room, with the oven still down from the morning bake, which the ledger records was the correct order of operations. The owner, a singer herself, of a smaller room than the record deserves, kept the album in the drawer of her practice piano for six years, where the two singles and the gatefold liner card were kept with it; the drawer explains the sleeve’s single crease, see the Condition Report. In 2022 the piano came to the household on Magsaysay Avenue with the drawer half full and the ledger page open to the line about impudence, which the household has since copied into its own records. The two singles are present: the cover of “Can’t Help Falling in Love,” 9 October 2015, and “Better,” 8 April 2016. The buyer is reminded that the pre-order opened on the same day as the lead single and that the household does not, as a matter of catalogue policy, date its affections to the day.

V. Condition Report

  • Sleeve: near fine; one diagonal crease from the piano drawer, original, to be kept, not pressed.
  • Disc: plays clean to the last track; the marks at the end are the household’s and go with the room.
  • Inscription: present, legible, word for word, on the inner flap; the pencil is soft but has held for the years it has had to hold.
  • Track list and both colophons, Ole above Red Dot, legible; the fusion of genres and eras remains audible and has not degraded with storage.
  • Chart record: number 22, Independent Albums; 7,500 units in the first week; both figures verified against the trade, the trade having verified itself against the chart, which the auctioneer considers sufficient.
  • The message of empowerment is intact. Under the household rule it cannot be voided.

VI. Notes of the Auctioneer

Lot 42 stands on the rostrum beside Lot 41, The Green-Gold Bog lamp, whose jar the album is not to be placed inside, the water being unboilable and the disc, on the evidence of the turntable marks, a morning thing. Both lots are offered from the same estate, the estate of Etta Collins, held in trust by the household, and both are sold under the first-page rule of the family recipe ledger, which is to say that whatever is voided, bake — and a record that has been playing in the next room since May 2016 is, under that rule, more an ingredient than an object. The catalogue itself opens at the first page of the household ledger, where the front matter of the house is kept and where this lot is bound as Lot 42. The buyer may note, for comfort, that the same household has written its own apologies in its own time — the letter at Dear Ashton Gate shows the hand that keeps the ledger and the hand that folds it back into a season ticket, and the auctioneer has seen no lot in this catalogue that cannot be forgiven in the same style: slowly, on the record, and eventually.
