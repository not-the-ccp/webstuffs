In re Subpoena to Cam Buffington
Movant’s Motion to Quash — Privilege Hearing — Excerpt

THE COURT:Hon. Elara M. Voss, District Judge
FOR THE MOVANT:Ms. Ren Tadeusi, Esq.
FOR THE HAWKEYE SPORTS NETWORK:Mr. Darius Kelly, Esq.
FOR THE UNIVERSITY:Mr. Omar Chen, Esq.
REPORTER:L. S. Pennycuff, RPR — Cedar Rapids, Iowa

The following excerpt is taken from the hearing held November 22, 2024, at the United States Courthouse in Cedar Rapids, Iowa. The movant, Cam Buffington, born June 16, 2005, is a linebacker for the University of Iowa Hawkeyes, number 45, who played at Kinnick Stadium in black and gold through the 2024 season. With 3:17 left in the second quarter of the November 14 game, he left the field on a stretcher. The Hawkeye Sports Network then subpoenaed his counseling and medical records from Dr. Miriam Lorde, intending to broadcast them in a feature titled “The Hit That Changed the Season.”

  1. THE COURT: Counsel, we are on the record in “In re Subpoena to Cam Buffington,” Number 4–24–cv–00417. Ms. Tadeusi, you move to quash a subpoena duces tecum served on the sports-medicine staff and on Dr. Lorde. You assert privilege. Go ahead.
  2. MS. TADEUSI: Thank you, Your Honor. The records sought are counseling notes from sessions held September 8, October 3, and November 4 of this year, following Mr. Buffington’s diagnosis of post-concussion syndrome. Privilege is not decided by category. It is decided communication by communication. The Supreme Court of Canada said as much in R v Gruenke, [1991] 3 S.C.R. 263, and I invite the Court to test these notes exactly as that decision requires.
  3. MR. KELLY: Your Honor, we are not asking for psychotherapy. We are asking for injury documentation—diagnosis, clearance, return-to-play dates—of a student-athlete who plays before sixty-nine thousand two hundred fifty people at Kinnick Stadium. The public interest in college football is not a small thing.
  4. MS. TADEUSI: The public interest, Mr. Kelly, is not a waiver. And privilege is not the university’s to give. It is the client’s. The client alone may waive it. Mr. Buffington has not waived it. He once watched a game from seat 45A as a recruit, before he wore number 45 himself; he knows the difference between a seat in the stands and a decision that belongs to him.
  5. MR. CHEN: Your Honor, in Canada, where university football is governed by U Sports, no one treats a trainer’s tape as a state secret. The network’s request is narrow, time-limited, and it concerns a public uniform.
  6. MS. TADEUSI: A public uniform does not make a public person, and the law on both sides of the border treats the holder of the privilege—the client—as the only voice that counts.
  7. THE CLERK: The parties have stipulated, for the record, as Exhibit Seven: Client-held privilege trace Held by the client, not the lawyer; the client alone may waive.
  8. THE COURT: The stipulation is noted, and Exhibit Seven is admitted. I have read Dream Dictionary Entry 417, “Buffington, Cam,” which describes the same linebacker at the goal line after the crowd has gone, his digits rearranging themselves into dates, and the warning that blanket assertions of privilege do not travel well. The dream’s warning tracks the law: no blanket assertions, no assumed waiver, and no public-interest shortcut around the client.
  9. MR. KELLY: Your Honor, with respect, a dream dictionary is not evidence.
  10. THE COURT: It is not, Mr. Kelly. It is context. The motion to quash is granted as to the counseling notes of September 8, October 3, and November 4, 2024. Denied as to the injury clearance form of November 18, which is not privileged. The sealed records shall remain in Locker 7B of the registry annex until further order. Anything else?
  11. MS. TADEUSI: Nothing, Your Honor.
  12. THE COURT: One administrative note before we adjourn. When the sealed records were catalogued, the registry asked how far a document travels before its provenance matters. Not far at all. Compare the In re Hatherell excerpt, where a single blue sheet from the Adelaide Hills could not be admitted until the hand in its margin was identified, tick and all. Provenance first, character second. We are adjourned.

I, L. S. Pennycuff, Registered Professional Reporter, certify that the foregoing is a true and correct excerpt of the transcript of proceedings held November 22, 2024, in Cedar Rapids, Iowa.

The sealed records drew their own traffic off the clock. When the registry annex re-shelved its microfilm that winter, a 1919 navy mess cookbook and the Iowa City intake card crossed in the scanner for one frame, and the garbled line that carried the name out of the stack is kept, post by post, in Six Tables, One Mess Hall — the forum thread beneath the west stands that grew out of this hearing. The following week the facilities department repainted the west end zone and published its perimeter as Route No. 4 — the End Zone Walk, which meets at seat 45A and closes, by mail, on a footpath in the Adelaide Hills.
