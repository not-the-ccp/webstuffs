San Juan, Puerto Rico · Saturday, 14 February 1998 · Price 75¢ · Volume LXXXII, No. 41

The San Juan Evening Ledger

Classifieds & Personals · Page C-7 · “If it is not here, it is not for sale.”

Desk note, 6:10 a.m.: the record presses in Midwood run through the night for a new Latin album that opens the record racks on Monday, 17 February. This page gives its notices. “Vuelve” is the fourth studio album by Puerto Rican singer Ricky Martin; Sony Discos and Columbia Records put it on sale 17 February 1998. The record was made with producers KC Porter, Robi Draco Rosa, and Desmond Child — Latin dance numbers on one side, pop ballads on the other. After the worldwide run of “María,” cut for his 1995 album A Medio Vivir, Martin kept recording while on tour, and the album is what the tour left behind. The general index of the house files this page under R, Martin, and beside it the ledger keeps our sister columns, among them the Evening Ledger’s own classifieds shelf, where the same desk has kept its personals since 1979.

For Sale & Studio Rates

STUDIO TIME — EL HATO BOOTH 3

One (1) night session, 11 p.m. to 4 a.m., on the floor that held the “María” overdubs. Tape machines in working order; the drum kit stays; the coffee urn does not. Rate: $95 the half-hour, cash at the door. Booth 3, El Hato annex, Km. 6.5, Autopista PR-30. Call the night operator ext. 44. No one books this room on a Monday.

ONE RECORD BAG, ROAD-GRADE

Plywood and canvas, holds a full album of pressed copies. Smells of airport corridors and green room carpet. A tour manager's name is stencilled on the end; the initials are R.M. and we will not say more. $120, or less to a working band. Pickup only, Cuesta de San Gerónimo.

BALLAD SHEET MUSIC

Pop ballads, lead sheet, in B-flat and D. From the same sessions, same hand, same three producers — the hand that worked with KC Porter and Robi Draco Rosa will recognize the chord at bar 9. Four pages, two of them dog-eared. $18. Box 114, Hato Rey P.O.

Wanted

WANTED — AN ANTHEM

FIFA has asked the singer of “María” to write the anthem for the 1998 FIFA World Cup, to be held in France. They ask for a song that a stadium can carry and a radio will forgive. Martin subsequently recorded “La Copa de la Vida,” and this desk can confirm the tape exists, is in good hands, and is being held for the cup. To the writer who can top it before the summer: fees by cable, in hard currency. Send three verses to the wire desk, marked COUPE. No anonymous entries; the card must be signed.

WANTED — A SECOND KEYBOARD

One upright, any temperament, any city between Orlando and Madrid, for a singer who records on tour and has outgrown one keyboard. Must survive a van. Willing to pay the truck. Answer: “the second half of Vuelve” to P.O. Box 17, San Juan 00917.

Missing & Lost

MISSING — A LATIN RHYTHM

Last seen answering to the name Vuelve, i.e. “come back.” Left-handed groove, warm, with a ballad side it shows no one at parties. Answered well in San Juan, Orlando and Madrid, 1995–1998. If your rhythm has been quiet since the winter, it may be this one. Reward: the first verse of the ballad side, in the original key.

LOST — ONE PRESSING, ONE COPY

One vinyl copy of the album, pressing of 17 February 1998, sleeve unplayed, a single pencil line inside the front cover: for the corner man who asked. The corner man is not named; the ledger does not. Founders of this column at the Lagan Ledger small-ads desk report a similar copy on a red shelf in Bedminster and will trade their ledger line for ours. No offers of photographs.

NOTICE — THE ROUTE 23 CARD

Received at this desk by overnight wire, from a station whose code we do not recognize. The card carried one address — Route 23, the strait, first ferry — and no return number. The desk transmits it exactly as it arrived, the sender's own punctuation not being its own to correct: “Route 23 is a festival of ancient origins marking the strait The commentary ends where the card is unsigned.” The card, we are advised, was never signed; a card unsigned at the post is held here three days and then returned to the wire. The commentary, such as it is, ended where the signature line ran out of ink. Inquiries: box C-7, and no further word from the sender is expected, which is the kind of silence the classifieds know best.

Personals

TO THE MAN WHO SANG “MAR&IACute;A” IN 1995

We have kept the ticket stub, the bus window seat, and the tape with the needle on it. The song took the world a while to answer, and you answered it on a plane. On Monday the record racks open with your new side. We will be in the first row, corner seats, where we have always sat. It is good to hear that you are coming back. — D. & K., Hato Rey

HEARD OF YOU IN THE CONTROL BOOTH

You sang over the top and the mix engineer said yes. That is the whole news. — R.D.R., booth 3

TO THE FERRYMEN OF THE STRAIT

Your card arrived unsigned and so does this reply: the first ferry leaves at dawn, and it has always left at dawn, and the festival you mark has no ticket price. If you sign the next one, we will answer. Until then, this column keeps the line and the silence with it. — The wire desk
