DEPARTMENT OF PATENTS & SPECIFICATIONS

Summary of Application, for Examination · Class 49 — Civil Government, Military Substitution

Specification No. 1836/42

A Method, and Associated Apparatus, for the Orderly Replacement of Civilian Government by Military Rule, and the Suspension of Civilian Legal Processes, for a Specified Duration or Indefinitely

ApplicantGALLIPOLIOU & SOUTHWEST PACIFIC RAILWAY, by its Station Master
Filed21 April 1836, received again 19 February 1942 (see ¶2.2)
SerialMX-TEX-1835-A / SWPA-DAR-1942-B, consolidated
Examiner assignedCol. Amos T. Fairweather, 4th Bench, with the 13-day allowance (see Prior Art No. 1)
StatusUnder examination; liberty to oppose granted until the dial is wound down

1. Abstract

The present specification describes an apparatus, hereafter the Declaration Set, by which a civilian government may be replaced by military rule and the civilian legal processes suspended in favour of military powers, so that the whole arrangement continues for a specified amount of time, or indefinitely, and the standard civil liberties are suspended for as long as it continues. The invention is not a weapon and claims no method of combat. It is a method of keeping: keeping the suspension on the ledger where it can be read, kept in order, and, when the dial reaches its stop, given back. Most often the apparatus is declared in times of war, or in emergencies such as civil unrest and natural disaster; alternatively it may be declared in instances of a military coup d'état, in which case the applicant advises that the examiner should be especially punctual with the clock.

2. State of the Art

The examiner is referred to the following prior art, of which there is much, and of which the Department keeps a full shelf:

Prior Art No. 1 — The Declaration of 1835, Coahuila y Tejas

In the autumn of 1835 a head of state, abrogating the 1824 constitution of his own country, put an entire province under the apparatus and turned the key. The result, recorded at length in the site’s own field notes, is The Runaway Scrape, Route 1836: the great eastward evacuation of some six hundred miles, wagons first, families close behind, from the Alamo to the San Jacinto swamp. Prior Art No. 1 is entered here both as the invention’s oldest working example and as a caution: a suspension declared without a dial, by a government that had already abrogated its own constitution, is a suspension that the civilian population must leave by wagon, in September, through the dust. The Department notes that the apparatus in this instance was eventually wound down at the edge of the Buffalo Bayou at 11:00 a.m. on 21 April 1836, in approximately one hour, the president of the abrogating nation being taken in a cane thicket — a termination speed the Department has not yet been able to replicate in the laboratory.

Prior Art No. 2 — The Darwin Night, 19 February 1942

At 11:06 in the morning, two hundred and forty-eight aircraft ran over the port of Darwin in waves, the largest air raid the Australian mainland has taken, and the apparatus was declared as the station master stood in the middle of it with nine of his own machines lost. The full working of this embodiment — the timetable, the DSO, the Medal of Freedom, the line in capitals from General MacArthur, and the one telegram the logbook kept verbatim — is carried in The Bostock Mail, Gallipoli to Darwin, whose Platform 3, the Platform by the Hush, is cited as the apparatus’s home-front companion record. Prior Art No. 2 is the embodiment the present specification improves on, and the station master’s rule of that night — do not flip the logbook while the raid is running — is herein adopted as Claim 6.

Prior Art No. 3 — The Coup, Unnamed

A further class of declaration, in which the apparatus is put in place not by the declared emergency but by the declarers, is entered under the general heading “instances of military coups d'état” without a named exhibit, the Department having a rule that Prior Art No. 3 is to be cited, not named. Suffice that it appears, that it suspends, and that the dial, in this class, is the one part of the apparatus the inventors most often forget to fit.

3. Summary of the Invention

The defects of the prior art are three. First, the suspension is written nowhere legible, so that nobody can say how long the standard civil liberties have been out, and no one can say by whose hand they went out. Second, the duration is either nowhere fixed or fixed in words only (“until further notice”), which is not a stop on any dial. Third, the termination, when it comes, comes by argument rather than by the apparatus, and is therefore late, uneven, and remembered inaccurately, the way the Scrape remembers everything except its first mile.

The invention therefore comprises: (a) a declaration bell, of the pattern the Department keeps in the fourth drawer, rung once at the declaration and never rung a second time except for the lifting; (b) a ledger, bound in the green cover of the service, in which every suspension, extension, and restoration is entered in the hand of the person who did it, what arrives arriving, undeliverable messages kept in place and not flung on the fire; (c) a duration dial with two detents, the lower marked SPECIFIED and the upper marked INDEFINITE, the upper detent being permitted but requiring the signature of the examiner in the margin; and (d) a restoration key, cut for one hand only, which returns the civil liberties to their racks in the order the ledger records them, the last liberty entered being the first liberty restored, as is proper in any honest cabinet.

4. Description of the Preferred Embodiment

Fig. 1 shows the Declaration Set in its housing: the bell on the left arm, the dial at the centre, the ledger on the right arm, the restoration key on its lanyard below. The bell is to be hung at a height that a man on a mule cart can hear and a president in a tent cannot ignore, the precedent for the latter being a cane thicket, which has done the work before. The ledger is of the pattern used on the platforms the applicant keeps, and is issued with the applicant’s standing rule plate fixed to its inner cover. The rule plate reads, word for word:

GALLIPOLIOU & SOUTHWEST PACIFIC RAILWAY The Bostock Mail keeps no spare tickets.

— Rule plate No. 3, fixed to the inner cover of every ledger issued with this apparatus

The rule is not ornamental. The apparatus carries its own ticket stock — one ticket per person, for one passage through the declared period, marked with the duration dial’s setting at the moment of issue — and it keeps no spare tickets, because a spare ticket is a suspension that has not yet happened, and the invention will not hold one. When the stock is gone, the stock is gone; the ledger says so, and the ledger is the whole of the record. The ticket stock of the applicant’s mail service, which has run on this rule since the Gallipoli siding and will run on it through Darwin to the Platform by the Hush, is therefore cited as the ticket stock of the preferred embodiment.

Operation. The bell is rung; the entry is made; the dial is set to SPECIFIED and wound to the declared term, or, in the emergencies the apparatus exists for — the war, the unrest, the flood the water keeps and the road does not — to INDEFINITE, with the examiner’s signature. While the dial runs, the standard civil liberties are suspended by the simple device of the dial’s running, and every hand that touches the ledger touches it under the Claim 6 rule: do not flip the logbook while the raid is running. When the dial reaches its stop, or when the restoration key turns — which in the case of INDEFINITE is the whole of the invention’s quietest trick — the bell is rung once more, the entry is made, and the liberties come back off the racks in ledger order. The set is then shelved, the key returned to its lanyard, and the next season’s emergency, when it comes — and it comes — is met by the same three pieces of brass and one green book.

5. The Claims

  1. Claim 1. A method of replacing civilian government by military rule, and of suspending civilian legal processes for military powers, characterised in that the replacement is made by bell, entered by ledger, and terminated by the same apparatus that made it.
  2. Claim 2. An apparatus as claimed in Claim 1, in which the duration of the suspension is set on a dial bearing a SPECIFIED detent for a stated term and an INDEFINITE detent for so long as it continues, the latter requiring the examiner’s signature in the margin.
  3. Claim 3. An apparatus as claimed in Claim 2, in which the standard civil liberties are suspended for as long as the dial runs and restored, upon the dial’s stop, in the order the ledger records them.
  4. Claim 4. An apparatus as claimed in Claim 1, comprising ticket stock of one ticket per person for one passage through the declared period, and no spare tickets.
  5. Claim 5. An apparatus as claimed in Claim 4, in which the ticket stock is that issued by the applicant’s mail service on the route from the Gallipoli siding to Darwin, under rule plate No. 3.
  6. Claim 6. A method of keeping the ledger, characterised in that the logbook is not to be flipped while the raid is running.
  7. Claim 7. An apparatus as claimed in Claim 1, for use in times of war, in emergencies such as civil unrest and natural disasters, and, alternatively, in instances of a military coup d'état, in which last class the dial is fitted first and the rest follows as best it can.
  8. Claim 8. A restoration key, cut for one hand only, for the return of suspended liberties, which key shall not be cut a second time without the first key having been returned to its lanyard and the bell rung once.

Notice to the public

Persons under a declared suspension are advised that their liberties are not lost but shelved, and that the ledger says when they come back, provided the dial has a stop on it. Persons in the class of Prior Art No. 3 are advised that the dial is the one part of the apparatus the inventors most often forget to fit, and that the examiner is watching for exactly that.

The bell is rung once. The ledger says so. That is the whole of the record.
