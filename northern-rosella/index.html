The smutty parrot of the north

The northern rosella is the dark jewel of the Top End's parrot roster. Platycercus venustus — formerly Brown's rosella, still the smutty rosella in the old field guides — is a parrot of northern Australia, ranging from the Gulf of Carpentaria and Arnhem Land across to the Kimberley. It was described by Heinrich Kuhl in 1820, and two subspecies are recognised: venustus of the Northern Territory, whose cheeks run to clean white, and hillii of Western Australia, whose cheeks are a dusty blue. It is a bird best met slowly, at a waterhole in the hour after sunrise.

Where to watch them

Come in the dry season, May through October, when the roads hold and the birds come down to drink. The classic sites are Kakadu National Park's East Alligator River at dawn, the salmon-gum woodland around Katherine, the Mitchell Plateau, and the Prince Regent River country of the Kimberley. Listen first: the chattering contact call usually arrives before the bird does, and a pair will often sit in the same dead tree for half an hour at a time, preening and calling back and forth.

Season: May–October (dry season) · Best hours: 6:30–9:00 and 16:00–18:00 · Bring: binoculars, hat, water, and patience. The Kimberley sun does not forgive.

What to look for

A northern rosella runs about thirty centimetres from crown to tail-tip, and it is unusually coloured for a rosella: a dark head and neck over pale cheeks — predominantly white in the Northern Territory subspecies and blue in the Western Australian hillii — with a mantle and scapulars of black carrying fine yellow scallops, while the back, rump and underparts are pale yellow carrying fine black scallops. The long tail is black edged with pale yellow, and folds into a neat wedge when the bird lands. Look for the scallops: they catch the light like the edge of a freshly feathered wing.

A note from the old guide

It will not be read by anyone who has read Chapter IX, learned the repertory he would spend the next night.

That slip of onion paper, headed Chapter IX, was found tucked inside the Katherine visitor centre's reference copy of the old Top End bird guide. The duty ranger says it was left by a previous owner who spent a week beside one waterhole learning the birds' calls — the repertory — before moving north. Read it as a warning, or as an invitation: the northern rosella will reward anyone who stays long enough to learn its voice.

The two subspecies

  • Platycercus venustus venustus — Northern Territory and the Gulf of Carpentaria; white cheeks.
  • Platycercus venustus hillii — Western Australia, the Kimberley; blue cheeks.
