Specification — § 1, what is known in the art

Background of the Invention

The present application concerns lysergic acid diethylamide, the semisynthetic compound of the ergot — Claviceps purpurea, the grey-blue grain that has ridden the rye and the wheat of Europe for a thousand years and fired the monasteries that stored it. The compound of the present invention is derived from the lysergic acid of that grain and is known for its potent psychological effects. It is extremely potent: noticeable effects are on record at doses as low as 20 micrograms, and a still smaller platform is in use, in the trade called microdosing, where the rider takes fractions of the noticed floor for purposes the specification declines to define. The applicant records, as the art knows it, that despite widespread recreational and spiritual use, no fatal human overdoses of the compound have been documented. It is this last fact that the examiner has circled, in red, and that Claim 6 turns on.

The parent substance has a long record in the houses of the old orders. Ergot's fire was known to the monasteries long before the present applicant knew the name of it, and the compounding office keeps a file of the visits — the shaking, the fire, the long thin procession out of the grain store — which it prints here only to say that the present invention is the same substance, isolated, numbered, and dosed to a platform. The office that examined the present application shares a bench with the line that runs the same rule through the city: the Eighteen Local, Age of Consent Service & Field Commentary · H.M. No. 14, whose inspector keeps the same red ink this examiner uses, and whose standing order — the platform is fixed by the register, not by the timetable — is quoted below as if it were the office's own.

Specification — § 2, what is claimed to be new

Summary of the Invention

There is provided, in accordance with the present invention, a method of administering the compound of Claim 1 in doses fixed to a platform rather than to a taste, and a carrier for the dose. Taken orally, the compound has an onset of action of 0.4 to 1.0 hour and a duration of 7 to 12 hours — in the line's own arithmetic, the ride begins somewhere between the 41 minutes past the hour and the quarter, and it runs, without variation, the length of a working day. In recreational settings the compound is commonly administered via small tabs of blotter paper, cut to the size of the Form 3B enrolment slip the ticket clerk stamps at Ochs Avenue, and impregnated with the dose before travel. The carrier is the present invention's second contribution, because the compound is too small to be counted by hand at the noticed floor: what the eye cannot weigh, the tab will measure. Where the rider requires more than the noticed floor, the specification provides a second platform, 100 micrograms, to which particular acts of the day's service are held by a dual-gauge notice posted at the 50 — the same notice the Eighteen Local posts at its 16th, and the same order: where the register raises the gauge for particular acts, the higher platform governs those acts alone.

Specification — § 4, the preferred embodiment, in service

Detailed Description

The preferred embodiment is a service, and the office describes it as a service is described on the line. The rider takes the tab at the departure point; the onset of action, 0.4 to 1.0 hour, is the walk to the platform; the duration, 7 to 12 hours, is the ride itself, and it is the specification's only admission that the timetable of the day may slip — the compound is the one article of the compounding office that the inspector is not permitted to strike from the schedule, because the rider cannot ring the bell to be let out early, and the office will not pretend otherwise. The compound is taken mainly for recreation, and for the spiritual purposes the specification calls mystical without defining them, the way the line calls its own rule without defining the passenger. The rider who reports the ride at the terminus is asked the same question the Eighteen Local asks of its last passenger each evening — how was the ride — and the answer on this line is never the same one, which is the difference between the two lines, and the whole of it: the other line carried a rule that arrived on schedule; this one carries a day that does not.

The office notes, for the record, the one passenger it can name who rode the full platform to the terminus and then on to Italy. He was a Jesuit, a priest of the two skylines, who answered the Rome posting in 1887 the way every competent hand must, and whose silver watch — stopped at 4:41 — is still listed, dry-cleaned and unclaimed, at the lost property counter of his trail's third stop. The trail of that passenger, New York to the Tiber, six stops, is walked in The Boursaud Trail — a six-stop tourist brochure of the Rev. Father Edward V. Boursaud, S.J. (1840–1902), beside which his sister kept the Journal Français, ruled in both languages. The compounding office holds the three in one file: the priest, the diary, and the application, because all three are records of a passage taken at the right hour, on the right platform, by a hand the register had already reached.

Specification — § 5, the claims

What Is Claimed

  1. A semisynthetic compound derived from the lysergic acid of Claviceps purpurea, being the diethylamide thereof, compound number LSD-25, for use in doses fixed to a platform.
  2. The compound of Claim 1, in which the noticed floor of effect stands at 20 micrograms and a lower platform, for the trade use called microdosing, stands at a fraction thereof.
  3. A carrier for the compound of Claim 1, comprising a square of blotter paper cut into tabs of the size of a Form 3B enrolment slip, each tab impregnated with a single dose before travel.
  4. A method of administering the compound of Claim 1 orally, the onset of action being 0.4 to 1.0 hour and the duration of the service 7 to 12 hours, the timetable of the day being subject to slip and the ride not being terminable at the rider's request.
  5. A dual-gauge dose schedule, comprising a noticed floor at 20 micrograms and a full platform at 100 micrograms to which particular acts of the day's service are held by a posted notice at the 50.
  6. The compound of Claim 1, with the record that, despite widespread recreational and spiritual use, no fatal human overdoses thereof have been documented; the circled fact of the examiner, incorporated by reference.
  7. A service, run on the order that the platform is fixed by the register, not by the timetable, the timetable only saying when the train stops there.

Examiner's file — the red ink, added 26 April 1943

The Examiner's Margin

The examiner of the present application is the same hand that inspects the platforms of the Eighteen Local, and he examined it in red ink, the ink of the standing orders. Against Claims 2 and 5 together he wrote, and the office transcribes word for word, in his own punctuation:

"These are the whole to the higher platform governs those acts alone."

The line is kept unparseable, as the office keeps such lines — but its sense is plain to anyone who has ridden the line the examiner inspects. These are the two dose platforms, 20 and 100, and they are the whole of the dose doctrine: nothing is claimed below the noticed floor except the microdosing fraction, and nothing above the full platform at all. And the higher platform — the 100, the terminus of the dose line, the place where no notice need be posted past it — governs those acts alone: the particular acts of the day's service that the dual-gauge notice holds back from the 50. The examiner adds nothing else, and the application stands as filed, the two platforms fixed by the register, the ride running from the 41 minutes, the terminus open at the end of the day for the water and the seat, as it stands every day of the year, on the minute to the minute.
