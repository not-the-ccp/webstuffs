Coventry, 14 November 1940

A radio play excerpt · BBC Home Service · transcribed from the broadcast of 15 November 1940

First transmitted — as far as the record shows — between the all-clear and the morning papers, the night the Luftwaffe flew Operation Mondscheinsonote, "Moonlight Sonata," over the Midlands.

Dramatis personae

  • HARRY VANE — American correspondent, Chicago Daily News, writing his column from a fire-watch post on Broadgate.
  • WARDEN NELLIE PRITCHARD — ARP warden, Station 12, Coventry.
  • FIRE WATCHER TOM CREIGHTON — on the roof of St Michael's Cathedral, with sand buckets and stirrup pumps.
  • ANNOUNCER — BBC Home Service.

Excerpt

[Air-raid siren. Distant explosions. Anti-aircraft fire, thickening.]

ANNOUNCER. This is the BBC Home Service. The time is ten minutes past seven on the evening of Thursday, 14 November 1940. Coventry is under attack. The first sirens sounded at nineteen-twenty; the bombers of Luftflotte 3 are still overhead. We go now to Broadgate, where Harry Vane is watching the city burn.

VANE. Thank you, London. This is Harry Vane, Chicago Daily News, speaking from the fire-watch post above Broadgate. Coventry is a furnace tonight. St Michael's — the Cathedral, fourteenth century, its spire two hundred and ninety feet above the street — caught fire at eight o'clock, and the lead is running molten down the tower. I can see it from here, twenty blocks away, and the High Street is a line of sparks behind me.

WARDEN PRITCHARD. (Calling across the roof.) Mr Vane, you ought to be in the shelter. High Street took a stick at seven-thirty-five. Three shops down, and the market hall has lost its roof.

VANE. And here I am, Nellie, with my notebook and my column. Read the note from my berth in Great Lakes, so the column will not be closed until they are. My editor wired that from Chicago before I sailed. I still do not know who "they" are. Tonight, I think, "they" might be all of us on this roof.

[A bomb whistle. A heavy crump. Glass breaking.]

FIRE WATCHER CREIGHTON. Incoming! That one is magnesium, not high explosive — pass the sand bucket. We have put out twelve incendiaries since nine o'clock, and twelve dozen since midnight. The Daimler works on Radford Road is lit like a fairground, and Gosford Street is cinders.

VANE. Five hundred bombers came in from Orleans and Chartres under a full moon, and they are not finished. It is eleven o'clock now. The all-clear will not sound until morning.

WARDEN PRITCHARD. They will say five hundred and fifty-four dead, eight hundred and sixty-five injured, four thousand homes destroyed. The medieval city — the Old Grammar School, the timber yards on Spon Street — gone in one night.

CREIGHTON. The Cathedral fell at twenty to one. The roof went under at midnight and the walls held only until dawn. We watched it burn from Broadgate, and we kept pumping.

VANE. They will coin a word for it — coventrated — to mean a city erased from the air. I hope they never have to use it again. My column closes when the all-clear sounds, and not before. Until then, this is Harry Vane, in Coventry, signing off under fire.

[Siren wail, fading into the all-clear tone. Wind through broken glass.]

The night of the raid — timeline

All times local, 14–15 November 1940. Select an entry to mark the hour.

— selected
  1. 19:10

    Yellow warning

    The air-raid warning sounds over Coventry. Streets darken; the blackout holds.

  2. 19:20

    Red warning; the first wave

    Bombers of Luftflotte 3 cross the coast under a full moon. Operation Mondscheinsonote — "Moonlight Sonata" — begins. The first bombs fall on the city centre.

  3. 20:00

    The Cathedral burns

    St Michael's Cathedral catches fire. Watchers mount the roof with sand buckets and stirrup pumps against magnesium incendiaries.

  4. 22:00

    High Street ablaze

    The peak of the raid: more than two hundred bombers overhead. High Street, Broadgate and the market hall burn; the Daimler works on Radford Road glows like a fairground.

  5. 00:30

    Fifteen November

    A lull between waves. Fire wardens stirrup-pump the rooftops while the all-clear is still hours away.

  6. 00:50

    The roof collapses

    Just before one in the morning, St Michael's roof gives way and the Cathedral walls stand as a ruin.

  7. 03:00

    The final wave

    The last bombers cross back over the Channel. Gosford Street is cinders; four thousand homes are destroyed.

  8. 06:15

    All-clear

    The raiders have gone. Coventry counts the cost — around five hundred and fifty dead, eight hundred and sixty-five injured, a medieval centre erased. The word "coventrated" is born.
