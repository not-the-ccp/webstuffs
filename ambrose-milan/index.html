A Letter to the Editor

To the Editor of Webstuffs, from the clerk who keeps the ledger at sixteen frames per second. Dated 21 March, at 6:40, while the city is still cool.

Sir, — We write on a matter of correspondence material. The safety data sheets in this house have a hole in their alphabet, and we have decided the hole has a name: Aurelius Ambrosius, whom the liturgical books call Ambrose of Milan, whom the older tongue called Ambrosios, who lived from c. 339 to 4 April 397, and whom the Latin lists canonised as Saint Ambrose. The draft of his sheet — we are calling it SDS No. AM-397 — is on the desk beside the khash pot, waiting for the rest of this house to agree on its pictograms, and in the meantime a letter is the honest form. We will not pretend a bishop of Milan is a substance. We will say only that the file has come loose, as our other files come loose, and that the editor's pages are where this house sets down what it finds.

Train him first as he was trained: rhetoric and law. He was the Roman civil governor of Aemilia-Liguria, resident at Milan, a man who had signed dispatches and sat in court, and in 374 the see fell vacant with the appointed candidate, an elderly bishop named Wadda, dead before he could take it up. The cathedral filled, the bishop of Mediolanum's people would not let the Arian party keep the chair, and the cry went round until even the governor could not hear himself think: Ambrose episcopus! — Ambrose bishop! He was a catechumen that week, not yet baptised, and history recorded the acclamation at a public building while he was out on official business. He refused, of course. He is recorded as refusing three times, or four; the number drifts the way all numbers do in a chancery. The emperors were consulted, the emperor's mother, Justina, was consulted, and a man who had never once wanted the office was consecrated at Milan and held the see from 374 until his death.

What he did with the office belongs to the letter, not the sheet. His preaching and his writings made him one of the most influential Christian figures of the fourth century, and as bishop he became the prominent defender of orthodoxy, particularly against Arianism, holding the city to one confession when the court would have softened it. He was at the centre of the quarrel that split the empire: between the emperors Theodosius I and Magnus Maximus he attempted to mediate, and when Theodosius's troops broke the massacre at Thessalonica in 390, Ambrose did what a bishop does — he walked out, he excommunicated, he made the emperor kneel in penance in the narthex before he would come to the altar. The child's story goes with him: the bees settling on his face as he slept, the little comb of wax found on his lap by his schoolmaster, the first prophecy that this boy would feed a nation on his word. It is the sort of detail a chancery keeps for reasons it declines to state.

Our own ledger keeps its details at ledger speed. The sheet this one cross-files with — SDS No. CA-215, Clement of Alexandria, catechist, the positive form of the law of reciprocity, Rev. 68 — was revised on 21 March 2025 at 6:35, the same hour of the same date we are writing by, and it stands as our sister file at SDS No. CA-215 — Clement of Alexandria · Safety Data Sheet, 16 Sections. Clement taught at Alexandria the way Ambrose later preached at Milan: an educated man holding a rule against the whole room. Between the two of them the alphabet has a spine.

And one more line, set in the margin word for word, which the editor may find odd until he has read the clipping it came from — a far-future obituary, clipped from Edinburgh and pressed between the pages of the draft, concerning a writer of the Jean le Flambeur books, full account at Hannu Rajaniemi (1978–2047) — Obituary:

Remembrance A private burial will take place at Warriston Cemetery on 21 March.

Twenty-first of March. The revision of the sister sheet, the date of this letter, and the day a stranger's ledger closes at Warriston — a cemetery we cannot visit from here, in a city of rain and universities, kept in the same form as everything else this house sets down. We do not ask the editor to explain the coincidence. We ask him to publish the letter, to set the clipping where we have set it, and to leave the draft of AM-397 in the file where the alphabet waits. The city of Milan is mapped for us only at a distance — for the geography of what we can reach, the editor will have to take up the guide entry at Yerevan — Guidebook No. 88: The Pink City, where the hill, the fountains and the pot are all where the ledger says they are. We will not hurry Ambrose past 7:00.

We remain, sir, at the ledger speed,

The Clerk of the Chancery of the Hrazdan
with the pot, the garlic plate, and the marshrutka drivers' regard
