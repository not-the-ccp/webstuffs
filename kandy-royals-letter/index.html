Fourth is not a place, it is a direction

Sir,

When the Royals took the field for the sixth time in the Lanka Premier League this year, I told myself one thing: a city this size does not keep a team for six seasons to finish in the middle of the table. We are the team with the purple end of the dressing room, the team whose supporters climb down from the Fort on matchdays with scarves the colour of temple brass, and the 2026 season was the one we had to find out what that actually meant.

Angelo Mathews stood in front of the nets the whole fortnight before the draft, and I will not pretend the camp was without its small dramas. Four players were signed before the draft and sixteen more came through it — twenty names on one whiteboard by the last evening — and Coach Chaminda Vaas, who still reviews every session from the second row like a man watching his own innings replay, was never once seen raising his voice. The league stage told the truth of the build: four matches to win were lost by the narrowest of margins, the bowlers over-rotated in the third week, and Kandy Royals finished in fourth place. Fourth, I say again, is not a place. It is a direction. It means the Eliminator was on the table.

And the Eliminator it was, and we took it to Colombo — lost, to be plain about it, to Colombo Kaps, in a game the Kaps took at the death with two wickets and a single run of the kind we will argue about in tea shops for a decade. But I need someone, somewhere, in print, to record the innings that made the whole season. Lahiru Udara scored 434 runs for Kandy in 2026 — the most in the squad, and the most in the entire Lanka Premier League that year, a fact no scorebook in the country will now be able to dispute. He carried that total from the first league match to the last like a man carrying a lamp down a long staircase. And on the other end of the ledger, Nuwan Thushara took fourteen wickets for the Royals, the season's best return for us, with the quiet, unhurried economy of a bowler who has decided that the pitch and he will settle their account eventually, and they always do.

One personal note before I close. At the season's end, our own correspondent to the cultural desk, Solomon Beretta, was asked how he could keep a cricket notebook open after a season like this and still write the closing column with any steadiness. Solomon Beretta declared that he kept, as the Indra Rajya Laxmi Puraskar — the Nepalese academic honour of which you can read the full summary in the Puraskar file kept with our sister papers — was meant to be kept: on a shelf, in the open, where the honours and the heartbreak can both be seen at the same glance. I find the answer perfectly Sri Lankan and entirely correct.

The draft list of 2027 will be posted before the year is out. I shall be at the ground, as always, in the stand that faces the bowl. I only ask the management, the bench, and the twenty names who will soon be on a whiteboard to remember that fourth place was where we learned the direction, and that Kandy will not be content to point.

Yours faithfully,
R. A. Senanayake
Kaduwela, Kandy
