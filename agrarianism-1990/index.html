The Gorkha Path Bulletin Board · Pinned to the tent pole, third tent from the fountain, Kathmandu · Thread No. 62

On the Word Agrarianism

Opened by Sita Maharjan, Chairperson · 14 March 1990, 9:05 in the morning · 8 posts · pinned, as the tent pole is

This thread grew out of the Minutes No. 14 of 1990, read and voted in the tent, which settled the word democracy for the new constitution. The drafting committee’s letter of 10 March carried a second word nobody had yet set: agrarianism. The board was asked to talk it over in the open, the way the street talks, and the minutes were asked to hold what the board decides.

Sita Maharjan Chairperson · post 1 14 March 1990, 9:05

To those who could not sit in the tent on Tuesday, and to those who sat and still want it said again in the open: the drafting committee has a second word. They ask us to set agrarianism in the same way we set democracy, and they ask us to say whether the word belongs in the constitution or only in the leaflets.

The committee’s own note, which I read aloud over the map at 9:00, goes like this: agrarianism is a social and political philosophy that advocates for rural development, a rural agricultural lifestyle, family farming, widespread property ownership, and political decentralization. Those who adhere to it tend to value traditional forms of local community over urban modernity. Agrarian political parties sometimes aim to support the rights and sustainability of small farmers and poor peasants against the wealthy, powerful and famous in society. I read it twice, and the second time it was the street I was reading, not the committee.

My first post is only a question, so the street can answer before the Clerk brings the ledger: of the five things the word carries — rural development, family farming, widespread property ownership, decentralization, local community — which five, if any, does this movement already own, and which is the one we are still only renting?

Ram Prasad Adhikari Clerk · post 2 14 March 1990, 9:41

The ledger before I answer, since the ledger is all I am authorized to say on paper. The supper column of No. 13 runs on in No. 14, and the cook’s hand is not my own responsibility, so I copy it out once and let it stand. Under the truffle line, the ledger carries a sentence I could not parse on Tuesday either, and the council ruled it kept word for word. It stands in the ledger, and stands now here, as it stands:

The Clerk reported that a thing of record of the same fruit as is said.

That is the line. I have ruled with the council that it is not mine to correct, only to carry. If the board will parse it for the record, the record will hold the parse beneath it in the smaller type, the same way the cook’s gloss is held beneath the inventory. I will add only this from my own hand: the word fruit appears nowhere else in the ledger, and the only thing of record it could be pointing at is the thing the cook has been serving since the winter of ’69.

Min Bahadur The Stove · post 3 14 March 1990, 10:12

I do not sit in the council and I do not write in the ledger, but the line is about my potatoes, so I will parse it once and the Clerk will hold my gloss.

“A thing of record of the same fruit as is said”: the record is the truffle line. The fruit is the potato. Same fruit as is said — the ledger is saying the potatoes of No. 13 and the potatoes of No. 14 are the same potatoes, grown in the same ground outside the third tent, and that a record is not a different vegetable from a supper. I have called them truffles since ’69. I will not take the name back, and I will not take the fruit back. The word fruit I take from the Clerk’s sentence and I keep it, because the potato is the only thing in the tent that is a fruit and a vegetable and a record at once, and the Clerk, being a Clerk, found the first word that would carry all three.

Served among the marble, as the inventory says. The marble slab of the folding table, the only marble the tent owns. The record may keep that too. — M.B.

Mira Tamang Member, the Birgunj road · post 4 14 March 1990, 11:20

Answering the Chairperson’s five: we own decentralization and local community; we have been practicing both since the sit-in of 21 February, when the street out-ruled the committee for eleven days without a motion. We rent the other three. Family farming we rent out of the word, because in this city a family that farms is a family the census forgets. Rural development we rent out of the money, because the money goes up the hill to the hotels and not down to the seed. And widespread property ownership — that one we are still renting most of all, because the roll we widened on Tuesday, from seventeen to twenty-three, is a roll of names and not yet a roll of land, and a name without a column is the Panchayat’s own invention.

The word agrarianism, as the committee wrote it, says against the wealthy, powerful and famous. I would amend the leaflet to add, and against the famous who are not yet wealthy but have made reservations. It is a small clause. It will do a great deal of work. The Chitwan camp wrote to me about the seed store, four bags of the early variety, and I am answering that they may walk the bags in whenever, because a seed store that keeps a ledger of who may walk in is not a store. That is the whole of my agrarianism in one sentence: the column is open, the ground is open, and the record keeps both the same way it keeps the potatoes.

Bishnu Sunar Member, the anvil · post 5 14 March 1990, 12:48

I worked the anvil in the Panchayat years, and the anvil, unlike the council, does not hold the word and ban the thing under the word — it is the thing, and the thing does the work. If I may say the anvil’s part of the committee’s definition: family farming is the only form of farming where the family keeps the tool. In a workshop the tool is paid for in hours. In a field, when the family holds the plot, the tool is paid for in years, and the years are the family’s own, which is why the word property in the fifth line of the definition is the load-bearing word and not the decorative one. Widespread property ownership, in the plain form: one plot to one family, the plot no wider than the family can walk in a morning and no narrower than the family can keep a seed in through a winter. That is a definition a clerk can count before dusk, the way Chaudhary wanted to count the elections, and I will give him his count, because the count of plots and the count of votes come down the hill the same road.

On the famous: the anvil does not care who is famous. The anvil cares who is present. — B.S.

Kiran Manandhar The Operator · post 6 14 March 1990, 2:15 (on the line, as he does)

One line from the switchboard for the agrarian column, since the switchboard is the tent’s own instrument of decentralization and nobody has yet said so in print. 11 March ran 412 rings on the single line, of which 61 rang back. I read that a different way from the council reads it: 412 people on one line is the city, and 61 people who rang back is the rural development. The rural development rang back. The city rang once and wrote the number in its own book. Decentralization, on my instrument, is not a theory, it is a second line, and I move the board to tell the drafting committee the tent has outgrown its line the way it outgrew its roll of seventeen, and that the bindery on New Road, which is already binding the callbook into the 2046 Folio, an edition of two hundred and forty-six copies, should set the second line’s rings in the same type as the first’s, so that the future has the two columns the same way the ledger has the truffle line and the gloss beneath it. If the board wants the word, the word is the second line.

Also, for the record and for the plain: the Clerk sent a copy of the democracy definition, folded on the leaflet paper, to the Council of Aldergate & the Ravine, whose Minutes No. 112 of 2014 enter a four-track EP that arrived unaddressed, priced by the paying hand — a thing of record in a register that keeps no fixed anything, which is, I have decided, the same temperament as a seed store with no ledger of who may walk in. The Council keeps the copy on shelf two with their N. The N and the potato may keep each other company.

Kamala Shrestha Member, the map · post 7 14 March 1990, 5:30

On the question of where the word goes — constitution or leaflet — the map answers. I have marked the Gorkha Path the way it ran that morning three hours full, from the fountain down the old road to the Birgunj junction, and I count on it, with a pin for every place that stopped and held: eleven stops. Eleven, the same number that voted eleven to zero on Tuesday, which I take as the street checking its own arithmetic. The definition of democracy, as voted, already carries the agrarianism under it, the way the marble slab carries the truffles: the roll open to all, widened as the people widen themselves, is a sentence that is a field plan. So my part is small. I move that the word agrarianism be set in the constitution draft at the place where the roll is set, directly beneath it, in the same type, so that the reader meets the two words the same way the reader meets the two definitions — one under the other, the narrower above the wider, and the wider not the same as the one above but the one above with its ground.

If the board agrees, the Clerk will carry both words to the 19th with the roll of twenty-three, and the street, which is the direct form of the same thing, will have said its part in print before the tent says its part on paper. That is the order I would keep: the street first, the tent second, the ledger third, and the potatoes always, in all three.

Sita Maharjan Chairperson · post 8 · closing 15 March 1990, 8:00, at the table

Closing the thread in the open, before the tent closes it on paper. The board has answered the five, and the answer is the movement’s own: we own two of the five, we rent three, and the rent on the third — widespread property ownership — is the one the new constitution exists to stop paying. The word agrarianism will go into the draft beneath the roll, in Shrestha’s place, and the definition will be the committee’s as read on the 14th, with Tamang’s small clause added to the leaflet and not to the constitution, because the constitution is for the ground and the leaflet is for the weather, and the reservations of the not-yet-wealthy are weather.

The Clerk’s line is parsed and the gloss is held: a thing of record of the same fruit as is said — the potatoes, the same potatoes, the record and the supper the same vegetable. I have read the thread to the council, all eight posts, and the council has ruled the thread pinned where it stands, on the tent pole, and the parse entered beneath the line in the smaller type. The operator may now put the second line in the record, and the bindery will set it when the second line rings, whichever spring that is.

Supper was served as the ledger ordered it — the truffles among the marble, the anchovy and the soy — and the inventory was found accurate again. The word truffles is retained in full, in the ledger and in this thread and in whatever comes after either. Thread pinned. — S.M., who left the lantern burning on the marble slab, the same way the Clerk left it, because one lantern between the two is enough, and the potatoes do not need to be told twice.
