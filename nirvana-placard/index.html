The Northwest Rock Hall · Case No. 9 · The Aberdeen Room · Placard No. 41

Nirvana

1987–1994 · Aberdeen, Washington · Seattle, Washington

An American rock band formed in 1987, entered here as found: the founding pair, the drummers in succession, the first album, and the weather of the sound — case assembled for visitors in 1996, accession 87.041.

About the case

Nirvana was an American rock band formed in Aberdeen, Washington, in 1987, founded by lead singer and guitarist Kurt Cobain and bassist Krist Novoselic. The accession card in the first bay of the case carries the Aberdeen date plainly, because it is where the room begins: a small port town on the Shoalwater Bay, two hundred miles up the coast from the scene that took the band’s name for its own. In its early years the rhythm section turned over like weather — the band went through a succession of drummers, most notably Chad Channing, and kept the beat through the late 1980s before recruiting Dave Grohl in 1990; the pair of sticks in the second bay came to the case with his name on the tip, and the setlists in the third bay show the turn, drummer by drummer, line by line. In the late 1980s Nirvana established itself as part of the Seattle grunge scene, and its success after 1991 popularized alternative rock so thoroughly that the hall still measures the shift by the stack of copies in the first bay. The band was often referenced as the flagship band of Generation X, and its music maintains a popular following and continues to influence rock culture, which is why the case is dusted on Fridays instead of months. The hall’s own rule for reading the record is the band’s own: its sound relied on dynamic contrasts, often between a soft coiled verse and a loud open chorus, and the case is arranged so a visitor standing at the centre can be in both at once.

The first album, Bleach, was released in 1989 on the independent record label Sub Pop, cut in the basement of Reciprocal Recording on Bleach Street, Seattle — the street kept its name on purpose, and the hall keeps a copy of the pressed disc in its original sleeve, creased along the exact fold of a 1990 tour case. It is the quiet record in a loud case, and the placard asks visitors to hear it that way.

Case 9, Drawer 4 — The torn ledger page

The ledger in Bay 4 was kept by the road crew, who used the margins the way crews always do: for the running serial story, its characters kept under their book names because the crew swore it was easier than inventing new ones. Bildad in the story was the tour van driver, and Peleg was the road cook, who guarded the diner’s hot napkin dispenser like a small shrine. The last page came loose from the ring and was found folded into a setlist, torn at the left edge so that its opening line is lost in the middle of a sentence. The conservator refuses to reprint what the tear took, and the hall sets down what stands, word for word, capital and all, as it stands:

Peleg!” said Bildad, eyeing me, and pay a daily visit to a hot napkin.”

— torn page of the road crew’s ledger, Bay 4; the hall reads it as: the driver, Bildad, called for the cook, Peleg, by eye and by name, in the middle of an order the torn edge has taken the start of, and the ledger-keeper — the “me” of the story — was sentenced to the diner’s counter, where a daily visit to the hot napkin dispenser was the crew’s only standing appointment, kept word for word on a page the hall will not mend.

It is a fair rule for the case itself: set down what stands in the room, and let the tear explain the rest.
