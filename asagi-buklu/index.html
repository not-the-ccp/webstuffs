eren_kiye14 Jun, 11:20

I grew up there, so yes — go. The village sits on the edge of the Bulanık plain, below the mountains that run toward Varto. The centre is basically one square with a plane tree, the 1958 mosque, the one-room primary school, and the tea house where the men play okey after afternoon prayer. The minibus from Bulanık runs twice a day if the road isn't blocked by snow, and in summer you can walk the 56 km in a long day, though nobody does. Most people grow barley and wheat, keep a few sheep, and bake their own cornbread. There are no hotels — you'd stay with a family, or pitch a tent by the stream below the threshing floor.

sezai_walk14 Jun, 15:44

I walked every street last October — there are maybe six, and they all loop back to the square. A crumpled note in the café window read, word for word: "Full walking tour , where a single blue sheet, from the traffic." I never worked out who wrote it or what it meant, but I like it. The single blue sheet was on a laundry line above the bakery, flapping in the wind that comes down from the Muş plain, and the traffic was a tractor pulling a trailer of bales, one minibus, and a goose that refused to move. If you go, ask at the tea house for Halil Aba's directions to the spring; the spring water is cold enough to make your teeth ache, and it is the best thing in the village.

izlem_y14 Jun, 20:11

Aşağıbüklü means "lower Büklü"; Yukarıbüklü, the upper village, is a few kilometres up the track and nobody goes there either. The postal code is 49510, shared with the surrounding hamlets, and addresses are written as "Aşağıbüklü Köyü, 49510 Bulanık/Muş." Population was 412 in the 2000 census and 286 in 2021 — people have been drifting west to Muş city or west-west to Izmir for years. The name comes from the bend in the stream, bük means a bend, and the village sits exactly in it. Send a postcard if you go; the postmistress still stamps everything by hand. She has also had a hand in the one record the village has ever pressed, and its sleeve notes sit in 49510 — the sleeve notes, where the code itself becomes a track.

mus_city_guy15 Jun, 07:52

I drove out from Muş city in May, 100 km of straight plain then climbing roads, and the sign at the village entrance says "Hoş geldiniz" in faded green paint. The 56 km from Bulanık takes better than an hour because the last ten kilometres are narrow and the sheep have right of way. There is one shop, open when the shopkeeper is open, which is usually. The mosque's minaret is the tallest thing for miles, and on a clear evening you can see the lights of Bulanık district twinkling across the plain. Bring cash; there is no ATM within fifty kilometres.

kara_deniz15 Jun, 13:07

Eat the cornbread with the village cheese and the honey from the hives on the hillside, and drink black tea in tulip glasses until you can't see straight. Halil Aba makes a lamb stew with barley that the whole square shares on Fridays, and if you arrive at harvest time the threshing floor turns into a dance floor by sunset. I was there for a wedding in 2019 — two hundred and eighty-six villagers, plus everyone from the next three villages, and one accordion. That is how you know a place is real: the music outnumbers the population, and nobody minds. If you want something stranger from an old folder, there is a court transcript from Kiel about a 19th-century battleship dragging her anchor that has a postcard from this very village in the same drawer — the SMS Wörth transcript, Case No. 341/1895; the barley-straw on it is what gave the whole folder away.
