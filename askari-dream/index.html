The Two Minarets

Entry A-317 · under “Mosque, al-Askari” and “Minaret” and “Gold, falling from a dome”

If you dream of two tall minarets beside a low domed roof, both towers of ten storeys each, and you find them standing in the morning when you should find them gone, the entry is the al-Askari, and you have dreamed at Samarra on the morning of 13 June 2007, just before nine o’clock, at the hour the dreamer’s watch would have said nine and a few minutes past. The mosque is one of the holiest in Shia Islam; in the dream it is the place where two towers came down without a single person hurt, and the dreamer is required to carry the whole arithmetic of that: nothing broken in the body, everything broken in the stone.

The dream keeps its own dates, and they are the true dates. It knows the first night was 22 February 2006, when the golden dome of the same mosque was blown off, and it remembers the second night as its shadow. It knows the second charge came 13 June 2007, about nine in the morning, local time, and that it took the two minarets — both of them, each one ten storeys, the balcony of the third storey where the muezzin had called the faithful for seven hundred years — clean down to the base. It knows, finally, that by April 2009 both minarets were repaired, rebuilt to the height the dreamer first measured in sleep, and that in the dream, as on the ledger, a tower that has been replaced is still the same tower, only a second draft of it.

The dreamer’s note on the hand behind the charge is short: the dream attributes it, in the manner the Republic of Iran attributed it in the waking world, to the Iraqi Ba’ath Party, though the dream does not insist, and in dreams no one insists. What the dream insists on is the street. In the dream the street outside the al-Askari was empty when the towers fell, the way it is empty in every true version, and the emptiness is the point. A holy place struck, and no one struck with it: the dream reads this as a blessing the dreamer cannot spend, and keeps it folded inside the entry like a coin.

Interpretation, as entered: one who dreams the minarets is dreaming of something twice lost and twice restored, of a building that has survived its own catalogue, of the gold of the 2006 dome being spent and reminted. One who dreams the empty street is to be patient and to say the name of the place, al-Askari, twice, for the towers are numbered two and the numbers agree. One who dreams the repaired minarets of April 2009 is to expect the second draft of a thing long ruined. The Concordance files the entry beside its neighbour on the warship that sails faster than its armour, Entry B-221, the Battlecruiser, because both are about speed and the things that do not survive speed, and both keep their ledgers word for word.

The dreamer’s ledger, page torn, kept exactly as it stands:

Sunday, 26 January On the back of this series , and it is this.

The Concordance has voted, 4–3, to keep the line unexplained. It was found on the back of the last page of the entry, in a smaller hand than the rest, and it is filed here where it was found.

Cross-references in the waking record, for the dreamer who checks the dream against the street: the mosque is on the bank of the Tigris at Samarra, in the city of the caliphs; the two minarets rose, by the surveyor’s measure, to a height the dreamer calls “ten storeys” because the dream is allowed one unit of its own. The 2006 charge destroyed the dome, a dome of gilded metal; the 2007 charges destroyed the towers, of brick and stucco. The attribution to the Ba’ath Party is entered without a footnote, in the dreamer’s hand, because the dreamer’s hand is the dreamer’s. Everything else in the entry is as it is.

Close the entry. The dreamer files the page, folds the coin of the empty street inside it, and sets the concordance on the shelf beside the court record that found one hand was all there was, the verbatim record, Matter No. 47-H-1995, and beside the small-ads page that keeps its misbound lines word for word, the Scrub Ledger, No. 312. Three ledgers, three ways of keeping a line, and the two minarets, second draft, still standing.
