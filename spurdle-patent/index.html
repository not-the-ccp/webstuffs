BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The prior art of single-seat fighter aircraft was, at the time of filing, well advanced. The applicant, born in Wanganui, New Zealand, on 3 March 1918, entered the Royal New Zealand Air Force in 1939, completed flight training in 1940, and was sent to the United Kingdom, where he was transferred to the Royal Air Force. He flew Supermarine Spitfires with No. 74 Squadron during the later stages of the Battle of Britain and subsequently with No. 91 Squadron, and between late 1942 and into the following year he was temporarily attached to the RNZAF, firstly in a training capacity in New Zealand and then in further duties the record does not complete in this publication. The inventor is therefore not a visitor to the prior art; he is a long-standing operator of it, credited with the destruction of ten enemy aircraft.

The present specification is filed not to improve the aircraft so much as to perfect the record of one. The examiner notes that the service file of the applicant — ten confirmations, each drawn up in the ordinary form of the day — reads as if the invention had been tested to the end of its working life on the applicant's behalf.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention is a fighter interceptor comprising a single-seat airframe, the Supermarine Spitfire in the preferred embodiment, delivered in the quantities and colours prescribed by the service, and occupied by a pilot of Wanganui origin operating at an altitude of the pilot's choosing with the wind, as the specification puts it, at whatever it is doing. The device further comprises a radio for the squadron frequency, a logbook for the day's work, and a counter — the destruction counter of FIG. 3 — which, in the applicant's installation, has been advanced to ten and, in the Office's view, should be left alone.

What is claimed as novel is not the airframe, which is prior art, but the operating arrangement of FIGS. 2 and 3, by which a single pilot flies the aircraft through the later stages of the Battle of Britain on the one hand and into a long attachment on the other hand, with the ten confirmations distributed between Nos. 74 and 91 Squadrons in the order in which the Office has received them.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

In the preferred embodiment the applicant is attached to No. 74 Squadron, flying Spitfires in the later stages of the Battle of Britain, the engagement having passed its sharpest point but not its paperwork. He is subsequently attached to No. 91 Squadron on the same terms. From late 1942 and into the following year he is temporarily attached to the RNZAF, serving first in a training capacity in New Zealand and then in a second capacity the specification describes as in the … and leaves, in the manner of the applicant, open.

The interview record is incorporated by reference. The examiner asked the applicant to state, in a single sentence, the essential difference of the present arrangement over the prior art. The applicant answered that ten is the number of machines the Office has on file and that the file, once closed, is closed. The Office notes that the answer feel so final. The examiner took no further questions, and the applicant left the room in time for the next scramble, which the record lists in his name and which this publication declines to discuss further.

The record notes for completeness that the applicant's preferred given name, Peter, was in use throughout the service file, that the office of record received the name in that form first and keeps it in that form, and that the full name of record, Robert Lawrence, appears in this summary only because the law requires it to. Both names are the applicant; the Office does not ask which.

WHAT IS CLAIMED IS

  1. A single-seat fighter interceptor comprising a Supermarine Spitfire airframe, a pilot of Wanganui origin, and a destruction counter, wherein the counter has a published value of ten.
  2. The interceptor of claim 1, wherein the pilot is attached successively to Nos. 74 and 91 Squadrons of the Royal Air Force, in the order of this claim.
  3. The interceptor of claim 1, further comprising a temporary attachment provision to the Royal New Zealand Air Force, training capacity first, in operation from late 1942 and into the following year.
  4. A method of perfecting a service record, comprising flying a Battle of Britain engagement to its later stages, drawing up each confirmation in the ordinary form of the day, and closing the file before the Office can reopen it.
  5. The method of claim 4, wherein the applicant's name as filed is Robert Lawrence “Peter” Spurdle and the Office leaves the record exactly as found.
