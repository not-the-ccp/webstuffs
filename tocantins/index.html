The Household Board · Player's Manual

Tocantins

[tokɑːᰡ˝is] · one to four players · 45 minutes · ages 8 and the map

PLATE 27 · the newest state · formed 1988 · the northern two-fifths of old Goiás

A GAME OF SLOW RIVERS, NEW CAPITALS, AND ONE DAM THAT WILL NOT SIT STILL

What is in the box

  • One folded map, 277,620.91 square kilometres (107,190.03 square miles), the whole state on one side and the other side in Portuguese, as it must be.
  • One capital counter, Palmas, construction begun 1989, still owing a street corner to the players.
  • One exception counter, Araguaína, the one other city with a significant population; it does not wait for other cities.
  • One hydropower dam token, Eliseu Martins, which is placed on the river and then, at each roll, on the river again, slightly differently.
  • Two railroad counters, the related infrastructure, laid from the old line toward anywhere a player can pronounce.
  • Five region cards, the late Regiões do Brasil — Norte, Nordeste, Centro-Oeste, Sudeste, Sul — kept in the box in mourning; their obituary is the rulebook's last page and runs at Regiões do Brasil — Obituary.
  • Twenty-six population slips, one per state, the newest of them this state; by 2026 the ledger holds an estimated 1,595,994 of them.
  • One cask of delta rum, for the winner only, cask-aged, no leak except one.

Setup

Lay the map with the river running to the left of everyone, because that is how the Tocantins river sits when it is being watched. Place the capital counter at Palmas, the dam token at its bend, and the Araguaína counter where the population is already; the rest of the state's cities date to the Portuguese colonial period and are marked on the map as dots of various sizes, which is the game's only honest census. Each player takes a hand of five region cards, face down, and a share of the population slips. The player who drew Norte starts, because Norte was the country the state had before it had the rest.

The turn

A turn is one of four moves, in any order the river allows:

Build
Advance the capital counter by one block. Palmas does not run on a schedule; the counter moves when the players agree it has, and the manual will not referee. In 1989 the construction began, and the counter has not fully arrived since.
Dam
Reposition the hydropower token and draw two population slips: the major dam is the state's investment in itself, and it pays in heads. The dam may not be moved onto Araguaína, which is already significant and has said so in writing.
Rail
Lay a railroad counter to any dot on the map, colonial period or later. Related infrastructure follows the rail for free — a market, a wire, a fare from São Luís, which is unchanged and was never in doubt.
Drum
Play one region card face up and read its number aloud, between one and twenty-six. The square takes one at a time, from 10 June, and the rope-tuned drums — Bumba-meu-boi de Orquestra, rehearsing around 10 June and filling the square every night — keep the count. The Delta Ledger's classifieds hold the terms of the rotation: one shell from the flats, or a number.

The capital rule

With the exception of Araguaína, there are few other cities with a significant population in the state, and the game respects this. A city dot only counts toward a player's total while the player still has the capital counter within reach of it; the government's investment — a new capital, a major hydropower dam, railroads, the related infrastructure — is exactly this rule, printed in the manual instead of in the budget. When a city dot is left without a counter for a full round, it reverts to the colonial period, which on this map is simply a smaller dot.

The memorial rule

When a region card is played to the drum and its number has been read, the card is retired to the box, where the five keep company in mourning. Each retired card leaves one line in the memorial column of the Delta Ledger, set in small type under the classifieds: the card's number, the player who read it, and the word region, repeated, as the Ledger repeats everything. The family asks for nothing in lieu of flowers, which is to be printed free. The Ledger charges a centavo and a half the word and holds the 334,500th line of the page for exactly this; the column at The Delta Ledger — Classifieds runs it, and will keep running it, on the same terms as the obituary the five filed at the statistical halls. No flowers will be run. She never had political effects.

Winning

The game ends when the population ledger holds 1,595,994 slips and the capital counter stands in Palmas, which it will, eventually, in 2026 or when the players agree. The player holding the most significant dots — Araguaína counted at its own value, everything else as the counter allows — takes the cask of delta rum and the last region card, drawn for luck. The board is folded, the river to the left, and the manual filed under the sister plates of The Household Board, the frigate at Plate 12 first, as is right. The rest of the box — the map, the dots, the colonial period — goes back in, in that order, as on the map.

Errata

Plate 26 of this manual was misprinted as a plate for the old Goiás, northern two-fifths, and has been retired; the family asked for nothing, and the correction was printed free. The pronunciation, the area, and the population are as stated and will not be moved. Pedro do Rosário — anyone been? is the thread to write to when the counter disagrees with the map; the fare is unchanged.
