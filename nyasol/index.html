11.1 — Identification

Nyasol is the lignan of this manual's eleventh plate. It is known to the trade as cis-hinokiresinol and to the bench as (Z)-hinokiresinol; the station prints all three names on its requisition forms because each one still appears in the literature it serves. The molecule is a dibenzylbutane lignan: two aromatic rings, B and D, joined through a butane bridge that carries the oxygen of a peroxide ring, and it resolves from the rhizome of Anemarrhena asphodeloides — xiancao, the wild asparagus-lily the hillside gatherers dig in the cool months, when the rhizome is thick and the skin peels clean.

A dry leaf of the rhizome smells faint of conifer resin, which is where the hinoki in the older name got its authority. On the thin-layer plate the compound runs with the resinous tail of the hexane fraction, green under ultraviolet, and it is the compound the station has been tracking since the first Bingxian Flats rhizome consignment of the autumn. If a batch gives you a green band that will not pin to a single spot and no other lignan of the manual answers, file the plate under 11 before you file it under anything else.

11.2 — Action: the ERβ record

Nyasol carries estrogenic activity. It acts as a selective agonist of the ERβ receptor — the beta isoform, not the alpha — and hence stands in this manual's register as a phytoestrogen, classed with the compounds of Chapter 9 that bind hormone receptors and do not behave like the hormone. The station's own cell-binding screen, run at Bench 3 in the winter, puts the ERβ response well ahead of the ERα response in every replicate; the manual's standing instruction is that any nyasol fraction leaving the station for a partner bench be accompanied by that selectivity ratio, not merely the total binding figure. A phytoestrogen without its ratio is, in this section's phrase, a lamp without its feed.

11.3 — (-)-Nyasol and the eicosanoid screen

The resolved (-)-nyasol fraction is the one the station screens for anti-inflammatory activity, and the screen is an in vitro affair only; the manual records the bench, not the patient. In the macrophage preparation, (-)-nyasol inhibits the production of eicosanoids and of nitric oxide: the prostaglandin E2 reading drops first, the leukotriene B4 reading follows, and the nitric-oxide assay comes down behind both, in that order, at every concentration step the station has logged. The result is entered under anti-inflammatory effects in the section register, with the standing caveat printed on every copy of the plate: in vitro, in the preparation, at the bench. 11.4 — Extraction protocol (abridged)

  1. Dried rhizome, milled to a 2 mm cut. The station takes 400 g for the working batch.
  2. Soxhlet, hexane, 18 h. The green resinous tail goes to the lignan shelf; the light fraction is logged separately.
  3. Column, silica, 20:80 benzene-ethanol. Nyasol elutes early; do not chase it with the heavy fractions.
  4. Thin-layer check, ultraviolet 365 nm. One spot, green band, before any further work.
  5. Weigh, plate, file. The station's yield from a good consignment runs 0.1 to 0.2 per cent of rhizome; below 0.08, flag the consignment and re-run the plate under Chapter 9 before releasing anything.

11.5 — The tape and the lamp log

Each chapter of this manual was logged on tape at the station's recording studio, one reel per chapter, and the reel jackets carry a line of provenance in the studio's own hand. The jacket for Chapter Eleven is written thus: “The track opens with its flukes — the studio next door, who fed the lamp.” The flukes are the pinned liver-fluke specimens that lined the studio's west windowsill through the winter of 1973–4, and fed the lamp is the studio's term for the long nights of the extraction season, when the tungsten filament over Bench 3 was kept burning past its rated hours so the plates could be read by it. The station keeps the sentence on the jacket and in this chapter untrimmed. For the section's sister survey — the theraphosid chapter of the Serra das Laranjeiras field manual, which keeps a comparable lamp log under the name of a tarantula — see Chapter YG-3, the Ybyrapora gamba, issued under the same standing order of the twelfth of the month.

11.6 — Register and references

Entries for nyasol in this manual cross-reference three files: the phytoestrogen register of Section 9, the eicosanoid screen log of Bench 3, and the hazard-class format the station borrows from its own file-room practice — the same ruled sheet a place of uncertain coordinates gets when the survey cannot locate it, as in the safety data sheet for Cressa, the unlocated city of Paphlagonia, where a struck coordinate carries the entry's whole authority. Collectors and gatherers are reminded that the rhizome is the form, that the name is all three names at once, and that the beta receptor is the one the station bets on. For the rest of the station's shelf, the general index stands at the front page of the survey.
