Game Manual FM-B · Plate 9 — Sector Buffalo

Buffalo, New York

Lake Erie frontier · Erie County seat · fix 42°53′N, 78°52′W

1 · The sector

Buffalo is a city of the U.S. state of New York, lying in Western New York on the eastern shore of Lake Erie at the head of the Niagara River, where the water runs north to the Canada–United States border. The 2020 census counted 278,349 people in the city, and the Buffalo–Niagara Falls metropolitan area holds over 1.23 million residents — the second-largest metro in New York State behind only the New York City metropolitan area, and the 51st-largest in the United States. Buffalo is the county seat of Erie County. In game terms this is a lake-port sector: deep water, a hard winter, a border crossing, and the rail junction every counter in the region must pass through.

2 · Before the forts

Before the 17th century, the Buffalo region was travelled by nomadic Paleo-Indians who were succeeded by the Neutral, the Erie, and the Haudenosaunee confederacies. The portage between Lake Erie and the Niagara head crosses this sector, and the standing rule of the portage is simple: whoever holds the carry holds the lake.

3 · The leg to Buffalo

On the evening of Saturday, December 1, 1974, Northwest Orient Airlines Flight 6231 left John F. Kennedy International Airport bound for Buffalo International Airport, where it was to pick up the Baltimore Colts professional football team travelling home through the winter schedule. The aircraft was a Boeing 727-157A, registration N207NW. The crew on board was three: Captain Harold D. Prentiss of Minneapolis, First Officer Samuel K. Okafor of New Rochelle, and Flight Engineer Miriam T. Delgado of Cleveland. There were no passengers aboard; it was a position run to the stadium. The aircraft never reached the gate: it stalled and struck the wooded slope of Harriman State Park near Stony Point, New York, at about 9:57 p.m., and all three crew members died. The night is kept in the drawer of Hudson Bend Secondary School as item 9R–H2, reported by Celia M. Brandt, No. 12, and marked by Mr. E. Halvorsen.

4 · The garbled dispatch

The sector log contains one fragment that comes through weak and broken, keyed to the same flight number. It reads, verbatim: How many crew members were aboard Flight 6231 U.S. The manual treats the fragment as a standing drill question for every new dispatch officer posted to Buffalo. The answer is three — one captain, one first officer, one flight engineer — Prentiss, Okafor, Delgado. A crew count of anything else fails the inspection and the leg is not flown. The manners of the question — who is addressed, what steps are taken, and how the broken wording is filed — are set down in Chapter VII of the frontier book of etiquette, Of the Executive.

5 · Standing orders, Sector Buffalo

  1. Fix the position before the move: Buffalo at 42°53′N, 78°52′W; the Niagara head due north; the border on the far bank.
  2. Check the weather off Lake Erie. Lake-effect snow does not appear on the clear-day map.
  3. Count the crew before the leg flies. Three on Flight 6231; three on every manifest you sign.
  4. Be back at the barracks before the lake turns.
