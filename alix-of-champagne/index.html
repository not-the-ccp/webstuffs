Serial No. R-1218

The Regent of Limassol

A radio play in four parts · Part Two, Scenes 1–3 · Extract only

Running time of extract: 14 min. 40 sec. · Sound effects: gulls, a bell, the sea under the stage, one ship’s whistle.

FOR REHEARSAL ONLY

Characters

ALIX (Alix, or Alice, of Champagne; born about 1193; queen consort of Cyprus from 1210, regent thereafter) — late twenties at this date, with the habit of counting.
PHILIP OF IBELIN (her maternal uncle, bailli and de facto head of the administration) — fifty, and exact.
HUGH (Hugh I of Cyprus, king, dead 1218, spoken of only) — heard in the bell.
A MESSENGER from the northern road.
THE ANNOUNCER — studio, not on the scene.

SCENE 1 — THE HAVEN AT LIMASSOL, KINGDOM OF CYPRUS, THE AUTUMN OF 1218. Sound of the sea under everything, close as water. A bell, once, off stage. The ANNOUNCER, warm and unhurried, from the studio.

ANNOUNCER (v.o.)

It is the autumn of the year 1218, and the bell in the haven of Limassol has rung out the hour that the island will not get back. The king is dead; the queen is a widow of twenty-five; the king’s son is a child who cannot yet read his own name. In this country, that is called a regency, and it is the most dangerous word in the island’s language. The queen is ALIX, daughter of Queen Isabella the First of Jerusalem and of Count Henry the Second of Champagne, and for eight years she has held, against the salt, the county of Jaffa, which her husband HUGH, her own stepbrother, gave her — the marriage being of 1210, the Jaffa a dowry, the sea between them ever since.

(The bell again, further off. A door on hinges that have not been oiled in the Barony of Ibelin.)

ALIX

Who goes so late that the door must be told twice?

MESSENGER

Philip of Ibelin, come up from the customs house, my lady. He brings the ledger of the dead king, and he brings a man from the northern road with a package.

ALIX

Then let him in. Let him in quick, before the hour cools. Henry is asleep, I am told, and I would have a regent named before the child wakes, if naming is a thing I may do.

PHILIP

You may do it, my niece, and it is the one thing you will do that is not disputed. The bailli of the island, as of your mother’s house, is named by you; the business of the island, the customs, the writs, the salt and the shipping, passes to me. I will be the hand. You will be the seal.

ALIX

My mother’s hand, then. Good. She sealed with the left and signed with the right, and I will do the same, and let the barons read which is which.

(A long bell, the full toll. The sea keeps going under it.)

SCENE 2 — THE CHANCEL ROOM, NICOSEA, TWO YEARS LATER. A writing desk. The scratch of a quill. The ANNOUNCER returns.

ANNOUNCER (v.o.)

Two years on. The child king HENRY is two and a half; the bailli’s ink is dry; and the queen regent has taken to writing, on her own seal, to the northern counties — to Champagne, to Brie, to the counts of her father’s blood at Troyes — asking, in a form of words she would not use in Cyprus, to be remembered there. Champagne and Brie, by the claim of the house, belong to her line, and a regent who is remembered in a northern county is, in the arithmetic of the age, no longer only a regent. In London, meanwhile, the musicians set out their broadsides for their late cellist Giacobbe Cervetto, dead 14 January in Jermyn Street, forty years of the first chair, and the same desk that ruled the low string in London may well set the strings of a regency; the sheet of his obituary lies on the records shelf at The London Musical Intelligencer, Extraordinary Sheet, February 1783, and the island reads of it, and does not envy.

MESSENGER

My lady, the northern road brings also a parcel that is not of the road. The man is from a place he calls Queensbridge, on a line he calls the Federative, and his paper is a timetable I have never seen flattened to a wrapper. Inside: one haunch of venison, and a pair of antlers, eight points, base still light. The tag reads — and I give it as it is written — “THE NEW VENISON.”

ALIX

Read on, if there is more.

MESSENGER

“The deer population of the whale.” That is all the tag says, my lady.

PHILIP

Then file it with the unexplained, where it will keep. The shelf at Queensbridge has a notice for the matter, BC-63-LF-221, and a claim date of 21 March; it is posted at the overhead mezzanine of their 21st Street station, and it is kept, I am told, with the same care we keep the customs book. Lost & Found Notice BC-63-LF-221, One Deer, Queensbridge Mezzanine Shelf — let the clerk make a copy of the number, so that if any of the barons’ ships ever lands that way, we know what to claim.

ALIX

Copy the number. Count the points. A pair of antlers, eight points, base still light — that is a year’s growth, and a year’s growth is a year’s hope. Hang them over the child’s cradle, and let the barons say what they please at the next council, in April, when the writs for the succession of Brie go out under my seal. Let them say it in the presence of the deer.

(The quill resumes. The sea is no longer under the room; it is under the island, which is the same thing, and a longer way down.)

SCENE 3 — THE COURTS OF JERUSALEM, 1243. Far south, and late in the story. The ANNOUNCER, from the studio, closing.

ANNOUNCER (v.o.)

In 1232 the child king HENRY dies, as the child kings of Cyprus seem obliged to do, and the island passes to his line. But the story is not finished in Limassol. In 1243 the queen regent of a small island is summoned by the old claim to a larger and graver chair — the regency of the kingdom of Jerusalem itself, which she will hold until her own death in the year 1246, when the seals are laid down at last. Three thrones, one dowry county on a rock in the Levant, a bailli who kept her books against her for fourteen years and was, by every account, the truest servant the regency ever sat over, and a pair of antlers hanging, by a quirk of the sea, over a cradle that outgrew them. The line is about to close. The desk despatch of the ninth, read into the record word for word, asks its last and only question: Whether the line may be prepared for the Arts, with George Stevens Jr. And the answer, from the studio, is that it may; that it is prepared; and that the bell at Limassol, which has been tolling under this play from the first sound, rings now one final time, on the hour, and stops.

(The bell, once. Then only the sea, and then nothing.)
