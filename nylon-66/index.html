ABSTRACT

Nylon is a family of synthetic polymers characterized by amide linkages, typically connecting aliphatic or semi-aromatic groups. Nylons are generally brownish in color and can possess a soft texture, with some varieties exhibiting a silk-like appearance. As thermoplastics, nylons can be melt-processed into fibers, films, and diverse shapes. The properties of nylons are often modified by blending with a variety of additives. Numerous types of nylon are available. One family, designated nylon-XY, is derived from diamines and dicarboxylic acids of carbon chain lengths X and Y, respectively; an important example is nylon-6,6. Another family, designated nylon-Z, is derived from a single bifunctional monomer of carbon chain length Z, and the plain keeps a vat for it as it keeps a vat for the others. The present application claims a process for preparing the amide-linked thread of the above families in the oak-bark vat of a tannery yard, the fibers, films, and diverse shapes melt-processed therefrom, and the goods of the yard made from them, priced at the Baku mill and entered in the ledger in triplicate.

BACKGROUND ART

Leather is a strong, flexible and durable material obtained from the tanning, or chemical treatment, of animal skins and hides to prevent decay, and the craft at the Goygol tannery has been practiced, in one form or another, for more than seven thousand years, so the prior art of this yard is not thin: it is deep, it is wet, and it is entered in three ledgers at once. The district register of hides — cattle, sheep, goats, equine animals, buffalo, pigs and hogs, ostriches, and the aquatic animals, seals and alligators, which the tannery accepts only when a player is willing to pay for the trouble of them — stands as the standing inventory from which the present applicants depart. The oak-bark vat, the dry, the lime, and the fleshing station, and the shared gate with its split padlock key, are described in full in the player’s manual of the yard, The Goygol Tannery, Third Edition, and are incorporated here by reference in the way the ledger incorporates the ledger: without paraphrase.

The want in the art was this. The good that leaves the yard — boots, handbag, saddle, drumhead, tool roll — is priced against the plain, and the ledger prices a good against the days: at the end of the week of seventy, each completed tenth of a day adds 1 manat to every good on the yard, up to 7 in all, because leather lasts for decades and the ledger believes it. But the leather of the yard is heavy, and it waits for the hide. The applicants wished a material that is strong, flexible and durable in the same way as leather, yet light enough to be spun, thin enough to be a film, and soft enough, with its brownish color and silk-like appearance, to pass at the Baku mill as what it has always been called there: thread. The shelf of Coopers’ Lane keeps its metals — see Aluminium (Al), found under the second shelf, where the same discipline is kept for a soft metal that is not a polymer at all, entered and shelved with its weakness stated — and this application is the shed’s entry for the thread itself.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a family of synthetic polymers characterized by amide linkages, typically connecting aliphatic or semi-aromatic groups, prepared at the yard in the oak-bark vat. One family, designated nylon-XY, is derived from diamines and dicarboxylic acids of carbon chain lengths X and Y, respectively. An important example is nylon-6,6, whose repeating unit is (−C(O)(CH2)4C(O)−NH(CH2)6NH−)n, which the applicants note is why the application carries the number it carries: the vat that brewes it, on the yard, is the sixth of the six, and the ledger has kept the pair since the winter of 2021. Another family, designated nylon-Z, is derived from a single bifunctional monomer of carbon chain length Z, of which the applicants file the vat but reserve the claims, the way the yard reserves the coop: the hens — 112 of them — are not in the rules, but they are in the record, and the record is what this application plays by.

Because the nylons of the invention are thermoplastics, they can be melted and processed into fibers, films, and diverse shapes, and the applicants have melt-processed them, in the oak-bark vat and on the dry, into the goods the yard already prices: a fiber of the silk-like kind for the handbag (14 manats, the mill’s price, kept on purpose), a film for the drumhead (5), a diverse shape for the tool roll (6), and the saddle (23) in the shade of brown the vat gives the material when the frost is off the plain. The properties of the nylons are often modified by blending with a variety of additives, and the applicants blend: waxes and woads in the usual measure, and, per Claim 4, one frost token of the yard per hundred kilograms of resin, at the applicant’s discretion, so that a gate opened outside the hours costs the vat in the material it brews rather than only in the ledger.

CLAIMS

  1. A process for preparing a synthetic polymer characterized by amide linkages typically connecting aliphatic or semi-aromatic groups, comprising brewing the same in an oak-bark vat of a tannery yard for one full day, and drying the same on the dry, provided the gate is open during the posted hours and only under the split padlock key.
  2. The process of Claim 1, wherein the polymer is nylon-XY, derived from diamines and dicarboxylic acids of carbon chain lengths X and Y respectively, and wherein X and Y are each 6, and the vat is the sixth of the six.
  3. The process of Claim 1, wherein the polymer is melt-processed, the same being a thermoplastic, into fibers, films, and diverse shapes, and wherein the fibers exhibit a silk-like appearance and a soft texture, the shade being the brownish of the vat’s own water on a Tuesday.
  4. The process of Claim 1, wherein the properties of the polymer are modified by blending with a variety of additives, comprising waxes, woads, and one frost token of the yard per hundred kilograms of resin.
  5. A good of the tannery yard — boots, handbag, saddle, drumhead, or tool roll — made from a fiber, film, or diverse shape of any of the foregoing, priced at the Baku mill at 14, 23, or the ledger’s own number, and entered in triplicate: one for the office, one for Baku, one for the bench in the shed.
  6. A fine of one fleece, 2.71 kilograms, payable into the fines pool of the yard, where the fleece is the currency of the fines pool and where the fines pool is sold at 14 manats the kilo and divided equally between the players, so that a vat broken for this invention ends up feeding the very table it was broken for.

EXAMINER’S REMARK

The examiner has read the application against the prior art of the yard and against the 3:20 card — door, 03:20, frost, from the ledger of 14 March, preserved exactly as written, the same morning this application was filed at the same hour — and the record of the examination keeps, in the examiner’s margin, one line she could not parse, entered uncorrected and read in by the clerk. It stands in the docket as follows, and this summary carries it over as it stands:

“The work culminated in the oak-bark vat, and ends on the most violent knockings?”

No one at the bench has reconstructed the last clause — the vat culminates, the record concedes, but it does not knock, and the knockings are not on file in the yard, in the coop, or in the stream that feeds the plain — and the bureau, in the manner of the graders of transmitted lines, did not throw the line out for being unparseable; it marked it, and it is in the docket with its weakness stated. The first half, for its part, is a sentence the entry on the shelf beside this one — Encyclopaedia Entry No. 41, on the hadith, the chain of reporters — wrote first, of the collectors who took six collections to stand as the received core; the examiner borrowed culminated and changed the vat. That is the same rule both benches keep: enter it while it is hot, with the names attached, however strange the hour. The classification is Granted as to Claims 1–5, Claim 6 entered with its weakness stated, the margin line is carried over word for word, and the application is entered, graded, and shelved with the rest of the plain — where the yard prices it in manats, alongside the fleece of 2.71 kilograms and the 112 hens, in the classified column of the Goygol Plain Gazette, No. 118, the same column that keeps the cream line.
