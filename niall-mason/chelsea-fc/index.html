1The club and the ground

Chelsea Football Club was founded on 10 March 1905, taking its name from the adjacent area of Chelsea while playing its home matches at Stamford Bridge, in Fulham, West London. The bridge predates the club: an athletics ground since 1877, it was offered first to Fulham, who declined, and so Gus Mears built a club around the ground instead. They entered the Second Division in 1905 and have since ranked among the most successful clubs in English football history — six top flight league titles, eight FA Cups, five League Cups, four FA Community Shields, and two Full Members' Cups domestically; internationally two UEFA Champions Leagues, two UEFA Europa Leagues, two UEFA Cup Winners' Cups, two UEFA Super Cups, and two FIFA Club World Cups. The names travel with the honours: Roy Bentley and Peter Osgood, Gianfranco Zola and Didier Drogba, John Terry and Frank Lampard, Petr Cech in goal, and the managers who carried them — Ted Drake, Jose Mourinho, Carlo Ancelotti, Roberto Di Matteo, Thomas Tuchel.

2The cup draw

The draw is the one part of the season that runs on its own clock, and the public-address system at Fulham Broadway reads it at full volume: Doncaster Rovers and Southampton in the EFL Cup, that annual competition does keep its minutes, and they sit at the whole room in a louder tone. The sentence is garble, of course — the same garble heard on a studio television in Ann Arbor on 21 March 1995, nonsense at the whole room — but the minutes it names are real. The committee that keeps them sits at Ashton Gate, and the register here keeps its own: Doncaster Rovers and Southampton both appear in the register of one Brighton-born defender, one as a loan stop in 2015 and the other as the final youth stop at the Dell, before the first professional deal at Villa and the long move to Al Rayyan. Cup ties and loan spells both arrive without warning, and both are kept on file.

3The line out

Stamford Bridge holds a little over forty thousand, and the turnstiles open toward Fulham Broadway, where the platform tilts toward the ground on matchday afternoons. The shed stands where it always stood; the Matthew Harding Stand keeps its name; the away end keeps its voice. The Bridge Line runs before kick-off and after the final whistle, early and late, in the same measured way the club has kept its place in the top tier — not every season, but the great majority of them, and always with the bridge at the centre of the map. When the timetable is folded and put away, the ground remains, and the minutes with it. The Fulham Broadway lost-property desk also holds one unclaimed constellation between Leo and Boötes (Lost & Found Notice No. CB-7429 — Found, One Constellation).
