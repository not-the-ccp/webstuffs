The Cairene Ledger

Number 9,813,000 · the year after the city was founded, and the thousandth year since · all columns kept in the bakkala way: names first, then the numbers, and never, under any provocation, without the total at the end of the line.

Notice of the City

Cairo is the capital and largest city of Egypt, and of the Cairo Governorate with it. It is home to more than 9.8 million people, and it is the city at the heart of the largest urban agglomeration in Africa, in the Arab world, and in the Middle East: the Greater Cairo metropolitan area holds over 22 million people, which makes it one of the largest in the whole world by population. It stands near the mouth of the Nile Delta, in the ground of the old Fustat, and was founded in the year 969 and made the official seat and capital of things — and it has been growing, without any advertisement of the kind, ever since. Areas of what would become the city were inhabited from the pre-dynastic and early-dynastic times of ancient Egypt, some 6,000 years ago, for the Giza pyramid complex and the ancient cities of Memphis and Heliopolis are today within the city, and the predecessor city of the whole business was Fustat, whose bakkala quarter still keeps its copper lane, its scales, and its rule.

The Ledger accepts advertisements in all three alphabets of the quarter. The rate on every line held in this column is 5% per month on the balance, and the line is yours again at redemption. See the House of Wuhsha on Copper Lane for the discipline as it is kept in the kitchen.

Line No. 14,209

Rooms to Let

Two rooms with a water-jar and a copper shelf, directly over the bakkala at 12 Copper Lane, the bakkala quarter. The floor below is the House of Wuhsha, the pawnbroker's kitchen of Karima bint Ammar; the rent is settled at her counter, in dinars or in pledges, and the ledger at the counter is the landlord. Tenants are admitted at dawn and admitted nowhere at the second adhan. No conversation about other debts: that is not conversation, it is inventory. A child of the house, Abu Saʿd, inspects every loaf that passes the door and bites the crust first; if the crust holds, the tenant is forgiven. Rent: two dinars a month, redeemable in full with a promise, as the house allows.

Entered by the house · 12 Copper Lane · answer at the counter

Line No. 14,210

Lost: a Cow, Name Moti

LOST from the quarter of the river-road, or possibly never in it at all: a grey cow, name Moti, known for a steady disposition and a habit of sitting down under whatever is being written. She belongs to a cousin of Meherpur, who says she kept his first transcription of his grandmother's paddy recipe company by sitting under his pen, and who names her first in every roll he writes, because names come first and the numbers come after. She was last seen chewing a reed near the bakkala and regarding the green-cloth ledger as if she had a line in it. Reward: a measure of rice, a warm word from the chairman of a union, and her name entered in the roll of names at the door of the pot, above the others the family has forgotten.

The latter half of the line is confirmed: the cow is Moti, the pen was the forager's, and the sitting is her custom. The first half has been read four times by the broker, twice by the scribe, and once by Abu Saʿd, who does not read; the "double-bolted land" may be a deed for a bolted parcel, a locked chest, or nothing at all, and it is entered in the ledger as found, squared, and left open, the way the house leaves everything it cannot price.

Entered by the forager of Meherpur · reward in rice · the pen is still in the shop

Line No. 14,211

By the Water

Boats at the bakkala quay, from the old Fustat stairs: a morning passage up the Nile to the Giza pyramid complex, six thousand years of neighbourhood and not one of them advertised; a half-day passage to the ruins of Memphis, and a longer one to Heliopolis, both of them inside the city now, which the river has had to be told. The fish for the pepper-and-coriander glaze comes off one of these barges before the first adhan, the bones picked by a crew of three. Passage: one dinar up, the fare back priced in pledges, and nothing — not the pyramids, not the reeds — leaves the water without a line in the ledger.

The quay, bakkala quarter · boats at the first adhan · no conversation about other debts

Line No. 14,212

Service: a Scribe for the New City

WANTED, for a city of 9.8 million people that keeps on growing faster than its columns: a scribe to run the classified columns of the greater city, one of the largest metropolitan populations in the world, on the Fustat method — a name for every thing, a thing for every name, a total at the end of every line. The shop on Copper Lane will take applications at the counter; the application is the applicant, priced at the day's rate. A Sicilian with a satchel and an appetite of no visible owner need not apply; the last one of that description was married briefly and the marriage ended in the way such marriages do, and the quarter still brings it up at the scales.

12 Copper Lane · the ledger in its green cloth sees applicants

Line No. 14,213

Found: a Ledger in a Green Cloth

FOUND lying open in a neighbour's doorway on the morning the bakkala was closed: a supply diary in a dead hand, every page ruled, every total squared, its owner unknown, its figures correct. It is being kept on the copper shelf beside the pawned notebook and the reed. Claim it at the counter with a total of your own, squared and entered; the house will check the working. Whoever claims it, the rule travels with the book: the meal is the loan, the months are the run of it, and the rate is 5% per month on the balance you still owe the room.

The shelf · claims at the counter · the total at the end of the line

Line No. 14,214

For Sale: one Stone of the Wall of 969

FOR SALE, from the rampart built the year the city was founded: one stone of the wall of 969, the wall that made Fustat's neighbour into the official seat and capital. It is a good stone, dark at the edges the way a good coin is dark at the edges, and it has held for a thousand years, which is longer than the loan on it has held. Sold with a line in the old ledger and a certificate from the broker, priced in dinars, redeemable in pledges, and, if the buyer insists, in a promise — the house allows one promise, the way it allows one crust, and only one of either. The stone will not be delivered to anyone who keeps his grass unmown on double-bolted land, on whatever terms.

The broker's yard · see the stone before the second adhan
