Eleven of twelve lights in the beam. Quorum is seven, by the manual; the council proceeded. The absence of light 12 is entered, as is the custom, and it will not count either way tonight.

II. Approval of the Minutes No. 12

The minutes of the preceding session, in which the score sheet of the season before was found still open at row nine and the sheet was ruled to remain up through the winter, were read by the clerk. They were approved, 11–0, without discussion, which is unusual for this council and which the clerk noted with evident surprise in the margin.

III. The Paste Order

In the third hour, while the walkie was being re-hung on the door, the clerk found a note still taped to the inside of its case, under the channel 2 label, in a hand the block has not placed. She read it aloud, once, and it is entered below exactly as read, the council so ordering:

Bungle away at the time the paste does not hold, return the whole five years of the suburban patrol, built from the substance is thick and very quickly.

The council then applied the standing reading the manual allows for such paper. The chair ruled the note to be a paste order: an instruction on the posting of the season's watch poster, garbled the way paste garbles anything it has dried over. At the time the paste does not hold — that is, when the poster lifts at the corner — bungle away at it, do not scrape; the paste of this block is old and thick, and it sets very quickly, so the whole of the work must be done at once. And “return the whole five years of the suburban patrol”: bring back, to the kitchen door, the five years the watch has actually run on this street, 1994 to 1999, kept then in paste and a pencil, before the kit came and the sheet became printed. Those five years are the substance the block is built on, and they are thick.

Motion of light 3, seconded by light 7, that the line be kept word for word in the margin of the score sheet, on the ground, which the clerk supplied from the manual, that a printed row does not forget. The motion carried 11–0. The note itself was returned to the inside of the case, face down, where the council agrees it belongs.

IV. A Certain Director, Entered in the Ledger of the Block

It was moved, and the council spent the fourth hour on it, that the score sheet — twelve rows by six columns, one row per household, printed to be filled in row by row — be allowed a standing entry: a certain American filmmaker, actor, and author, Quentin Jerome Tarantino (pronounced, the clerk had it marked, TARR-ən-TEE-noh), born the 27th of March, 1963, which is to say, on the very day this minute is taken, forty-nine years to the day. The block's reason for the entry is entered with it.

The record as the council kept it: Tarantino began his life in the trade the way this block began in its own — at the register. He worked the counter of the Video Archives in Manhattan Beach, California, and his first writing is said to have been a map of the store. From the shelf he moved to the frame: an early acting part in the 1980 picture Fame, more small roles through the eighties, and then the screenplays, Roger unmade and True Romance sold. His first direction, Reservoir Dogs (1992), was financed out of his own pocket with a closed room and a clock. Pulp Fiction (1994) followed, and the block's own four Thursday viewings that winter were kept in the margin of the sheet, one line per household, the way a score is kept. The record notes the rest in the order the street names it: Kill Bill in its two volumes (2003 and 2004), Death Proof (2007), Inglourious Basterds (2009), Django Unchained (2012), the year of this minute, and later Once Upon a Time in Hollywood (2019), which this block will allow was the year it was ready for it.

The council's findings, entered for the row: his works are characterized by graphic violence, by extended dialogue often carrying much profanity, and by references to popular culture, which is to say that a Tarantino room is this council's own room with more chairs — a closed space, a roster of voices, one discovery held long enough to become a fact. His films have earned a cult following alongside critical and commercial success, and he has been named by some as the most influential director of his generation. The ledger of the block records his honors: two Academy Awards, two BAFTA Awards, four Golden Globe Awards, a nomination for a Primetime Emmy, five Grammy nominations, and more than $1.9 billion taken at the worldwide box office by his films collectively. On the violence, the council entered the same finding it has entered before about anything that answers on channel 2: the block is not a party to it, the block is a witness to it, and the witness writes the row.

The clerk asked the question the sheet forces on every entry: what moved, what did not, and what was said? What moved, the council found, was the whole street, four Thursdays. What did not move was the sheet. What was said is too long for the row, as is the custom, and so the entry stands at row 13 in pencil, to be inked, as all rows are, only when the twelve are signed.

The motion to enter the standing entry carried 11–0, light 12 absent and not counting either way.

V. The Number Five

The clerk observed, and the record accepts the observation, that the paste order speaks of the whole five years of the suburban patrol, and that the box kept on the shelf beside the kit — Campaign Set No. 63, the War of the Armenian Succession, A.D. 58–63 — is boxed as the whole five years of the only major foreign campaign of Nero's reign. The same number, the clerk noted, in two different keys: one campaign of legions and one campaign of flashlights, both five years long, both fought over a border that is also a curb. The two boxes have been ordered together from the same curb for three seasons. It was resolved, 11–0, that they remain side by side and that both be kept in balance, and that the balance sheet of either be read before the balance sheet of the other, starting with the Roman one, on account of seniority.

VI. Orders of the Next Session

Patrol at 11:15; the row written by 11:40. If the thing answers, hold the channel until the row is written. The note is to stay face down in the case. The five years are to stay on the kitchen door, pasted, not scraped. The next session is called for the 27th of March, 2013, same kitchen, same hour, on the ground that the standing entry at row 13 is made on a day that repeats, which the council found to be a fact and a method at once.
