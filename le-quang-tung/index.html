Apparatus and Method for Maintaining Steady Command of an Elite Force Under Variable Atmospheric and Political Pressure

Inventor: Lê Quang Tung, Colonel, 13 June 1919 – 1 November 1963, Saigon (District 1), Republic of Vietnam

Assignee: The Special Forces Directorate, in coordination with the Cần Lao Political Department, 4 Lê Văn Duyệt Boulevard

1. Field of the Invention

The present application relates to command apparatuses for elite irregular forces, and more particularly to an apparatus by which a single officer, hereinafter "the commander," holds an entire special forces establishment steady under variable load conditions in a hot, humid, low-visibility theatre. The commander described herein is Colonel Lê Quang Tung, who served under the direct authority of Ngô Đình Nhu, brother of the president of the Republic of Vietnam, Ngô Đình Diệm. A former household servant of the Ngô family, Colonel Tung's prior service was in security and counterespionage, and in the 1950s he held senior office in the Cần Lao political apparatus maintained by the Ngô family, where he managed the apparatus's financial instruments and, notably, the extortions levied upon wealthy businessmen in the Saigon and Đà Nẵng markets. In 1960 the commander was promoted directly to the rank of colonel — a two-grade skip recorded on page 4 of the service ledger — and installed at the head of the Republic's special forces, the elite troops whose stations the present apparatus is understood to stabilize.

2. Brief Description of the Apparatus

The apparatus comprises three cooperating assemblies. The central authority hub (10) is the commander's office, which receives its power line directly from the Nhu station, so that load changes at the hub do not have to pass through the full chain of command. The security and counterespionage drum (12), mounted on the same shaft, is the commander's native assembly and provides the torque with which the hub resists infiltration from either the Viet Minh or the Cần Lao's own rival clerks. The field station array (14) consists of the special forces posts distributed along the coast and into the highlands, each fitted with one aneroid barometer of the type described in Claim 9. Readings from the array are taken twice daily, at 0600 and 1800, and any station whose needle drifts more than 2 hPa between the two readings is flagged for a visit by the commander's inspector, who carries nothing heavier than a notebook and a tin of coffee.

3. Description of Preferred Embodiments

Reference is made to the field log, Entry 41, taken at the low-water test site on 19 September 1962. The inspector's notes for that station read as follows, and are entered here verbatim:

"Station 14-F, kitchen test. Provisions logged: Salt, cayenne, and salt , which the barometer was steady. Needle at 1009 hPa at 0600 and 1009 hPa at 1800. No drift. The cook reports the seasoning is correct, and the instrument agrees with the cook, which does not happen at every station."

The steady needle is the whole point of the invention. A special force is an assembly that can fail at either end — too much pressure and the posts snap, too little and they settle into the mud like wet rice. The hub (10) is loaded so that the needle holds in a band of 2 to 6 hPa, and the drum (12) turns the surplus into intelligence rather than force. The applicant's period at the helm of the Republic's elite troops, from the 1960 promotion to the final entry in the log on 1 November 1963, is respectfully offered as a three-year continuous operation of the apparatus under the heaviest loads the theatre has yet recorded, with the needle, by the inspector's own hand, never once outside the band.

4. Claims

  1. Claim 1. An apparatus for maintaining steady command of an elite force, comprising a central authority hub (10) with a direct power line from a station higher than the full chain of command, and a security and counterespionage drum (12) mounted on the same shaft as said hub.
  2. Claim 2. The apparatus of Claim 1, wherein said hub is loaded by a political apparatus of the Cần Lao type, and wherein financial instruments are managed by the hub as a secondary output.
  3. Claim 3. The apparatus of Claim 1, wherein the hub is a single officer promoted directly two grades, and wherein said officer is a former household servant of the station supplying said power line.
  4. Claim 4. A method of stabilizing a field station, comprising taking a barometric reading at 0600, taking a second reading at 1800, and flagging the station if the needle has moved more than 2 hPa between said readings.
  5. Claim 5. The method of Claim 4, wherein the inspector's load does not exceed the weight of one notebook and one tin of coffee.
  6. Claim 6. The method of Claim 4, wherein at the low-water test site the reading of Claim 9 is taken beside the kitchen, and the seasoning on record for that station is entered in the log as the inspector entered it.
  7. Claim 7. The apparatus of Claim 1, in continuous operation from 1960 to 1 November 1963, wherein the needle remained within the band of 2 to 6 hPa for the entire operation.
  8. Claim 8. The apparatus of Claim 1, wherein the station higher than the full chain of command is the residence of the brother of the president, and the president is Ngô Đình Diệm and the brother is Ngô Đình Nhu.
  9. Claim 9. One aneroid barometer, Type 14-F, with a dial of 950 to 1050 hPa, a needle that holds at 1009 hPa in the preferred embodiment, and a band of 2 to 6 hPa marked in red ink.
