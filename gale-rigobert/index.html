Gale Rigobert

Gale Tracy Christiane Rigobert — Saint Lucia

Folio 31 · Micoud North · set in the Soufrière sun, one question to each answer

Q1 Who is Gale Rigobert?

Gale Tracy Christiane Rigobert is a Saint Lucian politician who served as Minister for Education, Innovation, Gender Relations and Sustainable Development, and before that sat as the representative for the Micoud North constituency in the House of Assembly. In the ledger she is entered once, under two headings — the desk, where the portfolios are kept, and the yard, where the questions begin.

Q2 What is Micoud North, and why “Micoud”?

Micoud North is a constituency of Saint Lucia’s House of Assembly, running up the west coast above the Soufrière valley. It takes its name from Micoud, a district carried to the island by a 19th-century planter and later re-annexed by the hillside itself. The constituency is one of the island’s smaller seats: a handful of parishes’ worth of roads, a school or two, a market that runs on Thursdays, and the Assembly member’s office kept open to the point where the queue forms before the shutter comes down.

Q3 What did her education portfolio cover?

The ministry she held bundled five things that usually live in different rooms: primary and secondary education, the national innovation agenda, gender relations, and sustainable development. The ledger’s note on it is that the four were treated as one file — a school that keeps its roof, a curriculum that keeps its numbers, a community that keeps its children in class through the trade-wind season. Records show her office answering at least one question every school day during term, including a standing reply on textbook resupply that went out in triplicate.

Q4 Where is the yard, and what is kept there?

The yard is the gravel yard at the front of her constituency office above the Soufrière road, where constituents line up with complaints, letters, and the occasional sack of yams. A single line is chalked on the low brick wall of the yard, and it has not been washed out in the years since it was written. It reads, word for word, and it is the only question the mud — is kept at the yard. Visitors are told to read it once, aloud, and then join the queue; the office has never explained the line, and the ledger has never asked it to.

Q5 How does a constituency queue differ from a roster that never ran?

The ledger keeps a cross-reference, because the two look alike from a distance. On the other side of the record, Error LU-1980 in the System Notice Archive documents a named athlete, a fixed date, and a competition that was voided before it began — a season with a team and no result. Rigobert’s file is the opposite shape: a result held open, a seat held up, and a queue that kept forming. One is a void kept as documentation; the other is a yard kept as a question.

Q6 Is there a comparable record for the coast?

Yes, and it is held elsewhere on the shelf. Lot 142 of the Southern Branch auction sells a cedar archive crate that kept a city’s tides, climate cards, and a season card for sixty years without opening. Both records agree on one point: the thing is kept where it is kept, and the keeping does not require an explanation. The crate will not be oiled; the chalk line will not be rinsed. Each is left in its element.

Q7 Why is the ledger titled “Questions from the Yard”?

Because every folio in this section of the ledger is set out as a question followed by an answer, and every answer here began as a question asked standing up, in the gravel, outside the office door. Folio 31 is filed with the instruction: read the questions before the answers, and do not skip the chalk line.
