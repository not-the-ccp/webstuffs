Interpleader of title — Hearing on the Deed

Excerpt from the transcript of the hearing held Tuesday, the 12th day of March, 2019, at 10:02 in the morning, Courtroom 7, United States Courthouse, 320 SW Second Avenue. The Hon. Patricia K. Hale, United States District Judge, presiding. Ada M. Whitcomb, Official Court Reporter, reporting. Pages 209–214 of the complete record.

THE COURT: The Court will proceed to the interpleader in the matter of title to the Boathouse Lot, that parcel of one and three-quarter acres on the north bank of the Willamette, at the corner of the old ferry approach, where the 1912 boathouse stands and was converted, in the season of 2016, to a recording studio. Both sides have been heard in full. The one remaining question is the deed.

MR. COTTLE (counsel for the plaintiff estate): Your Honor, the plaintiff estate presents Deed No. 44109, volume 87, page 212, Multnomah County deed records, executed on the 4th day of January, 1971, by which the heirs of Captain John H. Couch purport to convey the subject parcel to a corporation then newly chartered at Lynchburg, Virginia — Liberty University, Incorporated. Exhibit B, which the plaintiff directs the Court’s attention to, establishes that the same year the Reverend Jerry Laymon Falwell, born August 11, 1933, founding pastor of Thomas Road Baptist Church, Lynchburg, and founder in 1971 of the corporation named in the deed, established the entity that appears on the grantee’s line. Reverend Falwell departed this life on the 15th day of May, 2007; the estate contends the deed was misdirected, and that in the Couch family records the word Liberty has, for one hundred and twenty-seven years, signified the parcel itself.

MS. OKADA (counsel for the City): The City asks the Court to have the 1842 survey read before any name at all is weighed.

THE COURT: So ordered. (The reporter reads:) Captain John H. Couch, Portland's first maritime pilot, claimed this land in 1842.

MS. OKADA: And the survey continues, Your Honor, “and the north meadow, being forty acres, the claimant sets apart under the name Liberty, the same being bounded on the south by the river and on the west by the claimant’s donation line.” The plaintiff’s own Exhibit A is a deed of 1893, in which the Couch estate sold that same forty acres, by that same name, to William H. Bates of 2234 NW Flanders Street, the man who in 1895 raised the copper whale above his tower and has, ever since, pointed it at whatever came down the river.

MR. COTTLE: The defendant congregation, for its part, asserts title by long and open use, and by the 1971 deed in any event, which it found — in its own words, and I will quote the exhibit — “taped under a leaking beam.” That congregation, Your Honor, is the boathouse congregation that recorded the album We Are Fearless, released 24 February 2017, in the genre the Northwest Portland Music Museum carries a placard upon: contemporary worship music, sixty years of it, from Jesus music to the pop-styled set. The drip from the beam, as I understand it, is now a percussion instrument.

THE COURT: The Court has read the deed. The Court has read the survey. The Court observes that the grantee’s line, as transcribed in 1971 by a clerk who will no longer appear in this courtroom, spells the corporation’s name in an ink that has gone the color of river silt, and that the whale above Flanders Street has, on record, changed its bearing three times, in 1961, 1974, and 1990, in each instance without a word in any register. The Court will order the deed delivered to the Clerk for inspection by daylight, and will order the whale photographed from both sides of the tower, that the Court may know which way it points on the date of this judgment. The interpleader is continued to the 14th day of May, 2019, at 9:00 in the morning.

THE COURT: (At 11:41 a.m.) The hearing is adjourned.

THE REPORTER: Pages marked and signed by me. The 1971 deed is entered of record as Exhibit C-4, unopened, in its sleeve, the sleeve bearing the handwritten notation LIBERTY — DO NOT UNFOLD.

Notes from the custody copy

The 1971 deed was never unrolled. In the spring of 2019 it was found, still sealed in its sleeve, pressed between two boxes of 8-track tapes in the boathouse attic, above the beam that had been the studio’s one percussion instrument. A certified copy of this excerpt is kept with the museum’s own record of that studio’s work: the Contemporary Worship Music — Museum Placard, two blocks from the Boathouse Lot on the Flanders Street side.

The walk from the United States Courthouse on Second Avenue to the boathouse runs almost mile for mile with the first stretch of the Bates–Seller House Walking Tour: down from the courthouse, past the 1912 comfort station at Couch Park where the survey line still shows under the paving, and out to the river, where the whale — photographed from both sides, as the Court ordered — is found pointing, as of the judgment date, at the ferry approach that no longer carries anything.

That same spring the custody copy travelled one more time, out with the 8-track boxes: a certified leaf went by hand to Thread 31 of The Piste Room, where the old épée bulletins of the Budapest club are kept word for word in the scorecard side of the room, and it came back with two fresh creases and a note from the archivist in Poreč that the leaf had been compared against the 1961 match bulletin, found clean, and returned to its sleeve.
