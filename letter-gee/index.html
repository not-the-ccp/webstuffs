Recalde Press & Cooperage · Council of the Shed · Minutes No. 41

Minutes of the Forty-First Meeting

On the matter of the letter G — the seventh letter — held in the shed behind the bakery, Tuesday 7 October 1999, 9:10 to 9:52 p.m.

Attendance

Present: the desk (chair), Mara Vale (melodica, acting clerk), Elias Rook (concertina), Tessa Cooper (cooper), and N. Halvorsen, tape custodian. In their seats and silent by arrangement: the vinegar crock and the galvanized cattle trough, in which the San Juan de Portugal rides at 1:64. Quorum verified at five, the crock counting by arrangement and not by law. A barrel of salt beef, newly hooped, stood against the door and was not opened, in keeping with Chapter VII; it was entered by error in the beverage column of the inventory, with the desk’s apology standing among the drinks, and the matter closed in Build Log No. 12, The Thirty-Eighth Parallel, the bench’s account of the Korean War layout, where the barrel now stands by the south well.

I. Reading of the previous minutes

The minutes of the fortieth meeting, at which the album Latin Alphabet No. 9 of Code Page 28605 — recorded in this same shed in September, between hoopings — were taken under advisement, were read aloud in full. At the passage concerning track 9, Elias Rook raised the question of the tape box, which still bears its first label, older than the album’s final name and older than the euro’s arrival at 0xA4. The clerk referred the council to the liner notes, where the question is already settled and word for word: “The tape box is labelled Latin-0, and it will stay that way.” The point was put and carried five to nil, no division demanded; the chair abstained in favour.

II. Reading of the correspondence

A letter from a school in San Juan de Portugal, asking what the seventh letter of the Latin alphabet is called when spoken, not set. The desk answered from the shelf: G (minuscule: g) is the seventh letter of the Latin alphabet, used in the modern English alphabet, the alphabets of other Western European languages, and others worldwide; its name in English is gee, pronounced /dː˜/, plural gees. The school was to be told there are seven gees in the week, none of them silent, and that the eighth, if anyone claims it, is a rumour carried in from the front of the alphabet. The reply was left in the out-tray beside the peppercorn cones, and it is the reply that the school then wrote back to, and that the desk apologizes for in the letter of apology on the matter of català, which the clerk kept in the out-tray, third drawer, where the minute book could find it.

III. On the two forms of the minuscule

It was recorded for the minutes that the lowercase letter can be written in two forms: the single-storey, sometimes called the opentail, form ⟨ȩ⟩, and the double-storey, sometimes called the looptail, form ⟨g⟩. The former is commonly used in handwriting and in typefaces based on it, especially in texts intended to be read by children; it is the style used by most sans-serif typefaces, such as Helvetica. The latter is used by most serif typefaces, such as Times.

Motion, by Tessa Cooper and seconded by the tape custodian: that the house admit both forms — that the opentail g be set in the children’s chapbooks and in the hand-ruled pages the shed lends to the school, and that the looptail g keep the navigation chapbooks and whatever else leaves the press in a serif, and that neither be asked to stand down.

In debate, Mara Vale spoke for the opentail, on the ground that a child’s first g is written with one story and should be printed with one. Elias Rook spoke for the looptail, on the ground that the loop is a hoop and the shed is a cooperage. The chair reminded both that the hinge harp, like the g, keeps two forms and is not asked to be either. The motion was carried five to nil.

IV. Orders of the house

Ordered, that the shelf in the shed keep the gees in their numbered places, one to seven, with the seventh facing the door. Ordered, that the opentail and the looptail each hang one specimen from the pegboard, and that the label on each read only what it is. Ordered, that peppercorns continue to be counted one cone at a time, as fractions are counted, and that the crock be returned by the door when the meeting ends. Ordered, further, that any crate brought into the shed keep its count open Thursdays, in the manner directed for the gift of machines by the miscellany’s own Chapter XIV, Of the Imperial Gift, and that no hoop be set on such a crate before the pilot signs; the order was carried by the same hand that kept the gees in their numbered places, and the two are not, the chair noted, the same letter, but are, on Thursdays, the same habit.

V. Adjournment

At 9:52 p.m. the barrel of salt beef was checked for hoops, the San Juan de Portugal was allowed to settle in its trough, and the meeting was adjourned to the forty-second.
