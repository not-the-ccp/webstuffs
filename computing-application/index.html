Abstract

The present invention concerns computing, which is any goal-oriented activity that requires, benefits from, or creates computing machinery. Computing includes the study and experimentation of algorithmic processes, and the development of both hardware and software, and it encompasses scientific, engineering, mathematical, technological, and social aspects. The present application describes one such activity in its entirety as it was worked at the La Caleta Coastal Station, sector DR-7, in the hour between 06:52 and 16:41, when the Atlantic feed was down and the console printed the country in a hand the relayman has not been able to explain. The method requires a single instrument and two operators: a console, a service piano, and a night astronomer. The apparatus already existed. What is claimed is the running of it, in nine claims, and the keeping, uncorrected, of the line it left behind.

Cross-Reference to Related Art

Attention is directed to Specification LCT-48671, the liner notes to Astronomer, by the same first inventor, in which the nine tracks of the present invention were first cut and are kept in the order the relayman logged them, which is the order the station keeps them. Attention is further directed to Lost & Found Notice No. 221 of Bearclaw Ridge Station, one wine-cap Chroogomphus vinicolor, pine spike, found 9 October, the same morning the feed went down, which the present specification cites as the still object against which the method is timed. The household archive is cited in its entirety; nothing in it has been corrected.

Background of the Invention

The term computing is also synonymous with counting and calculating, and the invention stands on both its older names. In earlier times, computing was used in reference to the action performed by mechanical computing machines — the brass and the wheelwork, the crank, the punch card, the card that carries its hole to the right column and the column takes it — and before that, to human computers, the men and women whose whole trade was to do, by hand and by rule, the reckoning that the machines now do by habit. The present invention does not improve on either. It merely records what the feed did not: that a computation does not stop when the line stops, and that the last thing a console prints is not always the first thing it is asked for.

The canteen ledger of the station, which doubles as the prior art file, carries the whole history of the earlier human computers in a single line, entered by a hand the office has not traced and copied here exactly as it stands, because it is the only place in the file where the old trade is described at table service:

“There are some large establishments of princes, noblemen, and very palatable.”

The examiner has been asked what is palatable, and the station has no permission to answer twice, only once, and once has been used. The line is entered here as background, not as a claim.

Summary of the Invention

The invention provides a method for the goal-oriented running of computing machinery in which the goal is named before the feed is lost, the machinery is a console already in service, and the operators continue their work in nine steps that the station has since learned to call tracks. The major computing disciplines are recited in the specification in the order the work touched them: computer engineering (the console, its keying key, its hour), computer science (the algorithmic processes, worked at the auxiliary telescope), cybersecurity (the refusal on the first claim, party not named), data science (the checksum), information systems (the table, line seven of it), information technology (the feed, and its failure, and its restoration), and software engineering (the drawer, which was asked not to open and did not). The apparatus of the invention is a small wooden drawer, a service piano, a relay, and the canteen, the canteen's course of the day — the only thing printed on the board, a single course, “Stewed Rump of Beef à la Julienne, removed by Lobster Salad” — arriving during the sixth step and set down on the desk at 12:14.

Claims

  1. Claim 1. The Handshake. A method of computing, comprising the step of playing a station's own call sequence on the service piano in the office at 06:52, and playing the refusal back on the console's keying key, party not named; the first step being the only step with no named second operator.
  2. Claim 2. The Country in Eight Lines. The method of claim 1, further comprising the step of reading the country at the pace the feed printed it, eight lines to the line, the eighth line being the checksum itself.
  3. Claim 3. Santo Domingo, Named Three Times. A method of verifying a computation by repetition, in which the name is spoken, and again, and again, and the third speaking is the check and not the count.
  4. Claim 4. Five-Eighths. A method of partitioning an island shared, not split, the share being kept in the register by the word shared, the partition being worked in five eighths and the remainder held in the drawer.
  5. Claim 5. Planetary. The method of any preceding claim, wherein the algorithmic process is the planets in their order, worked at the auxiliary telescope in the manner of an astronomer, the field being kept outside the scope of the Earth.
  6. Claim 6. Solar. The method of claim 5, further comprising the step of working solar astronomy during which the canteen sends in one course to the control room, the tray being set down on the desk at 12:14, the computation not stopping for the tray.
  7. Claim 7. Star Birth. A method of the origin or evolution of stars, in which the question of how a star gets to be born and how it gets to spend it is worked to completion in the down hour and the answer is kept, uncorrected, at six minutes and thirty-three seconds.
  8. Claim 8. Formation. A method of the formation of galaxies, comprising a single long take of seven minutes and forty-seven seconds, during which the relayman keeps his ear on the feed in case it comes back; it does not come back; the take is not interrupted.
  9. Claim 9. E–1940, the Line, Kept. A method of keeping an uncorrected line, comprising the steps of: at 16:41, the console printing a line that is not an error code, is not a checksum, and is not in the relay's hand; the feed holding the open channel for the length of the line and no longer; and the station taking no further action, the line ending with the name Abell, which is a name the station knows from the Book of Etiquette for English Surnames, where the surname stands in for the forecast. The apparatus of the claim is the small wooden drawer, which the notice of the pine spike asks not to open, and which opened once, for this pressing, and closed flat again.

Examiner's Note

The application is not allowed yet pressed. The first inventor has demonstrated that computing, in the sense claimed, is any goal-oriented activity that requires, benefits from, or creates computing machinery, and the station will accept the definition as filed, in all its scientific, engineering, mathematical, technological, and social aspects. What the application does not claim — the universe as a whole, the subject called physical cosmology, which is a related but distinct subject — the examiner notes was not pressed into any claim, and was not served as a Dish. The nine claims stand as cut. The line of claim 9 stands as kept. Line seven of the table, the small ads that feed the drawer, is not to be flipped; the examiner declines the request to do so, and no one at the office has flipped that line since 1961, when it was flipped once, on purpose, and the ledger never sat flat again. The application is shelved at shelf seven and a half, against the register that numbers itself by the square kilometres under it, and beside the record it was cut into, which is its echo, at the speed of sound. The record and this specification were logged on the same day, on the same line of the Atlantic, and the office keeps the two of them against the same shelf, which is a way of saying the whole catalogue in nine claims.
