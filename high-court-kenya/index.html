In the High Court of Kenya at Nairobi

Criminal Case No. 312 of 2023

Republic v. Peter Maina Kamau & John Otieno Odhiambo

Before: Hon. Justice Agnes Wanjiku Muli  ·  Court Room 4, Milimani Law Courts, Nairobi  ·  Thursday, 14 March 2024  ·  10:15 a.m.

FILED Transcript certified by Peter Mwangi, Senior Clerk of the Court. Exhibit A: certified copy of the record, 14 March 2024.

THE COURT:

Appearances: Mr. Samuel Njoroge, Senior Prosecuting Counsel, Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions; Ms. Atieno Wekesa, advocate for the first accused; Mr. Kiprotich Rono, advocate for the second accused. The accused are present in court and in the dock. The charge, read and explained, is one of handling stolen property contrary to section 322(1) of the Penal Code, the property being sixteen ledger folios removed from the registry of the Magistrate’s Court at Kajiado on the night of 11 January 2024.

MR. NJOROGE:

My lord, the High Court of Kenya is established under article 165 of the Constitution of Kenya, 2010, as part of the Judiciary, and it enjoys unlimited original jurisdiction in criminal and civil matters. This court at Nairobi sits within that jurisdiction; its supervisory jurisdiction extends over all subordinate courts and over every person, body or authority exercising a judicial or quasi-judicial function. The record we are completing today travelled first through the senior principal magistrate’s court at Kajiado, and it properly belongs here.

MS. WEKESA:

We do not dispute the jurisdiction, my lord, only the chain of custody. The folios were logged at Kajiado at 9:47 p.m., sealed in envelope E-4412, and handed to Police Constable Daniel Mutua, badge number 88417, who signed the exhibit book at 10:03 p.m. Between 10:03 p.m. and 6:00 a.m., the envelope was, according to the station diary, left on the counter of the duty room at Kajiado police station. There is no signature for that interval.

THE COURT:

Note the objection. Call the complainant.

BENSON ONDIAGO, clerk of the Kajiado registry, sworn:

Examination by MR. NJOROGE. Mr. Ondiago, you are the clerk of the court at Kajiado? Yes, my lord, since March 2021, appointed by the Chief Registrar of the Judiciary. On 11 January 2024, at approximately twenty minutes to nine in the evening, you secured sixteen ledger folios in the registry safe? Yes, my lord. And you handed them to Constable Mutua? Yes, my lord, against his signature in the exhibit book, exhibit E-4412. Did you see the envelope again before this morning? No, my lord. It was produced from the evidence safe at 8:40 a.m. today by Sergeant Wanjala.

MR. NJOROGE:

Mr. Ondiago, at any point did the second accused, Mr. Odhiambo, come to the registry that evening? He did, my lord, at about half past eight. He asked for the register of criminal appeals. THE COURT:

Note of the court

The High Court of Kenya was known as the Supreme Court of Kenya until 1964. Its name has remained unchanged since then, while a new Supreme Court of Kenya was established under Article 163 of the Constitution of Kenya as the highest court in the country. This court therefore sits in a room whose name is older than the court that outranks it, and its jurisdiction — original and unlimited in criminal and civil matters, supervisory over every magistrate’s court, kadhi’s court, court martial and tribunal in the land — is entered in the register before the bench sits and remains after it rises. The bench itself, seized with the sixteen folios, has since been delivered to a workshop at Martinsville where it is kept on a pegboard shelf among a 500-lap record; the log of that bench stands with the related entries below (The Martinsville Bench — Build Log). The practice of carrying an odd sentence into the record verbatim is not confined to this court: in the Military Criminal Court of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia at Belgrade, Kralj v. Radovanović & Zmaj d.d., Case No. 214 of 1941, the sentence of a farm instruction leaflet was read and entered into the record exactly as printed, and the certified excerpt of that sitting is kept in the archive (Case No. 214 of 1941 — the Hurricane register of the VVKJ).
