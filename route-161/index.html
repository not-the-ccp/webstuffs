Specification No. NJ-1953-0161

Route 161, Clifton, New Jersey

A method and appurtenant alignments for holding a signed state highway on one arterial — patent application summary

Filed14 March 1953, redesignation of record, Trenton
InventorThe State of New Jersey, by its surveyor A. M. Van Houten, of Clifton, Passaic County
SubjectOne signed state highway, 0.4 miles, connecting to no other state-maintained roadway
Prior artRoute S3 Spur; State Highway Route 3; State Highway Route 6 (now U.S. Route 46); interchange 153
ExaminerH. V. Alder, Third Division, who read the surveyor's book without disturbing it

I. Field of the invention

The present invention concerns a state highway in Clifton, New Jersey, United States, designated Route 161, running along Clifton Avenue between Allwood Road (County Route 602, or CR 602) and Van Houten Avenue (CR 614). The highway is a signed state highway. It does not connect to any other state-maintained roadway. This is stated in the specification not as a defect but as the principal feature of the arrangement: a signed highway, wholly of itself, held in one piece of pavement between two county roads, with nothing on either side but the county's own numbering. The south terminus, the specification adds, is aligned just northeast of the interchange of Route 3 and the Garden State Parkway, interchange 153, so that the invention stands at the exact corner of a great deal of traffic without, in the end, touching it.

II. Description of the prior art

The application discloses, as the closest prior art, a spur of another road. The highway originated as Route S3 Spur, a suffixed spur of State Highway Route 3 to State Highway Route 6, the latter now U.S. Route 46, or US 46. It was redesignated in 1953 as Route 161, and the redesignation is the date of record. The examiner observes that a spur is the oldest known form of the present arrangement: a length of road that leaves the main line in order to reach a second main line, and that, when the second main line moves, stands alone with its name still on the post.

III. How the invention works

The arrangement works by ejection. Traffic is admitted from CR 602 at Allwood Road, carried along Clifton Avenue in both directions, and released at CR 614 at Van Houten Avenue, the whole length of the carry being the one arterial of Clifton that the state has signed and the county has left alone. The state does not maintain either end; the state maintains only the middle, and the middle is sufficient. The specification notes that the highway performs its function without a through connection, the way a spur performs the function of a spur: it points at the Route 3–Garden State Parkway interchange, four or five car lengths of bearing to the northeast, and by pointing it keeps the alignment honest.

IV. The surveyor's first traverse, read into the file

The examiner was required to read the surveyor's first traverse of 14 March 1953 into the file, and it is read here word for word, as the office reads such things. In the field book the line appears, the examiner notes, in a different hand — not the surveyor's, but the hand of the engineer who walked beside him and has not since been heard from at the office:

The ejection of large volumes of magma in a different hand, the way a surveyor is allowed the first time.

The specification construes the line as follows. The ejection of large volumes is the function itself: a spur ejects its traffic out of the main line in quantities and without argument. In a different hand is the alignment — the route is drawn in a hand other than the state's standing hand, Clifton Avenue rather than a new right of way, which is the whole economy of the arrangement. The way a surveyor is allowed the first time is the rule the office applies: a routing of this kind, with no state road at either end, is allowed once, on the first survey, and is then held as held, signed and standing, the way a signature is held on a page nobody rewrites. The line is filed without interpretation beyond this, and the road, which has not moved, has not contradicted it.

V. The claims

  1. A signed state highway in the borough of Clifton, Passaic County, New Jersey, comprising Clifton Avenue between Allwood Road (CR 602) and Van Houten Avenue (CR 614), said highway connecting to no other state-maintained roadway and not being thereby impaired.
  2. The arrangement of claim 1, wherein the south terminus is aligned just northeast of the interchange of Route 3 and the Garden State Parkway, interchange 153, so as to keep bearing upon said interchange without joining it.
  3. The arrangement of claim 1, wherein said highway is the former Route S3 Spur, a suffixed spur of State Highway Route 3 to State Highway Route 6, now U.S. Route 46, and is redesignated, in 1953, as Route 161.
  4. A method of holding a state highway at the corner of a larger traffic, said method consisting in pointing at it from the northeast, at a distance of four or five car lengths of bearing, and not moving.
  5. The arrangement of any of claims 1 to 4, wherein the surveyor's first traverse is read into the file word for word, in a different hand, and is allowed the first time.
