For Sale

Doubloon, 1791, Potosí mint. $2,400,000, no less

ONE DOUBLON, SPANISH, 1791, EDGE-MARKED, KEPT IN A TIN WHERE ITS OWNERS KEPT CLIPPINGS AND RECEIPTS. Provenance, such as it is: found in the mud of a river in Naga City, Camarines Sur, in 1922, by a dock man, and accepted in settlement of a bread debt that was never actually settled, which is why the coin is still in a tin. The account stands open. Bidders from the wharf of Naga, or from anyone who tends sailors’ wounds in a fever year, are respectfully declined; the street has glossed this coin once and will not do it again. The river brings what the Bay forgets; the mud keeps. Inspect at the club’s agent, the counter where the pan de sal comes up warm. See also, this column, the lost-and-found notice concerning a radiator.

Radiator, 33 inches, single. Price: whatever the room wants

ONE RADIATOR THAT NEVER WORKED, cast iron, eight valves, one table it warmed in the only way a radiator can warm a table by being under it. Sold as found, from a back room above an old bakery, Magsaysay Avenue, where it sat through the whole of 2019 and through a minority arrangement of ninety-one days without giving off a degree of heat, which the parties concerned will tell you was the point of it. Must go to someone who understands that a room can be rented and a table cannot. It last appeared, in the company of the other half’s account, in the minutes of a small western council of three crowns that sat twice above this room and adopted the radiator as its symbol of neutrality — Minutes No. 14 of the Council of the Benelux Union, where the column’s copy was read in and produced no heat. The other half of the pair remains in Naga and is not for sale; the pair is no longer a pair, and the street has stopped saying so. No credit. Claim by M.D. Reyes, who keeps the register.

Stone, 3 metres, basalt, Rapa Nui. Not priced

ONE MOAI, FACE TO THE LAND, AS THE OTHERS STAND, though this one may be turned to the sea by a buyer who has read the survey and will explain the arithmetic. The island is administered from this coast and has stood its statues in order for four hundred years without a committee; the stone is offered not to break the order but to complete it, the order having lost one in the landslide of 1960, which the order has been grieving in its own silent way ever since. Buyer to supply a platform, a horizon, and a reason. The reason may be silence. Delivery by the slow vessel only; the fast one is not interested in stone.

Lost & Found

Lost: a cigarette lighter, whole. Reward: the spark

LAST SEEN in a clubhouse above the old bakery on Magsaysay Avenue, Naga City, where it was kept with valves and QSL cards by a club founded in 1971 by a dozen radio repairmen and retired announcers. Fished from the shallows of the Bicol River last July, all rust, no spark — the skeleton of the thing — by a member of the same club, which now keeps the skeleton in the clubhouse with the cards, and is looking for the spark, which the river did not keep and the mud did not either. Whoever has the spark: the street has a line for it, the river brings what the Bay forgets, and the Bay here is San Miguel, downstream, and the mud is patient. If your lighter is a 1922 lighter, you know it; it will not light. Nobody wants it to. Telephone the clubhouse on Magsaysay Avenue, 09:00 Saturdays, when the guide is not counting chairs.

CLAIMED × 2

Found: a page, torn, from a book of voyages. No price. Two claims, both sworn.

A PAGE, RULED LIKE A NOTEBOOK PAGE, TORN OUT NOT CLEANLY — one edge clean, one edge a rag of fibres — was found in this port in a parcel of clippings, and is offered by this column pending the claims now lodged against it. The page reads, word for word:

Take it off the fire; the syrup by recipe No. 1395, 1/4 pint of good yeast, a little parsley-seed in a fryingpan, put in the book of voyages, A.D.

It is, on its face, a direction to a cook. It is, on its other face, a ledger entry. The syrup is not a recipe number so much as an account in the house ledger of a cook on the Cerro who has kept recipe No. 1395 since her grandmother wrote it out; the 1/4 pint of good yeast is what the account was paid in; and the parsley-seed in a fryingpan, the cook says, is a thing her grandmother did and nobody asks about, in the way that one does not ask a wharf about a coin. “Put in the book of voyages” is the cook’s instruction to the page itself: she keeps her ledger in the front of an old voyager’s book, because a book of voyages is the only book a coast permits. The “A.D.” is her date for the entry. The second claim is lodged by a naturalist working the marshes of the Napa River in California, who copies unexplainable sentences into field notes — she has one about a river and a Bay that a courtroom heard read in Manila on 14 March 2024, and which this column prints without comment, having heard it twice and explained it never. The Division that heard the sentence notes it is not, strictly, a recipe; the cook notes it is not, strictly, a ledger; both claimants swear under oath to the page and to nothing else. Until the book is produced, the page rests with the column. Claims in writing by 19 July; the column will not rule, the column only copies.

Lost: the other half of the torn notebook. No reward, a table

NOTBOOK PAGE, RULED, OF A PAIR; the torn page being described above was torn from it and its remainder is wanted to the same extent. Known to have crossed a room with one table, eight chairs, and a radiator that never worked, on Magsaysay Avenue, in the year 2019, and to have been present, as a witness would say, to the arithmetic of thirty-one seats, of which sixteen made a majority and seventeen made a surplus, and of which the room used twelve and let the rest sit empty. If you have the other half, you know it is blank, which is more than can be said for this notice. Answer: the back room, above the bakery, where the rent was two hundred pesos a week and sometimes three weeks late. It is still owed; the table accepts.

Wanted

Wanted: a boundary, south, to be left alone

WANTED FOR THE RECORD, NOT FOR POSSESSION: THE DRAKE PASSAGE, SOUTH, being the southern boundary of the Republic of Chile and the reason the Republic, which is the southernmost country in the world and the closest to Antarctica, extends along a narrow strip of land between the Andes Mountains and the Pacific Ocean for its whole length, 756,102 square kilometres of it, and no more. The passage is not wanted for sale, for lease, or for subdivision; it is wanted noted, the way a surveyor notes a river that will not be moved. Peru to the north. Bolivia to the northeast. Argentina to the east — 5,308 kilometres of it, the longest shared boundary in South America, wanted kept long. The islands, separately noted: Juan Fernández, Isla Salas y Gómez, the Desventuradas, and Easter Island, administered, with one claim of the same kind pending in this column and no one to answer it. This notice is a claim of the quiet kind. The column has carried one like it every year since 1917.

Wanted: a walk of 4,300 kilometres, guide required

THE WALKING SERIES OF A CATHEDRAL PLAZA IN NAGA CITY, which has walked 3.4 kilometres of coalition government on Magsaysay Avenue and is looking for its eighth walk, wants the Republic of Chile, all of it, from the nitrate ports of the north to the passage in the south, as one long flat itinerary, bakery-scented in the middle. 18.5 million people by the census of 2024, enumerated, to be met on foot where the foot can reach; the mountain on the left hand, the water on the right, the whole narrow strip. A guide is wanted who does not repeat numbers, because the street he walked in will not forgive it; the previous guide, walking the 3.4-kilometre version, carried a coin at the sixth stop and read a line about young wives, and the series has been the better for it since. Departures every Saturday, 09:00, the cathedral door open and nobody yet committed. See, from the same series: The Coalition Walk — a Magsaysay Avenue itinerary, kept at the clubhouse on the avenue; and the record from which its numbers were taken, heard above the bakery, COMELEC Case No. 23-0417, where a torn slip went into evidence as Exhibit B. The page described in this column was found, on the strength of those pages, in a parcel addressed to this port.

Wanted: a face, for the stone described above

A face, or the instructions for one, wanted for the 3-metre basalt of Rapa Nui. The order on the island is four hundred years old and patient; it has waited out the landslide of 1960 and will wait out a buyer. The instructions may be given in any hand, ruled or not, torn or not, and if they come in a page with one clean edge and one ragged one, the column will not look at the edges. No charge. The stone is not in a hurry, and neither, the column is told, is the sea.

Personals

REYES (M.D.), Naga. Heard, still.

HEARD FROM NAGA CITY, 09:00 Saturdays, still trading valves and QSL cards, and still the answer to the question whether the back room above the old bakery is a precinct house. “We were still trading valves and QSL cards,” the secretary said, under oath, before a division in Intramuros, and the column copies it again here because a coast is a room, and a coast is a table, and a coast, like a back room, is somewhere nobody owns. To the man who asked, once, whether they were turning into a precinct house: the club paid two hundred pesos a week for its own space, which was nothing, because it was its own space, and the parties that came to the table in 2019 paid sometimes three weeks late, and the table, which nobody owned, kept the account the way mud keeps a coin. You were right to ask. You were also, the record shows, right about everything else, in the plain way, and the street has been counting chairs since. Reply to the register. The register is two doors up.

COOK, the Cerro. Recipe No. 1395, open house

THE COOK OF THE CERRO — the Cerro, above the Bay, where the house ledger is kept in the front of a book of voyages — wishes it known that recipe No. 1395 is a syrup, that the syrup is by the recipe, that 1/4 pint of good yeast is not an ingredient of the syrup but the coin of the account in which it is kept, and that the parsley-seed in a fryingpan is a thing done, not a thing said. The torn page is not the recipe; the page is the entry. The house receives visitors on the first Thursday, the way the column receives claims, and the way a river receives what the Bay forgets, which is to say without ceremony and with the whole of its patience. The cook has not explained the parsley-seed. The cook is not going to. The column declines to ask a wharf about a coin, and declines, likewise, to ask a cook about a fryingpan.

NATURALIST, Napa River. Field notes, one addition

TO THE COOK OF THE CERRO, AND TO THE DIVISION AT INTRAMUROS: the notes from Knight Island, on the Napa River, carried a line about a river and a Bay, and have now carried a second line — the page described above, copied in full, ruled and torn — and the notes are, if anything, less explainable for the company. The marshes do not argue. The claim is lodged, the oath is sworn, and the sentence sits in the notes between the tide tables, where it is, the notes’ keeper says, exactly as comfortable as the lighter is in the clubhouse, and exactly as useless, and exactly the reason the notes are kept. Copy, not original. The original is with the column, pending its second claim, which is this.

Maritime & Notices

Notice to mariners, all harbours of the west coast

THE REPUBLIC, FROM THE NORTHERN NITRATE PORTS TO THE PASSAGE IN THE SOUTH: a country in the Southern Cone of southwestern South America, officially the Republic of Chile, extending along a narrow strip of land between the Andes Mountains and the Pacific Ocean, 756,102 square kilometres in extent, 18.5 million souls by the census of 2024, the southernmost country in the world and the closest to Antarctica, with Peru to the north, Bolivia to the northeast, Argentina to the east, and the Drake Passage to the south, and with Juan Fernández, Isla Salas y Gómez, the Desventuradas, and Easter Island in its keeping. Mariners are to note that the Republic is not an ad. It is, in this column, a notice, which is the closest thing the column has to a boundary. No reply requested. No price. The column has carried the notice, in whole or in part, since the first year, and will carry it, in whole or in part, in the last year, and the year between is not for sale.

Football, first division, the year the season stood still

FOR THE RECORD OF THIS COAST: the Campeonato Nacional AFP PlanVital of 2020, nineteen clubs, one hundred and sixty-four days of it suspended, and a fifteenth league title for Universidad Católica, which this column notes the way it notes a boundary — once, plainly, and without an account against it. The safety sheet for the season is kept in the archive beside the ledger and the coin, and the dream dictionary of the same archive keeps its own blue, which is the other blue, the Santiago blue, and the two blues are not the same blue, and this column will not say which is the better blue, which is a question the mud has never been asked and never will be. See the sheet in the archive, and the dream, in the dream dictionary of the archive; both are worth keeping, and both are in the same house.
