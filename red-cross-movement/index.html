A Sealed Crate in the Game Larder, Caithness — Seven Principles on the Lid

Started by LarderKey on 14 October 2026, 21:04 · filed under Found & Sealed, cross-filed under The Long Winter

LK
LarderKey Larder Keeper, Caithness Post No. 1 · 14 October 2026, 21:04

Members of the Register. In August, when the house at the far north opened its game larder for the season, we found a crate where no crate had a right to be. White-painted pine, lashed with green twine, the lid stencilled in faded oxide red: a cross inside a circle, and beneath it, in a clerk’s hand, the seven words the Movement has carried since before most of us had grandfathers — humanity, impartiality, neutrality, independence, voluntary service, unity, universality.

Inside: a first-aid kit stamped 1942, its straps still buckled; a bundle of folded bandage paper in a black-and-white pattern I do not recognise; a slip of card taped to the inside of the lid that is not, I think, a recipe; and one disc, twelve tracks to a side, pressed at 33⅓, in a sleeve stamped before a plant in Hamburg was taken. The whip from the Saltfleet loft was hanging by the larder door where it has hung for years; someone had looped the crate’s twine around the whip’s hand as if the two things had agreed to share a hook. I did not cut the twine. Who among us does?

Before I open anything else, I want the board’s reading of the lid. Seven words, and a Movement of roughly 16 million volunteers, members and staff, all founded on the business of protecting human life and health, ensuring respect for all human beings, and preventing and alleviating suffering. That is what the lid is asking us.

NG
NeutralGround Board Moderator, Geneva Post No. 2 · 14 October 2026, 22:31

LarderKey, read the lid in order and you have the whole charter. The short history the lid assumes: the Movement grew out of the 1863 first Geneva Convention and the organisation that pressed for it, the International Committee of the Red Cross, founded the same year and still sitting in Geneva; the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies came in 1919, and the national societies around it have made the seven principles into a working habit — neutrality and impartiality in the field, independence from the governments we serve alongside, voluntary service on the rolls, unity one society per state, universality every one of us equal in a single Movement. The Conventions have been met and re-met since 1864; the fourth, in 1949, is the one the larder is closest to.

The Movement’s oldest habit, and the one the board borrows, is to keep what is given without striking out a single line. Post the slip as it is. We will make sense of it together, or we will not, but we will not tidy it first.

WardKeeper40 Volunteer, East End Ward, 1939–1945 Post No. 3 · 15 October 2026, 08:12

I will make sense of the card, though. I was in the East End when the blackout hours were being cut, and the broadcast that ran at four o’clock on 13 September 1940 — the kitchen one, the one every side-street kitchen answered that day — is the only thing I have ever seen that sounds like a recipe and is not. The same hour took a fire to Buckingham Palace, and the Queen said, in so many words, that she could now look the East End in the face. She kept her word; the tours of the bombed streets, the hospitals, the factories, went on for the rest of the war, and the household that kept her — Glamis, Bruton Street, Piccadilly, the Royal Lodge — is kept in full at Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother — Queen Consort, 1936–1952. Sixty years on, her widowhood and ours share a habit: we do not leave the room the fire was in.

The card in the crate is a sister of the one that went under the static on that same day. Ask the disc.

GK
GrooveKeeper Archivist, Municipal Collection Post No. 4 · 15 October 2026, 11:47

Asked the disc. It is the pressing the liner notes call The Thousand-Year Record — a twelve-track record of the years 1933–1945, one of the three surviving copies, and apparently the one the Caithness larder was always going to keep. The sleeve carries the kitchen-broadcast recipe card of 13 September 1940, the one with the line that is not a recipe, and a tracklist slip in a clerk’s hand, the same hand as the lid. The slip lists only two tracks, and I am posting them word for word, as the board requires:

B1 — September, Thirteen 6:19 B2 The Long Winter (1942–43) Stalingrad.

Two tracks, the whole war as a volunteer sees it: the night the palace took its first fire and the kitchens of the East End answered a broadcast at four o’clock, and then the winter that lasted past both of them. B1 is six minutes and nineteen seconds of a kitchen under static; B2, in the liner notes, is seven minutes and four seconds cut at 45 revolutions so the winter would fit on the side, and in it the wind stops for eleven seconds — the only place in the record where it stops — at the moment the Sixth Army surrendered at Stalingrad on 2 February 1943. The Red Cross at Stalingrad in that winter is not on the record; it is on this board. That is what the slip is for. It pairs the kitchen with the winter the way the Movement pairs the room it stands in with the one it is walking toward.

LashMaker Rigging Loft, Saltfleet Post No. 5 · 16 October 2026, 17:05

The twine on the crate is not ours, but the hook is, and the loop is right. Whoever looped the crate around the whip’s hand knew the braid: it is stitched by hand over a cane spine, bridle leather cured against the salt air, oak hand from a single piece of green oak — the same lash the household kept in that larder, hanging, to remind itself that duty, like a well-made lash, holds best when it is made by hand. The same sentence is on the lid of every crate we have ever sent out, in a smaller hand, under the cross. If a volunteer at Caithness looped a sealed Movement crate around a hand-tied whip and left the two on one hook, that is not a mistake. That is the whole instruction set, in one gesture: keep the hand-made thing and the sealed thing together, and neither of them lets go of the other.

NeutralGround Board Moderator, Geneva Post No. 6 · 17 October 2026, 09:20

The reading of the lid is unanimous, so the lid stands read. The crate remains sealed until the larder is closed for the season; the bandage paper is going to the municipal archive with the disc, and the disc goes back to the sleeve with its card and its slip. The seven words on the lid will not be abridged to seven letters, and the two tracks on the slip will not be merged into one; unity is one society per state, not one line per slip. Volunteers of the Register: 16 million of us, one Movement, and the larder at the far north is ours too. Post what you keep, word for word, without striking out a single line.
