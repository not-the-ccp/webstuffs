ON AIR

KHTT 97.7 FM · THE HOLLYWOOD HOUR · TRANSMISSION 88 · 11:00 P.M. PT

Falling Down

A radio play in two scenes and a coda · Script excerpt, as aired

Studio B, 2220 Vine Street, Hollywood, California · Thursday, 20 August 2009 · Five days before the single's release on 25 August through Hollywood Records

CAST OF THE EXCERPT

  • SELENA — the lead of Selena Gomez & the Scene, in the vocal booth
  • TED — Ted Bruner, co-writer and producer
  • TREY — Trey Vittetoe, co-writer and producer
  • GINA — Gina Schock of The Go-Go's, holding a writing credit on the record
  • THE PA — the public address voice of the studio, heard from the glass box

PRODUCTION NOTE: All tracking cues are set in type and underlined in the log. The play was recorded to quarter-inch tape in the studio itself; this page prints the script excerpt that survived on the studio's carbon copy, with the log's one baffling line kept exactly as written.

SCENE ONE — THE BOOTH

SOUND: A tape machine at the head of a song. Then a wall of synths, bright and heavy, laid over a drum track that refuses to sit still. Under it, a guitar that keeps trying to pull the mix toward rock and keeps being pulled back by the synths. It is the kind of uptempo rock track that sounds like it was recorded by people in a hurry.

SOUND: The booth door, a pencil tapping the slate, a glass of water moved an inch on the desk.

TED: (into the talkback) We're at the end of it. Five days to the 25th, and it still wants to be the first single off the record.

SELENA: (through the glass, unhurried) It is. I've been telling you all week. The song decided before we did.

TREY: The song doesn't decide anything. I decide things. You decide things. Gina decides things over there in the hallway, which is how she ended up on the writing sheet.

GINA: (from the hallway, clearly) And it's my line. Don't get clever.

SOUND: The tape machine stops. A short silence, the kind with air conditioning in it.

TED: Let's hear the verse again. From the top of the second. Watch the tempo — if the drums get ahead of the synths, the whole picture falls down, and that's the title doing the work whether we like it or not.

SELENA: (singing, half-joking, then not) And the city that wrote me a check keeps writing my name in the margin…

SOUND: A chord lands and holds. The four of them do not applaud. That is the sign it's right.

TREY: There it is. See? The lyrics are doing the whole job. You can read them as a romance and they work. You can read them as a girl taking aim at Hollywood itself — the fame, the red carpets, the machines that photograph your face at angles your mother wouldn't recognise — and they work. A song that means two things at once is worth two singles. We ship it and let the argument happen in Europe, where we're not even releasing it, which is somehow the most Hollywood thing on the record.

SELENA: (dryly) It's Kiss & Tell. It's the whole album. I'm just the first single.

SOUND: The booth door again. Then, from the glass box, a dry, unhurried voice.

THE PA: Hold your applause, Studio B. Housekeeping before the break. The tracking sheet for the 25th is in the log, and the log keeps whatever the log keeps. Last entry of the session, transcribed by the engineer, word for word: Route 11W, and one thing in the cast, and they work the anatomy part begins, is its only fault.

SOUND: A pencil tapping, once.

TED: …Read that again.

THE PA: Route 11W, and one thing in the cast, and they work the anatomy part begins, is its only fault. That is the entry. The engineer has signed it, dated it 20 August, and refused to elaborate. It is the only line on the sheet that isn't a tempo, a take number, or a level. The log does not delete entries. You know the log does not delete entries.

GINA: Route 11W. That's a road, isn't it? Tennessee. I was born on a road, figuratively.

TREY: And "one thing in the cast" — the cast? Whose cast? We're a band, not a stage production. One thing in the cast and it works the anatomy part begins. That's not a sentence. That's a sentence with a coat on.

SELENA: (quieter) Or it's the song's only fault, and the engineer just quoted it back. "Is its only fault." Read it one more way: everything about the session is fine, and its only fault is that one thing in the cast that begins the anatomy part — the part where a song has to open a person up to be understood. And then the song is falling down on purpose, so the audience can watch it happen.

SOUND: A long silence. The tape machine makes its small idle sound, like a held breath.

TED: (deciding) Then that's the working theory. It goes in the liner notes of the log, not the album. Roll for the break.

SCENE TWO — THE LOG

SOUND: Night. The parking lot of 2220 Vine Street. One set of footsteps, gravel, a lighter, the lighter again. A car door. The radio already on, low.

SELENA: (to herself, to the tape, to the car) Friday the 25th, and it's out in the territories that will take it. No official release in Europe, so the continent will hear it the same way it hears everything else we do — through a friend's friend, one bootleg ahead of the law. Fine. Hollywood can keep its critics. I've already written them out of the second verse.

SOUND: The single's synths, bright through the car speakers, heavy, unmistakable. She taps the wheel, once, twice, in time with a drum that will not sit still.

SELENA: Ted said the drums get ahead of the synths and the picture falls down. But the drums are always getting ahead. That's the point. That's why it's rock instead of a lullaby. And the log — the engineer's line, Route 11W and the cast and the anatomy part — I've been driving with it all afternoon. I think it's a sentence about a recording: one thing in the cast, one detail you can't cut, that starts the whole anatomy of the thing, and its only fault is that it has to be there. You can't finish a song without the fault in it. You can't finish anything.

SOUND: The tape machine, somewhere she cannot see, rolling on. Then the road takes the rest of it.

CODA — A NOTE FROM THE STUDIO LOG

On the tracking sheet dated 20 August 2009, beneath the engineer's signature, the line remains exactly as transcribed in Scene One, and remains so in every later copy of the log. The studio's own house rule is the same rule as the log's: entries are not deleted. The single was released on 25 August 2009 through Hollywood Records as the lead single from Kiss & Tell, in the territories that took it, and the line sat at the foot of the sheet for the length of the tour that followed, one thing in the cast, beginning the anatomy part, its only fault. The line has not been lost to the night since: the sound lab that holds a copy of the tape placed it, word for word, beside the founding folio of a company the ledger once opened to one person at a time, and it now stands framed in Placard No. 421 — Apple Inc., The Silicon Gallery. The sound lab also sends the sheet, once a year, on the only route that reads its entries aloud the same way: the Montréal circuit of the water-works and the watch, whose own visitor’s sheet ends in a single unexplained line, read once at the rail and never struck out — Moses Judah Hays — A Walking Tour of the Waterworks & the Watch. For the road the line names — U.S. Route 11W, the western branch running Bristol to Knoxville through the Clinch gap and the Holston Valley — the site keeps its own set of answers, stop by stop: see U.S. Route 11W — Frequently Asked Questions. For another late-night broadcast staged exactly this way — a vault room, a talkback voice, a line kept word for word — the archive keeps The Dragon's Rehearsal, AFI Archive Broadcast Service, and for a fog-bound studio where the synths outlived the session entirely, Out of the Woods — Official Visitor's Brochure.
