SAFETY DATA SHEET · FOURTH REVISION · 19 NOVEMBER · PARA RIVER ENTOMOLOGICAL STORES

Cosmosoma evadnes

A moth of the family Erebidae · described by Caspar Stoll in 1781 · found in Suriname

1Identification of the substance and of the supplier

Product nameCosmosoma evadnes, whole, pinned or in flight
Catalogue indexCE-1781-0342 (Stoll number 342 of the Surinamese series)
FamilyErebidae — the moth answers to no other name than its own
Original descriptionCaspar Stoll, 1781, from material taken in Suriname; the description was entered in the office on the first night it arrived, which was a dark night
SupplierPara River Entomological Stores, No. 14 Kruiskade, Paramaribo, Suriname
Emergency contactThe night lamp, St. Louis landing, Para River; keep the line clear of banana leaves
RevisionFourth revision, 19 November. Third revision withdrawn for a smudge on section 9; the smudge was a moth.

2Hazard identification

⚠Exclamation
🔥Lamp-flammable

H272 — Will alight on the lamp with some force. H273 — May alight on the face of a person reading near the lamp; the landing is brief, the memory of it is not. H411 — Toxic to nothing, which is the only thing a substance of this family can promise.

P233 — Keep container closed unless a reading is in progress. P280 — Wear dimmed eyesight when working at 40 watts or less. P501 — Dispose of the shed scales in the rain, which in Suriname is not difficult to arrange.

No further hazards identified. The substance is not an oxidizer, not a corrosive, and has never once been known to damage a book. It has, however, sat on one.

3Composition / information on ingredients

Forewings (2)dark umber, fuscous along the costa, with a faint broken band; 44.5 mm to the apex on the reference specimen
Hindwings (2)paler, the margin dusted with a brown that the stores call, for want of a better word, evening
Bodytufted thorax, 1.9 g on the brass pan at Paramaribo; the tuft is what settles on the reader
Wingspan82 mm, the figure the ledger has kept since 1781 without complaint
Scales97% of dry mass; the remaining 3% is the part of the substance that cannot be pinned

4First aid measures

If alighted on the skin: hold the hand still and let the substance decide; it departs on its own schedule, which is quick. Do not blow, do not fan with a newspaper. If scales enter the eye: rinse with rainwater, preferably from the Para River, for a full minute, and note the colour of the water in the day book. If inhaled: nothing to inhale; the scales are too large and too light, and they go where the light goes. Main symptoms: none, except a small and unaccountable pleasure, which is not a symptom and is therefore not treated. Do not call the clinic on account of a moth; the clinic has its own lamp, and its own visitors.

5Firefighting measures

The substance burns with a clean flame and no smoke worth the name, but it is not worth the fire, and no one at the Para River Entomological Stores has ever been paid for a rescue of it. To put out the lamp to which it is attached: close the door, and the flame, and wait. Do not throw water at the lamp; the water is for the pots, and the pots have their own instructions. Fire fighters should approach the specimen by the far window and keep their hats on.

6Accidental release measures

If the substance is released into a room — a box left open, a window left to the night — darken every lamp in the house and open one window only, the one that faces the rain. The substance will leave by the lightless side, because it reads the dark the way other animals read the light, and by morning it will be in the trees. Do not chase it with a book. Do not chase it at all; the chase is how specimens become losses, and the losses are entered in the ledger with a heavier hand.

7Handling and storage

Handle with clean, dry, still hands, one at a time, over the cedar box and not over the table. The reference collection is stored at 65% humidity in the lower room at No. 14 Kruiskade, in cedar boxes with the lids fitted, the boxes standing on the brick so the river's moods do not reach the pins. Keep away from strong light between dusk and four of the morning; between four of the morning and dusk, store as any other quiet thing. It keeps to itself, and asks nothing of the neighbours, which are other moths, and of those it is the most patient.

8Exposure controls / personal protection

No occupational limit has been set, because no one has exceeded one. The lamp used for the evening work should not exceed 40 watts; the stronger the lamp, the more the substance arrives, and the store has found that more is not better. Nitrile gloves are not required. A held hand is the entire standard of personal protection, and it has served the store since Stoll's series came in.

9Physical and chemical properties

Appearancenocturnal; when not flying, folded, patient, easy to mistake for a small dry leaf, which is a kindness it does not advertise
Flight seasonthe wet months, February through July, heaviest at the turn of May when the Para rises and the lamps are put out on the banks
RangeSuriname — lowland forest at the edges of the big rivers; it is found in Suriname and the stores know of no obligation to be anywhere else
Stability in the opengood in rain, excellent in the dark, poor under a bright lamp for longer than it must be

10Stability and reactivity

Stable under all conditions at which it is normally kept. Incompatible with xylene, with the strong sun, and with anyone who taps a glass of water. Does not polymerize, does not precipitate, and has not, in two hundred and forty-four years of record, reacted with anything except light, which it accepts in the ordinary way of its family.

11Toxicological information

No toxicological data are available because no poisoning has been reported. The substance is not eaten in Suriname; it flies at the hour when the family is asleep, and the family, waking, takes the sight of it for the weather. Acute oral effects: none known, and none sought. Chronic effects: a window left open once in May, and the memory of the wing on the curtain, which the stores record as the whole of the long-term picture.

12Ecological information

Mobile by night, stationary by day in the low branches. The habitat is the lowland rainforest of the Para River basin and its tributaries in Suriname, where it passes its one season each year without disturbing the ledger of anything else. Not migratory. Not invasive. The store's field note on the ecology is one line, and it is entered under the month of May: the trees are loud with it, and nobody in the boat says so, and the boat does not stop.

13Disposal considerations

Shed scales: to the rain. Spent lamps: to the metal man at the corner of the Kruiskade, who weighs them by the kilo and does not ask what they were for. The specimen itself, when its pins have given out and its boxes have faded, is dried and returned to the cedar, where it will outlast this sheet, which is the only disposal the store offers a thing of this family.

14Transport information

No UN number, no class, no special permit. The substance travels in a cotton bag on the passenger's lap, the bag held in the left hand, the window shut on the far side of the river. For road, river and rail the only declared quantity is the one that will not sleep. Sea transport is not recommended for reasons the store declines to argue; a crossing is a crossing, and the substance has opinions about crossings that it keeps to itself.

15Regulatory information

Entered in the office on the strength of the original description of Caspar Stoll, 1781, and re-entered at each revision. No restriction in force in Suriname. No entry in the waste lists, for the simple reason that the substance does not become waste; it becomes memory, and memory is regulated by no one at No. 14 Kruiskade.

16Other information

Slipped into the reference box at St. Louis one night in the wet season, between the label of the Para River specimen and the card on which the store keeps the lamp's oil count, the office found a paper it did not put there. It is transcribed below without correction, because the office's rule is that a record is a record and the rule is not for argument:

Office Record · twelve tracks, the artist's own singles returned to Felix Nori his lamp back, on the Solent before the needle moves.

Felix Nori is not listed in the store's books, and the Solent is not on the chart that hangs by the river door, and the needle — which the oil count implies is a needle — has not moved since the slip was found. The office has entered the line under Other, which is where it keeps the things it is not ready to explain, and has left the lamp burning at 40 watts until it is. The sheet is otherwise closed. The shelf under the lamp also keeps the family recipe the store cooks on Saturdays after the long nights — every ingredient on the card is called by its two Latin names, and one pencilled line sits in the margin that the office has not yet been ready to explain: Stoll's Two-Name Braise, a family recipe with the long preamble. Behind it, pinned to the same board, the office keeps a torn page from the Paramaribo paper — an obituary for the country the moth came from, filed under Suriname: Obituary — Suriname. It is entered under the same rule: a record is a record, and the rule is not for argument. Return to the Webstuffs archive, where this sheet is filed, and compare the field diary kept across the water on the Lichen Geometer, Archephanes zalosema, a moth of the same long evening and the same rule: it answers to the lamp, and the lamp answers to nothing. The sheet's own questions — identification, hazards, the snap of a book at the far end of the order — are answered in full at the Blue Gum desk's ledger of questions on Lepidoptera, where this substance is entered under the whole order, 180,000 species strong, and found wanting in none of them.
