Los Angeles · Evening Ledger · Twenty Cents

Small Advertisements

Wednesday, 11 November 1931 · Column Six · Filed by order of the Red Squad, Central Division

The following advertisements are set in this column under the standing arrangement between this office and the Central Division of the Los Angeles Police Department, the arrangement having been made, as the division's file has it, at the insistence of Sergeant Red Hynes, the Red Squad's head, and of his right-hand man, Luke M. Lane (badge 4473), the man the Squad calls, when it calls for anyone at all, simply “Lane”. The column accepts no advertisement it does not need, and has not declined one since 1930. All replies to care of the Ledger, P.O. Box 1411, Hollywood.

Wanted

One red ledger, cloth cover, water-damaged at the lower corner, the kind of book that keeps nothing written on the page. Last seen 9 November, in the Crescent Room at Fourth and Hill, in a jacket that was not, on inspection, the jacket of any member of this department. The ledger is worth more than it weighs; the man who hands it in at the Central Division, between the hours of the watch and the end of it, will be paid in a way that will be described to him, in person, by a square-jawed sergeant in a grey suit. Lane keeps the list of what the ledger has said. Lane does not publish it. This office does not print it.

Missing

One pocket almanac, old style, the moon phases set down in the Julian fashion, found to be thirteen days adrift of every wall calendar in the house. Belonged, by a pencilled line inside the cover, to an officer who is no longer in the column's service. Whoever holds it is asked to hold it a little more carefully. The almanac copies itself in other hands, and this column does not much care for that. A full sheet on the family of the almanac is kept in this miscellany, in the file on the Los Angeles, Guidebook No. 47, where the city itself is reckoned, street by street, in the same patient way. Also wanted, one dream reported the night of the 9th: a tall, soft-spoken man in a khaki necktie at the top of a long wooden building, speaking of handbooks and training centers; the miscellany keeps a sheet on the man, in its dictionary of dreams, at the entry Goodman, E. Urner, Entry G-13, where the dream's own date of 1931 matches this column's to the year, which the Squad has noted and will not explain.

Found

One brass shield, number 4471, found on the wet side of Broadway at the corner of Spring. It is not the shield of the sergeant who keeps this column, whose is 4473, and it is not, the Squad has said, the shield of any man on the current roll. It has been washed, oiled, and hung on the nail outside the Central Division's record room, where it stays. The man of 4471, when he comes, may take it down and go. No questions on the nail. There have been questions at the counter, but the counter is a different column.

Wanted to Hire

Wanted, one man, steady, who can drive a V8 in the rain and keep his mouth shut in a moving car. No questions asked as to the road taken, as to the load, or as to why the same corner of the city is being passed twice in an hour. Apply in person, Central Division, the room with the map on the wall. The pay is honest and is paid in cash. Lane drives his own car; the man wanted here will drive the other one, and will be asked, on the first day and none after, only this: which way was the truck going when it left the yard.

For Sale

One 1928 Ford V8, black, left hand on the left, the paint gone at the doors and the clock stopped at 4:12. Sold at cost to a man in a brown suit who came in on Tuesday, paid in a coin the counter had to look at twice, and left without the keys, which is why the price is what it is. Cash or otherwise. The car has been, in the file of this column, a car with a past; it is offered now as a car with a present. Lane inspected it, said it was a good car, and wrote two words in the margin of the inventory sheet. The two words are not for this page.

Beers & Bottles

For the gentlemen who have come out of the division with dry mouths and the taste of a hard day in them: A and B Bitter and A and B Brown, the cold Surinamic beers, now kept behind the counter of the Vries & Daughters Company, importers, on the wharf. The bottles come up from the southern sea in crates of twenty-four, the paper labels still damp, the brewer's own line set on every carton in full: A AND B · No. 14 Kruiskade, Paramaribo, Suriname. The brewer's cellar, it is said, is troubled by a pale moth found nowhere else in the whole of the trade, a certain Cosmosoma evadnes, on which this miscellany keeps its own sheet, Safety Data Sheet No. CE-1781; the Vries & Daughters Company denies the moths and sells the beer. Lane takes a bitter, standing, at the counter, and Hynes takes none at all, which is what makes it a counter worth keeping. Also held at the counter, pending its owner: one timber crate arrived from overseas in the winter of '22, stencilled at the head with a number the size of a man, its contents counted three times and its count kept deliberately unbalanced, for the book that keeps the etiquette of such machines directs that a gift still be findable, and the count of a case is never closed before the pilot signs it; that book is kept in this miscellany, in Chapter XIV, Of the Imperial Gift, where the crate's owner is still wanted in the same patient column.

Personal

To the man who wore the brown coat in the Crescent Room and left the ledger in the booth: the rain on 9th was cold, and you are not a man the cold should follow. The column will print this once. The squad is patient, the column is not, and the red book is heavier in the file than it looks on the counter. To the counter girl at the Vries & Daughters, who asked who keeps this whole page, and was told: a sergeant, in a grey suit, with a bad left knee and a good eye for trucks. You may keep the chalk dust; it is the only thing on this column that is not paid for. — R.H., with the consent of L.M.L., both of Central Division, both of whom are, at the hour of printing, still at their desks, the one reading and the other watching the door.
