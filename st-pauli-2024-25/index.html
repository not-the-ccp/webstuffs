Dream Dictionary · Entry 723

St. Pauli

proper noun · season 2024–25

St. Pauli is a quarter of Hamburg, a football club, and, in this dream, a season that played itself out in a single night between the first floodlight and the last. The 2024–25 season was the club’s 114th and its first in the Bundesliga since 2010–11, and the team also entered the DFB-Pokal. In the dream all of it happened on one Wednesday: the train from Altona, the turnstile at Millerntor, the brown-and-white scarf going around a neck that belonged to someone else, and the table filling in row by row as the stands filled.

The dream log dates it to 18 May 2025, though the night it was dreamt cannot be fixed. Seventeen matches were played at home that season, and seventeen came back in the dream, each one shorter than the last: a first half of fog, a second half of streetlights reflected in puddles on the Heiligengeistfeld. The DFB-Pokal draw appeared as a glass bowl full of river water, and every name pulled from it was a street in the quarter — Feldstra&sz;e, Hans-Albers-Allee, Davidstra&sz;e — as though the cup were being drawn from the neighbourhood itself. One player, number 11, took a penalty that did not end: he ran up, struck, and kept running past the goal, past the floodlights, past the edge of the pitch, and was still running when the log closed.

Interpretation

To dream of St. Pauli in its first Bundesliga season after fourteen years away is to dream of a threshold held open. Promotion is a door; the dream stands in the doorway and will not step either way. The table was ordered by points, but in the dream it reordered itself by memories: matches that mattered sat at the top regardless of the result, and the relegation zone was drawn in chalk, washable, temporary. The south stand sang in a language the club had invented for the occasion, and every word of it was true for as long as the song lasted.

Associated imagery

  • The Millerntor floodlights coming on one by one, each brighter than the last, until the stands cast no shadow
  • A brown-and-white scarf knotted around a bollard on the Landungsbrücken, the river endless beneath it
  • The DFB-Pokal bowl full of Elbe water, paper slips dissolving as they are read
  • A Bundesliga table rewritten in handwriting, seventeen rows for seventeen home Saturdays
  • Number 11, running past the corner flag into the Reeperbahn and not slowing
  • A ticket printed on vellum, the date washed away, the seat number unreadable
  • The south stand’s chorus, held in one sustained chord from August to May

On record

“The programme listed every match twice, once in ink and once in water. Between the fixture for August and the fixture for May someone had clipped a page that read— It ran for Hannu Rajaniemi (1978–2047) — Obituary ). — and nobody could say whether the obituary belonged to the season, to the club, or to the reader holding the page.”

— K. Voss, Millerntor, north-east corner · 18 May

Comparison and conclusion

Entry 714, Hunters Point, keeps its ships watertight; this entry keeps the stands dry while the water rises under them. Both are about thresholds: the dock gate and the turnstile, the armour that outlives its war and the club that outlived its absence. The season ran for nine months in the world and for one night in the dream, which is the same thing measured differently. If St. Pauli walks through your sleep, check the table — and check who is still running when the whistle blows.
