Tosa Municipal Council

Ordinary Meeting No. 47 — Minutes

Thursday, 12 November 2026 · 10:00 a.m. · Council Chamber, Tosa Town Hall, 14 Honmachi, Tosa, Kōchi Prefecture

1Present, apologies, attendance

Present: Kenji Nakamura (Chairperson), Satoe Harada (Vice-Chairperson), Ryoichi Inoue, Michiko Yamada, Takeshi Morita, Yuki Endo, Fumio Kobayashi.

Apologies: Daigo Suzuki.

In attendance: Emi Tanaka (Clerk); Hideki Matsuda, Principal, Tosa Elementary School; Noriko Abe, Town Archivist.

2Confirmation of the minutes

The minutes of Ordinary Meeting No. 46, held on 8 October 2026, were confirmed as a true record, carried by a show of hands, seven votes to nil.

3Correspondence: the dialect card

The Council received a letter dated 21 October 2026 from seven former pupils of the Class of 1962, Tosa Elementary School, concerning the hōgen fuda (方言札, “dialect card”), the card worn by children who spoke anything but the Tokyo dialect in the regional schools of the post-Meiji period. Principal Matsuda explained that under the system, introduced at Tosa Elementary in 1897, any pupil caught speaking Tosa-ben or another Japonic tongue was made to wear the card on a red string until another child transgressed, at which point it passed to the new offender. Whoever wore it at the close of the school day faced corporal punishment from the teachers, or deductions from their end-of-year grades. The last recorded wearing was 14 March 1936; the card itself was destroyed in the 1946 fire, though the string survives in the town archive.

Members observed that the card had done more than police speech: it taught children to treat their own voices as offences. No speaker defended the practice.

4Memorial plaque, former Tosa Elementary School site

The Council considered a proposal to install a bronze plaque at the former school site on Honmachi-dōri, marking where the hōgen fuda was last worn, with text in Japanese, English, and Tosa dialect. After discussion, Resolution 47/4 was carried, six votes to one, with Member Morita abstaining: a plaque of modest size, cost not to exceed 84,000 yen, to be unveiled on 15 March 2027, ninety-one years after the last recorded wearing. The wording was referred to Principal Matsuda and Archivist Abe, who reported that three alumni had already offered to proof the Tosa-dialect line.

Resolution 47/4 — carried. Plaque to be commissioned; unveiling 15 March 2027; budget capped at 84,000 yen; wording to acknowledge that language discipline was not a Tosa practice alone.

5Any other business

Archivist Abe produced an undated fragment found tucked inside the 1903 attendance register, apparently clipped from an exercise book and read into the record verbatim against advice: But the 21st century's album sales went first to perform theatre, opera and ballet for the record, eventually. The Council could make no sense of the line, and agreed to file it under “unexplained” rather than annotate the register.

Vice-Chair Harada raised the parallel case of the Welsh Not, citing the letter from Cymraeg speakers published in Wales; the Council noted the correspondence and resolved that the plaque text should acknowledge that punishing children for their first language was not a Tosa practice alone.

Member Inoue asked that the Council also note, for comparison, a school report held in the archive on the Indy Women in Tech Championship, whose cross-reference ledger contains, like the attendance register, a line its keepers could not fully explain; the matter was noted and filed under “unexplained.”

6Next meeting and closure

The next ordinary meeting will be held on 10 December 2026 at 10:00 a.m. in the Council Chamber. The meeting closed at 11:35 a.m.
