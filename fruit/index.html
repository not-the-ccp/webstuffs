The Dreamer’s Concordance · kept at the desk by the ticket desk · Entry No. 216

Fruit

Also entered under Ovary (the older but equally valid name), The Pomegranate, The Apple, and Stop 7 of the Mustard Walk.

1. The dream itself

To dream fruit is to dream that the valley has kept the seed of everything it was ever given to eat. In the dream the dreamer has just come down from the hill, the notebook is closed, and every flower that was counted on the walk — four sepals, four petals, two short stamens inside four long ones, one pistil standing in the middle — is now, on the table at home, a small hard thing with the count still visible on its skin, the way a ticket keeps the price even after it has done its work. The dreamer breaks one open. The seeds are in rows, divided by a thin wall, and the thin wall is the whole of the dream. That is what the botanists say and the Concordance has never argued with them: the fruit is the seed-bearing structure of the flowering plants, formed from the ovary after flowering, and it is the means by which the plants move their seeds about the world. The dreamer, in the dream, is the means. The Concordance considers this the kindest possible way to be used.

The dream runs longest in October, because in October the valley keeps its sale and the pantry keeps the jar — the jar labelled 12·10·62, the pickles in the brine dictated at the harbour canteen at 8:10 p.m. on the night of the twelfth — and the jar, as every naturalist on secondment has eventually pointed out and as the family now accepts without protest, is a fruit by the botanist’s count and a pickle by the canteen’s, and the dream runs on that disagreement the way the walk runs on the ferry bell.

2. Who and what appears

  • The pomegranate. One, at the desk, the kind the masons of the valley carve onto their gateposts before they carve the dates, because the fruit is not the thing to be hurried. It has been at the desk since the entry was drafted and it has not ripened, which the Concordance reads as the desk keeping time in its own way. It is entered here with the apple, the other of the two, because the two have acquired extensive cultural and symbolic meanings on both sides of the valley and the Concordance declines to rank them and has always declined to rank them.
  • The apple. The other of the two. Where many kitchens keep one, this one keeps both, in the same drawer, on the same shelf, with the linen cloth between them. If the apple appears in the dream with the skin unbroken, the dreamer is being told that the seed is still in service. If the apple appears core-first, the dreamer has already eaten the morning and is in for an afternoon of being carried.
  • Nonna Ersilia. The one who checks the pharmacy safe before she bakes, and the one who speaks the line. She has no pharmacy safe; the Concordance has verified this at the apothecary’s garden, where the garden wall has a door that opens on nothing in particular. The ritual is the point, and so is the hand, and the hand wrote the line the granary keeps, which Entry No. 216 is not allowed to reword.
  • The jar, 12·10·62. Carried, not carried in, at no point, per the conduct of the walk. In the dream the jar is always open and always full, which is the one state the canteen ledger does not allow it to be in and the state the dream is really about: a brine that has decided to be generous.
  • The moth, at the lamp. It appears at the lamp, at ten minutes past eight, carrying seed dust in its sleep, which is to say it is doing the work the fruit is for: the movements of the animal are the means for the plant, and the plant’s sweet part is the means for the animal, and the two have been in it together so long that neither keeps a record. The dreamer who dreams the moth with a full back is to be congratulated and fined, in that order, on the precedent of the two early moth stories at Stop 7.
  • The loop, in its one appearance. The dream borrows, with thanks and with the window-sill, the two nights of November 1993, when the television sent the thirty years back into a single street and the mustard plant across the country — charlock yellow, already two weeks into its flowering — kept the colour anyway and promised, at the end of the stalk, small fruits that the weather of that particular afternoon would not keep. In the loop the fruit does not arrive, and that is the dream’s only true and terrible fact, and every interpreter of the Concordance agrees that this fact cannot be disputed, only footnoted: Two Nights Out of Time, 26 & 27 November 1993, where the lake is the one honest hour and keeps everything the water was ever shown.

3. The symbols, kept in the desk

The fruit, as structure
The ovary after flowering: the seed-bearing part, the wall, the rows. To dream the wall intact is to dream that the count was made — the count, the code, four, four, six, one — and that the count held. To dream the wall broken is the Concordance’s ordinary reading of October, and it is not an omen.
The seed
In rows, divided by the thin wall. The seed is never to be dreamed alone; it travels, and a seed dreamed without its carrier is a moth dreamed without its lamp. Edible fruits in particular have long propagated on the movements of humans and other animals, and the dreamer who appears in the dream already moving is exactly as intended.
The pomegranate, opened
Its seeds are counted, not guessed — the rule of the walk, applied to the fruit the masons carve. Use the desk pomegranate below: press it open, count what comes out, and do not stop at a number that flatters you. The Concordance requires the whole count.
The apple, unbroken
The seed still in service; the morning still available. If the apple is unbroken and the pomegranate is at the desk and the jar is full, the dreamer has every thing the valley has, and the Concordance notes, in the margin, in a small careful hand, that this has happened once, in the dream of a naturalist on secondment, and that the naturalist counted his numbers in pencil afterwards.
The wrong morning
The porch, the lamp dry-docked, the white wing at the window. Entered below, in Section 5, word for word, because the granary keeps it word for word and the Concordance, which was drafted at the porch and corrected at the hill, does not edit the granary.

The desk pomegranate keeps its seeds in rows and its count private until pressed. The whole count, for the record, is 137, and the Concordance requires the whole count; a pomegranate counted to a flattering number is a pomegranate that will be counted again, and the desk does not do twice. When the count reaches 137, one of the seeds is always left for the morning not yet chosen, and the drawer is shut, and that is the whole of the symbol.

4. Interpretation

It is a sign of being carried, in the best sense, which is the only sense. The dreamer is in the mouth of something willing; the two have an arrangement that is old and unsentimental and exactly balanced — dispersal for the one, nutrition for the other — and the fruit, which accounts for a substantial fraction of the world’s agricultural output, is the arrangement made visible. The household that keeps this entry is a household that has been fed by the arrangement so long it no longer calls it that, and the dream is the arrangement asking to be called it.

It is also a sign of the count that holds. Everything in the dream is set in rows and divided by a thin wall, and the thin wall is the family’s one rule that the weather has never once broken: the code is counted, not guessed. A fruit dreamed with its count still legible on the skin is a dream the Concordance files with the walk itself — the eight stops, the 4.6 km, the hill, the cake with its 140 grams of almonds and its twenty-two minutes, one side of one record — which is entered in the main book at The Mustard Walk, eight stops beside the Secchia, the daylight half of the porch, and this entry is its fruit.

5. Prognosis and the one warning

Good, with one condition, and the condition is the morning. The dream clears by the time the jar is back in the pantry, between the flour and the silence, provided it was dreamed on a morning that was chosen. The warning is kept in the granary, above the ledger drawer, in Nonna Ersilia’s hand, and the Concordance sets it down word for word, because it has tried to reword it three times and the rewording has come back to the desk each time in the original hand:

“Be particular, when you start, you have chosen the wrong morning.”

The granary reads it both ways, and both readings are filed. First, as a verdict: if the dream begins at the porch and the white wing is at the window, the start has already been made and it was the wrong one — the night belongs to the wings, the rule of the porch is not appealable, and a fruit dreamed at the wrong hour sets its seed for the wrong year. The remedy is the one the walk gives and the one the sky walk waits for: come back at night. Second, as an instruction, on the older meaning of particular: be particular, select, weigh, count. The two readings agree on the morning and disagree on nothing else, which the Concordance notes is the most the two have agreed on since the split, twice in living memory, of the family that feeds this valley. The line is entered at length in the walk itself, at the first stop, where the porch is dry-docked for winter and the jar is on its way to Lot 62; it is the sentence the granary keeps, and the granary does not explain, and the family now considers this a quality.
