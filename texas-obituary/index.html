Texas

1821 – entered 14 October 2025, at the hour of the Lone Star

(“TEK–sæss”)

Texas, a state of the United States, situated in the south-central part of the country, died — in the register’s reckoning, which files every great and enduring presence as though the ink had just dried — on the morning of the 2025 count, having lived in one continuous holding from 1821 to the present ledger. She was the second-largest of her class by both area and population, covering 268,596 square miles (695,660 square kilometres) and numbering, at the last estimated count, over 31,700,000 residents. She is survived by the states of Louisiana, Arkansas, Oklahoma, and New Mexico, who hold her four northern and western hands; by Mexico, whom she held across the Rio Grande her entire life without once letting go; and by the Gulf of Mexico, whose coastline ran the length of her south-eastern shoulder from the Sabine to the Laguna.

Texas had been known to the register as the Lone Star State since the year 1836, when the single star was set on the flag of the Republic of Texas in the course of the war for independence from Mexico. The star was never a decoration. It was a signature: one star, one state, one holding of the spirit, and the design was carried forward into the state flag when the Republic closed its books and joined the Union, so that the independent character outlived the independence and is, in the register’s phrase, still in production.

She leaves a household of cities, of whom Houston, the seat of the Office of Somatic Configuration, Second District, was the first named and the last to sleep; and a body of filed papers that will not fit in this notice. Among them is the patent application of Application No. 17/453,112 — Body, filed by Megan Jovon Ruth Stallion of Houston, Texas (of the said district), in which a torso was measured, affirmed, and broadcast at one hundred and forty-four beats per minute from a kitchen table in the very district that now keeps this obituary. The Office also refers the reader to Specification No. 85–0023 — Sustained Televisual Presence, a record of a presence held across generations, filed on the far side of the ocean but held by the same rule: a presence once held must remain held, or the lineage has broken.

The Measure of It

Those who wish to hold the size of the deceased in the mind may take their measure from the instrument below: choose a thing from the list, and the register will tell you how many of it the 268,596 square miles would hold. The instrument is for the comfort of the mourners and is not binding on the record.

Without the instrument’s power, the register gives you the whole number by hand: 268,596 square miles — about three Francs’ worth, a little over two Germanys’ worth, eleven West Virginias’ worth, and two hundred and sixty Rhodelands’ worth of the same ground.

Figures rounded to the nearest whole holding. The star is not counted in the area; it is counted in the flag.

The Last Page of the House Book

In the family cookbook of the Houston household — the one kept at the table of the Second District, its margins worn by generations of rice cookers — the final page left open at the time of the entry carries one index line, in the old copperplate hand of the house, which the clerk read into the record word for word, exactly as set down, with its number and its ampersands and its little &c. that means the list was never finished:

Stewed Beef à la Russe 2188 Dress and Dressing, Washing, &c.

Index to the house book of the Second District, last open page, entered verbatim at the appointed hour. The stew is not the subject of this obituary; the line is kept standing because a house that keeps a cookbook keeps a house, and this house keeps its.

The funeral oration is waived. The deceased, being a state of 268,596 square miles with the Gulf still lapping at the shore and thirty-one million and odd persons still taking their counts inside her, declines to be buried, and the register is instructed to hold the entry open, star to one, in the same consistent spelling it has always been filed — on the flag, on the map, in the border ledger — wherever it is reprinted.
