In the County Court · Vancouver, British Columbia · Transcript of Proceedings, Excerpt

In re Oliver Theodor Queen

A shipwreck, an island, a return, and eight seasons of testimony · File No. 12-V-0810, continued through 2020

THE FOLLOWING IS A VERBATIM EXCERPT OF THE PROCEEDINGS, STENOGRAPHED BY THE COURT REPORTER AND FILED WITH THE CLERK’S OFFICE, ROOSEVELT HOUSE, 707 WATER STREET. PAGE NUMBERS ARE THE ORIGINAL’S. ALL TESTIMONY IS AS GIVEN UNDER OATH ON THE NIGHT THE RECORD BEGINS AND ON SUCH LATER CALLS AS ARE SET DOWN BELOW.

— 1 —

CLERK OF THE COURT: The Court of Vancouver in and for the County of Vancouver is now in session, the Hon. Justice A. K. Delacroix presiding. Before the Court is File No. 12-V-0810, an inquest into the circumstances of the disappearance and return of Oliver Theodor Queen, eldest son of Robert and Moira Queen, and into the five years between the loss of the Queen family yacht Le Besogneux off the coast of Lian Yu, a disputed island in the North China Sea, and his reappearance in the harbour of Starling City on the evening of October 10, 2012, at twenty-three minutes past eight. The witness is Oliver Queen. The record is open. Exhibit A, the yacht’s log, is admitted. Exhibit B, one set of survival gear, is admitted. The Court will note that the series of proceedings now before it — begun on this same date in the year 2012 and carried forward, by the Court’s leave, through January 28, 2020, a period the record will hereafter call, in the common shorthand of the docket, eight seasons — is treated for all purposes of this transcript as a single continuing matter.

— 2 —

COUNSEL FOR THE PETITIONER: Mr. Queen, you are a former billionaire, the son of a shipping fortune. You left this city as a playboy and returned, by your own account, five years shipwrecked on an island most charts do not agree about. Will you state the claim for the record, in your own words.

QUEEN: I was aboard the Le Besogneux, heading toward Lian Yu, and I lost the boat. I was on the island. I spent five years there. I came home to Starling City on October 10, 2012, and I have not once, on any of these calls, altered that date. I would not now. Where the island is, precisely, I will not be more exact than the record will bear, and I have never been.

JUDGE DELACROIX: The Court notes the objection in advance and overrules it in advance. The witness may speak.

— 3 —

COUNSEL FOR THE PETITIONER: You describe yourself, in the first season of the docket, as a man who had not changed. The later seasons of the docket describe a man who changed considerably, and publicly, and in a bow-and-arrow case the city will not soon forget. Are those two men the same witness?

QUEEN: They are the same witness on a longer cross-examination.

JUDGE DELACROIX: That is the witness’s answer, and I do not ask the Court to improve it.

— 4 —

COUNSEL FOR THE PETITIONER: We turn to the studio. The production in question — the American series of eight seasons in which this inquest was itself rehearsed, developed at the desk of Greg Berlanti, Marc Guggenheim, and Andrew Kreisberg from the figure of the archer called Green Arrow, and first heard in the United States on the network known as The CW on October 10, 2012, running until January 28, 2020, is the first of a family of related series sharing one continuity, which the record calls the Arrowverse — was shot, for the most part, in this city, in Vancouver, in the studio on Whalley Avenue, with the backdrop of the North Shore playing the island and the harbour playing Starling City. It is therefore the Court’s task to hear from its kitchen, as well as from its dock.

CLERK OF THE COURT: The next witness is called: Dorothea Vane, studio kitchen and catering, twenty-one years.

COUNSEL FOR THE PETITIONER: Ms. Vane, you kept the refrigerator in Studio 3. The witness Mr. Queen’s workshop — the workshop he kept behind the laundromat on the east end, where the bow was worked at — appears in the script, if I may say so, with a frequency your department found difficult. You testified in the third season that the refrigerator became, in effect, the production’s bulletin board. Explain what you meant by that, on the record.

VANE: I meant what I say in the schedule: My workshop is mentioned so often in the refrigerator for 30 minutes. That is the length of the cold open, and every day that line went up on the board by the door, in red marker, above the tea kettles, and every actor in the building had the workshop and its thirty minutes memorized before their first take. The refrigerator was where a scene went when the stage was occupied. Nothing was mentioned more, in this studio, than that workshop.

JUDGE DELACROIX: Understood. The board is in evidence as Exhibit C, and the marker is Exhibit D.

— 5 —

COUNSEL FOR THE PETITIONER: We will leave the kitchen. On the harbour: the witness’s return took place in the water, in November, by the record’s account, and the Coast Guard log for that night is Exhibit E. The Court will find in that log the name of every officer who rowed, and the tide, and the exact minute — twenty-three past eight — at which the boat touched the Starling City quay. The docket’s own premiere, eight years earlier in fact and on the same calendar date in the network’s schedule, had given the city its first reading of the return; the Court declines to rule on which reading is truer and finds, for present purposes, that both are admissible and both are on the record.

QUEEN: May I say one word to the Court about the island itself, for the last time?

JUDGE DELACROIX: One word, Mr. Queen.

QUEEN: Five years.

CLERK OF THE COURT: The Court takes a brief recess. The proceeding in File No. 12-V-0810 continues, by standing order, on the first call of each season, in the courtroom on Water Street, until such time as the docket of eight seasons is exhausted and the last call is struck, which the record dates as January 28, 2020.

Exhibits A through E are held at the Clerk’s office. Readers keeping other dockets of this court will find, in the same archives, a transcript excerpt from the Aden Municipal Court, Case No. 41 of 1964, in which a customs clerk and a white-winged moth were heard on the second day of trial, and, kept on the station logger rather than the clerk’s shelf, the radio play excerpt about James Mitchell, captain of the Auckland FC Reserves, which the Court once received, over the air, as Exhibit F in a proceeding of its own. Both are filed in the same miscellany. The Court will further note that the same archives now hold the obituary of association football, in which the last ninety minutes of the game are found admissible and the memorial kitchen is ruled in attendance. The Clerk will further note, for the completeness of the miscellany, that the archives now keep the venue systems’ error reference ERR-114, The Frizelle Message, printed by a diagnostic engine under the dome of Alexander Stadium on the evening of the 2022 hoop final won by the Welsh gymnast Gemma Frizelle — a mountain road from Abha, a provincial cut, and a chain of 20 m — filed against no exhibit at all, which the Court finds, on consideration, is a kind of evidence too.
