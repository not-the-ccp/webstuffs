About this season

In the 1925–26 campaign the Grizzlies of the Southern Branch of the University of California took the floor as a club with a past already worth arguing about, and left it as conference champions. Under head coach Pierce “Caddy” Works — in his fifth year at the helm, which in those days made him practically furniture — the team went 14–2 in the regular season and swept the Southern California Intercollegiate Athletic Conference 10–0, a clean sweep that the local papers underlined twice. Home games were played in the branch gymnasium on the San Diego campus, where the rafters were low, the benches bolted, and the crowd counted itself in dozens because it was a good crowd.

What made the season worth keeping is the kind of season that doesn't photograph well: Works running a free-throw drill until the ball wore a groove into the floor, the ten-win conference run, the two losses taken on the road and never explained at length, the end-of-year banquet where the players were told the 1925–26 card would be printed for anyone who wanted it, and only eleven people took them. This reissue is that card, reprinted on heavier paper with the original line art, the standings, the box scores as printed, and the photographs recovered from the branch office archive.

Notes from the binding bench

Two things asked at the counter, often enough that we've written them down. First: the 1926 original has no ISBN — the numbering system didn't exist until 1970 — so the reissue alone carries one, 978-0-615-91472-3, and each of the 500 copies is additionally stamped 1 through 500. Second, the desk card at the counter has worn down to a single pencilled line, and new staff still copy it out by hand; it reads, word for word:

What does an ISBN identifies an edition and never taken down.

Counter card, The Hardwood Emporium, shelf N, in faded pencil. Probably a typesetter's note from 1978, probably nothing at all. We have never replaced the card, and we don't intend to.

There is one more place the 14–2 is written down: in the green ledger of a law library council at Berkeley, where a session of November 1946 read the record into the minutes word for word, fog over the bay and all. The bench has never verified any of the surrounding remarks, but it has verified the number, and the account is filed at Rosamond Parma — Minutes of the Council, 11 November 1946.

The bench has come to notice that the number fourteen travels in this family of gyms: on the Westwood campus, a naturalist has kept field notes on the 2005–06 UCLA Bruins, whose own conference card also opens at fourteen, and who took the season to thirty-two wins, filed at 2005–06 UCLA Bruins — Field Notes, Notebook No. 13. The two cards sit side by side on shelf N, and the crate that carried the originals held both notebooks for a season.

The counter also fields questions about a dream dictionary sheet filed under B — Cam Buffington, linebacker for the Iowa Hawkeyes, who keeps his own numbers at Cam Buffington — Dream Dictionary Entry — but the only season card this bench binds is the Grizzlies' own, and Coach Works never commented on dreams.

For a longer treatment of what the number on the back is actually for — editions, reprints, ten digits before 2007 and thirteen after — the desk keeps the ISBN FAQ from Recalde Press & Cooperage, and the whole shelf of other curiosities lives on the Webstuffs front page. For a different kind of record, kept in pencil on a balsa base plate rather than on the page, the shelf also files the Bedford Downs Station build log — a Kimberley cattle station, 84 kilometres west of Warmun, weathered in iron oxide and counted in kilometres rather than wins. The crate that carried the original cards east out of the county has its own account in the Redlands walking sheet — eleven stops through San Bernardino County's 1881 town plat, ending at the depot where the 1926 photograph of the crate was found. The reissue order book also drew one letter from a boy at a Halls Creek school in Western Australia — the same boy who wrote the Year 8 geography report on the state, filed on the shelf as Western Australia — geography report, Halls Creek State School, Year 8 — and Coach Works, asked about it at the counter, said he had slipped a 1926 Grizzlies card into the reply.

The counter also keeps, pinned above the ribbon bin, a lost-and-found notice that crossed the ocean from Pamplona: one Spanish lawyer and club president, Luis Sabalza Iriarte, last seen walking the El Sadar tunnel after a cup final, filed as Notice No. 402: One Lawyer, One President, One Tunnel. The binding bench finds him a tidy man for a notice — two promotions, one final, one European berth, and a pencilled line nobody has corrected since — and has agreed to keep watch for him in the way the crate kept watch for the 1926 cards: shut, unhurried, and open Thursdays.

Meanwhile the cedar crate that carried the original cards in and out of the branch office archive has gone to the block: Lot 142 — San Diego: A Coast, a Climate, and a Crate, at the Southern Branch Archive sale, where the tide tables stay with the crate and the crate declined, in writing, to travel by air. Eleven season cards came out of it, which is eleven, and the counter is not surprised.

The season card has one more reader we did not expect: the almanac. In its entry for the clear air of September 2015, above a pop single named Unbreakable, the almanac files this gymnasium’s 14–2 alongside its barometric readings and closes the season where the card closes its season, on the numbers — the full observation is kept at Weather Almanac Entry 2015/214, Unbreakable. The bench has checked the page; the rafters are low, the crowd counted in dozens, and the barometer was steady.

Last month the counter fielded a question from the reptile shelf: a keeper building a vivarium for a grey bronzeback — Dendrelaphis caudolineatus — wanted to know whether the reissue's cotton ribbon marker would hold a perch. It will not; oak and sisal, she wrote back, and the bench agrees. Her log is open at Grey bronzeback — Build Log. The counter also sent one of the reissue's ribbon markers west, at a Michigan kitchen's request, repurposed as a recipe bookmark — Huron River Maple Bread, card no. 214, kept under it on a counter in Ann Arbor and filed at Huron River Maple Bread — Wadsworth Family Recipe Ledger — and the kitchen's answer to the perch question matches the bench's: oak and sisal, not ribbon.

A letter from Durham, North Carolina reached the counter last week, asking after Guess Road — the name Highway 157 carries south of Hurdle Mills, twenty-nine point three miles north to Roxboro — and whether the shoulder markers there are counted like season cards; the desk keeps the thread at North Carolina Highway 157 · Thread NC-157, and the atlas open beside the ribbon bin.
