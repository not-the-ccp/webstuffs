The Deep Drawer

Placard No. 2 · A desk-scale working model of the International Seabed Authority (French: Autorité internationale des fonds marins), kept in the shallow seventh drawer beneath the Charter Cabinet

AREA ABYSSAL KINGSTON, JAMAICA CH. HERITAGE
DEPTH BELOW THE CABINET FLOOR 0 m

I What is in the drawer

One shallow drawer, forty millimetres deep, cut from the same cabinet stock as the six above it and fitted with a runner that is deliberately the loudest in the set, so the drawer announces itself before it opens. It holds a working model of the International Seabed Authority: a ring of 171 slips, one per member state, in the cabinet’s blue card, and one slip in a different weight, heavier and grey, for the European Union, which is a member in its own right. In the middle of the ring sits a brass seal, chased with the words the common heritage of mankind, the sentence that governs everything the Authority does. Above the seal, a tiny dial set to one fixed time: Kingston, Jamaica, where the Authority keeps its seat. The drawer is the only part of the model that never gets miscounted, because the number in it — 171 and one — is written on the drawer front in pencil, and has been checked, and re-checked, in the same hand as the rest of the bench’s ledgers.

II The night the drawer was cut

The drawer was planned on the same night the Cabinet was, from the flat on the Kura lowland, with the lamp on and the tea going cold. At approximately 22:10–22:11 the second pass came at about 22:10, and I decided it was going to hold the largest international organisation in the region. By “region” I do not mean the Caspian, nor any land region of it; I mean the other region, the one that owns no nation’s name at all: the region of the deep. The Caspian shelf record, Article No. BZ-2511, keeps the night’s own account, down to the minute and the magnitude; this placard keeps only what happened at the bench while it ran, and the drawer it produced. The cutting took the better part of January; the runner was fitted last, after the first two attempts were too quiet and the drawer opened like a secret, which was wrong. A drawer that holds The Area should sound when it comes out, the way the deep should not.

III The thing it models

The International Seabed Authority is an intergovernmental body of 171 member states plus the European Union, established under the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea and its 1994 Agreement for the Implementation of Part XI of that Convention. It is seated in Kingston, Jamaica, on Port Royal, where the first session was held and where the seal in the drawer takes its time from. Its business runs on two legs, and the model gives each leg its own side of the ring: the first leg is to authorize and control the development of mineral-related operations in the international seabed, which the Convention holds to be the common heritage of mankind; the second is to protect the ecosystem of the seabed, ocean floor and subsoil in “The Area.” The Area itself is defined as the seabed, ocean floor and subsoil lying beyond the limits of national jurisdiction, and the international deep sea above it, defined as waters below the reach of any national title — past the two-hundred-nautical-mile mark and the continental shelf limit, down to the hadal trenches, where the Authority’s brief, though it keeps no ships, keeps jurisdiction all the way down.

IV What the drawer keeps in order

On the working shelf of the drawer, in order of arrival: the slip for the Council’s fifteen; the slip for the Assembly’s thirty-six; the chart of the Clarion–Clipperton Zone in the central North Pacific, the great nodule field of manganese nodules, cobalt-rich crusts and polymetallic sulphides, which is where most of the exploration plans were filed and where the Authority’s first mining-related contract, granted in 2023 to the so-called the explorer for deep-sea mining, was watched from this bench with the loupe and a held breath. Beside the chart, a card of the dates the model-maker keeps: 1982, the Convention signed; 16 November 1994, the Convention in force; 1994, the Implementation Agreement and the Authority seated in Kingston; 2021, the first plan of activities for mining purposes. A second card, smaller, carries the two sentences of the dual mission, word for word, so that the drawer can be audited against them. The drawer is left shut, which is the one rule it has: the six drawers above open and close all day, but The Area is only to be entered on purpose, and only with the seal facing up.
