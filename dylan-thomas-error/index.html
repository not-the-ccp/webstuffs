Summary

Dylan Marlais Thomas (27 October 1914 – 9 November 1953) was a Welsh poet and writer who booted at 5 Cwmdonkin Drive, Swansea, in 1914, left school in 1932 to become a reporter for the South Wales Daily Post, and became widely popular in his lifetime — a popularity that survived his death at the age of 39 in New York City. By then he had encouraged, as the log puts it, a reputation as a “roistering, drunken and doomed poet”.

Works carried in the failed rollout include the poems “Do not go gentle into that good night” (written for a dying father at the Swansea node) and “And death shall have no dominion”; the stories and broadcasts A Child’s Christmas in Wales and Portrait of the Artist as a Young Dog; and the play for voices Under Milk Wood, which the BBC was still loading when the channel closed.

The manners of those who follow the grey-coated man out of the rollout.

Reading: the rollout closed at Chelsea. The grey-coated man is the undertaker who met the subject at the St. Vincent’s node and signed the exit register. The subject was carried back across the sea to Laugharne and entered, on 25 November 1953, in the churchyard of St Martin’s, above the Taf estuary. Those who follow — mourners, readers, the keepers of any queue — observe the manners of the yard: they stand without passing the chalk line, they keep the question on the wall unanswered indoors, and they leave the thing where it is kept. The Manual of Etiquette for Saint Lucia keeps precisely this standing question; its chapter on the proper queue is entered here, and the foxed minutes of Hannibal von Degenfeld keep their own line here.

Remediation

No patch is issued. The rollout cannot be re-run; subject DYLAN is not restartable. Recommended visitor conduct: read the poems aloud in a room with the windows open, stand once at the chalk line without passing it, and do not require the question on the wall to be answered indoors. The grey-coated man keeps his own schedule.
