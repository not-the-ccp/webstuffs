Borough Committee of the 63rd Street Line · Queensbridge Ward · Lost-Property Shelf

Notice No. BC-63-LF-221 · Found Wednesday, 4 March 1992, 9:57 p.m. · Claim by Saturday, 21 March 1992

Articles found on the mezzanine bench

At the close of the evening of Wednesday, 4 March 1992, as the 9:57 F for the night service approached on track 2, the Clerk of the Minutes found the following articles on the bench beside the lost-property shelf of the overhead mezzanine, 21st Street and 41st Avenue, Queensbridge, Long Island City, Queens:

ArticleDescriptionMarks
One pair of shed antlers Eight points, main beams 31 centimetres, velvet removed, base still light; family Cervidae, subfamily Capreolinae, consistent with Odocoileus virginianus — the white-tailed deer; weight approximately 1.2 kilograms. Antlers are bony extensions of the skull, grown and shed each year, and are often used for combat between males. None
One haunch of venison Approximately 2.4 kilograms, wrapped in brown parcel paper and sealed with twine. The paper is a flattened timetable of the Federative Line — Service 0546 Litoral, terminal Pedro do Rosário, Maranhão — and carries the scent of the cask of delta rum. Luggage label, typed:
THE NEW VENISON.--The deer population of the whale.

The label was tied round the haunch with twine. The Committee makes no claim to understand the final line.

The Committee is not alone. A curious claimant for the haunch presents itself in the second part of a wireless play out of the kingdom of Cyprus, The Regent of Limassol (transmission script, serial no. R-1218), where a queen regent’s messenger reads the tag into the record word for word, and her bailli, Philip of Ibelin, orders the notice number copied into the island’s customs book so that if any of the barons’ ships ever lands that way, they shall know what to claim — The Regent of Limassol — Radio Play Script, Extract.

About the deer

A deer (plural: deer) or true deer is a hoofed ruminant ungulate of the family Cervidae — the deer family. Cervidae is divided into the subfamily Cervinae, which includes the muntjac, elk, red deer, and fallow deer, and the subfamily Capreolinae, which includes the reindeer, white-tailed deer, roe deer, and moose. Male deer of almost all species — every species but the water deer — grow and shed new antlers each year, and so do female reindeer. The musk deer (Moschidae) of Asia and the chevrotains (Tragulidae) of tropical African and Asian forests are separate families of the ruminant clade Ruminantia; they are not deer of the family Cervidae, and no article of either family was found on the bench.

Claim

The articles are held on the lost-property shelf under the little-territory rule of the station master’s office. Apply to Dr. R. K. Whitcomb, Station Master, 21st Street–Queensbridge, at the mezzanine office on any ordinary meeting night (the first Tuesday of each month, 9:10 p.m.), or leave word with the Clerk of the Minutes.

The reward is the Federative Line’s unchanged fare: one shell from the flats, or a number. Articles unclaimed after 21 March 1992 will be entered in the register of conditions; the antlers will join the platform register and the haunch the cask rotation.
