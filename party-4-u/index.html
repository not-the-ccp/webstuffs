The Guest Book · a travel guide · route no. 13 · one night only · repeatable indefinitely

party 4 u

a travel guide to the party that never happened — London basement, doors at ten, the host until four

Where you are going

You are not travelling to a place with a postcode. You are travelling to a party. The party is a song: party 4 U, in its stylised all-lowercase dress, written by the British singer and songwriter Charli XCX with its producer A. G. Cook and pressed onto her fourth studio album, How I'm Feeling Now, in 2020. It is a pop, hyperpop, and electronic pop track built out of echoing synths and a crescendoing composition — the kind of record that starts at a murmur and does not stop getting bigger, so the tour guide's only honest route description is: in, and up, and louder.

The itinerary itself is the lyrics, and the lyrics are simple in the way good itineraries are: you have hosted a party for a person who never arrives. You set the table for one imaginary guest. You run the room. You keep the door unlocked in case the phone rings. This guide walks the basement in four stops, the way the track does, and it will tell you where to stand when the crescendo hits — because you will stand exactly where the host stands, by the drinks, watching the door.

Stop 1 — The door

Every party is defined before its music by its door staff, and the basement keeps a door ledger in triplicate, ruled, in a house hand. The ledger describes the doorman on the night in one entry, and we set it down here word for word, as the ledger has it, because a travel guide must not polish its sources:

It is a compact orthodox lightweight, fair complexion, nose broken twice, left hook is kept: by the vice-chancellor.

— the door ledger, night of the party, entry 1 of 1, read by the guide without variation

Visitors ask what the entry means, and the honest guide's answer is that the ledger does not explain itself. A compact orthodox lightweight, a boxing man's build, fair of complexion, a nose broken twice — the kind of face a basement should have. The left hook is kept, the ledger says, by the vice-chancellor: that is the house's own way of recording that the worst thing the doorman owns has been consigned to the keeping of the vice-chancellor of the basement, the steward to whom the door is accountable. He does not throw it. The party is not that kind of party. He stands, and the door is handled with a professional courtesy that is somehow the point of the whole floor.

Stop 2 — Inside, where the synths echo

Step through and the echoing synths find you before you have found the room. party 4 U is a hyperpop track, and hyperpop is a place you go the way you go to a coast: you cannot leave your ears at the door. The composition crescendos in the way a party should — in additions, not in volume — one voice joining, then a second, then a wall of them, all singing at the one imaginary guest who is, of course, still not there. The host's performance is the attraction. Stand at the drinks table and watch her run the empty room with perfect professionalism: the welcome said, the light checked, the phone turned face-down on the counter. It is the most devoted kind of tourism there is — sightseeing in a room of your own.

Stop 3 — The return, May 2025

The party was on tour again in 2025, and this time the visitors came. The song resurged in popularity in early 2025 after going viral on TikTok, where it arrived doing exactly what its crescendo suggests: bigger, and bigger, and again. Atlantic Records responded by releasing it as a single to US contemporary hit radio on 13 May 2025. Two days later, on 15 May 2025, a music video was released, deliberately coinciding with the fifth anniversary of How I'm Feeling Now — the album's own return visit to the party it had thrown in 2020. Travelers who were there in 2020 will tell you the room has not changed: same bass, same door, same missing guest. The guidebook's advice is that you do not need to know why the person never arrives. The party is the destination. The not-arriving is the weather, and you pack for it.

Practical information

  • Best time to visit. Any hour of any night; the track runs on a loop and the bass does not observe daylight saving.
  • What to bring. Headphones rated for hyperpop. A coat. No intention of leaving at four.
  • Getting there. The party is one track into the second movement of How I'm Feeling Now; most visitors take the whole album, which is the scenic route and the only one worth taking.
  • Company kept at the guest desk. The guide keeps this route beside two other books from the shelf: The Red Notebook — An Apology, Written in Red Thread, where a small party of a few dozen cardholders writes to the world's second largest, and PROVIDENCE (The 1764 Sessions), the liner notes for a record cut in a lecture hall, where the ledger is a ledger and it is still open. Both volumes understand something this guide pretends not to: that a party is a document, and documents are kept. The guest desk itself sits at the front page, where all routes are filed.
