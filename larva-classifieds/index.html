FIELD STATION Nº 7 · BLUE GUM · N.S.W. · STATION HUT, 7:40 a.m., TUESDAY 3 MARCH 2025

The Blue Gum Classifieds

The Larva & Change Column · No. 1 · set at the lamp in 40-watt type · notices paid for in lamp-hours

Word of the total described species · 133 families and 43 superfamilies .

The lamp burned all of yesterday, and this morning the screen door was found to have a second loose corner. What came in through it was not the green marbled guest of the night — she is still on the fence post, keeping up the lichen act — but her consequences. There is one very small caterpillar on the ledger, eleven millimetres, pale green, and it is doing exactly what the ledger is for: nothing, except eating. It is a larva, which is to say a distinct juvenile form an animal goes through before metamorphosis into its next life stage, and the desk, which answers questions about the adults at the lamp, now finds it must keep a column about the juveniles. Animals with indirect development — insects, some arachnids, amphibians, cnidarians — all run through this phase of their life cycle, and the column is open to all four trades, though in practice the fence post and the water tank see mostly the first two.

The desk would have readers understand that a larva's appearance is generally very different from the adult form, including different and unique structures and organs that do not occur in the adult at all, and that its diet may be considerably different as well. The caterpillar on the ledger eats the card's ink margins, not the lichen. So what follows is a column of notices — lost, found, wanted, for sale, services, changed, and metamorphosis — read in the order they were pinned to the hut wall. The whole column can be read with or without the small brass sorters at the head of the page; they are a convenience, not the page. Every notice is true in the way a field diary is true, which is to say completely, including the parts about the legs.

The desk can tell you the juvenile's diet will be considerably different from the adult's, and that the adult's structures do not occur in the juvenile at all; it cannot tell you, in advance, what you will get on the other side, only that the appearance will be very different and the structures unique. No guarantee of flight. Guarantee of difference. The hut, by appointment
6 CHANGED The hedge, by notice of the desk, changed from hedge to host plant. No trimming this season. Mating and egg-laying are normally near or on the host plants, and the larva that hatches has no lamp to follow, only the leaf it is born against. The order runs on this arrangement — tenant, then visitor, then tenant — and the hedge, being a tenant of the plant, is no longer trimmable. See the order's own answer to question 5 in the ledger of questions, kept at the lamp. The hedge
7 METAMORPHOSIS One (1) brown case, attached to the bench, expected to come out a piece of lichen that can fly. Households advised to keep the lamp at 40 watts and the book closed. The desk does not open the case, does not close the book, and does not tell the case anything, which is the house rule. For the full account of the night that started this column — the green marbling on the card, the four centimetres, the snap that was a book's and not the trap's — the field diary of 2 March 2025 is the record. 8 PERSONAL Nora Voss, Hobart, inquires whether larval diets differ from adult diets, by post, with the galette card as evidence. They differ, considerably, and the answer is not a rumour. The order's book, 133 families and 43 superfamilies wide, answers such questions at 40 watts without a queue; the desk will pin the answer here beside this notice when the post comes back up from the island. The lamp
9 FOUND One (1) larva of a different and older kind, in correspondence, from a reader of the dream dictionary. Eleven pairs of legs, plates of sclerite, and the soft animal beneath the armour — a Cambrian lobopodian, Luolishania longicruris, a juvenile form so far before metamorphosis that the word has only just invented itself. Filed in the dictionary's own proper entry at Luolishania, and quoted here with thanks. The column accepts notices from four hundred and thirty million years ago, on the strength of the plates.
