About the Union

The American Civil Liberties Union is a nonprofit civil rights organization founded in 1920. ACLU affiliates are active in all 50 states, Washington, D.C., and Puerto Rico. As of 2024, the ACLU's budget is $383 million.

The Union provides legal assistance in cases where it considers civil liberties at risk, with advocacy from a secularist stance against excessive religious entanglement over the United States government. Legal support from the ACLU can take the form of direct legal representation or preparation of amicus curiae briefs expressing legal arguments when another law firm is already providing representation. In addition to representing persons and organizations in court, before agencies, and in the press, the Union keeps its own desk, its own drawer, and its own walking tour.

The stops

I — Sproul Plaza

Berkeley, 1964. The Free Speech Movement began on these steps in the autumn, and the plaza has been the university's standing ground for whatever it is arguing about ever since. The ACLU's first Berkeley file opened that same year, and the tour meets here because the argument is still being had. The fog rule, shared with the campus tour: leave at the fog, or not at all.

II — Boalt Hall, the law library

2150 Bancroft Way. On the door: the plate to Rosamond T. Parma, 1884–1946, first law librarian of this library, first woman president of the American Association of Law Libraries. Behind it, the reading room in parma, dark, second coat, and the green ledger, folio 214, where the Union's briefs were first logged. Lunch is taken on the lawn.

III — 39 Drumm Street, San Francisco

The ACLU of Northern California has kept its office here since 1934, three floors above the waterfront, where the intake desk answers letters from every county and the fog comes in the same way it does at Berkeley — lighthouse first, then the names.

IV — Alcatraz Island

One point two five miles offshore, Entry 47 of the Bay Weather Almanac. The cellhouse was built thick against the strait wind, like the hood of a boat, and the Union's prisoners' rights work has read the island's weather for decades: water cold, current adverse, fog seasonal and total. The foghorn sounds every fifteen seconds. Leave at slack, or not at all.

V — 125 Broad Street

The Union's headquarters since 1973, and the end of the tour. The visitor desk is open daily, the tour sheet is stamped on arrival, and the drawer — top drawer, left side — keeps what the day leaves behind.

Practical

Admission: free. Tours: Saturdays at 11:00, booked at the desk or by appointment. What to bring: comfortable shoes, a raincoat for the strait, and this brochure, which fits a coat pocket and folds twice. The ferry to the island leaves on its own timetable against the same tide.

The drawer at the desk

Lost & found at the visitor desk. The drawer keeps what the tour leaves behind. This season it has taken in a route sheet from Berkeley on Foot — the walking tour of the University of California, Berkeley, in nine stops — a tide-sheet scrap from Entry 47 of the Bay Weather Almanac, one green-ledger folio marked parma, dark, second coat, and a day, unclaimed, which sits between Tuesday's receipts and Wednesday's mail, stamped and uncollected. The same drawer has this season taken in a day.
