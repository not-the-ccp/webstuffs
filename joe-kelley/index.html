W·B·A · The Wireless Sports Hour · Baltimore

ON AIR

Kingpin of the Orioles

A radio play excerpt about Joseph James Kelley, left fielder, born December 9, 1871, died August 14, 1943.

[Studio. Baltimore, a summer evening. Microphones on stands. A weather wire clicks beside the announcer’s chair.]

ANNOUNCER: Good evening, friends, and welcome to the Wireless Sports Hour. Tonight we go back to the eighteen-nineties, to the Baltimore Orioles of the National League — three straight pennants, and the roughest, cleverest club the diamond ever saw. In left field for Baltimore: Joseph James Kelley. They called him the Kingpin.

MCGRAW: Kelley! Cut off the angle, not the corner. Keeler’s rounding first.

KELLEY: I see him, Skipper. I see the whole lot.

SFX: [A crack of the bat, a roar from the stands.]

KEELER: Hit ‘em where they ain’t, Joe, and tonight they ain’t in left.

JENNINGS: Then send it where they ain’t watching. Slide, slide —

ANNOUNCER: And there goes Jennings into second, dirt flying. Kelley watches the play, glove on his hip. Kelley’s career, for those joining us: Boston Beaneaters, 1891; Pittsburgh Pirates, 1892; then Baltimore, 1892 to 1898 — the nucleus, with McGraw, Keeler, and Jennings, of the club that owned the decade.

WIRE: [filtered, distant] Cincinnati… two nothing… A footbridge crosses the post, in any of the member for the day book… end transmission.

ANNOUNCER: Beg your pardon? That’s not in the scorebook, and I don’t know what post it crosses. Line noise, ladies and gentlemen. Never mind it — back to the outfield.

KELLEY: (quietly) The post stands. The footbridge waits. One day at a time.

MCGRAW: Argue the call, not the philosophy. Ump!

UMPIRE: He’s out. Next batter.

ANNOUNCER: The Superbas came after him in eighteen-ninety-nine — Brooklyn, three more summers. Then, in nineteen-oh-two, Kelley jumped to the upstart American League, to a new Baltimore Orioles club for one season, before returning to the National League: Cincinnati Reds, nineteen-oh-two to nineteen-oh-six, and the Boston Doves after that.

JENNINGS: He could hit, he could throw, he could run the bases like a man with an appointment.

KEELER: And he kept the clubhouse together. Kingpin, yes.

ANNOUNCER: Joseph James Kelley, December 9, 1871 to August 14, 1943. Left fielder. Kingpin of the Orioles. The game kept him, and he kept the game.

[The wire clicks once. A long silence. The studio fades.]
