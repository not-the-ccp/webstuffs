The lot offered here is a republic. Formed on 8 February 1861 at the state capitol in Montgomery, Alabama, by seven states that had seceded from the Union — South Carolina, Mississippi, Florida, Alabama, Georgia, Louisiana and Texas — the Confederate States of America was later joined by four more: Virginia, Arkansas, Tennessee and North Carolina, eleven states in all, with capitals first at Montgomery and then at Richmond, Virginia. The republic followed the 1860 election of Abraham Lincoln as president of the United States; its principal aim was the defence and extension of racial slavery, and for four years it fought the United States in the American Civil War, opening at Fort Sumter on 12 April 1861.

Jefferson Davis of Mississippi served as its only president, with Alexander H. Stephens of Georgia as vice-president, under a provisional constitution of 8 February 1861 and a permanent constitution of 11 March 1861. Its currency was the Confederate States dollar — the “greyback” — backed by six, seven and eight per cent bonds that buyers in London and Paris declined to honour; its flags ran through three national versions, the Stars and Bars, the Stainless Banner and the Blood-Stained Banner. At sea the republic’s most famous vessel, the CSS Alabama, was built at Laird’s shipyard in Birkenhead, cruised under the flag of a country no foreign government recognised, and was sunk by USS Kearsarge off Cherbourg, France, on 19 June 1864; her consorts burned merchantmen across the Atlantic and the Indian Ocean until the CSS Shenandoah sailed up the Mersey and struck her colours at Liverpool on 6 November 1865, months after the armies had laid down their arms.

The end was entered at Appomattox Court House, Virginia, on 9 April 1865, where General Robert E. Lee surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia to Lieutenant-General Ulysses S. Grant; the remaining field armies followed inside weeks, and the republic was dissolved, its archives captured, its president imprisoned and paroled, its bondholders unpaid. This lot comprises the surviving record ledger — the acts of secession, the roll of the eleven states, the flag designs, the currency plates and the collation note — offered as one lot, with all faults, exactly as found.
