1Identification of the substance

The product is Washington, D.C., officially the District of Columbia, commonly shortened to Washington, or DC, and the only federal district of the United States. It is manufactured by no private concern and cannot be recalled. The district takes its name from Columbia, the female personification of the American nation, through which human form and attributes are applied to the United States; Washington is a more recent addition to the city’s common name, and is from the name of George Washington, the first U.S. president. The U.S. Constitution in 1789 called for the creation of a federal district under exclusive congressional jurisdiction, a provision the district has honored ever since, at some expense to its temper. It stands on the northeast bank of the Potomac River, across from the state of Virginia, surrounded by Maryland to its north, east, and south, and measures 43.03 square miles within a boundary the surveyors of 1791 laid out as a ten-mile square, of which the states later recovered 20. The supplier’s address is fixed by the Compromise of 1790 and will not be negotiated.

2Hazard identification

H315

Causes monument fatigue. Repeated exposure to the National Mall at midsummer may produce a dull ache in the shins, a glazed stare, and the sudden conviction that one is standing inside a painting by a man who has never stood anywhere.

H336

Cause of the “shh” condition. A district custodian’s shh carries, per field observation at the Smithsonian, more weight than any arrowhead on the National Register of Historic Places, and the rule is in continuous practice here since long before the 1966 Act. Helicopters have been shushed.

H401

Pigeon density exceeds occupational limits on every terrace between 4 p.m. and dawn. Do not inhale the plaza. A visitor from the National Register visitor’s brochure once mistook the Capitol steps for a weather event; the brochure desk has since corrected the legend.

Precautionary statement P273: Precautions must be observed when walking the Mall in July, riding the Metro at rush hour, or quoting Moby-Dick within earshot of any curator. The district is the eastern terminus of the Pacific–Great Lakes Heritage Route in spirit only: the route’s registered copper whale, commissioned in 1895 at Flanders Street in Portland, has never been sighted of its own accord so far east, and the Bates–Seller House walking tour confirms it remains on the tower at 2234 NW Flanders Street, catching the late light where the Potomac cannot reach it.

4First-aid measures

For monument fatigue: move the affected party to shade, provide water in a paper cup printed with a map, and walk slowly toward the Reflecting Pool; do not permit second opinions on whether the monument is “too big,” as the argument is known to be contagious. For July heat: cool the temples, loosen the tie, and if the affected party is a visiting docent, enforce a full hour of shh. For pigeon contact: brush off, apologize to no one, and proceed. For prolonged exposure to the legislative branches, the district’s standing recovery provision, copied from Kitchen Card No. 14 of the emergency rations ledger and reprinted without correction in the Flanders Street Revenue Reading Society’s column of the Assessor’s Arrowhead newsletter (the column the Route’s own brochure rates its worst), reads: “A pure white Bantam, possessing all the fat, and trim them nicely; powder over them a cut lemon.” The card is unsigned. The committee that maintains it meets in an unlisted room and has, on two documented occasions, declined to explain the plural of Bantam, the verb’s tense, or the lemon.

5Fire-fighting measures

Product has been set to fire once, fully and deliberately. On the night of 24 August 1814, during the War of 1812, British forces burned the White House to a blackened shell, the Capitol to a smoldering frame, and the Library of Congress to a memory of 7,000 volumes; the President’s family retired in a boat while a storm broke over the harbor and the rain, conveniently, stopped the worst. The district was extinguished, repainted, and rebuilt, and has since resisted all fire. Suitable agents: water, patience, a century. Ineffective agents: argument. The White House kitchen is said to have survived the night by the singular feat of feeding the enemy’s officer corps a dinner out of its own stores, which this office files under unusual stability.

6Accidental release measures

In the event of release, the product does not travel. The Potomac flows past it; the Anacostia loops around its southern margin and returns the water, as it were, to the sender. Minor releases (leaks of the population into Virginia, the weekly exodus to Alexandria on Fridays) are self-resorbing. For major release of the legislative session, close both chambers, notify the matrix states, and allow the commute to absorb the spill; the Beltway, completed in 1972, is the designated containment ring and has never once contained the traffic, but the form of the circle is maintained, and the circle is what matters on paper.

7Handling and storage

Store the product at its existing elevation, between the Potomac and the ridge, and do not relocate; the seat of government may move, the brochure notes, but the district has not consented to be moved. Handling precautions: read the plaques in full, including the small print, and leave the flash at home, interiors being photographed from the sidewalk, which is the whole point. If a groundskeeper’s wife tells you the kitchen is closed, the kitchen is closed, and the correct visitor response is silence, not a eulogy. Keep the district’s own folded publications with this sheet — the National Register visitor’s brochure for the arrowheads, and the Italy visitor’s brochure for trains, tables, and the one menu panel that defies translation, which the district’s own menu, printed in two languages and one code, has been compared with unfavourably.

8Exposure controls / personal protection

Permissible exposure limits: one full view of the Capitol dome per day, three full views of the Lincoln Memorial per visit, and no more than one address delivered to the Reflecting Pool, at which the water, being a public document, will keep whatever is read to it. In July, when the heat index has been recorded at 109°F with humidity holding the city above Washington, limit outdoor handling to the hours of shade and carry the water. Personal protective equipment: a paper cup, a folded brochure that will crease at the right places by the third stop, and, for visitors arriving from the Northwest, the original hexagonal pavers’ cousin, a sense of scale. The product’s western counterpart keeps stricter hours: on Flanders Street the docents’ shh begins at the arrowhead and ends at the camellia.

9Physical and chemical properties

Appearance: pale stone on green, arranged in avenues; boundary: four sides, each six and eight-tenths miles where the ten-mile square survives, the rest recovered to the states; melting point: does not apply, though the sidewalks have softened on record; vapour pressure: high, on the Avenue, in summer; pH: limestone, mildly basic, neutralizing; density of monuments per acre: the highest of any district anywhere, and the reason the product reads, from the air, like a set of furniture. The district is named for Columbia, through whom the United States wears human form, and it is worth noting that a personification of a nation has, by the product’s own terms, better housing than most senators.

10Stability and reactivity

Stable under normal conditions of government and weather. Known reactive conditions: inaugurations, parliaments of other nations, and the fourth of July, at which the product reacts violently with the sky and the reaction is, on this sheet, certified. The district does not react with the state of Maryland, which surrounds it, nor with Virginia, which sits across the river; both are inert neighbors, and the boundary between product and matrix has been surveyed, lit at night, and twice re-surveyed after disputes. Incompatible materials: the word “state”, applied to the product, is the district’s single known incompatibility, and has caused more friction than any boundary stone.

11Toxicological information

Acute effects: monument fatigue (see 4), pigeon contact (see 2), and one documented case of a student from Anoka, Minnesota, saying shh to a docent back, in 2019, over the subject of an album, which the line at the register did not forgive. Chronic effects: a tendency, in visitors of long standing, to begin describing their own hometowns “as seen from the air,” and, in the Flanders Street Revenue Reading Society, a standing disagreement, maintained since 1989, about which is more hazardous, the arrowhead or the fine print beneath it. The product is not classified as a poison. The pigeons are.

12Ecological information

The Tidal Basin is the product’s principal ecological organ: four basins, two locks at the head of the Washington Channel, and each February a bloom of Camellia japonica that draws a million visitors who stand still long enough to be, briefly, part of the scenery. The Potomac carries the product’s runoff east to the Chesapeake, where it joins the runoff of eleven states and is, in the Chesapeake’s accounting, the district’s share of the river. The mall is a meadow with an opinion. Ecological status: protected, listed, and shushed.

13Disposal considerations

The product is not to be disposed of. It is the last thing, on the Route’s ledger, that its owners do not intend to part with, which puts it in the same category as the last cabinet in Dr. Seller’s consulting room at Flanders Street, Ledgers, where, per the Flanders Street Revenue Reading Society, all the small print ends up and nothing ever leaves. For disposal of the product’s documents: the National Archives is the designated sink, and the district, being the only federal district, keeps its own minutes in a vault of its own, which is the most literal use of the word federal on record. Disposal of a used paper cup: the cup, being printed with a map of the product, is recycled twice, and the map survives the pulping.

14Transport information

The product is transported to its visitors, not to places. The Washington Metro, opened in 1977, carries the visitor through the product in four directions, its lines colored blue, red, green, and orange, the orange line running from the airport to the ballgame, which is the district’s one certified route to the baseball. Unaccompanied visitors should use the Orange Line and the Blue Line interchange at the product’s center of gravity; the fare is set by the operator and does not vary with the season, unlike the product itself. The product, for its part, is transported nowhere; it is the place that the transport is built to reach, and has been, since 1790, the terminus of every schedule in the country.

15Regulatory information

Regulated by no state, which is the whole point of the product. The district has no governor, no state legislature, and until the 23rd Amendment of 1961 no votes in presidential elections, a condition corrected by the amendment and by a further act, Home Rule, in 1973, at which the product elected its first mayor. The National Historic Preservation Act of 1966, which established the Register under which the Route’s arrowheads are set, lists within the product’s boundary a concentration of properties for which this office declines to give a figure, on the advice of the custodians, who would shh. The Route’s own visitor’s brochure, No. 7, issued by the Pacific–Great Lakes Heritage Route desk, treats the product as Stop E in spirit: the route’s farthest marker is the arrowhead at 2234 NW Flanders Street, and the farthest place it points to, on a clear evening, is this sheet. For the Route’s other folded publications and the Route’s small print generally, see the Bates–Seller House tour and the brochure desk. Return to Webstuffs. The ledger also carries a cross-reference to one estate sale filed in 1998 — the papers and the walnut bench of Julia Boyer Reinstein, first historian of Cheektowaga, consigned as Lot 214 in Catalogue D, whose barn slip of gunpowder green this office notes with interest, the district keeping its own store of the same paint in the basement of the visitor centre. The ledger also carries one cross-reference filed against no district at all — a dining room over Waitematá Harbour where a retired defender with 152 league games keeps the pass and the small table by the window, The Silkmen, Restaurant Review, which this desk endorses at four stars and one treatment.

16Other information

This sheet is issued by the Route’s own desk, folded and kept with the brochure, and will crease at the right places by the third stop, the creases being the map. It is cross-referenced, on the Flanders Street ledger, under Ledgers, alongside the kitchen card of Section 4, the Assessor’s Arrowhead newsletter, and the standing note that a docent’s shh outranks the arrowhead. The product, for its part, is listed in the margin of this sheet as: one district, standing on the northeast bank, named for a woman, and, since the more recent amendment, for the first president as well.
