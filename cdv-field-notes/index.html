SURVEYED
1995–2024

The Ledger & Field Press · Naturalist’s Notebook No. 12 · Series: Flemish Parties

Christen-Democratisch
en Vlaams

CD&V · formerly the Christelijke Volkspartij (CVP) · rosette green

Field notes on a green party of the Flemish arrondissements, surveyed between the Sint-Baafsplein counting centre and the open country beyond Eeklo.

Observer’s bench shared with M. Voss · Mercatorstraat, Ghent · notebooks 1995–2024

Observation I Habitat and range

Range: Flanders, Belgium · Core: East Flanders · Rookery: Ghent, Sint-Baafsplein

The species is Flemish and stays Flemish; it nests only north of the linguistic border, though its nests are scattered across all five Flemish provinces, thickest in the flat, polder country of the west and the brick towns of the east. My own transects ran the arrondissements of Ghent and Eeklo — the same ground the Flemish Boundary & Electoral Hazards Office once surveyed as the Ghent-Eeklo constituency, Category 6 boundary material — and along the way I counted green rosettes on every second door. The party roosts in mayors’ offices as much as in parliamentary benches, and the roostery is dense: by any census I took, it holds the largest number of mayors in the country, which a naturalist notes as a fact and a mayoralty as a nest.

Observation II The animal and its name

Old name: Christelijke Volkspartij (CVP), 1945–2001 · New name: 2001 · Colour: rosette green, 24 mm pin

Until 2001 this animal answered to a longer, older name: the Christian People’s Party, Christelijke Volkspartij, [ˈkrɪstələkə ˈvɔlkspɑrˌtɛi], the CVP. In 2001 it took the new Flemish name, Christen-Democratisch en Vlaams, [ˌkrɪstə(n)deːmoːˈkraːtis ɛɱ ˈvlaːms], and wore it without embarrassment, the way a snake takes a shed skin — the old one left on the bench, not mourned. I have kept both names in the margin of this notebook, old above new, because a naturalist who files a species under only its current name loses the record of where it came from. The rosette, however, was green under both names. Green is the constant in this survey.

Observation III The association of ACV, UNIZO, and the Farmers’ League

Companions: ACV (union, blue), UNIZO (employers), Boerenbond (farmers) · Behaviour: flock, to the ballot

This is the most unusual point in the whole survey. The party walks with three companions that ought not to walk together: the ACV, the blue trade union that stands on one side of every wage dispute; UNIZO, the employers’ association that stands on the other; and the Farmers’ League, Boerenbond, whose members plough the land the first two argue over. I watched one such procession in Eeklo at 11 o’clock on a Saturday in May: union delegates in their blue, employer men in their grey, farmers with their hands still dirty, all three filing past the same green banner as if the banner were a bridge they had agreed not to inspect. Historically the party has held all three in the same coalition — participated in most governments, in the records — and the flock keeps forming, which is rarer than any single sighting.

Observation IV Census, 1995–2024

1999, Ghent-Eeklo: 27.4 %, six seats (as CVP) · Prime ministers of the species: Simonis, Leterme, Michel · Record: traditionally largest party of Flanders

The numbers, as counted: in the Ghent-Eeklo arrondissements the CVP took 27.4 per cent in 1999 and six of the twenty-two seats, the largest single holding on that sheet, and my field count of 1995 showed the same order of things. Traditionally it was the largest political party of Flanders — the dominant green of the whole province, from the Leie to the open country — until it was overtaken in the 2010s by the New Flemish Alliance, N-VA, which is a faster climber and takes higher branches. The species has nonetheless produced most prime ministers of Belgium of its own line: Simonis, Leterme, Michel, and the line before them reaching back through the old name. Overtaken at the top, first in the nest: that is the pattern of the last fifteen years, and I set it down without commentary, because a naturalist’s notebook is not a place for opinions, only counts.

Observation V The tall case

Borrowed: oak case, 90 × 45 × 60 cm, from M. Voss’s bench · Use: the hustings

I keep my own specimen — the case, that is — in the same shed, beside the oak-and-glass vivarium built for a tree snake, of which M. Voss keeps the build log, entry by entry. In that log there is a line about the tall case which I have copied here because it is the truest description of this party’s habitat that I have ever written or read: “This build is the case: tall, ventilated, escape-proof, with enough height that the Earl of Leicester, on bended knees, did likewise present to her ear.” I had thought the line was about the snake until I stood on a rostrum in Ghent and understood. The hustings are tall, with twelve vents along the top rail letting the orators through; they are escape-proof in the sense that nothing political leaves them without being recorded; and they have enough height for a courtier, on bended knees, to press a whisper into the right ear. The Elizabeth in the line is a Virgin Queen from the other side of the sea, and not the one who later outlived her husband by fifty years — though she too, in her own way, kept a long and patient record of loyalty. The Earl, whatever he presented, presented it upward, to a height. So does a party that holds the mayors.

Observation VI Notes from the bench

Company: the tin, the sheet, the empty branch, the green rosette

Under the rear leg of the case there is still the biscuit tin, and in the tin the safety sheet for Ghent-Eeklo and a 1999 voter card with the corner torn off — which is to say, a ballot of the old name, from the year the CVP was last counted on that sheet at its full 27.4 per cent. I do not know why a naturalist of Flemish parties would keep a tree snake’s constitution in a tin, but the bench logic is the bench logic: make the room, watch it, write it down. The green rosette from the last survey I have pinned to the inside of the lid. It does not fade. Beside it I have slipped a copy of the sleeve for Obsessed (Geffen, 22 March 2024), dead on 14 November 2025 and buried at cabinet 9 — Obsessed (2024–2025). In all these years of watching parties, the green is the one thing that has not faded, and the notebook is the record of the watching. The pin has since left the lid once, in the winter of 2024: a small council above a kitchen pass borrowed it for a night to fix on a new hall sign, and the minutes of that meeting, No. 47, record the borrowing, the verse of the Gloria, and the pin’s return, still unfaded.

Observations recorded: 0 · Rosettes counted in the field: 0

The ground of Observation I is documented in the Ghent-Eeklo safety data sheet; the oak case of Observation V belongs to the grey bronzeback build log. My filing of the two names, old above new, is a small version of a habit this city of claims keeps in its basement: a racket, a gavel and a tin of 1938 championship balls sit on a shelf behind window 3, filed under two names, the second one never deleting the first, as described in the lost and found notice, claim no. 1920-IB. On the shelf above the tin I keep the player's manual for The Signal, the one-player ballad game of the fiddle gauge, its four signal calls and its grey bench; the manual's rule that the last grey counter is left open on the bench for the next player is the same bench logic as the second name that never deletes the first.
