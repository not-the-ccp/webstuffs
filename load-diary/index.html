SESSION DIARY · PRIVATE · COTAGE STUDIO, THE HILL ABOVE SAUSALITO, CALIFORNIA

Load — Diary of the Sessions

Keeper of the runner's ledger, fourth and final entry · the day before the New York flights

Tuesday, 9 April 1996. Last full day at the cottage before the New York sessions. Bob called it a wrap at half past five, which is the earliest the console has been dark since May, the day he walked in with the cartons and said the recording starts now. May 1995 it began, really, and I have been keeping count ever since: 314 days, 29 songs onto the two-inch, and a running coffee bill I have given up itemising. It was the most relaxed session of the four of their lives and the most productive, and nobody agreed on either adjective. That is the whole secret of this album and I am writing it down here so somebody knows, if the tapes survive us, that the good stuff fell out of a room where the shouting had stopped.

Bob produced the 1991 one and everybody was expecting him to produce the 1991 one again. Instead he produced a summer. He came in with no running order and no rules, only a rule: nobody leaves a take bad on purpose. The tracks stacked up so fast by the second week of March that he had to make the call about the double. A double album, on the table, argued for a full evening at the piano. Then Lars said half of it is a record and the other half is a record, and James said he was tired, so it splits. The first half ships June 4, 1996 — Elektra in the States, Vertigo everywhere else, same sleeve both sides of the Atlantic — under the name Load. The other half gets its own name and its own patience. I do not yet know what that name is. I know the songs, though. I have carried their amp cables. Fuel went to sixteen takes before Bob let it land, and it landed the way a plane lands: all at once, with the brakes already on. Wherever I May Roam is the one the housekeeper asked about by name. King Nothing got the living room. Bad Seamstress’ Tale got the kitchen, and the kitchen has not been forgiven for it.

This morning I found a flashcard in the couch drawer, under a drumstick and the 1994 Vertigo press sheet. Big letters at the top: ALGORITHM. Under them, in the same hand, the card reads, word for word: “A finite sequence of mathematically rigorous instructions, typically used to solve a class that argued the other side is for the harbor.” I have read it twice a day now, because the first half is the dictionary definition, clean and schoolbook, and the second half is somebody’s law-note bleeding onto the same line — a class, a counter-argument, a harbor. Then I looked out the window. The harbor is right there, at the bottom of the hill, the one the ferry crosses twice a day when it is not fog. Whoever made this card was studying something, and then stopped, and the sentence just kept walking, all the way down to the water. I have taped it inside the console lid. It is the truest thing pinned in this building.

At nine we pack the outboard for New York. The 2-inch goes on the first flight. The tape that says 29 sits in my bag, not theirs — it is mine. If you are reading this on the far side of the bay: the sessions were May 1995 to April 1996, the producer’s name is on every take, and the ferry still crosses. — D.
