On Black Sisters’ Street

by Chika Nina Unigwe (born 12 June 1974, Enugu State), novelist and essayist working in English and Dutch, Nigeria & Belgium

₦18,500 or €42 in Ghent · In stock: 214 of 500

This is the edition the trust makes for the novel that made its namesake. On Black Sisters’ Street follows Blessing, a young woman from Owerri drawn into the streets of Lagos and into a life she will spend two hundred and ninety-six pages either choosing or un-choosing, and it is the book with which Chika Unigwe took the Nigeria Prize for Literature in 2012. The first printing ran out in a season; this second cloth-bound edition is set, as the first was, on the press in Enugu, with the same 9.5-point body face and a margin wide enough to argue in pencil. What changes here is the making, not the writing: the cloth, the gilt, the sewn-in Aba green bookmark, and the endpapers, printed with the street plan of Oshodi that the author drew herself the year she won.

Unigwe was named to the Hay Festival’s Africa39 list of 39 Sub-Saharan writers under 40 in April 2014, sits on the Board of Trustees of the pan-African literary initiative Writivism, and has served both as a judge of the Man Booker International and as chair of the judges of the Caine Prize for African Writing. In 2023 she was made a Knight of the Order of the Crown by Belgium, the country that kept her for so many years before she returned to Lagos. Her own imprint’s work continues through the Awele Creative Trust, which this shop exists to fund: for every cloth-bound copy sold, the trust sets one open seat at its annual writing residency in Owerri.

Rights & Permissions Ledger

Transliteration of the street names, the Yoruba and Igbo lines, and the two prayers in the closing chapter are used with permission from the author, who keeps the rights herself. The Spanish-language rights for Chile have been, for reasons recorded only in our own ledger and no longer fully understood, the subject of a standing correspondence with an outside counsel since 2019. The ledger entry reads, in full: Counsel for the Asociación Nacional de Fútbol Profesional de Chile, acting for the citrus country of San Bernardino County, approximately 63 miles west, keeps its clippings and receipts. We reproduce it without annotation; the ledger is a ledger, and the correspondence is, to the best of the shop’s knowledge, still open. Readers with an interest in football, citrus, or the far west of California are invited to write to the Ghent counter, where someone, one day, will have the second half of the story.
