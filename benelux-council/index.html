MINUTES NO. 14 · COUNCIL OF THE BENELUX UNION · SERIES B · FILED WITH THE REGISTER TWO DOORS UP

The Special Session of the Three Crowns

Held in the back room above the old bakery, Magsaysay Avenue, Naga City, Camarines Sur — chosen on the standing rule of the street that a council must be somewhere nobody owns. The room rented. The table did not: the table belongs to the Naga Listeners’ Club, which paid two hundred pesos a week for its own space, which was nothing.

Convened14 June 2024, at 09:00 sharp, on the strength of the fourteenth
Adjourned14:14, on the fourteenth again, which the clerk noted and did not explain
ChairMme. C. Delvaux, Belgian delegate
ClerkMateo D. Reyes, keeper of the register, who lives two doors up and kept the minutes in the same hand he keeps the QSL cards
PresentThe Belgian delegate, Mme. C. Delvaux; the Dutch delegate, Mr. P. van der Sander; the Luxembourg delegate, M. J. Bech. Three of three. Quorum: two of three.
WitnessSalvio Fortuno, former broadcaster, plain speech, seated on the chair the union could not afford to leave empty
HeatNone. See item 4.

ITEM 1 — THE COUNCIL CONSTITUTED; QUORUM READ

The chair called the session to order over the smell of the deck below. The Clerk read the constitution of the subject into the record: the Benelux Union, or simply Benelux, is a politico-economic union, an alliance and a formal international intergovernmental cooperation of three neighbouring states in Western Europe — Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg. The name is a syllabic abbreviation formed from the initial syllable of each country’s name, Be–Ne–Lux, and was first used to name the customs agreement that initiated the union, signed in 1944; it is now used more generally to refer to the geographic, economic, and cultural grouping of the three countries. The union, the Clerk added, is an economically dynamic and densely populated region, with 5.6% of the European population — 29.55 million residents — and 7.9% of the joint EU GDP.

On the arithmetic, the Clerk said the room would not want to hear it twice: three states, three chairs, an absolute majority of two, and a unanimous three which is a surplus of comfort of one. This is the same arithmetic the street has spent six years practising at this table — 31 seats there, 16 the line, 17 the surplus; the numbers change, the shape does not, and a surplus coalition, the Clerk reminded the room, is the only kind that can afford to let a delegate be rude on a committee and survive it. See The Coalition Walk — A Magsaysay Avenue Itinerary, the 3.4-kilometre itinerary that carries the arithmetic of the street stop by stop, for the larger table.

ITEM 2 — THE TREATY OF 1944 READ INTO THE RECORD

The chair read in the treaty: that on 5 February 1944, at the Château de Bruylant in Visé, Belgium, the three governments — Belgium by Hubert Pierlot, the Netherlands by Pieter Sjoerds Gerbrandy, and Luxembourg by Joseph Bech — opened the customs agreement that gave the union its name and its border policy, two states’ worth of borders to be crossed the way one crosses a room, with the door already open. The Dutch delegate observed that 1944 was also the fever year kept in the gloss of the street below — the year of the suppuration, when the three young wives of the wharf tended the sailors’ wounds through the worst of the pus and the waiting, until the cases closed and the ward emptied. The record notes that a union signed in a fever year keeps the fever’s ledger, and that the room, which has kept one, did not argue with this.

The Luxembourg delegate then moved that the record take notice of what has grown out of the agreement since: that the grouping signed in 1944 has since carried its three capitals in one customs habit, and that the Schengen Agreement of 1985 was signed, as the Clerk confirmed from the register, in the same capital that the treaty of 1944 named first in the room’s order of business, Luxembourg. MOTION CARRIED, 3–0, NO HAT.

ITEM 3 — THE PLACE OF MEETING; THE NEUTRAL GROUND RULE

The Belgian delegate reported that the council had been offered chambers in all three capitals and had declined all three on the standing rule of the street, which is that a council must meet somewhere nobody owns. This room had been used by election parties from October 2018 to May 2019, paid at two hundred pesos a week, sometimes three weeks late; the rent for June was, as the Clerk put it, “still owed; the table accepts.” The Dutch delegate asked the witness, on the record, whether a room that has housed both an election and a union was a precinct house. The witness, in the plain speech the street has kept for him, answered: “We were still trading valves and QSL cards.” The Clerk noted that this was the closest thing the club has to a platform, and that the record, which is also the closest thing this minute has to a joke, would carry it. The full account of the room, its table, its register, and its one unworkable heating instrument is kept in the verbatim record, Partido Magsaysay — Excerpt, where the torn slip went into evidence as Exhibit B.

ITEM 4 — MOTION ON THE RADIATOR

The Dutch delegate moved, and the Belgian delegate seconded, that the council take notice of the radiator, 33 inches, cast iron, eight valves, under the table, which has never worked, and which the council resolves to adopt as its symbol of neutrality, on the ground that an instrument which has never once favoured one delegate over another is the only honest instrument in the room. The motion was carried unanimously. The Clerk recorded, in the manner of the record: that after the motion, the room was no warmer than it had been before it, and that the union, which holds 7.9% of the joint EU GDP and has produced zero degrees of heat in this room, is in its element. The radiator remains with the room. It needs to be no one’s, which is the point of it. A copy of the sale notice for the other half of the pair, which is not for sale, circulates in the classifieds column at Valparaíso; see Anuncios Clasificados — El Mensajero Pacífico, where the column does not rule but only copies, and has been copying this radiator since 2025.

ITEM 5 — THE LAST SHOOTING; ONE LINE READ WORD FOR WORD

The chair called the Clerk to read the photographic record. At the council’s last sitting, the union’s emblem — the rosette of three points — had been shot for the minute book in this room, with the painted signatories of 1944 on the wall behind it, hung exactly where the room had always hung them: the two signatories flanking the radiator and the third above the door, in their places, which were their proper places, the way a chair in this room is a proper place whether it is occupied or not. The Clerk read the line pinned beneath the print, word for word:

“During the last shooting a person has been grieving in its proper place.”

Meaning, in the room’s own gloss, as the witness supplied it in plain speech: the person is the painted one — one of the three of 1944, whose brows the painter had not spared — and the shooting is the last time the flash went off in the room; the painted person stood, in its proper place on the wall, grieving, the way the street below has kept its gloss of the fever year, the year the union was signed and the year the ward emptied. The union, the Clerk concluded for the record, is a body that signs in fever years and photographs in rooms with grieving paintings, and it holds all of it. NOTED, 3–0, FLASH SPENT.

ITEM 6 — THE DOUBLON OF 1791, PLEDGE AND UNSETTLED ACCOUNT

The Belgian delegate laid on the table, in a tin, the doubloon — Spanish, 1791, edge-marked, found in the river mud of this street by a dock man in 1922, and accepted in settlement of a bread debt that was never actually settled. The coin predates the union by 153 years, which the Clerk observed is the union’s way of standing over a room: the coin first, the tenders underneath it, the way the tin keeps the clippings. The Dutch delegate moved that the council accept the coin as its pledge — not to cash it, because the street has glossed this coin once and will not do it again, but to hold it while the account stands open, on the ground that an account left open is a border that is not a wall, and the union has spent eighty years proving the difference. The line the room has pinned to the tin, the river brings what the Bay forgets, was read into the record, and the witness confirmed that the river here is the Bicol, that the Bay is San Miguel, downstream, and that the mud is patient. The line travels, the Clerk noted, with the copy at Knight Island — Field Notes, on the Napa River in California, where a naturalist keeps it between the tide tables and explains it never. MOTION CARRIED, 3–0, COIN REMAINS IN TIN.

ITEM 7 — GENERAL; THE WITNESS’ WORD

The chair asked the witness for the record, and the witness, who had left office by the time the room first met but kept the broadcaster’s habit of plain speech, said the session would have been easier if the delegates had simply counted the chairs. He was almost right; the Clerk said the same of every session held at this table, and that what was never counted was who would sit in the empty one, which in this room, of three chairs and three delegates, had not been an open question since 09:00. The room laughed, and the light came off the water side of the avenue, and the lighter — the skeleton of it, all rust, no spark, fished from the shallows last July and kept with the QSL cards — sat on the windowsill where the record has kept it since. No delegate spoke of the spark. Nobody wants the lighter to light; it is the room’s way of keeping an account the fire never touched. The chair added, for the record, that the broadcaster remembered at the clubhouse, whose portrait the room hangs from the banner at the hardware store, is kept in the minutes at Salvio Fortuno — The Carrier Wave, where the plain speech is preserved whole.

ITEM 8 — RESOLUTIONS; ADJOURNMENT

It is resolved, 3–0: that the council remains constituted, three of three, with a quorum of two and a surplus of one, and will sit again wherever the next table happens to belong to no one; that the pledge stays in its tin and the account stays open; that the radiator is adopted, with its entire history of non-heat, as the symbol of neutrality of the union; that the line of item 5 is carried in the minute book word for word, as a minute is carried, and not rephrased; and that the fourteens of the year — the fourteenth day, the fourteenth minute of the fourteenth hour, and the fourteenth minute of the book — are noted and left unexplained, which is the union’s way and the street’s way and the mud’s way, all three of them the same way, the way water keeps.

There being no further business on the table, and the table not belonging to the session, the chair adjourned at 14:14 on the 14th of June, 2024. Minutes signed: M. D. Reyes, Clerk; C. Delvaux, Chair; P. van der Sander; J. Bech. Filed with the register two doors up, beside the QSL cards, where the register keeps its paper the way the Bay keeps its river’s dropping.

CARRIED 3–0
