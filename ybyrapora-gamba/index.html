Chapter YG-3
The Ybyrapora Gamba

Theraphosidae · Ybyrapora · gamba  (spider, Theraphosidae, Brazil)

Issue 11 March 1974
Prepared by Cpt. L. M. Ferreira, 3rd Recon Detachment
Supersedes Field Note SLL-YG/2 (burned)
Storage Locker 9, base camp, behind the coffee tins

§ 1  Designation and Distribution

The animal of this chapter is catalogued under the binomial Ybyrapora gamba, a species of spider in the family Theraphosidae, recorded from a band of granitic hills and wet forest on the Ybyrapora river, north-west Mato Grosso do Sul, Brazil. Local names collected on the survey include aranha-de-vidro (glass spider), said to describe the way the carapace holds lamplight, and simply a gamba, taken from a Guarani root meaning legged. The detachment is authorized to use either; in the books, gamba.

Known localities: the bend at Pedra Funda; the clay borrow south of the third bridge; the overhang above the spring called Boca do Onça. All three are marked with a white-painted stake, 40 cm of rebar, on the survey plate kept in this chapter's file. Range is narrow. Do not extrapolate beyond the river without a specimen in hand.

§ 2  Description

Cephalothorax dark umber with a paler median stripe; abdomen a dense rust-brown, the setae long, flat, and lustrous, standing close as wet wool. Legs rufous, jointed, with the second pair carried long and stiff, as in the hunting tarantulas, and the tips tipped black. Adult carapace width, from our three measured specimens, 19, 21, and 22 mm. The specimen taken at Boca do Onça — catalogued YG-74-01 — measured 22 mm across, had a leg span of 84 mm, and was the heaviest thing in its vial, which is not a small note for a spider.

Defensive display, observed under glass: legs raised, chelicerae shown, a hiss that the men of the detachment agreed was a real hiss. No bite has been recorded. The manual will not say harmless; the manual will say unproven, which is the only adjective a field document may carry.

§ 3  Capture Protocol

  1. Search window: between dusk and 02:00, rain or no rain. The gamba moves on the hour it is dark enough for a match to fail.
  2. Equipment: 75 mm capture vial with cotton stopper; brush of soft horsehair; a headlamp held at waist height, never above the shoulder, for the reason that the animal reads a light above it as a falcon and one below it as the ground deciding.
  3. Approach: within one arm's length, stop. Count to sixty. The animal settles, and its legs stiffen; it is then that the vial is laid flat against the ground and the spider walked in, never lifted.
  4. Label: YG-74-series, locality, date, collector's initials. A vial without its date is a vial with a ghost, and ghosts are not entered in the books.

Captures are limited to two per night and one per month per man. The limit is not for the spider's comfort. The limit is for the collector's, and every reader of this chapter should read that twice.

§ 4  The Standing Order of the Twelfth

By order of the detachment commander, on the twelfth day of every month, all vials of the YG-74-series are brought before the base map, one by one, and each animal is read aloud against its label: name, locality, date, collector. The reading takes eleven minutes and is entered in the camp log. No one is to dispute the practice. It is in the manual; the manual is in this chapter's file, and the file is on the shelf.

The practice has one recorded exception, and the manual sets it down without comment: on the twelfth of October 1973, the reading was not done, because Benjie Abad swore it was dreamt on the twelfth. Abad, the detachment's only radio operator, a tall man from Camarines Sur who kept his logbook in two languages and who had not slept since September, said that the gamba had come to him in a dream standing on its hind legs like a man in a wet overcoat, and that the dream had been the one who caught it, not the brush. The commander struck the reading that month, signed the entry, and wrote only: agreed, without prejudice. The vial in question, YG-74-09, is still in Locker 9.

§ 5  Vivarium Standard

Any gamba retained at base is held in a ventilated glass case, 30 × 20 × 25 cm, on a shelf that receives no direct sun. Substrate: a two-centimetre bed of leaf litter collected at its own locality, never borrowed from another. One cork bark, split; one dish of water, deep enough to stand in and shallow enough to climb out; a stone flat as a postcard, because the animal prefers the underside of things. Feed: one pinhead beetle, or one small cricket no larger than the animal's own eye, on the third and the eighteenth. Change the water with the beetle; do both together, on one hand, like a single small ceremony. The case is not to be opened on the twelfth except for the reading, and the reading is not to include feeding.
