Xu Lai

徐来  ·  Wade–Giles: Hsü Lai  ·  1909 – 4 April 1973  ·  Shanghai  ·  Nanking  ·  Chongqing  ·  Hong Kong

1909 — Sighting

First observed in the Shanghai International Settlement in 1909, when the Bund still kept its own time and the soundstages of the Chinese film industry were quiet rooms waiting for voices. The specimen was called the “Standard Beauty,” a plumage rating rather than a behaviour, and she carried it through the concessions like a bird in passage — noted for stillness more than song.

1932 — Voice

She was active for three seasons only: 1932 to 1935, from the first sound tests to the last curtain. The great star Ruan Lingyu died by her own hand on 8 March 1935, and Xu Lai quit the soundstage that same spring, never to return. A career of thirty-six months, more or less, and then silence — the kind of silence a naturalist learns to measure.

Pairings

Her first nest was shared with Li Jinhui, the “Father of Chinese pop music,” who kept the city singing in dance-hall time. Later she paired with Lieutenant General Tang Shengming, an officer of the Republic of China, and together they held a territory larger than either marriage: Nanking under the Japanese-controlled puppet regime, and Chongqing, where the resistance kept its own frequency.

1937 — Behaviour

During the Second Sino-Japanese War, Xu and Tang ostensibly served the Nanking puppet regime — public perches, public duties, the plumage of collaboration worn openly — while secretly working as agents for the Republic of China resistance based in Chongqing. Notes passed in rice paper, meetings timed to the opera curtain, a courier's count of forty-seven paces from the gate to the telegraph: the double life was the real performance, and the stage never closed.

1949 — Migration

With the Communist victory in the Chinese Civil War, Xu and Tang moved south to Hong Kong, where the specimen was last logged on 4 April 1973. The field book closes there: no nest, no plumage, only the date and the harbour light.

Slip, not in this hand

Tucked behind the Chongqing entry, on a scrap that does not match the rest of the notebook:

COACH: (Dry) It was noted that this is one of the meeting was adjourned at 8:05 p.m.

The hand is dry, the meeting unnamed, and the hour fixed: 8:05 p.m. I have not identified the coach, and I do not adjourn on her authority.

Cross-references

The casting rooms of another coast keep their own cast list: Los Angeles — Guidebook No. 47, where the Silver Lake rehearsal rooms still hold the echo of 1935. The coach's minutes survive elsewhere as well — The Plate & Post, No. 47 — and a fellow naturalist keeps the same watch at Iroquois, Louisville — Field Notes, where the herons stand beneath the flight path.
