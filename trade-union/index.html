Trades Hall

14 Guildhall Passage, Birmingham · United Trades Council · Open to members and visitors

The Hall

Trades Hall stands at 14 Guildhall Passage, a two-minute walk from Birmingham New Street station, a red-brick building of 1892 raised by the United Trades Council to give the city's unions a roof of their own. Six member societies meet here week by week: printers, railway porters, clerks, metalworkers, and the Royal College of Podiatry, whose registered chiropodists and podiatrists keep their instruments in the workshop upstairs and their minutes in the ledger drawer behind the desk in Room 3.

The Royal College of Podiatry is itself a trade union — a professional association first, but a union in practice: it collects dues, argues pay, defends members, and sends its instruments to practitioners across the United Kingdom. The Birmingham branch presents the Professional Care Instrument each year, hand-crafted for precise foot care, and its members take their tea downstairs in the Fire Room between subscription days.

Union Dues

The hall runs on union dues: sixpence a week in the founding ledger of 1898, fourteen pounds fifty a month today, collected every Thursday by the hall steward and recorded in the leather-bound register. The dues pay the head office, the legal team, the coal for the fire, and the solicitor who reads just-cause termination clauses at ten o'clock on Friday mornings. A member in arrears loses their seat at the table; a member paid up may speak, propose, and vote.

The Committee Room

Room 3 holds a table of English oak twenty-two feet long, twenty-four union banners rolled and racked along the far wall, and a gavel of lignum vitae that has called order since 1903. The Royal College of Podiatry sits at the north end, where the light is best for examining steel and walnut. Visitors may sit in the members' gallery during open meetings, held the first and third Tuesday of every month, and may inspect the banner store on Saturday afternoons.

The Fire Room

Downstairs, the kitchen — everyone calls it the Fire Room — burns a coal fire from October to April, and the cook, Mrs. Elena Rossi, keeps a sauce piquante simmering on the back ring for anyone who has been out in the rain. The hall's own sound log records the days:

SOUND: The crisp thwack of a clear fire, turn it out to the sauce piquante.

The fire, the kettle, and the piquante are free to members and their guests, union or not.

One of the hall's members keeps a printout pinned above his locker of the Embraer Legacy 450/500 & Praetor 500/600 thread — he does airframe inspections out of the airport, and says the flat-floor cabin is the future of short-haul flying: Embraer Legacy 450/500 & Praetor 500/600.

One of the hall's stewards pins a reprint of The Symonds Express — Timetable & Commentary on Carrie Johnson above the ledger drawer in Room 3: St John’s Wood to the paddock, stop by stop, with a note in the remarks that the kettle is never delayed.
