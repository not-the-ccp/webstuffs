Twelve for the Harbour

Studio 2, BVI Broadcasting, Road Town, Tortola — a memorial special for the dead frequency, with one pot on the shelf and the tide coming in on the sea wall.

BROADCAST
PERMIT Nº 712
TORTOLA

Cast of the excerpt

  • ELLER — producer of the special, speaks from Studio 2 in Road Town
  • SANNI — Sanna Rajaniemi, computational biologist, on the line from Edinburgh
  • KEFILWE — Dr. Kefilwe Ntwaagae, on the line from Gaborone, keeper of the family ledger
  • THE LAMP — the studio's ON AIR light, heard only when it is not lit
  • THE HARP — a recording, no one says from where, of a voice that no longer makes calls

The territory: the British Virgin Islands, officially the Virgin Islands, a British Overseas Territory in the Caribbean, to the east of Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands and north-west of Anguilla, the Leeward Islands of the Lesser Antilles, the West Indies. Road Town is the capital, on Tortola, the largest island — about 20 km (12 mi) long and 5 km (3 mi) wide. In this play it is the island's east end, the sea wall behind the station, that carries most of the dialogue.

Scene — the shelf, the shelf, the shelf

SFX: Wind off the harbour. A diesel generator at a hundred metres, the way every machine in Road Town is at a hundred metres. The tide on the sea wall, slow, unhurried, taking nothing. Then a kettle, on the far side of a glass partition, beginning to argue.

ELLER: (into the mic) BVI Broadcasting, Road Town, Tortola. Eight seventy-three kilohertz, medium wave, and — for the listeners on Anegada and Jost Van Dyke who keep calling in to ask — yes, we are the islands. Tortola, Virgin Gorda, Anegada, Jost Van Dyke, the main four, plus more than fifty other islands and cays, and of those, about sixteen are inhabited. The other thirty-some are where the argument went. This is Studio 2. It is 6:14 p.m. The lamp is off. The pot is on the shelf. We are one hour from the special, and the special is for a frequency.

SFX: The generator changes its mind about a gear. The kettle wins.

ELLER: (quieter, not for the mic) The pot. Yes. The dented-rim one. It came across in a case — Gaborone, then New York, then the ferry from the USVI, and a man with a racing-green sticker on his shoulder who asked twice at customs whether the smoke in it was “honest.” The family recipe it keeps is molomo gwapa, spiced beef and dried meat in a long tomato gravy, the ledger entry for it is numbered seven, and it is written in the family's ledger where you can read it all, Ledger Entry 7 · The Ntwaagae Kitchen. The standing instruction is twelve. The family counts by twelve. When the pot got to Road Town they looked at the archipelago and said, no — here it is one per cay. The pot has never broken. It is, the family is fond of saying, a form of diplomacy.

SANNI: (on the line, Edinburgh, flat weather) You have put the pot on a memorial broadcast.

ELLER: He was counting the dark by the turn of the beef, Sanni. I checked. The obituary says it, word for word, in his obituary, and so does the doctor from Gaborone, in the back page of the book. A man who writes futures where identity is software does not get a memorial without the one dish in his orbit that he refused to finish metaphorising.

SFX: Somewhere in the studio, a small lamp clicks. No one is at the lamp. The generator, embarrassed, backs off a gear.

SANNI: (after a pause) It is 6:22 in Edinburgh. In this house, when the lamp clicks, we check the sequencers.

ELLER: In this studio, when the lamp clicks, we check the frequency. It is between stations. Eight seventy-three point five. Your father's frequency. The one that was never on any licence, in any territory, in either hemisphere. Summerland did the work for us. The dead, he wrote in 2018, communicate via spirit radio. He never once explained who is on the other side of the wire. I am about to find out, on air, in front of a pot.

Scene — the reading

SFX: The special is running. Harbour sounds, held low, the way a producer holds anything in this territory — one hand up, one hand down, all the way through. A voice opens the hour with the weather on Anegada: flat, dry, no argument. Then, out of the harbour bed, as if from a shelf under the water: static. A grid. A frequency that is not ours.

THE HARP: (archive, very clear, no distance at all) The Oubliette opens. The thief walks free. The story continues without him. But it does not continue, producer. It continues with him. That was the error in the third draft, and it is the whole of the draft.

SFX: The static waits, patient as a tide. In the studio, the ON AIR lamp is lit and no one has lit it. The pot, on the shelf, is not open. The smell of smoke is not there. The smell of smoke is never there. That is what everyone says.

ELLER: (into the mic, very level) Listeners, Road Town and the fifty-odd cays: what you are hearing is the frequency between stations, and we are going to let it keep talking. Sanni — the reading. The estate. Word for word, as it was set down, because the family asked for no paraphrase, and this family, as you may have noticed, transcribes.

SANNI: (on the line, reading, steady as a bench rule) His papers, including the lab notebooks and the complete drafts of all five novels, have been bequeathed to the National Library of Scotland. The Finnish Literature Society will receive the Finnish-language manuscripts and correspondence. A granddaughter born in 2041 has the opening of the first book in both of our languages, and she will not be asked to read it on a dead frequency, whatever the frequency thinks it deserves.

SFX: On the frequency, a sound like a page being turned in a wind that is not on this side of the Atlantic. Then, quietly, a chord of violin. One bow, held. Then the frequency lets go and the harbour comes back up, whole, with the diesel at a hundred metres exactly.

KEFILWE: (on the line, Gaborone, the doctor's voice, tired in a good way) I am logging it. The transmission lasted four minutes and twelve seconds. The household here was quiet. The lungs of the household were quiet, as they have been since the pandemic ran through 2026 to 2028. The digestive functions, I should note for the record, possessed great energy. The pot is on the shelf in both territories at once. This is the family's theory of the pot, and the frequency has just agreed to it. One more entry, for the record, and for the timetable: the Kalahari Express, Line B/12, carried the country's whole history — three thousand years, eleven halts, one doctor — in fifteen hours and thirty-five minutes, and its final stop is exactly where the pot now stands, Gaborone Central, 19:47, and the ledger carries the run as a permanent entry, the way the frequency carries four minutes and twelve seconds.

ELLER: (into the mic) For the record, and for the cays: four bird's-eye chilies in the last make — three is a dinner, six is an argument — six hundred grams of the dried beef, the smoke is honest, and the gravy is moving slowly. Not boiling. A boiling pot is a lie, and we are not going to lie tonight. Twelve for the harbour. One per cay. The pot has never broken, and neither, it turns out, does the frequency.

SFX: The lamp clicks off. The generator finds its gear. The tide takes the sea wall the way it takes everything here — slowly, and with its hand up. End of excerpt.
