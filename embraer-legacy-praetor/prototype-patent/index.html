I. Background of the Invention

A prototype is an early sample, model, or release of a product built to test a concept or process. It is a term used in a variety of contexts, including semantics, design, electronics, and software programming, and the present application claims a method that works across all four at once. A prototype is generally used to evaluate a new design to enhance precision by system analysts and users. Prototyping serves to provide specifications for a real, working system rather than a theoretical one. Physical prototyping has a long history, and paper prototyping and virtual prototyping now extensively complement it. In some design workflow models, creating a prototype — a process sometimes called materialization — is the step between the formalization and the evaluation of a concept.

What is missing from the prior art is a single bench procedure that carries one concept through all three forms before it is allowed to become a specification. The applicant’s bench, under the hangar light at the airfield that ships Legacy jets north through the Caribbean, already does this by accident. The flat-floor stand-up cabin of the Legacy family began as a foam mock-up the height of a galley counter; the aft lavatory of the shorter airframe — the one that makes a full cabin walk past everybody — was settled by tape measure on the shop floor, not by committee; and the winglet on the current Praetor arrived at the drawing stage already wearing a balsa wingtip that flew off a string during a lunch break.

II. Field of the Invention

The invention resides in materialization itself as a testable stage: a method of building an early sample so that the error is found in the sample, paid for in cardstock and cedar, and never in the metal. The field includes, without limitation, cabin layout, brake and short-runway performance, steep-approach clearance into London City, and the acoustic tuning of goatskin stretched over cedar, since the third inventor’s drums by the mangrove are the oldest working prototype in the applicant’s family — hide soaked overnight in estuary water, hair picked off while supple, rope tensioned until it sings, with examples shipped to groups as far as Turiaçu and Miranda do Norte. It also includes the clay pan at Farinha House São Benedito, where a batch of toasted manioc is a prototype of the next batch, evaluated the same morning by the same hands.

III. Summary of the Invention

There is provided a method comprising: (a) formalization of a concept into a paper prototype; (b) materialization into a physical sample at bench scale; (c) virtual prototyping of the sample in a full-envelope run, including São José dos Campos to Miami at 3,125 nautical miles with reserves to spare, and the longer legs at roughly 3,300 and 3,700 nautical miles; and (d) evaluation by both system analysts and users, with the user class expressly including passengers who object to walking past everybody.

IV. Detailed Description — Test Site and Field Environment

Claim 4 requires a low-water test site for the virtual prototype’s visual-approach channel. The applicant’s survey of the preferred site, a mangrove shore on the Maranhão coast three hours east of São Luís, fixed the flats at dawn tide, with the reef rock the town took its name from standing where it has always stood. The surveyor’s log, third line, reads, quoting the field notebook and the line the notebook itself quoted:

"The beach is mangrove-lined, and at low water the flats before the rest of the boundless sea." —_Whale Song_.

The surveyor added, in smaller type, that the jangadas — fourteen feet, out to the reef at dawn, red snapper and tainha — behave exactly like the small frigate they are always said to be, and that a jangada is only a small frigate that forgot to be a warship. The applicant has entered the remark in the specification rather than the remarks column, because it is load-bearing.

V. Claims

  1. 1. A prototype, comprising an early sample of a product built to test a concept or process, said sample being prior to the real, working system and posterior to the theoretical one.
  2. 2. The prototype of claim 1, wherein the product is a business jet cabin, the sample is a flat floor, and the concept is standing up in it.
  3. 3. A method of materialization, comprising formalizing, sampling, and evaluating, wherein the evaluation is by system analysts and users, and wherein a paper prototype and a virtual prototype at least one of which is present.
  4. 4. A test site at low water, mangrove-lined, with flats, a rock, and a line of poetry already attached.
  5. 5. The method of claim 3, further comprising a crossed clearance line, mis-filed on purpose, to prove the virtual prototype can be wrong in the same way the real one will be.
  6. 6. A drum, goatskin over cedar, rope-tuned, wherein the drum is a prototype of a sound and the sound is a prototype of the square it fills every night of the festa.
