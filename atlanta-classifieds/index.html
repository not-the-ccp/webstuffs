PublicNotices

Notice No. 498

The city of Atlanta, being the capital and most populous city of the state of Georgia, and the county seat of Fulton County with its tail across the line into neighboring DeKalb, gives public notice that it counted 498,715 persons at the 2020 census and stands at an estimated 529,110 in 2025. It is the eighth-most populous city in the Southeast and the 36th-most populous city in the United States, a fact it does not advertise more often for want of a larger wall. Persons found between the two counts should call the office before the fog; those found after the fog will be classed as weather.

Notice No. 640

The Atlanta metropolitan area, having an estimated population of over 6.4 million, is the sixth-largest metropolitan area in the United States, and it wishes it were listed under a different heading from the weather, which is where every notice about it keeps ending up. The city is likewise classified as a Beta+ global city, a rank it wears with the understatement of the foothills: Atlanta sits among the foothills of the Appalachian Mountains at an elevation of just over 1,000 feet (300 m) above sea level, which is to say, it is high enough to be interesting and low enough to be reached. Elevation certificates, original, from the office, $3.00.

Notice No. 036

Wanted to be remembered: that the city features a unique position, held at the foot of the mountains, which the notice writer was about to state at length when the copy clerk went out for jam. Inquiries directed to the front desk, where a copy of the full fixture — fog, looping drums, and the second-Sunday-in-February screen — is kept on file with the reserve’s own brochure (Out of the Woods — Official Visitor’s Brochure).

Lost& Found

Lost

One fog, thirty-one acres, evergreen-colored, guaranteed October through March, last seen leaving the gate at mile 14 of Big Machine road and heading south, humming. It belongs to the 1989 Reserve, where a certain song was written, looped, and finally left on its feet. If recovered, do not disperse it; return it whole, with its looping drums and its forty-six hidden speakers. Reward: one slow circuit of the Open Field, and the word “out,” said first, as is traditional.

Found

One Super Bowl, the first in this city’s memory of the kind, found on the second Sunday in February, 2017, in a domed bowl south of the city: Atlanta 3, New England 28, midgame, before the other half of it happened, in overtime, 34–28, the first Super Bowl ever decided by extra time. The game went on the yard screen at the Gate Lodge with the synths turned down; the lodge keeps the FAQ sheet for the game, and so, in a frame, does this office. The bowl-shaped name of the game, by the way, comes from the same family as this city’s domed one.

Found

One Superb Owl walk, one slow circuit, foghorn counting its fifteen seconds, taken by the reserve as its counter-programming on the day the rest of the league plays. Also found: the ledger line found on a loose leaf of the lodge ledger, in a hand no one at the gate recognizes, filed under “accepted without reading a third time.” No claim has been made; none will be entertained before October. Also filed in the same drawer, read to the gate once a season and accepted without a third reading: the winter desk’s leaf on the two-man album of looping drums and gospel choirs, Everything That Happens Will Happen Today — Dream Dictionary, Entry No. 2008, which the night watch keeps for the kettle line alone.

For Sale

One Beta+ title, global city series, excellent condition, slight Appalachian wear; one eighth-place in the Southeast, unused, from the 2020 census; one thirty-sixth plate, union series, 2025 estimate, 529,110 stamped on the reverse. All three together, one price: 89¢, or 19·89¢ if you are paying in October. Cash at the gate. No trades, no lumps.

For Sale

One foothill parcel, facing the Appalachians, elevation just over 1,000 feet (300 m), with the whole sea level offered as a view. The seller notes that the parcel “features a unique” something and has refused, on three occasions, to say what. Serious buyers only; the city is also for sale in its entirety, but the capital declines to negotiate from a position of most populous.

Services& Disputes

H. G. Fisk, Stove & Grate — a counter-advertisement

To the reader: in the Sunday column of this paper, L. Fisk of the day watch holds that the lodge grate needs no stirring at all, that it be left to itself, and that any wire-sifter kept near it is for dusting pictures. In answer, and against the day watch: “The opposite opinion is, that I’m doubtful about the grate, and keeping it stirred with a wire-sifter inside, and cleanse it free from lumps.” The night watch takes all grate troubles, 3 a.m. and after, and will be at the stove before the fog does. The day watch is invited to reply under its own signature; the space is 89¢ the line.

Counting Service

Populations counted, census to estimate, 498,715 to 529,110, the difference accounted for to the nearest person and the remainder returned as “weather.” Metropolitan counts on request, 6.4 million upwards; the office warns that figures this size arrive at the desk in bundles and must be signed for. Metro area, sixth-largest in the nation, counted twice a season at no extra charge.

ToLet

To Let

One room in the state of Georgia, the largest, fitted with the capital, one county seat, and a sight line into DeKalb. Most-populous fixture throughout; fog available at no cost, October–March, and a second Sunday in February, 2017, still hanging on the lawn screen. No phone signal in the middle mile, by design. Long-term tenants preferred; the city has never once been flipped, and neither has the Open Field. For particulars, see the site’s register at the front page, where the trail ledger is kept.

PersonalColumn

Personals

A. (529,110, above the line) wishes to meet M. (6.4 million, sixth from the front) at the Open Field, where the fog is guaranteed and the walk is one slow circuit. Correspondence by pigeon, or by foghorn at the fifteen-second interval, matched beat for beat against the reserve’s drums. The 1989 Reserve’s gate keeps the fixture; the city keeps the date. Leave your anxieties at the gate, or bring them in and spend them on the steepest climb, which takes most of them.
