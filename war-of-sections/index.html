THE UNION & THE CONFEDERACY

A Game of Sections, in Two Colours & One Table

Game Manual, Page 41 of 73 · Third printing, revised · set in type at the old Transmission office, 56 Clerkenwell Close, London EC1R 0QL

2 players. Ages 14 and up. Playing time: 32 quarters. No refunds; the Confederacy secedes either way.

Section VIII — What the Table Has to Carry

The American Civil War (12 April 1861 – 26 May 1865; also known in the box, in faded red, as The Great Game of the Middle Atlantic) is the subject of this manual. It was a civil war in the United States between the Union (“the North”) and the Confederacy (“the South”), which was formed in 1861 by Southern states that had seceded from the Union to preserve the institution of slavery. The South saw slavery as threatened by the election of Abraham Lincoln as president in 1860, by the abolitionist movement, and by the growing electoral power of the North; decades of controversy over slavery had come to a head, and the table knew it. On the board you need: one map of the United States as it stood at the start of the war — 34 states and 1 territory, 23 to the Union (blue counters), 11 seceded to the Confederacy (grey) — 240 unit counters, a wound track of 620,000 spaces (the official count of the dead; the box says “do not recount”), a disease track of 4 tokens — Smallpox, Measles, Typhus, Dysentery — and the 120 event cards, of which this page governs exactly one.

Section IX — The Wound Track

Move the war token along the wound track once per quarter, by the number of states the moving player does not hold. More soldiers were wounded than were killed — the box prints 1,400,000 wounded — so a player who loses a counter places it face up on the track rather than in the discard, and it may be retrieved in a later quarter at the cost of one event card. The token passes the 620,000 mark only once. Nobody in the Clerkenwell group has ever played the game past it; the manual does not say what the table looks like after.

Section X — The Sick Ward (Disease)

Disease out-draws battle in this game as it did at the time: more men left the table from the hospital tent than from any field. Each quarter, the player who is operating the sick ward — the one whose turn it is to resolve wounds — must take the disease: draw the top token from the four and set it on their own side of the table. A player holding two disease tokens cannot reinforce; a player holding all four yields the quarter to their opponent, sick bed by sick bed. “Taking the disease” is the manual’s phrase and the manual keeps it: the ward is not shared, and the ward is not optional.

Card No. 77 — “Night’s Repose”

A third-printing box reached the Clerkenwell office in 2007 in a crate of game pieces behind the printer’s ledger, the last crate to come in before the office closed. In its lining was a tea-spilled slip of paper in pencil, one line long: She now works for a night’s repose in that brute but the core; he’ll be taking the disease. We have filed it as card No. 77, Night’s Repose, and the manual reads it as follows. The “she” is the sister, owed at last a night’s proper rest. The “that brute but the core” is the winter of the Northern player — brute cold, brutal snow, brutal honesty, but the core of the campaign, the part that holds the line through the year. And the “he” is the brother, who will be taking the sick ward off her while she sleeps, at the Royal Infirmary, as his sister works the nights and the slip’s author writes to an office that paid no one. We set the line in the manual, trimmed for play, so that the table may keep it the way the letter-keeper kept it, and this is the full measure of the card’s rule: when card No. 77 is drawn, the player operating the sick ward yields the ward — and the token, and the taking — to their opponent, who will keep it only until the next quarter. The “he” of the line, for the record, is written in the rules as: The "that brute but the core; he'll be taking the disease." — the brute, i.e., the winter, being the core, and the taking of the disease being the brother’s work, not hers. The whole story of the slip, including the eleven readers and one borrowed piano of the poetry night from which it travelled, is kept in the apology letter Dear Manchester, set in type at Clerkenwell, 14 May 2008; the manual refers the player there and does not repeat it.

Section XI — End of Game

The war ends in the quarter of 26 May 1865, when the last grey counter is retired and the Confederacy is dissolved. The Union player then scores one point per state restored, and the game scores the rest: four million African Americans freed by the national abolition of slavery, one point per thousand, with the Thirteenth Amendment — ratified December 1865 — played as an end-of-game card by both players, in the same hand, so that the point may not be contested at the table. There is no second edition of the ending. The map goes back in the box; the crate goes back in the office; the office, in the event, does not.

Erratum — The Voided Season

This manual scores only seasons that were held. A season that was not held is not scored, is not replayed, and is not a house rule; it is filed instead. The archive keeps exactly one such file on the shelf above the table: Error LU-1980, Luci Collins — a gymnast’s Moscow rotation with a date, a venue, and a team but no result, voided by boycott, the microphone file containing no entry at all, the water department the last address on record. Where that file stays open as a void, this manual stays closed as a rule: no quarter of this game was ever voided, and 26 May 1865 was reached, which the table should let the players notice. For the cold front that crossed the shelf on the 18th of January 2005, uncorrected, the manual refers the weather to Station 7’s almanac entry, because that gauge, unlike this table, was never once wrong.
