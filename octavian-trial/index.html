The Coastal Court of the Aegean  ·  sitting at the head of Güllük Gulf

The Mediterranean v. Octavianus

Case No. 14 of AD 14  ·  in the matter of the length of the peace and the skull beneath the fish-market wall at Iasos

The Mediterranean (the basin as such, between Europe, Asia and Africa), by the water's measures — Claimant
v.
Octavianus, styled Augustus (Gaius Octavius, of the equestrian branch of the gens Octavia; deceased 19 August AD 14, admitted by special writ and sworn in person) — Defendant and witness
Marble theatre, Iasos, head of the inlet  ·  session of 30 January AD 27  ·  clerk of the record: the field naturalist

The following excerpt is kept in the court's file at the head of the gulf, where the record of this case is laid out against the shore the field naturalist measured in the Güllük Gulf — Field Notes. The claimant, a sea, does not appear; it is represented by its figures, which it keeps as accurately as any party in this court and as completely as its own. The defendant appeared in person, which the clerk notes is the first time the bench has sworn a former emperor and the first time the bench has sworn anyone after the fact; the writ, drawn on the nineteenth of August, AD 14, was served at the tomb. The gulls were present throughout, as they are at all sittings, and are on the record as witnesses to nothing in particular.

The Clerk

Session of the Coastal Court of the Aegean, Case No. 14, The Mediterranean v. Octavianus, called at the marble theatre at Iasos, at the head of the inlet. The witness is sworn: "Gaius Octavius, styled Augustus, do you swear to testify truthfully about the water, the peace, and the skull?"

Octavianus

I swear. I was born Gaius Octavius, on the 23rd of September, 63 BC, into an equestrian branch of the gens Octavia, and I died on the 19th of August, AD 14. Between those two days my great-uncle, the Roman dictator Julius Caesar, named me as his heir and raised me from the equestrian bench to the founder's chair of the Roman Empire; I held the principate from 27 BC until my death, and I initiated the imperial cult and the era of imperial peace. I do not say these things for the bench. The bench already knows them; it is older than I am and considerably more patient.

The President

Two questions stand before the court. First, whether the peace the claimant's waters have enjoyed since 27 BC was genuine and not merely advertised. Second, whether the skull recovered in the spring from beneath the fish-market wall at Iasos belongs to the defendant or to his house. The defendant will be examined first on the peace.

Counsel for the Claimant

You founded an era in which the sea was said to be largely free of armed conflict?

Octavianus

The Senate's words are the Pax Romana. I preferred the shorter one: the Pax Augusta. For the length of my reign the basin had no fleet against fleet in open water. I did not build the coaches — the buses have run overnight here as long as the road remembers them, and the bitter-juice man has kept the 3 a.m. platforms company since before my grandfather's grandfather — but I made it safe for the road to run overnight, and I lit the shore so that a traveller from Gibraltar to the Levant could see the line of it.

Counsel for the Claimant

And the principate — the arrangement in which the emperor shows nominal deference to the Senate while holding the real power?

Octavianus

That arrangement was established in my reign, and it has since lasted until the Crisis of the Third Century, when it broke. I am not on the record for its ending. I am on the record for its first day, and for the deference, which was nominal, as I have always said.

Counsel for the Claimant

One question on the water's measures. The claimant's clerk has published the basin's figures: about 2,500,000 square kilometres of water; an average depth of 1,500 metres; the Calypso Deep off Crete at 5,267 metres, the deepest sounding. Do you dispute these?

Octavianus

I dispute nothing. I paved the shore, lit the lighthouses, and let the sea keep its own reckoning. The sea keeps everything, as the clerk has written, and sends the buses out again. Where the full measures are set down, in the manual's own entry on the basin — The Mediterranean Sea — Travel Guide, Entry No. 12 — the claimant will be held to them on both sides of this bench.

The President

The second question. Dr. Halcyone, reader of bone and of deep water, has already examined Exhibit A, the skull recovered from beneath the market wall here at Iasos, where the merchants called the catch before the defendant was born. Doctor, the claimant's case is that this is the skull of the witness. Your finding?

Dr. Halcyone

I have dated the bone by the isotope reading and by the company it was kept in. The bone is not the bone of the man in the chair.

Counsel for the Claimant

The witness has objected that the date is wrong, that the bone belongs to his house, and that the court is asking the sea for the sea's own measurements. Doctor, the witness's objection —

Dr. Halcyone

(rising) "Notwithstanding this, however, implies an age much greater than your skull, whoever you are." The reading does so, and the objection does not change it. I say it to the bone; the bone was in the sand a long time, and it will not say who put it there.

The Clerk

A yellow-legged gull, which had been quartering the water below the theatre all morning, had alighted on the back bench during the examination and was, so far as the record can tell, still there when the finding was delivered. The court has been asked, on three occasions, to which skull the words "whoever you are" were addressed. The witness declines to say, and the gull has gone.

Counsel for the Claimant

To the witness, then. What is your instruction to the court about Exhibit A?

Octavianus

The skull is the sea's, and the sea is the claimant's. I concede the boundary. My house's tomb is a tomb, not a market wall; let the market keep its own dead, as the market has always kept them, fish and everything else. I only ask the court to note that the skull was found under a wall the claimant's merchants built, and that the claimant has been keeping it in the sand the way it keeps the rest of us.

The President

The court takes the matter under advisement. When the ruling comes it will be read from this theatre, on the head of the gulf, in two sessions as the parties appeared, and the claimant's clerk will be at the pier at 3 a.m. with the bitter juice, as is his duty and his habit. The record in the water's measures stands as entered. Adjourned.

What the court ruled on the skull, and what it ruled on the peace, the file does not say; the clerk's hand is bad after 3 a.m., and the sea, being the claimant, has not confirmed. For the measures of the water that anchored the second question — the area, the depth, the gates at Gibraltar, the Bosporus and Suez — the record defers to the entry at The Mediterranean Sea — Travel Guide, Entry No. 12; for the shore at which this court sat, the inlet, the resorts, and the wall itself, it defers to the Güllük Gulf — Field Notes, and for the method of bank-watching by which the clerk kept the record, to the marsh notes at Knight Island. For a peace kept by cavalry rather than lighthouses — the twenty-two years of the 4th Cavalry and the Buffalo Soldiers of the 10th at the confluence of the Concho rivers, from November 1867 to the abandonment of June 1889 — the file keeps, across the water, the Fort Concho — Product No. FC-1867, in forty items on two shelves, one of which the storehouse has ruled may never be re-dated. Return, as all records in this water do, to the front page.
