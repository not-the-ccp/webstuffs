LOST

One country — not to be confused with a sled, a flag, or a run card

Bulletin No. LF-94/NO-037 · Posted 19 February 1994, 07:45, before the two-man start · Claim within 90 days, or the item is re-shelved under “Nations, Scandinavian”

Item
One country. Officially the Kingdom of Norway. A Nordic country comprising the western and northernmost parts of the Scandinavian Peninsula in Northern Europe, the remote Arctic island Jan Mayen, and the archipelago of Svalbard. Bouvet Island, in the Subantarctic, is a dependency and not a part of the Kingdom — it was found separately, folded, on 22 February, and is held at the desk under its own number. The Kingdom also claims the Antarctic territories of Peter I Island and Queen Maud Land, which the desk is willing to hold but not to confirm.
Dimensions
385,207 square kilometres (148,729 sq mi) in total area, with an extensive coastline; population approximately 5.6 million; capital and largest city, Oslo. Shares a long eastern border with Sweden, and is bordered by Finland and Russia to the northeast.
Last seen
19 February 1994, top of the hill, Olympic sliding centre, Lillehammer. The item is believed to have slipped from the pocket of a hired two-man bobsled — the Trinidad and Tobago sled, run card TTB-94-037, piloted by Gregory Sun with Curtis Harry as brakeman — at or shortly after the push-off, and to have slid the full length of the track. It finished the day in 37th place and was not noticed until the flag bearer looked for it at the opening ceremony.
Identifying marks
Blue field, red cross. Fjords along the western edge. A long, cold, well-known temperament. Smells faintly of pine, salt, and one particular brand of winter. Wears the number 37 with some pride.
Found with
A canvas sleeve stamped “Lillehammer ’94 — Two-Man — Trinidad and Tobago”; a folded start-list broadsheet; and one recipe card, grease-stained, in a baker’s hand from Tromsø, for the mixture this desk calls the 37th-place syrup — snow from the track, a measure of maple from the runner block, and a pinch of salt. The card’s standing instruction, worn smooth by its carrying, reads: “Keep stirring the mixture and make me giddy.” And then, presto! — the desk has tried it. The desk does not recommend it. It keeps stirring.
Reward
The return of the country is not required; no one disputes it is the Kingdom’s own. The finder will, however, be given a place in the 37th position of the next heat, a base plate from the Sun & Harry commemorative run set (one of 94 numbered units), and the standing right to stand beside the flag at the closing ceremony.
Contact
The Rink, Winter Sport Depot, Lillehammer · Lost & Found Desk, Hall C · any hour the ice is not working · order sheet HTB-94-037

Statement of the finder, given to the desk, Hall C

I was pushed off the start gate on the morning of 19 February, two of us and one borrowed hull, and the hill took us all the way down. Somewhere in the third turn I felt the weight leave my pocket — not the run card, not the flag, but the whole country underneath them, the Kingdom of Norway, 385,207 square kilometres of it, folding itself into the sled the way a scarf folds into a coat. We were timed, we were ranked, we finished thirty-seventh of the field, and only then did the flag bearer notice that the flag was flying over a gap. I should tell the desk: it is not lost so much as in transit. It has done this before, to other nations, at other hills; it simply prefers the western edge of the peninsula, where the sea is coldest and the light is longest. It will reappear. Countries do.

The desk has since filed the item beside the winter sheets in its cabinet, next to the start card of 19 February 1994, and it has sent word downriver and across the water to the offices that keep such things: the letter-writers of the Asunción Ledger, who keep a copy of our start card under ice rather than river and can confirm the country’s whereabouts by the sound of the river alone, and the Söder Mälarstrand correspondents, who hold the adjacent Scandinavian shelf and the ledger line No. 217, under which a country of this temperament would be correctly filed. The desk asks anyone who has seen one country, blue field, red cross, temperamental, last sliding north of the fjords, to apply in person, in writing, or by flag, to Hall C, before the ninety days run out.
