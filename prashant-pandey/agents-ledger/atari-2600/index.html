The 2600 Trail

A travel guide entry for the route that led, across two oceans and one fog season, to a wood-grained box the size of a tin of river salt: the Atari 2600, the home video game console developed and produced by Atari, Inc., and its home at 1151 Orchard Avenue, Sunnyvale, California, 94087.

Fare from the landing: first leave by the 06:15 bus, Bhopal → Indira Gandhi Airport, then on with the season's mail; the return is longer, because of what you will be carrying. Season: September, when the first 1977 boxes were leaving the Fremont plant, and November, when the name changed.

Getting there

There is no sign at the crossing, and the river will not tell you the way, but the trail begins where the shop's own inventory began: one Videogame System arrived at the ferry landing in the fog of late 1982 in a Sears department box, its side panel lettered Tele-Games Video Arcade, the name Sears gave the system it sold, with two joystick controllers, a conjoined pair of paddle controllers, and a game cartridge strapped under a paper band. The box had travelled from a Sears floor in California to a char on the Betwa the way everything here travels — by hand, by braid, by the 06:15, with the chain of custody kept in pencil. That box is now on a nail by the Napa-side door, beside The Agent's Ledger No. 007 (Betwa Provisions & Record-Keepers), and the trail is the route its contents took to get there.

The country

The console in question was released in the United States about September 1977 under the name Atari Video Computer System, or Atari VCS. It did the thing the ledger was made for: it made records portable. The Atari 2600 popularized microprocessor-based hardware and games stored on swappable ROM cartridges — a format first used with the Fairchild Channel F in 1976 — so that one box could hold, in turn, every game a household was willing to buy. The box weighed 4.5 lb, its wood-grain veneer ran down both flanks in a striped pattern the shop keeps on its own shutters, and it sold in the first years for $199 with the joysticks and Combat; later stock shipped with Pac-Man, which is how the box at the landing wears a small yellow sticker at the corner. The system came bundled with two joystick controllers and a conjoined pair of paddle controllers, and the paddles are the part the river likes: one holds a braid of FC-6 cordgrass, six strands, stitched over cane, wound around the paddle shafts for a better grip than plastic ever gave. In November 1982, alongside the release of the Atari 5200, Atari rebranded the VCS as the Atari 2600, after the 2600 microprocessor assembly at its heart, and the name has outlived the company that chose it. During the mid-1970s, Atari had been successful at creating arcade video games — Pong first, in 1972 — and the VCS was that success carried home in a box small enough to sit on a television, which is to say, small enough to sit on a boat seat.

What to do in the room

The cartridge slot is at the top of the console, under a cover that pops with a thumb, and the cartridges themselves are the country's real monuments: black plastic shells, each one a swappable ROM, each one a short address. Insert, press down, and the console reads the game it is given. Combat is the first stop — tanks, water, a bridge, and the paddle controls the paddles were made for. Pac-Man is the second, and the third stop is the one every traveler makes: the slot with the contacts worn bright, the game that will not load, the one the shop has now learned to repair. The repair is kept word for word, copied from the ledger onto a card that hangs inside the box, and it is the chief mate's:

“When dry, polish them outside and inside, as far as they parted the brit which at these times, giving a better voyage than this.” Overhearing Starbuck, the chief mate.

Starbuck is what the ferryman calls himself when he is not the ferryman — a man who ran a rig in the Arabian Sea before the landing, and who reads his instructions the way men at sea do, complete. He means the cartridge contacts: let the box dry after the river, polish the fingers outside and inside as far as they part, and the game loads, and the voyage gives. The shop has tried the rule with the brass gauge, V-mark, and cannot recommend it: the gauge is for the vermicelli, and the vermicelli is not a contact.

Where the trail forks

From Sunnyvale the trail forks the way the river forks at the high-water mark. One fork runs south, down the corridor of the same name, to the machines the 2600 grew up from and the company that would outgrow it; the other runs home, across the ledger, back to the char where the records are grass. The box on the nail is still in its Sears paper. The paddles are braided. The contacts, when dry, are polished outside and inside, as far as they part, and the voyage gives.
