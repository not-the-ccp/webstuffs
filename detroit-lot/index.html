Proceeds of the cedar crate · consigned across one river and one bridge

Lot 143

Detroit: A River, a Bridge, and a Ledger

( dih–TROYT, locally also DEE–troyt ) · Wayne County, Michigan · the river bank facing Windsor, Ontario

⚑ est. 140–180 USD

The lot, as consigned

Consigned by the Wadsworth kitchen at Ann Arbor, Michigan, and travelled in the same cedar crate that made Lot 142 on Day One, this lot is the crate's second day at the block. It crossed the state line as the first frost was up, went over the Ambassador Bridge at half past ten in the morning, and stood that afternoon in a kitchen where the bread is baked by the river's arithmetic and the ledger is kept in pencil. The consignment is one map, one card, one leaf, and one ribbon — four things, in a crate that held eleven cards and now holds its memory of eleven.

The map is a hand-folded sheet of the Detroit River bend, the river drawn twice — once in ink, once in dashes — with Belle Isle, the banks, and a pencil note on the far shore: Windsor, Ontario, the other side of the same water. Detroit, the sheet's own legend explains at full length, is the most populous city in the state of Michigan, the 26th‑most populous city in the United States, and the largest U.S. city on the Canada–United States border, sitting on the bank of the Detroit River directly across from Windsor. The 1926 Grizzlies season card, original, one of the eleven, is pinned at the bend with a red thumbtack — the bench at the Hardwood Emporium will recognize the line art, the 14–2, and the low rafters; the card's full account is at 1925–26 Southern Branch Grizzlies — Official Season Card, Centennial Reissue, and the card's reissue ribbon, the one that was sent to this very kitchen as a bookmark, is in the lot beside the original, like an echo wearing the same clothes.

One leaf of the Wadsworth ledger, ruled and dated, taken out of the book for the crate and never put back. The counts, as pencilled:

A cultural city, the leaf begins, and then runs out of pencil. The bench has left it unfinished, the way the kitchen leaves the bread: done when it is done, not a minute after.

Condition & the slip in the fold

Map: foxing at the first fold, one crease gone through; the second fold is strong, because it is the one the counts sat under. Card: as found in Lot 142's crate, the spine cracked at the fold, the 14–2 legible, the red tack in place. Ribbon: forest green with gilt, a reissue copy, worn to a soft edge from a season under maple bread. Leaf: pencil sound, corners squared by a thumb that knew what it was doing.

In the second fold of the map, under the river's dashed line and against the counts, the room found one more thing: a slip of kitchen paper, twice folded, with a single sentence in a hand neat enough to be electoral. The catalogue has kept it word for word, and the buyer will have it, because a lot is what is in the crate and nothing less:

Alice waited till she got back to the Editor .

Slip in the second fold of the map, pencil, the kitchen's own paper. Alice is the bookkeeper of the Wadsworth kitchen — the same hand as the counts — and the Editor is what the kitchen calls its ledger, which edits nothing and rules everything. The sentence makes sense exactly once: the counts of Detroit were pencilled not at the river, but at the kitchen table, and only after Alice had come home to the book that would keep them. The bench has checked the two hands against each other. They match.

Provenance & the perch, again

The chain of the lot is short, which the bench considers a virtue. The crate left San Diego with the tide tables and eleven cards at Lot 142. The kitchen at Ann Arbor took one card — the one for this river — and one reissue ribbon for the maple bread, card no. 214, kept under it on the counter and filed at Huron River Maple Bread — Wadsworth Family Recipe Ledger. In between, the bench's old perch question travelled the other way: a keeper in Ghent building a tall oak case for a grey bronzeback, Dendrelaphis caudolineatus, asking whether the reissue's ribbon marker would hold a snake; the answer was oak and sisal, not ribbon, and the keeper's log — vents, brass hook, tin under the bench — stands open at Grey bronzeback — Build Log. A ribbon that will not hold a perch has, at least, held a city's counts for a season, and the bench has decided that is a full working life. The bench has followed the ribbon one road north past this one: a cousin of the kitchen keeps the field notes of a naturalist at the shore of Lake Cadillac, where the forest-green-and-gilt reissue now bands the pages and the town's count of 10,371 sits underlined twice, the ledger way — Cadillac, Michigan — Field Notes of a Naturalist, No. 27.

Buyer's notes, Day Two

  • Estimate 140–180 USD; the room accepts that a map with eleven memories may price at twelve.
  • Lots cross with the crate's other days; a buyer of 142 and 143 together is offered the crate's lid, which has declined, in writing, to travel by air.
  • The slip in the fold passes with the lot. It is small, it is one sentence, and it was in the crate.
  • The Ambassador Bridge crossing is not included; the lot crossed once and considers itself delivered.
