2Hazard identification

Classified as Privilege, category 9: miscellaneous material, assessed case by case. Not a dangerous good under the Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act. The principal hazards are legal rather than physical — loss of protection through waiver, over-claiming (everything asserted privileged), and under-protection (privilege not asserted when it applies).

  • H-PRV-1 — Protection may be lost if the communication is disclosed to a third party (waiver).
  • H-PRV-2 — No established privilege class admits automatic protection; each communication must be tested on its own facts.
  • H-PRV-3 — The privilege belongs to the client; only the client may waive it.

CAUTION (legal)  Apply the Section 3 test before disclosure. See Section 15 for regulatory authority.

Before Gruenke, only communications falling within a narrow set of established privilege classes could gain protection. Since [1991] 3 S.C.R. 263 the test is applied communication by communication, and no category admits automatic protection.

4First-aid measures

Inadvertent disclosure: do not treat the material as waived outright. Notify the recipient, request return under seal, isolate the document from the record, and seek a sealing order or the court’s direction. Disclosure into the public record: apply to strike or seal; an in-camera review may be ordered. If taken up by the press: seek a publication ban and document the chain of custody from first disclosure.

5Fire-fighting measures

Not applicable; the material does not burn. In the unlikely event that a privilege claim ignites in the press, do not feed it. Withdraw, correct the record, and let it burn out. Historically, overbroad privilege claims have twice shifted the scope of protection by a full level of court.

Celluloid of the nitrate variety is less forgiving: the Eiter weather almanac measures a thunder cake's oven time as roughly 950 feet of nitrate stock, and warns that nothing so inflammable should be left in heat unattended (Eiter — Weather Almanac Entry).

6Accidental release measures

If privileged material is inadvertently produced in discovery, stop review immediately, isolate the document, and notify opposing counsel under a clawback agreement. Do not read, copy, or use inadvertently produced material; seek the court’s direction before further handling.

7Handling and storage

Handle on a strict need-to-know basis. Mark every copy “PRIVILEGED & CONFIDENTIAL — SOLICITOR–CLIENT”. Store in the solicitor’s file under access control; keep an access log. Never leave material on a press table, in a public docket, or in an unsealed envelope.

8Exposure controls / personal protection

No occupational exposure limit can be set for a legal privilege. Engineering controls: need-to-know access, confidentiality agreements, encrypted transmission. Personal protection: discretion — discuss privileged content only where it cannot be overheard or intercepted.

10Stability and reactivity

Stability: stable under stare decisis; the test has been reapplied in subsequent cases. Conditions to avoid: blanket assertions of privilege, waiver, and public-interest challenges. Incompatible materials: the phrase “everything is privileged”, press releases, and filing in the public record without a court order.

11Toxicological information

The doctrine itself is non-toxic. The waiver episode carries the only real toxicity: once privileged material is disclosed voluntarily and knowingly, protection may be lost for the whole class of related communications.

12Ecological information

Not applicable; the doctrine’s ecology is the common law of evidence, and it recurs wherever privilege is claimed — in Ontario, British Columbia, and every common-law province between.

13Disposal considerations

There is nothing to destroy. Privileged files are retained, not destroyed: closed solicitor files are held under provincial law society rules, commonly seven years after a file closes. Do not shred to conceal privilege; an intact file with a valid privilege claim remains protected.

14Transport information

May be transported between solicitor and client by post, secure email, courier, or in person; no quantity restrictions. Mark the envelope or subject line “PRIVILEGED & CONFIDENTIAL”. Do not file in the public record without a court order.

15Regulatory information

Governing authorities: common law of Canada; Supreme Court of Canada; Canada Evidence Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-5; provincial evidence statutes; law society rules of professional conduct. Key authority: R v Gruenke, [1991] 3 S.C.R. 263 (Sopinka J. for the Court).
