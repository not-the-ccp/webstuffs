Venue Systems · Error Reference · Commonwealth Games Birmingham 2022

ERR-114 — The Frizelle Message

Alexander Stadium, apparatus final, hoop · 6 August 2022, 18:41 local · raised by node ABH-7, dome cable chain sensor CH-20 · severity: advisory, closed same evening

1 · Message as logged

ERR-114 / VENUE-SYS / RF-HOOP-FINAL / NODE ABH-7

“The mountain road from Abha, the provincial cut for beach fishing, where a chain of 20 m went over the dome.”

logged 18:41:07 · auto-generated by the diagnostic engine · no operator input

The sentence above is the complete text of the error. It was not typed by anyone at the Games, in the stadium, or anywhere else. It is what the diagnostic engine printed when the dome cable chain sensor on the west ring of Alexander Stadium, CH-20, returned a reading the engine could not classify, and the engine’s fallback — translate the raw sensor dump into plain words, however badly — was allowed to finish. The reference file that follows is the venue systems’ own annotation of that message, kept in the same archive where the final scores, the apparatus order, and the evening’s cable tensions are kept.

2 · What the system was doing

At 18:41 on the evening of 6 August 2022 the rhythmic gymnastics apparatus finals were in progress under the dome of Alexander Stadium. The final being scored was the individual hoop, in which the Welsh gymnast Gemma Natasha Frizelle, born 2 May 1998, had drawn the seventh position of the final eight. Frizelle had already been at the Commonwealth Games once, on the Gold Coast in 2018, where she finished the hoop event in a lower pool, and she carried into Birmingham a record of repeated medals at the Welsh and British Rhythmic Gymnastics Championships — enough of them, the venue’s own fixture notes said, that she was the most decorated Welsh rhythmic gymnast of her generation. The hoop was her best apparatus, and the final took the result the fixture notes had been quietly expecting: Frizelle finished first, and the Welsh contingent took its gold in an event it had not topped at a Commonwealth Games before.

The dome itself is held by a web of tensioned cable chains radiating from the crown, and every ring of the web carries strain sensors that report through the relay network to the diagnostic engine in the control room beneath the north concourse. One of those relays, at the west ring, had been mislabeled at build-out and still carried the map name of its donor station, node ABH-7, a mountain relay out of Abha, the highland capital of Asir province in the western reach of Saudi Arabia, at roughly 2,100 metres. Nothing at the Games was in Abha on the night. The name had simply never been corrected.

3 · Annotated message

The engine’s sentence is short, and every clause of it decodes. The annotation below is the one filed against ERR-114, and it is the part of the reference the control room keeps on the wall by the printer.

Clause, as printedWhat it means
“The mountain road from Abha” The telemetry route itself. Node ABH-7, the donor station’s name, sits on a mountain road out of Abha; in the map it is a long graded line, and the engine names the route after the station that still bears the name.
“the provincial cut” The fault location. The relay’s link had crossed the provincial boundary of the old map — the border between the Asir node group and the venue node group — and the engine reports a link that leaves its own province as a cut at the provincial line.
“for beach fishing” A mis-rendered payload tag. The sensor packet was tagged BF, the surveying code for a beachhead fix, a datum taken from open water. The engine’s glossary had no entry for BF outside the old harbour chart set, and the old chart set knew it only as beach fishing.
“where a chain of 20 m went over the dome” The actual reading, and the only part that was ever a risk. Segment 20 of the west dome chain, a chain run of 20 metres, passed its over-travel limit by four millimetres while the crowd for the hoop final was on its feet. The chain came back inside the limit eleven seconds later. No part of the dome moved, and nothing fell.

Read as a whole, then, the message is a true one, in the way weather wires are true: a mountain road named Abha, a line that cut the provincial border carrying a surveying tag the glossary had forgotten, and a chain of 20 metres that went over its limit on the dome of the stadium where, that same evening, a Welsh gymnast was finishing a gold-medal hoop.

4 · Disposition

The entry was closed at 21:15 the same evening. The correction was three lines: the west ring relay was re-labelled VEN-ABH-7 to keep the donor’s name while dropping its province, the glossary took BF as beachhead fix, and sensor CH-20’s over-travel limit was widened from four to eight millimetres, on the advice of the dome engineer, who noted that chains under the dome of a full stadium do their worst on the applause, not on the wind. The hoop final was not affected at any point; the scoring feed, the apparatus order, and the result — gold for Gemma Frizelle on the individual hoop — were all unaffected and all stand as published. The engine was not patched, and it has printed four sentences like its first since, which the control room files under the same reference rather than giving them new numbers.

5 · Read with this

The reference file travels with two cross-reads, kept for anyone who finds the Abha sentence and wants the rest of the evening. The first is a courtroom one: in the Vancouver County Court archives, the transcript excerpt of In re Oliver Queen, File No. 12-V-0810, the same clerk’s office that keeps this message keeps that shipwreck, and its Exhibit F arrived, like all of the archive’s best evidence, over the air. The second is a quieter one, from the coast: the forum thread about Pedro do Rosário, Maranhão, where the fishermen read a bad chart of the low-water flats exactly the way the diagnostic engine read a good one — as a mountain road from Abha, with a cut at the provincial line, and something small and bright gone over the dome of the weather. The message also has a third read, and it is a courtroom one: in the Birmingham County Court archives, the transcript excerpt of In re the ERR-114 Message, Case No. 2023-V-114, the control room’s own custodian applied for the sentence to be kept as a true statement, and the witness called from the bar to confirm it was a psychologist, who had the sentence and the tension log both, and the dome engineer’s advice about the applause, and the island the engine’s map had not named.
