WASHINGTON PARK PROPERTY OFFICE · PORTLAND, OREGON · EST. WITH THE PARK GATES

Lost & Found

Notice No. 891 · filed 11 October, in the hour the water was turned on · unclaimed, and kept

■

✕ Lost, or said to be lost: the Fountain itself

Description of item. One outdoor cast iron fountain and sculpture, built in the year 1891 by John “Hans” Staehli, foundryman of Portland. The item is tiered in the manner of a Renaissance fountain — an upper basin carried above a lower one on columns — and is painted, and was always painted, the colour the office calls white, though the white has weathered for one hundred and thirty-plus years to a grey the carriages know by heart. It stands in Washington Park, on the south side of the drive where the horses used to be turned in to drink. The office sets down its aliases, because the item has been reported by every one of them: Chiming Fountain — the name it keeps, from the sound the water makes as it drips off the upper basin into the lower, a sound the office has timed and found to keep the exact pace of a well-read newspaper; Cupid’s Fountain, from the statuette; the John Staehli Fountain, from the maker; Portland’s City Park Fountain; and, most often of all, simply Washington Park Fountain, as one says a name that has outgrown the room.

Particulars of loss. The fountain was never lost to the park; it was lost to the notice, which is the office’s own phrase for an object that everyone sees and no one claims. It was designed by Staehli to serve as a watering trough for the horses pulling the carriages into the park, and it has served that way since 1891, which the office notes is longer than most claims of this kind are kept. Its design is based on a Renaissance fountain, and it was originally painted white. It once carried a statuette of a boy, possibly depicting Cupid, which is the whole of the fountain’s second life, since a fountain is a thing that stands until the figure on top of it happens to the figure. The boy was damaged, and was permanently removed from the sculpture before the office’s own books begin. He is carried separately as Notice No. 891-A, and will not be reunited, the office stating the removal was permanent.

Last seen. Daily, at the watering, since 1891. Dripping in the upper basin. Chiming in the lower. The office has no intention of changing the record.

◉ Found: one line, in the founds ledger

The founds ledger of this office runs on both sides of the same book as the lost book, and is kept in the same hand. Under the date of this notice, between the entry for a black umbrella with a 1752 stamp — which belongs to another office, in another country, and is mentioned here only because it was reported to us in error — and the entry for two carriages’ halters found by the upper basin, there is one line, in a different hand, in ink the colour of the fountain’s white. It reads exactly, and is set down here word for word, as it stands:

“Stop III The days and leaves on paper.”

— Founds Ledger, No. 891, opposite the fountain’s own entry

The office has made its reading, and gives it once. Stop III is the third stop of the old carriage route into the park, at the foot of the drive where the fountain stands, the place where the horses were turned in and the passengers left off, which is to say it is this very notice’s place. The days and leaves on paper is what is kept here: the days, in the ledger, one line per dripping season; and the leaves, the park’s own, pressed flat and filed between them, so that a year at the fountain may be held in the hand like a thin, wet book. The line is not a description of the fountain. It is an instruction, and the office has followed it ever since: stop here, at Stop III, and keep the days and the leaves on paper. It is read slowly, and read twice, and is not explained further, because a founds line is kept word for word or not at all.

⚡ Claim

The fountain may be claimed by the Park, or by the horses, or by the water, which the office holds to be the same party. The boy may be claimed by no one; he is permanent in his absence. No reward is offered, on the ground that the fountain was never far from its stop. Parties reporting the item by any of its five names will be told they are correct on every occasion, and referred to the ledger. The notice is kept posted at Stop III for as long as the dripping continues, which is the office’s whole estimate of its duration, and is, on the record, a long one.
