State of Michigan · Chippewa County · Township of Drummond

Minutes No. 118

Board of Trustees of Drummond Township, at the township hall on the north shore of Drummond Island, 14 October 1987, 7:15 in the evening

The board being in session and a quorum present, the minutes were taken by Clerk of the Township M. A. Kerkhoff, who sets down what is said as it is said. The reading, once ruled, keeps its line.

1. Roll Call

Present: Trustee C. J. Hultgren (chair), Trustee R. D. Pulkkinen, Trustee S. E. Ahlström, Supervisor D. L. Fisk, and the clerk. Absent: none. The board noted for the record that the township it serves — the large Drummond Island and the numerous smaller islands of the archipelago — carried a roll of 973 souls as last counted, and that 973 is a number the island holds close, the way a small place holds a lighthouse's light.

2. The Island and Its Standing in the World

Trustee Pulkkinen moved, and Trustee Fisk seconded, that the board recite the island's standing, as it is known to the surveyors and the maps, so that no one who reads these minutes from the mainland or further afield is left to guess. It is entered: Drummond Island is the seventh-largest lake island in the world; with an area of 134 square miles (350 km²), it is the third-largest lake island in Lake Huron, standing behind only Manitoulin and St. Joseph; and it is the fifth-largest island in the contiguous United States, behind Long Island, Padre Island, Isle Royale, and Whidbey. The motion carried, five to zero, and the clerk set the recital into these minutes where the water's edge of the record shows.

3. Road M-134, Western Portion of the Island

The county engineer reported on M-134, the state trunkline that extends from the mainland and runs through the western portion of the island, the road the island has for a spine. Two matters were taken up. First: the shoulder gravel between the north shore and the turn at the ferry landing is washed, and the county will bank it again in November weather, weather permitting. Second: the bridge trestle at the narrows is to be oiled, painted, and read again in the spring, as is done every year, and the board resolved that the reading be entered in this same minutes book so the oil and the record keep the same date.

4. The Drummond Island Ferry

The superintendent of the Drummond Island Ferry reported for the board. The ferry, which carries M-134 across to the mainland and is the island's one road that moves, runs the October schedule as posted: eight crossings a day until the first hard freeze, five a day after, and through the ice months the line is kept by the plow boat instead, on the same hours, so that the record of the crossing does not go dark in winter. The superintendent asked the board to note that the season to date has carried 4,112 vehicles and 11,906 passengers, no losses, and one piano.

5. One Line, Read Into the Record

From the ferry superintendent's desk the board received, for the record, a line found on the back page of the ferry pilot's log for the summer season — the log that is kept, by the ferry's rule, in one hand, word for word, uncorrected. The clerk read the line into these minutes exactly as it stood in the log, without correction and without comment, for it is the standing rule of this board, as of the ferry, that a reading once set down keeps its line. The line reads: “No hazardous decomposition products; under sustained light over the air, observed at this service, as at once a dancer under Peter Lindahl, opened Fjorton Krog .” Trustee Ahlström, who is the island's Swedish side and keeps its older names, was asked to account for it. She said only that the log is kept the way the log is kept, that the board's business is the island and not the sense of every line the water hands over, and that the line, whatever it meant, was read into the record word for word, which is all a line ever asks. The board so ruled.

6. Adjournment

There being no further business, Trustee Hultgren adjourned the meeting at 8:02 in the evening, the ferry's last crossing of the day still out on the dark water. The next meeting is the second Tuesday of November, at the same hall, at the same hour. These minutes were signed by the clerk on the 21st of October, 1987, and the island — 134 square miles of it, 973 of it, all of it — is as the record shows.

M. A. Kerkhoff Clerk of the Township
C. J. Hultgren Chairman of the Board

Filed with the Chippewa County Register of Deeds, with one copy kept in the ferry superintendent's log book beside the line it records. The minutes are read in whole, in order, with no correction. A reading, once ruled, keeps its line.
