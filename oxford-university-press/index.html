The Press — a game of books and charters

Rulebook, page 14 of 22 · The Fifteen Delegates and the Year of the Charter

The box

The Press is a game for two to four players, ages 1478 and up, for one hour or one thermos, whichever empties first. In it each player runs a university press from the first book printed in Oxford in 1478 down to the present, and the object is to out-print, out-catalogue and out-sell the other players before the year counter wears through. The press at the centre of the game is the publishing house of the University of Oxford — the largest university press in the world — and the box holds: one board showing the College side and the river bank; fifteen Delegate tokens, each a named academic; one Vice-Chancellor token; one Charter die with faces of 1478, 1534, 1586 and 1600; one small press stand, or in the scrub edition the flat lid of the thermos; a ledger of seed, the currency of the game; and one misbound page from a 1923 school prize, bound in cloth, with a single caption on it. That page is not filler. It is the rule of the paddy, and page 16.

One seat at the table is the senior chair. The second-oldest university press is this one, Oxford, after Cambridge, which was founded in 1534; the player who draws the Charter die showing 1534 at the setup deals takes that chair, deals in, and is treated by the rules as first and as last in the same breath. The senior player acts first in every round, but a senior player who scores on the last action of the round gives the points to the press that moves next. Cambridge keeps its chair. It is a small thing, and it changes everything.

Setup — the appointment of the Delegates

The press in the game, as the press in fact, is a department of the University of Oxford, governed by a body of fifteen academics, the Delegates of the Press, appointed by the vice-chancellor. At the table, one player is named vice-chancellor for the sitting — the player who named the ford first at the last sitting, or, at a first sitting, whoever is closest to the water — and the vice-chancellor appoints the fifteen: one token to each of the player's presses in turn, then the remainder to the common pool at the middle of the board. The Delegates of the Press are led by the Secretary to the Delegates, a token apart from the fifteen, who serves as chief executive and as the press's major representative at the other tables. The Secretary belongs to the vice-chancellor for the sitting and may be placed with any press once per round, adding that press's task for that one year to the score as if it were worth double.

A round — the year

Each round is a year, and the year counter begins at 1478. In a round, every Delegate on the table performs one task on one press:

  • Print — a Delegate at the press stand. Three print points. A press with three Delegates printing in one year completes a full title; the first full title of the game scores ten points and is called the First Book, and no press may claim another First Book while the 1478 counter stands.
  • Catalogue — a Delegate at the shelf. Two catalogue points, and the title is protected from the paddy for that year.
  • Sell — a Delegate at the river gate. One point, and one seed from the ledger is struck out in that press's name.
  • Charter — a Delegate at the crown table. No points on its own, but see the year 1586.

The game is a department of the University, and the University does not hurry; but the counter does. When the counter reads 1586, the year the press was granted the legal right to print books by decree, the charter is read aloud and any press with four or more Delegates at the crown table takes the charter bonus of five points and a strand of the double helix. The double helix is the game's long memory: a player who has held one through a full year of 1586 and the next may spend it once to appoint one extra Delegate that round, even if the pool is empty. It costs the strand. It is worth it, as the thread at the ford found out.

The paddy — from the misbound page

Every box contains the misbound page: a single leaf, set in 1923 cloth, that came out of the school prize shoebox at Maitland and was never explained at the library history hour. In the rulebook it is page 16, and it runs exactly as it stands:

IN PURSUING FOUR-FOOTED BEASTS OF GAME is called _paddy_, both in Chili and Buenos Ayres.

It is read at the start of the game, once, by the senior player, and then it is the rule. Each press keeps one beast of game, a four-legged token standing beside its titles on the river bank. Once per round, after all Delegates have acted, the player to the left of the senior chair moves the paddy — the single four-footed beast in the box, shared by the table, belonging to no press — one space down the river bank toward the nearest beast of game it has not already caught this sitting. When the paddy reaches a beast, that beast's title is pulled from the catalogue and reissued: it loses one point and is protected by nothing until re-catalogued. A beast standing beside a press that is chartered in that year is not touched; the paddy is a field creature and will not come to the crown table. The name is kept word for word because the caption was kept word for word, and because the Scrub Board has not yet decided whether the line describes a bird, a hunter or a book, and the rule works exactly as well under all three readings. The range of the beast is a matter for the ledger the registry keeps at Ojos del Salado — Field Manual FM-31, Chapter 14, where the corrections are entered in the same hand as the altitudes.

Scoring, and the scrub edition

The sitting ends when the counter reaches the fourth face of the Charter die, 1600, or when the seed ledger is struck out. Points as scored: print, catalogue, the First Book, the charter bonus, and one point per strand of the double helix still held. The highest total runs the press; the runner-up runs the annex. For the scrub edition, played on the grass at the ford rather than on the board, the currency is seed by the gram — two grams a strand, as the FOUND ad in the Scrub Ledger priced it for No. 418 — the thermos lid is the press stand, and the thermos itself is the clock. The thread that playtested it is Red-backed fairywren — The Scrub Board, Thread No. 418, and the sitting that fixed this page of the rulebook was the one clare_g called the thermos sitting, when the cock held his red through November and the paddy went the whole bank and caught nothing, which the ledger records as a draw, both kept. The Met's registrar has filed a claim on one of the box's components and may be reached at The Met Register, Classifieds & Museum Notices; the claim is under review, as all the claims are, in marble time.

Print a round — the Delegates' roll

Press the button to roll the fifteen Delegates for one year: each is dealt to a task — print (3), catalogue (2), sell (1), or the crown table (charter, five points for the press in 1586 and later with four or more on duty) — and the year advances from 1478. The running total is the press's standing at the end of the sitting, 1600.

The year stands at 1478. The Delegates wait at the college steps. The paddy is in the bank, facing downstream.
