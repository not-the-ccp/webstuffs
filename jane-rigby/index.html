Excerpt of the Record · Pages 88–92

GREENBELT — October 3, 2024 · 9:41 a.m.
Before the HONORABLE DALE R. PRITCHARD, U.S. District Judge
STENOGRAPHER: Colleen M. Voss, C.R. No. 203

MR. OKAFOR: Your Honor, the National Archive of Flight Records, on behalf of the petitioner, calls Dr. Jane R. Rigby, Senior Project Scientist of the James Webb Space Telescope, Goddard Space Flight Center, Building 7.

DR. RIGBY: (Oath administered by the Stenographer.)

Q. — Dr. Rigby, you joined the Goddard Space Flight Center in 2010 as deputy operations project scientist for what was then a planned telescope, a mirror of eighteen gold-beryllium segments, folded five times and carried out the length of a football field with its sunshield?

A. — That is correct. I came to Greenbelt in 2010. The telescope was still folded, still on the drawing and on the clean-room bench, and by the time I was named senior project scientist it had been unfolded in the void around L2 and was looking at things older than the first light the Hubble could reach.

Q. — This petition concerns a single draft of a speech, in the witness’s hand, dated the second week of July of this year, which the Respondent produced from the binder marked OFFICE OF THE SENIOR PROJECT SCIENTIST — CEREMONIAL DRAFTS, 2022–2024 in Room 718, Building 7?

A. — Yes. The draft was for the Presidential Medal of Freedom, the one I was to receive in July from President Biden. I wrote it for myself to say the plain thing: that a person from Seaford, Delaware, with a bachelor’s degree from the State University of Pennsylvania and a master’s and a doctorate from the University of Arizona, ends up with the most important job in her career attached to a mirror a hundred and two years of argument away from her home town. That is the whole speech. Seven hundred words, no adjectives.

Q. — The petitioner asserts the draft is a document of the public record, because the medal was delivered in the Rose Garden before the cameras, and the witness’s remarks there track the draft line by line?

A. — The remarks track the draft except for the last paragraph, which I rewrote on the stairs of the West Wing. I wanted to say something about the young students who come to Building 7 with the question about whether a person like them belongs in a room like that, and I wrote the last paragraph so that the answer is yes, and it is not a favor.

MR. OKAFOR: Your Honor, I offer Exhibit 14, the audio of the ceremony, for identification of the last paragraph.

THE COURT: Admitted. Ms. Voss, play the segment marked 00:14:07 to 00:15:31. (Audio played. The stenographer’s notes for the segment are marked AUDIO, not transcribed. The Court directs the audio be entered into the record as a writing.)

Q. — Dr. Rigby, the petitioner’s expert notes that one line on the audio appears to run together with a line from a separate recording on the same reel — a line about a racecourse, apparently. Can you tell us, on this record, what you think the line says, at the point where the two recordings overlap?

A. — (after the segment is paused, the witness listening, and a pause of some seconds) I think that is only a few kilometres down the backstretch ran its service times on the other New Zealander kept in grass since 1984.

THE COURT: Let the answer stand exactly as spoken. Page 90. We are not going to clean up the overlap, Dr. Rigby. The record keeps it.

A. — Understood, Your Honor. The overlap is in the tape, not in the speech. The speech is seven hundred words, and the last paragraph is the one I wrote on the stairs.

MR. OKAFOR: The petitioner rests its hearing on the transcript. We ask only that the draft be delivered to the Archive with the audio segment 00:14:07–00:15:31 annexed, unedited, the overlap and all.

THE COURT: So ordered. The Respondent will deliver the draft to the petitioner’s custodian, Mr. Haddad, by November 15, 2024, in a folder of its own, with the audio annexed exactly as it ran. A person who folds a mirror five times and unfolds it in the dark will not have her own words folded again on the record. Page 92 is so noted, and the answer on page 90 stands as the record speaks it.
