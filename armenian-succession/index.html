I. What the set is for

The subject is the war that was never quite a war: Rome and Parthia over the throne of Armenia, the buffer kingdom between the two empires, fought from A.D. 58 to A.D. 63. Armenia had been a Roman client since the days of Augustus, but in A.D. 52/53 the Parthian king Vologases I had his own candidate, Tiridates, installed on the throne with a Parthian garrison in Artaxata. The young Nero, fresh onto the imperial throne, took the insult personally; the war was the only major foreign campaign of his reign, and the only one he ever let a general run. That general was Gnaeus Domitius Corbulo, who arrived at the eastern frontier in A.D. 58 and began with the kind of rapid success the box art promises and the manual does not overstate.

The set gives you both halves of the argument. The Roman side: three legions in bronze and bone — X Fretensis, III Cyrenaica and XIV Gemina — plus the auxilia, the legionary fortress at the Euphrates landing, and Corbulo himself, 11 mm tall, in the pose he is known for: standing over a map of Pontus with his back to the room. The Parthian side: cataphracts with the horse's necks roped to the saddles, the archers of the parthica order, and the rival kings, Artavasdes II of Armenia and Tiridates with his diadem, both included because a succession war is not one man. The Armenian buffer is the third faction in the box, which is the whole point of the subject.

II. Contents, as inventoried

  • 480 figures, 2 mm at the crown, sprues of bone, unattached, in the order of the legions
  • 1 pontoon bridge over the Euphrates at Rhoda, 63 mm clear span, twelve piers, the deck re-pegged in this pressing
  • 1 die-cut Artaxata, the winter palace included, in card the thickness of the old eighty-column stock

  • 3 kings, 2 diadems (spare), 1 general with a map
  • 1 field of Rima, A.D. 63, in grey card, with the two army dispositions printed on the back of the box lid
  • The Operator’s Notes, sixteen sections, and the balance sheet of the campaign

III. The campaign, as the set plays it

Start where the war actually started. A.D. 58: Corbulo, proconsul of Pontus, crosses the Euphrates over the new bridge at Rhoda, and the box lets you cross it the same way — twelve piers, each one you have to peg. His first winter is spent on the road to the capital: the fortified camps go up in card, the supply lines run from Sinope, and Tigranes VI of Pontus is pressed into service as the Roman party’s candidate for Armenia, a move of a kind you can see the logic of only when the pieces are on the table. A.D. 59–61: the siege of Artaxata, Tiridates stripped and sent to Rome in chains, Tigranes installed in his place, and a Parthian counter-stroke that the set renders as one grey card storm pushing three files of cataphracts back across the river. The winter of A.D. 62 is the campaign the set was named for: Corbulo wintering on the far side of the Euphrates, feeding the legions, keeping the bridge, doing the quiet work that the box art is too small to show. Then A.D. 63: the field of Rima in Media, Corbulo against Vologases in person, the engagement that ends in the king of kings riding back to Ctesiphon and the throne of Armenia left, on the balance, to Rome.

IV. Operator’s Notes, standing orders

Section 3 — The diadems. Two are supplied for one reason: a succession war. If a diadem breaks before the field of Rima is fought, replace it from the box and do not discuss the casualty.

Section 5 — The bridge at Rhoda. The bridge is the longest single piece in the set and the one that fails. It is meant to fail at most once per campaign, and the piers are re-peggable; the second bridge in the history of the subject was real, and this one is honest about that.

Section 7 — The buffer. Armenia is not a side that joins one side. The kit plays it as a kingdom with its own files, its own supply line, and a throne that changes hands in year one and again in year five. Do not paint the Armenians in either army’s livery.

Section 9 — General Corbulo. He may not be removed from the table for the duration of the winter of A.D. 62, whatever is happening to his legions. The war was his; the kit keeps the point.

Section 11 — Paint. The grey card of the field is unpainted as supplied. The storm of the counter-stroke is the only weather in the set and it is played unpainted, on purpose, in the manner of the build log of the wagon line at 1:87, which leaves the winter weathering to the logbook and not the layout — a rule we read and adopted, and which this box obeys.

Section 12 — The accounting. The balance sheet of the campaign is in the lid: what Rome paid for Armenia, in legions, winters, and one young emperor’s patience, and what it had at the end. The arithmetic of the entry at our press, Recalde & the ledger that keeps the house admitting both forms of its own records, is the standing example of a balance kept without complaint; the set ships with its own sheet in the same spirit, eighty columns wide, and it balances.

Section 13 — Disposal and record. For the box, the figures, and the grey card storm: See Section 13 for disposal and Section 16 for the record, eventually.

Section 16 — The record. The record is the field of Rima with the armies left where the war left them, one diadem in Artaxata, and the bridge across. The kit ends the way the subject ended: on the bank, in the balance, with the throne kept. Return to the front page of the site, where the box is filed under Pressing No. 9.
