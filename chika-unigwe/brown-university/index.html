RUNOUT › SIDE A · “THE COLLEGE IN THE ENGLISH COLONY” · AWELE 1764 · G

Notes, by the counter

PROVIDENCE was not written so much as cut. In the winter of 2024–25 the Ghent counter of Awele Books & Papers received a crate from 14 Awo Road, Oshodi: forty-nine reels, a jar of maize, one Aba green cloth bookmark, and a note in Chika Unigwe’s hand saying only, Play it in the street, not in the studio. What follows is the record that came out of it — ten sides-long tracks laid down over two sessions, one in a lecture hall at Brown University in Providence, Rhode Island, and one in the window seat of the Ghent counter, where the lamps burn in two inks and the order ledger is never closed.

Brown University is a private Ivy League research university in Providence, Rhode Island, United States, and the seventh-oldest institution of higher education in the country, founded in 1764 as the College in the English Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations. It is one of the nine colonial colleges chartered before the American Revolution, and it was the first American college to codify that admission and instruction of students was to be equal regardless of the religious affiliation of students — a line the singers treat as the quietest melody on the whole LP, repeated in the third bar of Side A and never once announced. The university is home to the oldest applied mathematics program in the country and the oldest engineering program in the Ivy League, and it was one of the early doctoral-granting institutions, which is to say that the word doctor has been spoken in Providence, deliberately, for a very long time.

The title is not a promise. Providence is a name the city gave itself, and the sessions give it back. Side A is the charter; Side B is the works — mathematics, engineering, the doctorate, and the four brothers, one of whom, Nicholas Brown, is the brother the university is named for, the only one of the four to leave money to the institution that carried his surname. The outro is an endpaper: an Aba green cloth, sewn in, eighteen centimetres, doing what cloth does best, which is hold.

All rights in the music are reserved by the shop. The dirges are the singers’.

  1. A1 The College in the English Colony 4:17

    The founding of 1764, sung without an accent and against a slow organ: the college of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations before it had a surname, before it had the name of the brother, before the street had the name of the sisters.

  2. A2 Nine (Three Bars, Spoken) 0:52

    The nine colonial colleges chartered before the Revolution, counted down by four voices, one of them a librarian. The count stops at one on purpose.

  3. A3 Seventh 2:11

    Counting from the very first: the university is the seventh-oldest institution of higher education in the United States. Six older voices, one younger, and a silence where an eighth would be.

  4. A4 No Denominal Test 5:03

    The charter of 1765 as the longest sustained note on the record: the first American college to codify that admission and instruction of students was to be equal regardless of the religious affiliation of students. No one in the hall is asked who they pray to. The organ holds the bar.

  5. A5 Rhode Island & Providence Plantations 6:44

    The full colonial name, taken slowly, the way a train takes a plain — entirely and without argument. The longest piece on Side A and the only one that admits the weather.

  1. B1 Four Brothers 3:33

    The four Browns, and the one of them, Nicholas, whose bequest became the university. The other three founded a college of their own in New Hampshire and keep their own record; this one is only about the brother who stayed in the name.

  2. B2 Applied Mathematics (Oldest Room) 5:12

    The oldest applied mathematics program in the country, recorded in a room where the blackboard is older than the chairs. Sums are worked aloud; no one erases. The chalk dust is left on the microphone.

  3. B3 Ivy Engineering 3:48

    The oldest engineering program in the Ivy League, in a brass-key tempo. A beam is drawn, tested, redrawn. The engine that runs the track is the track.

  4. B4 Doctor 8:21

    The longest track on the LP, built from one word, because Brown was one of the early doctoral-granting institutions and the word has been spoken in Providence, deliberately, for a very long time. The word is never used as a title; only as a note.

  5. B5 Aba Green Endpaper (Outro) 1:59

    Cloth, sewn in, eighteen centimetres, doing what cloth does. The turntable slows to a stop while the words are still going, which is how the record ends, and how the ledger stays open.

The torn slip

Pressed into the sleeve of copy No. 041/500, between the track listing and the endpaper, the press run carried a slip torn from the counter ledger at 14 Awo Road, where two entries had bled together when the ledger was bound. The trust did not ask what it meant. The press did not ask either. The slip reads, in full and without annotation:

The book did it for her.” A. Okonkwo · Lagos · Ghent · verified purchase, No. 041/500 “De derde druk met gouden titel.

We reproduce it as it fell, because the ledger is a ledger, and the record is a record, and the copy in question is the copy A. Okonkwo took home for a mother who kept a street for forty years and never wrote it down. Whatever the slip meant, the record means it back. The ledger is, to the shop’s knowledge, still open; someone, one day, will have the second half of the story, and the second half will sound, we are told, exactly like A5. The counter keeps one more page in the ledger’s company: a travel guide to a party that never happened, where a host runs an empty basement room until four and the guest desk files the route beside this one — the guide’s advice is the shop’s: keep the door open, and keep the ledger open.
