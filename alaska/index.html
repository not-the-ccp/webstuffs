Council of the Far Bank · Quorum Book · Second Sitting, Uncorrected Record

Minutes No. 215 — the Matter of the Farthest State, Alaska

The state as entered in the atlas Aləsəkə (  Ι-LASS-kə) · also the far bank of the eastern shore, also, at the ferry house, the seat the car will take

A sitting of the Council of the Far Bank, held in the long room at the end of the quay on Halstrom Street, on the second Saturday of the month, at 14:00 sharp, the gate closed on time and the bench found in its place. Present to take the record: Marit Halstrom, clerk, in the chair; P. Steed, harbour master, chalk still on his sleeve from the three-week chalked on the wall; Elder Voss; R. Quill, the surveyor; and the ferryman, in person by the water — the ferry being that day, on the record, crossing. Quorum: four in the room and one proxy mid-stroke, the proxy counted, as always, as one seat and one vote, with the motion that it be so counted carried without dissent. The minutes of the sitting, in full and uncorrected, follow.

Item I. The Previous Minutes

The minutes of No. 214, being the sitting in which the southern sheet was ruled the far bank — the twelve sovereign countries at the long table, struck like clocks, all of them struck and none of them agreed to stop — were read aloud from the South America entry, No. 214, of the Dreamer’s Concordance, filed in this same miscellany. It moved by Elder Voss, and seconded by the surveyor, that the minutes be adopted into the record as weather, not dream, on the grounds that a fragment kept rather than struck is a promise in the middle of being made, and that the ferry’s half of that promise had, by now, been kept in full. Carried 4–2. The dissent was recorded in the margin in a smaller hand, and the record notes, for the sake of the next clerk, that the ink of the dissent is still wet.

Item II. The Matter of Alaska

The clerk then opened the atlas at the far corner of the sheet and read into the record, from the chart and not from memory: Alaska ( , Ι-LASS-kə) is a U.S. state located in the northwestern regions of North America, part of the Western United States region, and one of the two non-contiguous U.S. states, alongside Hawaii; the northernmost, westernmost, and, in longitude, the easternmost state in the union; a semi-exclave of the U.S., bordering the Canadian territory of Yukon and the province of British Columbia to the east; sharing a western maritime border in the Bering Strait with Russia’s Chukotka Autonomous Okrug; and closer to another continent — Asia — than any other U.S. state, with the Chukchi Sea and the Beaufort Sea of the Arctic Ocean lying to the north, the ice of which was visible, the clerk reported, from the quay, and which had not moved.

The harbour master rose on the last figure. “Closer to Asia than any other U.S. state,” he read back, “then the ferry’s business is not a river with a harbour master, it is a continent with one.” The surveyor answered that the figure is the best figure in the entry, meaning a run of form, and that a run of form, chalked wet, is an itinerary that will actually be walked. It moved, seconded, that Alaska be ruled the far bank of the eastern shore — that is, the destination the ferry has been steering for all along, on the other side of the Bering Strait, set, waiting, and not moved — and that the Saturday schedule be extended accordingly, the extension to be chalked on the wall beside the ice chart before the chalk dries. Carried 4–0, with one abstention, the abstention being the proxy by the water, who is presumed to have abstained because he was, on the record, mid-stroke, and because a vote cannot be cast from the middle of a promise.

Item III. The Bone, and the Banquet That Fills the Thirteenth Place

The clerk then produced the old catering ledger, the one the council keeps the way a surveyor keeps a broken baseline, and read the standing order for the banquet to be held in the long room upon the day the thirteenth place at the table is filled — the place, per No. 214, left for the car that was not coming: INGREDIENTS.--1 pint of porter, with a considerably smaller portion in the centre of the bone. Debate followed, of the kind the council allows when the ledger is in the room. The surveyor proposed the bone to be the ridge of the Bering Strait, which is a bone in the chart, long and hard and full of water in the middle. Elder Voss proposed the bone to be the long table itself, on the grounds that the whole southern sitting was a table and that a table this long must, by arithmetic, have a bone in it somewhere. The harbour master proposed the bone to be his, and the porter to be poured for him, at the pier, at 14:00 sharp, and not argued. The clerk reminded the council that the ledger is read, not rewritten, and that a considerably smaller portion in the centre is a smaller portion in the centre whether the bone is a ridge, a table, or a man. It moved that the line stand as written in the ledger, that the bone be left to the banquet to decide, and that the porter be kept dark until the thirteenth place is filled, which the record expects will be, in time, the way it expects everything it has expected so far: at first light, mid-crossing, with the far bank already set. Carried 3–3, and, the vote being equal and the ledger being in the room, the line stands, and the council was heard to say that is the same as carried, with the harbour master’s vote counted twice for the porter and twice for the bone.

Item IV. The Car at the Pier

The clerk reported that on Tuesday last a car was seen at the quay, parked at the end of Halstrom Street, engine running, driver not visible, and that the harbour master, asked, said the car had been there since the Saturday before, and that it had crossed, and that he would not be saying from which side. The council took the report into the record without further question, noting that in No. 214 the thirteenth place was set and then left empty on purpose, and that a place left empty on purpose is a place, and that the run of form, chalked on the wall, is the promise that whatever was being promised in the room with the bad scan will, at last, be said out loud in full. One member of the Ironbark Overland Club, present that day on the quay, said the engine note was one she recognised: it is the note, the club maintains in its minutes, of a car that has been defending a post-war country one town at a time from the driver’s seat, and the club’s full log of that September evening is kept at The Rumble Strip, No. 7, filed where the laundrettes can hear their own drums. It moved that the seat remain, that the car be expected, and that that is all right. Carried 5–0, the proxy voting this once, mid-stroke, which is the only way a proxy can vote, and which the record, on reflection, finds it could not have wanted any other.

Item V. Cross-Readings, and the Shelf This Folio Is Filed Under

Before the sitting closed, the council was asked how the present minutes sit with the entries of the Dreamer’s Concordance already filed in this miscellany. The clerk answered: the southern sheet that this council ruled the far bank in No. 214 lies down whole in its own entry, South America, Entry No. 214, twelve countries struck like clocks at a long table, and the ferry that crosses on Saturday; the blue that is also a whole city lying down, eighteen titles on the crest of it and a kitchen interpretation ending in a blue mould, is minuted at Sueño Azul, Universidad de Chile, Entry No. 143, and is received here as weather of the same class — a colour that keeps; and the walk that gives these minutes their Saturdays, gate to bench, is staked out in The Steakley Walk, 2.1 miles, 75 minutes, one marquee a bulb short of a dollar. The rest of the quarter’s field notes, and the shelf this folio is filed under, are kept on the front page of the miscellany, Webstuffs, where the reader is referred and discharged.

The sitting adjourned at 14:00, the gate opened on the quay, the ice in the Chukchi and the Beaufort reported unchanged and not moving, and the chalk on the wall reported, by the surveyor, still wet. The record was signed in the room, with the seal of the council pressed into the margin, and the minutes were filed under the shelf of the far bank, between the southern sheet and the blue of Santiago, where they will keep.

Signed in the chair — Marit Halstrom, clerk · Witnessed on the quay — P. Steed, harbour master · Counted mid-stroke — the proxy by the water
