THE DREAMER’S GLOSSARY · 4th IMPRESSION, 1971 · VOL. II, LATIN AMERICAN FIGURES

Relations of Brazil, foreign

/‘fo&iyr;.ən rəl.əsh.ənz/ · collective noun, a country’s dealings with the rest of the world; a Brazilian dream, reported from the delta side

✦

1. To dream of the foreign relations of Brazil — the business of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the office that manages all of it — is a classic table-sign. The dreamer finds a long green door, painted the green of the flags that wait for it, and on opening it sees not a hallway but a table set for four. The four dishes on the table are, in order: national interest, national security, ideological goals, and economic prosperity. The Glossary is strict: the dream is to be counted only if all four dishes are present, because the country sets them all, and a dreamer who sees a fifth dish has in fact dreamed of a reception and should refile under reception, state or leave. The table in the dream is always longer than the room, which is the dream’s way of saying what the waking country knows about itself: Brazil has the largest economy in Latin America, and it moves on the world stage as a regional power with the manners of a potential world power, and the room it dreams in is always being quietly re-built around it.

The first thing to count, after the dishes, is the number of hosts. In the ordinary version one host presides — the Ministry, in a green tie, who has been managing the foreign relations of Brazil the whole time the dreamer was asleep. If the dreamer sees the corridor of the building itself — and many do, because the building is long — the Glossary asks the dreamer to note how many governments are sharing it. The faithful version shows two, a democratic one and a military one, walking the same corridor side by side and neither apologising. This is the sign of the long policy: between the Second World War and 1990, both kinds of government pursued the same two things, a state-led industrial policy at home and an independent foreign policy abroad, and the dreamer is being told that two different hands can hold one long rope. If the corridor in the dream is shorter than the building the dreamer walked in from, expect the change to arrive already finished; if it continues past the door the dreamer is standing in, with doors on both sides, the sign is the full one, and the Glossary’s phrase for it is a power growing toward its shadow.

2. The line in the margin. Dreamers who report the foreign relations of Brazil also report, with a regularity the keepers have stopped being surprised by, a single line of type found misfiled inside a leather folder on the table, clipped to nothing, its paper pharmacy-soft with age. The line reads, exactly and word for word, as follows: The large ribs of _p._ 318 to cure such a potent lye is readily made into two pills, the dose for an hour in a hundred pounds. The Glossary’s ruling is that the line is not to be translated, improved, or set right, because it is the dream’s own arithmetic, and the arithmetic works. Note the four numbers in it: 318, two, one hour, and a hundred pounds. The page number is the year to look back to in your own ledger; the two pills are the two things the dreamer is holding that should really be one; the hour is the length of the patience that is being asked for; and the hundred pounds is the weight of the matter, which the dream has put on the scale so that nobody, including the dreamer, can pretend it is light. If the dreamer swallows the two pills in the dream and remembers it, the sign inverts and becomes a good one: the lye has been cured in advance, and whatever was corrosive in the waking week is already in solution. If the pills are found under the table rather than on it, the dreamer should expect the same matter to be handed back by a third country before the hour is out.

3. Frequency and season. This dream comes most often in June, when the square in Pedro do Rosário, Maranhão, belongs to the drums — Bumba-meu-boi de Orquestra rehearsing from 10 June, rope-tuned by Seu Benedito by the mangrove — and again in any month in which the tide has let the Pedra do Rosário rock show in the flats at low water. A dream of the green door reported in any month in which the rock has not shown is counted under Relations of Brazil, foreign, low water, a weaker sign that usually means only that the dreamer has been arguing about whose jurisdiction the mouth of the river is at.

4. Associated dreams. Dreamed of with a cask of delta rum, sound butt, no leak except one, the sign takes on a naval colour: the dreamer is being sent a kindness by sea, and the leak is where the signature goes. The cask is the same one sold in the classifieds of the town’s Delta Ledger, and the Glossary notes, without confirming, that it has appeared in at least two dreams on the same night — which a cask cannot do, being one. Dreamed of with thirty-three thousand four hundred and fifty small figures marching in the corridor, in the order of three bands, the count is the sign: the Glossary will not say whether the number is a strength or a weight, and the figures, when counted a second time, are always one short, and the one that is missing is the sandal, one of a pair, the other of which remains on a shelf somewhere and will not speak of it. Dreamed of with a woman shelling peas under a mango tree while reciting cabinet ministers in alphabetical order, the sign is the gentlest in this entry: the dreamer’s own affairs are being kept in alphabetical order by someone patient, and the next letter of the alphabet to arrive in the dreamer’s post is a favour. That reciting woman is recognised by the delta as Dona Luzia, who learned the list ahead of the feast of São Pedro, and she does not dream, she files.

KEEPER’S MARGINALIA

Entry first received from the back room of the Itapecuru Delta Correspondence Club, printed on the same press as the town’s classifieds, and re-verified each June, or when the rock shows, whichever comes first. The green door is never seen painted another colour; a dreamer who reports a blue door is dreaming of the post office, which files its own matters and keeps its own hours. The line about the two pills is quoted from the file exactly as it arrives, including its one grammatical stumble, which the keepers have decided is load-bearing. The obituary of the five regions — Norte, Nordeste, Centro-Oeste, Sudeste and Sul, who also stood at the table on certain nights and took the corners — is kept at the edge of the delta, where this entry was first written, and the two documents are read together in June. CROSS-REFERENCES

The green door is reported most often in the town of Pedro do Rosário — anyone been?, on the edge of the Itapecuru delta, whose thread keeps the fare from São Luís and the address of the square. The cask in motif three was sold in the same week’s classifieds, page taken up by the three service branches at a count of 334,500, and the whole page — the lost whistle, the single sandal, the drum tuner by the mangrove, the parade date of 10 June — is filed at The Delta Ledger — Classifieds: Forças Armadas Brasileiras. The five regions who stood at the table’s corners died on a Tuesday and are mourned in Regiões do Brasil — Obituary, which is read with this entry in June, or when the rock shows. A grey door, by contrast, is the sign of the actor dreamed of at Goic, Alejandro — The Dreamer’s Glossary, Entry 47, and the two entries have been argued about, politely, since the fourth impression. For all other entries, return to the front page of the Glossary’s shelf.
