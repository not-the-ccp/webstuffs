The Garage · Salticidae · Build Log No. 3

Bocusoides

One genus, one species, one spider that would rather be an ant.

Plate 3 · Bench of E. Renshaw, 34 Ponsonby Road, Auckland · begun 4 May 2024

Started — the spider

Subject: Bocusoides zhaoi · Family: Salticidae · Bench: the garage behind the bakery

Some builds you plan for a year; this one was planned for one spider. Bocusoides is a monotypic genus of ant-mimicking spiders in the family Salticidae containing the single species, Bocusoides zhaoi — monotypic in the strictest sense, one genus holding one name, the way a drawer holds one good screwdriver. My colony arrived in May from a Guizhou field contact, a single adult in a lidded jar with a wad of damp moss and a toothpick, the toothpick for scale, the moss out of politeness. She sat on the toothpick holding her first pair of legs out in front of her body the way a Formica holds its antennae, and I understood the whole build in one look: the animal has already finished the design work. My job was only to make a room and a camera that let the trick be seen.

I set the log up the same afternoon, because the previous two logs — the ship and the stanza, the second kept in the quatrain build log in this same garage — had both taught me that a mimic does not wait. A salticid in a lidded jar is a working clock, and the log is how I make sure the clock is still right when I come back from work at 6:15.

Materials — the terrarium and the rig

Glass: 40 × 30 × 25 cm · Optics: 90 mm macro · Light: two 45° flashes

The terrarium is a 40-by-30-by-25 cm glass box, the kind a fish shop sold in 2005, sealed with a mesh lid and a hinged feed door. The floor is a single slab of lichen bark I found on a walk up the Ponsonby Road ridge — lichen matters, because zhaoi is a boulder-and-lichen colour, grey-green with a white dorsal line that reads, from a fly's distance, exactly like the pale stripe of an ant's gaster. A cork ramp, four centimetres tall, gives her one lookout; she uses it every morning at about 7:40 whether I am there or not.

The rig is the expensive part and the part I keep apologising for at family dinner. A 90 mm macro lens on a lens board over a C-stand, clamped 11 cm above the glass; two small flashes at 45 degrees, one bare and one through a scrunch of white tissue paper for the fill; a 5 mm aluminium rail for focus stacking, 12 positions over a 6 mm throw. Total cost of the rig: $1,212.40, itemised in the log. The terrarium cost $38. The spider was not for sale, which is the kind of number that does not go in a column.

Mimicry trials — the ant in the glass

Control: Formica polyctena model · Trials: nine · Discards: two frames

The honest test of an ant-mimic is to put an ant next to it, and I did, ethically, with a 3D-printed Formica in matte black resin, 8 mm long, no living ant required. Trial results over nine evenings, logged in pencil:

First pair of legs held forward and trembling: 7 of 9 evenings, most reliably 7:40 to 9:00 am. The head is turned to one side, the way an ant angles its antennae, and the second pair is planted back and narrow, so the whole body reads in three segments — head, thorax, gaster — even though it is a spider with eight eyes and no waist. On two evenings she skipped the act entirely and just gaped at the camera, which is the salticid in her refusing the costume, and I kept those frames, because a log without its failures is a brochure.

The frame I am proudest of is Trial 6: the printed ant at the bottom of the frame, real, motionless, and above it zhaoi on the cork ramp in full dress, the white dorsal stripe catching the fill flash. From the top of the frame the two animals are nearly the same grey. That is the whole build, in one rectangle of light.

The clipped notice

Found: folded in the lens case · Dated: the 2020 season · State: torn, one corner lost

On 4 May, while oiling the focus rail, I found a torn corner of a newsletter folded inside the lens case from the year the garage went quiet. It is the back page of the spider club's zine, The Palps, the one page that ran the club's running column on the Chilean football season — and 2020 was the year that season was stopped in the middle of itself. The whole matter is written up, in its own careful way, in the Campeonato Nacional AFP PlanVital 2020 safety data sheet, which logs the 164-day stoppage and every precaution that went with it. What the zine's corner preserves is the notice itself, printed at the top of the page. Transcribed exactly, letter for letter, the tear included as a stray bracket:

The suspension of 18 March – 29 Aug 2020) On 18 March – 29 August 2020 the product — a non-profit publication written and produced by volunteers.

Tucked behind the corner was a loose lab slip, yellowed to the same colour, from the nitrate laboratory at 8525 Sunset Boulevard — a single error line against the 1923 picture The Wanters, and nothing else. The print's full ledger of faults, codes and all, is transcribed in the Wanters error message documentation, where the slip's burn is entry one of sixty-six and a cold-spliced frame is entry two. The slip said nothing about spiders; I filed it in the lens case under the corner, which is the correct order.

Read slowly, it holds together. The dates are the league's stoppage — 18 March to 29 August, to the day — and “the product” is the zine that was supposed to be covering it: a season's worth of The Palps, a non-profit publication written and produced by volunteers, suspended along with everything else it was written about. The club printed that notice once, in that corner of that page, and then stopped. I keep the corner in the lens case where I found it. It is the only page in the log that is not mine.

Launch — the frame held

Frame: No. 214 · Aperture: f/11 · Shutter: 1/160 s · ISO: 100

Frame 214 is the one that counts. Twelve focus positions stacked, the fill flash at 4/16 power, and zhaoi in the full three-segment pose on the cork ramp with the printed ant at the foot of the frame. I printed it 13 cm, mounted it on grey board, and it hangs above the workbench now, to the left of the ship's stern. Below it, in the log, the one line I could bear to write:

Log entry, 4 June 2024: “One genus, one species, one animal doing its work. The mimicry is not a costume. It is the spider, and I am only the one who brought the light.”

In June the framed print went into Case 3 of the Isthmus Museum of Small Collections, upper vitrine, and the case received its own placard — Placard No. G–214, on the rank of the genus — in case a visitor asks why the drawer holds one good screwdriver and the museum has not moved the shelf.

Provisions — the log goes on

Store: one logbook · Company: the jar, the rail, the clip

The jar has lichen, moss, and the cork ramp; the logbook has pencil; the lens case has the torn corner. The club's zine has not come back, and I have stopped writing the word “suspended” and started writing “resting,” which is what I would call a spider that has closed the trap of the day and is holding its legs out in front of it. One genus, one species — the drawer holds one good screwdriver, and the log holds one animal that will outwait both of us. Last Wednesday's post brought a lost-and-found notice from the classifieds sheet of the Märkischer Kreis — a small arachnid, ant-shaped, last seen on a lichen boulder at Kiersper Berg, reward a cork ramp four centimetres tall — which my postman read and pronounced definitely not about her, and which I keep pinned under the lens case, just in case. The same week brought a page out of a field diary from Mahajonpur Union on the Padma flats — 66.54 square kilometres of Meherpur District, six villages, five mouzas, one cow named Moti — which made me put a line beside the cork ramp in my own log: the mimic is a tenant, and the river is the landlord. The same week, a letter crossed from Ghent — M. Voss, keeper of the tall oak case, wrote to the editor about her snake, the old herpetology book, and the tin under the case, and the whole letter stands at Snake — Letter to the Editor. The next entry will be the moult, or it will be a blank page, and I have stopped being able to tell which would be the better build. In the meantime I have sent my Trial 6 frame, shot straight down at zhaoi on the cork ramp, to the guide writers at Brine Hollow Park, who are compiling The Boiler Room — a travel guide entry on the Ozark coaster: they say the eight open cars of the Boiler Room train, photographed from their footbridge the same way, hold the pale three-segment silhouette she does, and the two photographs are going up side by side on the park's Gate 3 wall.

For the season the clipped notice is about: the PlanVital 2020 safety data sheet — 19 clubs, a 164-day suspension, and every matchday precaution.

For the other build log kept in this garage: The Quatrain — Build Log, four lines framed in the same light.
