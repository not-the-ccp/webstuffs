MUSEO DEL BALÓN · GALLERÉ 2 · CASE IV

PLACARD Nº 12 · READ AT EYE LEVEL

IV 1982–1983

Exhibit · One midfielder, two titles, one unamendable line

JOSÉ DANIEL PONCE

b. La Plata, 26 June 1962 · midfielder · Estudiantes de La Plata, first team 1980–1988

The Man

He was born into the city’s grid in 1962 and, the club’s own record says, into the club’s colours in the same breath. In the red-and-white vertical stripes of Estudiantes de La Plata — El Pincha, the Pin, to anyone in the city who has had to say it — Ponce made his first-team debut in 1980 and stayed eight seasons: 214 league matches and 22 league goals, by a man the scoreboard kept having to re-justify as a midfielder. He ran the middle third of the Estadio Ciudad de La Plata’s turf, that long corridor between the touchline and the river road, on a street plan so strict the visitors used to say the players seemed to be filing themselves, square by square, before the whistle.

The Double

The case exists for two seasons. In 1982 Estudiantes de La Plata took the Argentine Primera División; in 1983 the club took it again — two back-to-back championships, which in a league of this temperament is a small geological event. The 1983 run closed in the week before his twenty-first birthday, and the away end, La Ribera, was singing a name that had been on the minutes books since 1980 by the time Ponce walked back through the tunnel on a Tuesday evening in June. The league table is reproduced on the wall to the left at 1:1; the 1983 page is the one visitors touch, and the case was rebuilt once, quietly, in 2009, to stop it.

1981–82 No. 5 shirt, club-issue, cotton, the left hem mended twice by the same needle. The 5 was his number for six of the eight seasons; in 1984 the club borrowed it for a guest match and returned it with a stain that the curator has declined to identify.

The Volume

On the brass plinth at the centre of the case stands the season’s minutes volume, the club secretary’s book, leather spine, silver clasp. Attendance, transfers, the two championships, and every fine in centavos are in its hand. One entry was never corrected: 26 June 1962 — J. D. Ponce attends. The date predates the season by twenty years. Whoever set it down meant, the curator believes, a birth, not a match — the boy attending, in the city’s grid, before he had a club. The club moved to amend it in 1985, and again in 2004, and both motions failed on the point that a minutes book records, it does not revise. The strap on the plinth is worn but true. The minutes volume with one strange, unamendable line sits fast.

Where Else It Is Filed

Argentina holds its seat at the long table in the concordance’s entry on South America, Entry No. 214, and the two 1980s stripes in that case are the La Plata rail of the table, second from the bottom. The question of what number a footballer takes — and the answer, 796.33, argued honestly — is worked through on The Stacks, Thread 67, where the pencil amendment outlasts the printed schedule; this placard is the shelf the pencil points at. The 1986 volume — the one that came home from Mexico in the trophy case’s shadow — carries its own unamendable line, and that line is now held in the record Error 1986-WC-24, The Mexico Cup Fault, filed one drawer down in the gallery’s system notice archive. The rest of the gallery’s cases, and the miscellany this case is lent from, keep their register on the front page, Webstuffs. Read the placard, then the table, then the line in the book, in that order; the case was hung for that path.
