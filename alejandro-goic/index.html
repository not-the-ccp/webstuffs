THE DREAMER’S GLOSSARY · 4th IMPRESSION, 1971 · VOL. II, LATIN AMERICAN FIGURES

Goic, Alejandro

/‘həle.шd’o h˫˥ɪ/ · proper name, actor; a Chilean dream, masculine

✦

1. To dream of Alejandro Goic — specifically, of the actor Alejandro Eduardo Goic Jerez, born 11 September 1957 — is a classic door-sign: your mind is trying to tell you that a door which has stood closed for decades will open, and that what is waiting on the other side is a company, not a person. The dreamer sees him at the threshold, usually in a grey coat, and notices that he has already been waiting there.

The number of figures in the dream is the first thing to count. In the ordinary version he stands alone in a Santiago hallway, a corridor the length of a film reel, and behind him the wall bears, in faint paint, a list of roles that runs longer than the corridor — in the life of the dream-actor, after all, there are more than fifty of them, running from 1988 to the present, and the dream keeps the ledger faithfully, never one fewer. If the list ends cleanly, the change coming to you is completed in your lifetime. If it runs off the wall and continues, uncounted, up a staircase, you should expect the change to outlive a season and be patient with it.

2. Frequency and season. This dream comes mostly in September, and the book is emphatic that the coincidence of his birthday is not to be shrugged off: 11 September is the day the entry is re-verified, and dreams of Goic reported to the Glossary in any other month must be counted under Goic, Alejandro, displaced, a weaker and less reliable sign, often nothing but the memory of a film seen in a cinema on Providencia Avenue.

Old interpreters attach a further reading to the coat. A grey coat means the dreamer is owed a public kindness; a dark coat means the kindness is already on its way; a white coat, which is reported rarely and never twice by the same dreamer, means the dreamer is to become the kindness, not its recipient, and must be warned that this is a role of greater risk than the first two.

3. Associated dreams. Dreamed of with a notebook full of pressed leaves, this entry changes register entirely: it ceases to be a door-sign and becomes a record-sign, meaning that the dreamer’s own patient observations, kept over years, are about to be cited by someone else in public. A naturalist’s notebook keeps the same way, carried in the syllabus: the Glossary borrows the line from a film-studies course at a Santiago university, whose students are told that the person on screen is the syllabus’s long example — that fifty films, seen in order, teach what no lecture can, and that the notebook of the observer who watched all of them is the assignment’s only accepted answer. The lesson, as the Glossary restates it, is that attention is a form of love, and the dream of Goic with the notebook is the mind confirming that the dreamer has been paying it.

Dreamed of with the sea behind him, the sign is ordinary and good: a border crossing, a farewell that does not hurt. Dreamed of counting the doors of a house one by one, the sign is the rarest in the book and must be reported within nine days of waking, with the dreamer’s name and the street of the house, to the keepers of the Glossary.

KEEPER’S MARGINALIA

Entry re-verified 11 September of each year. Fifty-two films counted in the 1994 collation; the count has grown since. The corridor in the dream is never carpeted; if a dreamer reports carpet, the entry does not apply. Seal of the Glossary, second impression

CROSS-REFERENCES

This figure is met on the roads of the Western United States, where a dreamer crossing the state matrix of California — Safety Data Sheet reports seeing him waiting at the San Andreas rest area, coat grey, list of roles unopened, which the sheet’s own handling rules would call a temporary boundary with lasting effect. He is also said to stand, at election night, in the counted country between the two Flemish towns of Ghent-Eeklo — Safety Data Sheet, where his face is mistaken for a candidate whose results have already been struck from the record. Field observers who keep pressed-leaf notebooks of their own may compare the sign with the gulf-side surveys of Gıllık Gulf — Field Notes, where the same patient method is practised on water. Travelers who follow the grey-coated man out of the corridor and eastward toward the wind have been known to end him in the left-field corner of The Curtright Circuit — Official Tourist Brochure, Left Field 1943–1946, where the door opens onto a fence line and the list of roles, in the league’s own ledger, runs exactly four seasons long — a completed change, by the book. For all other figures, return to the front page of the Glossary’s shelf.
