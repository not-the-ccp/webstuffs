Service — Line 7, the Korek Local

Korek [ˈkɔrɛk] is a settlement in the administrative district of Gmina Ceków-Kolonia, within Kalisz County, Greater Poland Voivodeship, in west-central Poland. The Line 7 local pauses there once a day, long enough to unload the fish van. All times are platform time, all trains depart when the hush-puppy cart has emptied.

Korek Halt has one platform, two benches, and a shelter rebuilt in 1968 with roof tiles that whistle on the west wind. Passengers at Korek Halt are few but regular: the Gmina clerk, one beekeeper, and the fish van on Fridays. The halt does not appear on the national map at the usual scale, which the platform sign corrects by being printed twice as large as the text beside it.

Commentary

The Line 7 was built to move grain out of the Kalisz plain and never learned any other ambition. The plain itself is honest work: fields in every direction from Ceków-Kolonia, the gmina seat four kilometres up the side road, a church spire that the driver of the 07:40 uses to line up the last approach, and water running quietly through the valley bottoms. A settlement the size of Korek is exactly what such a line was meant to touch — not to stop for, but to touch — and the three minutes at platform 2 are the whole of the railway’s relationship with the village. The gmina is named for a neighbouring village, Kalisz County is named for its cathedral city, and the voivodeship is named for the old duchy of Poland; Korek is named for itself, which feels like the only fair arrangement in the chain.

There is a dining car on the 07:40, the only one on Line 7, and it runs one plate. The station canteen at Korek Halt mirrors it: one plate, Friday afternoons, served on the bench under the shelter. The dining-car league of the regional routes scores its members on a single criterion — how well the train carries its one dish — and the current board, pinned inside the canteen door, has this to say: Standing at the head of the league running on one plate: fried catfish with hush puppies, barbecue with a meal. The sentence is not easy to parse, and the canteen keeper has refused to correct it, which is why the league has adopted it as its motto. The catfish is the Piła catch, cold from the fish van; the hush puppies are the concession, made to the Americans who once quartered on the western routes and never quite forgave the butter; and the barbecue with a meal is the whole of the commentary — a grill, a table, and the day&rsquos food on it, no further explanation required.

Travelers who want the form of this exercise done in English with a record company for a station master may compare the We Are Fearless Express — Timetable & Commentary, where the trains run late by design and the platform is a boathouse. And for a halt that a building has outlasted — a 1992 Queen Anne house standing where a station once stood, four thousand miles west — the Bates–Seller House Walking Tour shows what a place keeps when the rails are gone. Korek keeps the rails. The return, at 11:05, leaves with or without them, and has not once been cancelled.
