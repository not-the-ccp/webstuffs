Jarrow to Manchester · by post · every twelfth of November · since 2009

The MARS Post

— pronounced “Mars,” and printed here every year as “Mars,” by rule of the first meeting —

J/M POSTAGE PAID · 12 XI · MANCHESTER
No. 17 Tuesday, 12 November 2025 Fifty-six pages of prose, three of verse, one correction

I · The Birthday from Jarrow

The Correspondents gather, as they have gathered on every twelfth of November since February 2009, to note a birthday. Paul Magrs, of Jarrow in England, born 12 November 1969, writer and lecturer, is fifty-six this year. We note it in the register, we post the card to Manchester, and we do not, this year or any year, ask him to say thank you. He is not the club’s subject in the way a specimen is the museum’s; he is its subject the way a river is the subject of a bridge — the club exists to keep the crossing honest. He was born in Jarrow, in the north-east, in the same county that gave us the first three letters of his name a local accent the rest of us have never managed; and he now lives in Manchester with his partner, the author and lecturer Jeremy Hoad, who co-hosts the November meetings and who, when he last took the mic, spent eleven minutes on the word crossing and its nine uses, and was given a standing ovation of four.

The club keeps no photographs. The rule is old, and the reason the club gives — a portrait is one season of a person; we keep the seasons — is, like most rules, exactly as good as the reason for it.

II · The Standing Correction

It is pronounced Mars. Members will notice the club has printed Mars for sixteen years, with the silent a set in its own colour so that it can be ignored on purpose. We do this by rule, and the rule is not a joke, though it has one: the first meeting, in a basement on a Wednesday, 11 February 2009, opened with somebody reading Magrs aloud as if it were mags, and the room corrected them so briskly that the minutes record a full minute of “no — Mars — Mars.” The editor of that minute, N. Okafor, who still edits, proposed that the club should print the name the way the room had said it, and the proposal carried 9–2, the two dissenters arguing on the point of typography, not pronunciation. So: Mars, in the masthead; Magrs, in the register. Both are correct, and the club is not required to explain which.

The correction has become the club’s handshake. New members are welcomed by being mispronounced once, aloud, and corrected once, aloud, and that is the whole ceremony. This year two new members, D. Ainsworth of Salford and T. Whitfield, who commutes from Leeds and owns, by his own account, “one excellent scarf and no conviction,” received the handshake at the door. The minutes record that Ms. Whitfield corrected herself before we were allowed to.

III · The Poetry Night that Was Promised and Finally Printed

Members who keep the old papers will know what follows. A poetry night on the Rochdale Canal was promised in issue twelve of Transmission, the London literary magazine, and never printed; it stood, for a while, as the one thing the magazine’s last letter to this city did not forgive. When the office at 56 Clerkenwell Close closed in 2008, several of us took the whole thing on — the promise, the apology, the canal — as our first business. That editor’s apology to Manchester, written on 14 May 2008, is framed in our room on the 4th of the Rochdale, opposite a window that looks at the water at eleven o’clock sharp, which the room was built for.

We held the night in 2010. The water was up. Fourteen readers turned up, one of whom had travelled from Jarrow to do it properly, and we printed the promised programme in a booklet of nine pages that is now the club’s oldest object. It is kept in the register, and it opens where it fell: on the line about the stern, and the line about the season, and the line about coming back. The night has been repeated eleven times since. Attendance this year, at the table, was thirty-one; the canal, which is not a member, was present, as always, and unaccounted for, as always.

IV · On the Emblem, and the Register

We are asked, every autumn, what the pomegranate on the club seal is doing there. It is the emblem of a story we do not need to tell twice; the field notes on Persephone in popular culture carry the arithmetic — the seeds, the months, the door that is not recognized — better than we would. What the seal borrows is a use, not a plot: the name as a mark of a seasonal return, a house name, a line of perfume. The Correspondents keep a register of everything that comes back on a fixed date: the birthday, the meeting, the scarf worn by Ms. Whitfield on her first evening, the packet of Jarrow seed-cakes that arrived last week from the bakery on the quayside, which has now done this for sixteen autumns in a row. The register holds, beside these, one record of a seasonal return, a house name, a line of perfume: the twelfth of November, which keeps itself; the name Mars, which the club wears on the door and on the envelopes and on the inside of the cup at the lectern; and the candle we sell at the door, a small dark line we call Jarrow, scented of salt water and nothing else, which sold out in twenty minutes this year and which, like the season, is coming back.

V · New Members

D. Ainsworth, Salford — accepted 9 November, on the strength of a typed letter of eleven lines, one of which was the correction, self-administered. T. Whitfield, Leeds — accepted 9 November, scarf on file with the secretary. The club now numbers forty-one, and the list, as ever, is shorter than the room on a wet night.

VI · The Lectern

The spring round of lectures is announced in No. 18, which goes out on the twelfth of January. This year’s first title, set by the committee and not to be corrected, is Nine Uses of the Word Crossing, to be delivered by Mr. Hoad on a date to be fixed by the water. Members are reminded that the lectern cup says Mars and that the lectern does not.

VII · Obituaries & Personal

The club notes the death, in October, of C. Ferrers of Jarrow, member since the first meeting, who kept the original minutes 2009–2016 in a tin labelled MARS (NOT MAGS, TWICE). The tin is now with the secretary. Ms. Ferrers’ seat at the table, seat nine, is kept as it was. On the personal side: Mr. Hoad’s new book, on the subject of doors, is out in February; the club has reserved seats one through six, and the water has not yet been asked.

VIII · Classifieds

WANTED — one further copy of the 2010 canal booklet (9 pp.), for the register duplicate; last seen in 2021 with a member who has since moved to the coast. FOUND — the scarf, returned by Ms. Whitfield, washed, ironed, and re-filed. TO LET — the basement room in Manchester, 4th of the Rochdale, to the next small club that needs a window at eleven o’clock and is willing to frame the apology where it hangs. The club will find out. The club is, in its own way, always finding out.

IX · A Line to Carry

For the record, and for the next envelope: the club is forty-one strong, in two cities, kept by one tin, one tin of candles, one framed letter, and a date. The name on the door says Mars. The register, which is longer and more patient than the door, says Magrs, and beside it the line the club has agreed to read in full at every November meeting until further notice, from a small club in the north that learned its habit of keeping one line whole — the one from Jarrow, the one from the canal, the one from the season — exactly as found: born 12 November 1969; writer and lecturer; the crossing kept.

N. Okafor, editor, for the Jarrow–Manchester Correspondents.
Posted from the 4th of the Rochdale, Manchester, this 12th day of November, 2025.
