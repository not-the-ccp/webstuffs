The Golden Roll

A Pilgrim's Brochure for the Route of Canonization

San Pietro in Foro · the only town in the valley where a person is officially declared a saint on a fixed public timetable · pop. 11,204

Dear traveller, you hold in your hands the official brochure of the Route of Canonization. Canonization is the declaration of a deceased person as an officially recognized saint — in the strict sense, the official act of a Christian communion declaring a person worthy of public veneration and entering their name in the canon catalogue of saints, the authorized list of that communion’s recognized saints. In San Pietro in Foro the act has, since the decree of 1294, taken place on paper, on schedule, and in public, along one paved route of four stations. The route is walkable in a day; the tram takes forty minutes; the route of faith, in our town, is a matter of street numbers. Come early. The Roll is read aloud at ten, and the benches by the basilica step fill before nine.

Station Name & Address What you will find there Opening
I The Diocesan Tribunal
Vicolo dei Panni, 2		 The first office of the cause. Here the postulation begins: the servant of God is named, the file opened in red covers, the witnesses of the first decade questioned one by one at a bench that has not moved since 1411. The registrar will give you the cause number and a copy of the rules. Bring two references and a passport photograph of the candidate. Mon–Fri, 9–12. No photography in the questioning room.
II The Cause House
Via delle Candele, 9		 The Casa della Causa, where the postulator keeps the file and the candles burn in the shop window, one per petition sent in from the provinces. The window holds 412 candles at any hour; the keeper, Signora Fumagalli, will tell you which of them are prayers, which are thank-yous, and which are “just nice”. A cause file that has survived forty years in her care is the oldest open file in the valley. Daily, 8–19. Candles 200 lire.
III The Congregation’s Window
Piazza della Pilotta, 1		 The reviewing office of the communion, reached by the cobbled stair. No one may enter the upper floor; no one needs to. The window is where the verdicts are read to the square — venerable, beatified, canonized — and where, on the two mornings in the town's memory when the verdict was “the cause is closed and the name is not read”, the square has been silent all the way down to the ferry. The postulator’s office at the ground floor issues the feast-day circulars. Square open always. Window reads on feast days at 10:00.
IV The Basilica & the Golden Roll
Piazza del Foro, 1		 The destination. In the second chapel, behind the bronze grille, hangs the rotulus aureus — the canon catalogue itself: one scroll of vellum, 1,817 names in gold leaf, from the first entry of 1294 to the last of last autumn. The reading at ten takes ninety-one minutes; the roll is turned one name at a time by the same family of turners for six generations, and the turner’s brass bell is the sound you are actually travelling here to hear. Beneath the basilica, the reliquary hall keeps 340 relics on velvet, each with a ticket, each ticket with a cause number. Daily 7–18; bell at 10:00; last turning 17:30.

Notes for the walking pilgrim

Between Stations I and III the route passes the Ferry Landing on the Foro river, where the 06:15 boat still carries postulators’ files to the mill village, and where a scrubbed noticeboard of 1294 still posts the week’s causes. The bridge was rebuilt in 1862; the bell was not. At the foot of the Pilotta stair, the Foundry of Cistercian Street sells the pilgrim's brass bell (Product No. 21, 110 lire, struck in seven rooms before dispatch) — the same foundry that casts the turner’s bell every eleven years, which is why the two bells in the town answer each other across the square. If you arrive in the afternoon, take the tram from the ferry (350 lire, forty minutes) and stand at the rear window; the whole route is one long golden line along the river, and it looks, from the tram, exactly like a page of the Roll read upside down.

Feast day is the day the new name is read. The fair on the piazza opens at dawn with the candle-booths and the bridle-leather salt coils from Saltfleet, which the relic-hall has kept on its counter since 1902 because the coil, “bound by hand, holds best when it is made by hand”, in the words of the hall’s first ticket. In the evening the two old ledgers of the town are read side by side at the ferry landing, and the ferryman keeps his own line of the record. The full notes of both pressings are kept elsewhere on this site: the ferry’s two-disc record of the river company is pressed and filed at Saffron Sessions, BPR-007 — Liner Notes, cut at the very char beside our landing, and the kitchen's own account of what passed through the room during the feast seasons of 1969–1974 is balanced, to the farthing, on The General Ledger — Liner Notes, Sessions 1969–1974. Both books keep a line the town will not explain; we keep the bell.

The Parish Ground, Piazza del Foro South

Below the basilica, over the wall and past the relic-hall door, there is a football ground the size of the square, with one wooden bench and a brass scoreboard donated by the turner's family in 1958. It belongs to St. Aurelia's parish, whose patron is name 841 on the Roll, and it plays its fixtures the Sunday after each feast day, because a town that keeps a calendar this strictly must also keep a score. The ground's wooden scoreboard still stands in the original position, and the parish log of 7 May 1961, in the hand of the then-keeper, reads: We won 2-0, goals from Irvine and Bos. No other line of that season survives. Irvine, it is recorded, was a ferryman's son from the mill village; Bos kept goal. The logbook itself hangs in the cause house window among the candles, in a glass frame, where visitors ask about it more often than the verdicts. We have not been able to discover why the log's ink changed colour after the second goal. The keeper of the window says it is “just nice”, in the same phrase she uses for the candles that are not prayers.

Fares, timetables & the guestbook

The pilgrim's day: tram return 700 lire; candle 200 lire; brass bell 110 lire; relic-hall ticket 500 lire; the reading at ten is free and the benches are first come. The ferry runs 06:15 to 19:15, hourly, and will carry a cause file across for a stamp. Keep your cause number; everything in the town is filed by it, the candles, the relics, the bells, and, we are told, the football.
