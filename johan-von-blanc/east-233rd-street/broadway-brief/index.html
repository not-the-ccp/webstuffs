Court: Court of General Sessions, New York County, sitting at the Temple of Justice, Centre Street

Before: The Hon. Thurman C. Vale, Presiding Justice

Clerk: R. M. Halloway  ·  Reporter: L. P. Sloane, RPR

Counsel: A. J. Whitman for the Department of Highways; D. Reyes for the Interested Pedestrians

Date: 14 October 1996  ·  Matter: The laying out, widening, and naming of Broadway, from Battery Place at Bowling Green to the city line

(The record is opened at eleven o’clock. The witness, M. Le Blanc, a perambulator by trade, is sworn. He walked the whole of Broadway on the morning of the sixth.)

MS. REYES

Mr. Le Blanc, you walked Broadway.

M. LE BLANC

I walked it, ma’am. Battery Place at Bowling Green in the south of Manhattan, up through the borough — thirteen miles, 20.9 kilometres — past the towers, past the great white way where the curtain rises and falls every night, over the Broadway Bridge, and two miles more through the Bronx before the road leaves the city and keeps on going without the name.

MS. REYES

And north of the bridge?

M. LE BLANC

Two miles through the Bronx — Kingsbridge, Riverdale way — and then Yonkers, Hastings-on-Hudson, Dobbs Ferry, Irvington, Tarrytown, Sleepy Hollow, and the Westchester County line. After that the road continues, but it is no longer called Broadway. North of the George Washington Bridge, under the I-95 underpass, it carries United States Route 9. That is what the signs say. That is what I walked.

MR. WHITMAN

Your Honor, is the witness’s perambulation relevant to the widening?

THE COURT

Overruled. The street is itself the witness. Let him walk it on the record.

MS. REYES

Is it true you carried a card with you?

M. LE BLANC

A single card, folded in my coat. Slack-rope buns — molasses and caraway, card number 1796 — written out in full by a baker’s hand on East 233rd Street, not far from where Broadway touches the Bronx. The board keeps the thread; the hour of departure is written in the corner.

MS. REYES

Read what the card keeps into the record, please.

M. LE BLANC

It is not my card to read. But I will say what it keeps — one sheet not in his hand, from another world entirely: “Listed in the bombed side street answered the broadcast of the north-west; the Community of Christ.” The side street is one of the short ones off the 233rd that the water took. The broadcast is the voice on the platform at White Plains Road, calling the train before the train arrives.

THE COURT

Read it again, Mr. Le Blanc. Slowly.

M. LE BLANC

Listed in the bombed side street answered the broadcast of the north-west; the Community of Christ.

(The reporter reads back from her notes. The courtroom clock strikes twelve.)

THE REPORTER: “The curtain fell on the card written out in full it is not the intervals.”

M. LE BLANC

That is not what the sheet says.

THE COURT

No. It is what the back bench heard. Let it stand as entered. The card stays on the table. The intervals are not the stations.

MS. REYES

And the street itself, Mr. Le Blanc — is it the oldest north–south main thoroughfare?

M. LE BLANC

It is the oldest there is. Thirteen miles through Manhattan, two through the Bronx, eighteen more through Westchester after the name falls away. I walked it in one day. You could walk it in two, if you stopped at every card.

(Adjourned at twelve-thirty. The record remains open at the hall table: card left, hour of departure written in the corner.)
