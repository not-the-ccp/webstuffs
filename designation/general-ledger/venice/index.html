Office of the Lagoon · Specification & Claims · Filed under the Lagoon Act, between the Brenta and the Sile

Application No. VE-A-126/0472

A Method and Appurtenant Devices for Holding a Going Concern on One Hundred and Twenty-Six Islands, Separated by Expanses of Open Water and by Canals

Inventors: Roland Vane, of the kitchen on Hall Street, Birmingham (auditor of record), and the Municipality of Venice, in person · Filed 24 May 2026 · Published in the Lagoon Register, the same day, by gondola courier

Granted — Provisionally, at Low Water

Venice is a coastal city in northeastern Italy and the capital of the region of Veneto, as well as the Metropolitan City of Venice; that much is background, which the specification states in one line and then declines to repeat, the way a ledger states its opening balance. What is claimed is the rest: that the city is built on a group of 126 islands, separated by expanses of open water and by canals, portions of which are linked by 472 bridges, and that this arrangement — the islands, the water between them, and the crossings — constitutes, whether the inventors intended it or not, a device for holding a set of books in which no two ledgers are ever consolidated. The islands stand in the shallow Venetian Lagoon, an enclosed bay lying between the mouths of the Po and the Piave rivers, or, as the specification prefers, more exactly, between the Brenta and the Sile, and it is the shallowness of the bay that is claimed as essential: a lagoon deep enough to drown an account would not support this invention.

As of 2026, the city proper — the municipality of Venice — carries a population of 249,385, nearly 50,000 of whom live in the historical island city, the centro storico, while most of the population resides on the mainland, the terraferma, and about 25,000 more on the outer isles, the lagoon’s own working pages. The specification draws no conclusion from the distribution. The lagoon, which keeps the books, does not argue about where the entries are lodged; it only keeps them separate, and that, the inventors submit, is the whole of the invention.

1. Field of the Invention

The invention lies in the field of going concerns maintained over water. The nearest prior art of record is musical: The General Ledger (Ledgerdisc LD-24-51), the two-side pressing of the Hall Street kitchen’s books, 1923–1968, whose side B carries the fire of 1968, the sherry on order since 1974, and the rule — stated in the custard, per Card 37 — that accounts that do not consolidate are never netted against one another. The present application is the lagoon’s answer to that rule at city scale: 126 ledgers, 472 crossings, no consolidation, no netting, not even at high water. The war pressing, The Thousand-Year Record (Reichsplatte RP-12), is cited for the bridges alone: its track B3 ran across the crossings of 1944, and each crossing was a rest. Venice asks that its crossings be read the same way — 472 rests, none of them the rest that does not come back.

2. Summary of the Invention

What is claimed is not the islands. The islands are prior art; the Adriatic laid them down long before the specification was drafted, and the Office notes this politely on the cover sheet. What is claimed is the holding: a method by which a municipality of nearly a quarter of a million souls keeps its entries apart across open water, links them only where a crossing is claimed, and survives its own write-offs. The 1968 fire at Hall Street is admitted in the specification as the closest test yet run of the method on the mainland side — a kettle, the wrong chair, five o’clock less the usual tea — and the kitchen’s response (reopening the following Thursday and keeping the tea hour anyway) is cited as evidence that the method carries a write-off forward rather than avoiding it. A going concern on 126 islands does the same at the scale of a lagoon.

3. The Claims

1.

The Venetian Lagoon, as an enclosed bay between the Brenta and the Sile, a shallow bay adapted to hold an account without drowning it, substantially as set out in Figure 1 of the specification.

2.

A city comprising 126 islands separated by expanses of open water and by canals, wherein no two islands are claimed as one ledger, and wherein the separation itself is the device.

3.

Forty-seven and twenty bridges — 472 in all — linking portions of said islands, wherein each bridge is a crossing and each crossing is a rest, and wherein the crossings are claimed without being consolidated into a single crossing.

4.

A method of trussing, read into the record from the recipe card hung for years behind the pass on Hall Street, the card whose first line the war pressing keeps and whose last line the ledger pressing keeps, and whose middle line — the one the static left standing on the card, underscore and all — is hereby claimed in these words, word for word:

“Serve before the fire, to prevent its tainting the breath, should be trussed, _and do not consolidate are never netted against one another.”

The method is not a recipe. The method is the house rule: whatever is to be served before the fire is trussed first, and the books are beaten separately, per Card 37, each layer apart, never netted against the layer above it. The specification preserves the line as found, the way the lagoon preserves what the water leaves behind: intact, legible, and slightly wrong in a way that balances.

5.

A sherry, on order since 1974, lodged in the cellar on Hall Street and cited as a provision — a liability that will not be settled in the current period — held in pending order over the lagoon, as a device for keeping the invention resting rather than closed.

6.

The population of 249,385, distributed as found — nearly 50,000 in the centro storico, most of the remainder on the terraferma, about 25,000 on the outer isles — as the entries to which the device of claims 1–5 is applied, no entry being consolidated into any other entry of the municipality.

4. Description with Reference to the Drawings

Figure 1 is the lagoon itself, drawn shallow, with the Brenta on the right and the Sile on the left and the Po and the Piave noted, more roughly, further upstream, where the specification concedes that the map gets generous. Figure 2 is the 126, set out as the ledger sets them out: one island per line, debit and credit columns alike, nothing netted. Figure 3 is the 472: the crossings, each drawn as a rest on the staff, each rest timed to a kettle coming to the boil at the speed the kettle was recorded at, 1s. 6d. worth of water. Figure 5 is the cellar on Hall Street, the sherry standing where it has stood since 1974, and the stampers taken the spring after the album was mastered, when the bottle was finally decanted — which the specification treats, as it must, as a settlement in the current period, the only one.

The lagoon, being the examiner, has observed the invention at work since before the filing date and reports that the device holds at every tide. The office’s only reservation, entered in small type on the cover sheet, is the one the kitchen enters on every going concern it has audited: the account is balanced to the farthing, the farthing is kept, and the fire is the one event the method does not prevent — only carries forward. The trussing is for the breath. The water is for the rest.
