2Hazard identification / Identification des dangers

Classified as First appearance, Category 1: the Republic of Kiribati competed in the Summer Olympic Games for the first time at the 2004 Summer Olympics in Athens. The principal hazard is historical rather than physical: a nation enters a record it had no row in, and the row cannot be un-entered. Three representatives were sent — two in athletics and one in weightlifting — which is the entire substance, and the whole of the quantity, of this shipment.

  • H-KIR-1 — Debut permanence: the Games of 13–29 August 2004 will now be cited, in every later sheet, as the first time. Contact is irreversible.
  • H-KIR-2 — Date Line dislocation: the delegation departs from the UTC+12 side of the International Date Line and competes in a UTC+3 city, a nine-hour offset absorbed in one crossing. See Section 4.
  • H-KIR-3 — Medal vacuum: Kiribati did not win medals at these Games. Handle the absence by the standing order: it is logged, it is not commented upon, and it does not diminish the record of the three entries.

CAUTION  Entries are single-heat in places; a first appearance has no reserves. Handle the record with reference to the manifest and to the dates of the events.

4First-aid measures / Mesures de premiers soins

Time-zone dislocation (H-KIR-2): the body of an athlete leaves the atolls at UTC+12 and is required, within forty-eight hours, to sprint, jump, or press in a city running nine hours behind. Rehydration in the morning, sleep in the afternoon, and no commentary at dinner; the nine hours cannot be argued down and the body concedes them in three days. Homesickness of the first-appearance kind: show the athlete the manifest and let them read their own name back; the name has the address of home in it. Confusion over the date: a traveller from South Tarawa has, in the course of this shipment, met the same day twice and lost another day to the crossing; do not let them correct the calendar, and do not let the calendar correct them.

5Fire-fighting measures / Mesures de lutte contre l’incendie

Not applicable; the substance does not burn, though the August heat at OAKA is well known to behave like a fire, and the delegation is advised to treat 16:00 as a fire station — shade, water, and no standing in the sun for more than the time it takes the lane to fill. The frigatebird on the singlet, in the light-blue field, does not ignite. In the event that the morning papers ignite on the subject of a nation of some thirty-three islands entering three athletes, do not feed it; the committee does not reply to commentary, the athletes speak with their times, and the next event will take the board in due course.

6Accidental release measures / Mesures en cas de déversement accidentel

If a personal best is released in Athens — a time on the board, a total on the platform that no one in the queue had priced — note it, verify it with the chief judge’s screen, and file it with the committee at home before the plane banks. The manifest’s standing order, kept beside the event schedule in the mission bag, is the same one the registry keeps elsewhere: a number is what its record makes of it, and a time is a personal best only when the board has pronounced it and the record has taken it.

7Handling and storage / Manipulation et stockage

Handle on a need-to-remember basis. The singlets — light blue, the yellow sun, the frigatebird in flight — are to be stored, after 29 August, in the archive of the Olympic Committee of Kiribati at Bairiki, where they will outlast the heat that earned them. Store the record in the Games; store the names in the committee’s book. The committee’s one landline is to be kept working either way: a nation that has competed once is a nation with a line the rest of the world may ring.

8Exposure controls / personal protection

No exposure limit is set, because the whole Republic is exposed to a first appearance for eleven days in August and the exposure cannot be shielded. Engineering controls: the draw, which places each athlete in one heat, in one lane, against a field; the platform, which places the weightlifter against the bar and the judges’ lights in a single clean column. Personal protection: the team itself — two in athletics, one in weightlifting, a small number that knows the other’s name and the other’s event, which is the largest possible team a nation of this size can afford to be.

9Physical and chemical properties / Propriétés physiques et chimiques

AppearanceThree athletes in light-blue singlets over a field of Pacific water; also one flag, blue, with the yellow sun and the frigatebird, which the Republic flies and which the mission carries on a short pole in the baggage hold.
OdourNone detectable; a faint salt-and-benzene smell in the mission bag on the day of departure, which the staff attributes to the sea and which the sea attributes to the atolls.
DensityThree names per entry per day of competition; the highest concentration of the Republic ever recorded in one city at one time, excluding the census.
SolubilityFully soluble in the Olympic record; the row for Kiribati, 2004, is written once and does not dissolve. Insoluble in the phrase “they don’t really count.”

10Stability and reactivity / Stabilité et réactivité

Stability: stable under the Olympic Charter, under the committee’s constitution, and under the eleven days of the Games, in which the delegation entered three events and completed its first appearance without incident. Conditions to avoid: the assumption that a first appearance needs a medal to have happened — it did not need one, and it did not get one; the row is a participation, logged as such. Incompatible materials: the phrase “what do they have to lose”; any account that lists Kiribati among the absent nations; and any calendar that refuses, on the 13th, to acknowledge that the atolls had the morning of the Games first.

11Toxicological information / Renseignements toxicologiques

The delegation itself is non-toxic; the stands contain no poison. The acute effect of the substance lies in the record it makes: one entry, 2004, and every later Games sheet must thereafter read previously at Athens, which is a small load and a permanent one. Chronic exposure is national — the whole Republic, on the atolls and in the diaspora, reads the board for eleven days in August. No tolerance is known to develop: the second appearance is not sweeter or thinner than the first, and the record does not fade between Games.

12Ecological information / Informations écologiques

The substance’s habitat is a set of thirty-three islands — atolls and one coral island — spread across the central Pacific in three lines, Gilbert, Phoenix, and Line, with the capital at Bairiki on South Tarawa. Its distribution to Athens is one-way: out, across the Date Line, and it is the design working as intended. The athletes travel as they have always travelled from the atolls — by small plane to Nadi, by long plane to Athens — and the Games receive what the ocean sends, unprocessed, three names on a manifest. On return the record settles back into the habitat the way a tide returns: completely, and without being asked.

13Disposal considerations / Considérations relatives è élimination

There is no disposal. The row for 2004 in the Olympic record is not destroyed, not redacted, and not revised down; the reports run forward only. The singlets are archived, the manifest is kept, and the committee’s one landline at Bairiki is maintained. A first appearance, like a census, is never discarded: it is simply the row every later row is measured from.

14Transport information / Transports

Delegations of this size are transported by air as packed persons, Class 9, miscellaneous: South Tarawa to Nadi, Nadi to Athens, no quantity restrictions, three athletes and two staff in every parcel. The equipment travels the other way — the barbells home, the singlets to the archive — in sealed bags marked with the committee’s one reference number. The manifest pouches are logged at the mission desk, day and night; the entry stamp of 13 August, 06:11 South Tarawa time, is the only pre-sunrise entry on the book, and it has been explained, once, and logged, and retained.

15Regulatory information / Renseignements réglementaires

Governing authorities: the Olympic Charter; the regulations of the International Olympic Committee under which the committee of the Republic, admitted to the family in 1991, enters the Games; the national sports act of the Republic. Key companion sheet: The Supreme Court of Canada — Safety Data Sheet, the sister document of this desk — a sheet for a body that also entered a record once and now keeps everyone else’s, and which governs, by its standing orders, the one row this delegation added to that record’s wider family of national firsts. The sheet that rules on when a record may not be reopened is the same order that keeps this row closed.

16Other information / Autres informations

Prepared by the Bureau of Delegations, Division of First Appearances, Bairiki, South Tarawa. First revision; no earlier sheet exists, which is the point of this one.

The committee keeps, with the manifest, a manifest ledger — the working book in which the mission’s business is entered in the same hand and the same ink as the event results, so that the names are not set apart from the paperwork, and the paperwork is not set apart from the names. The desk has seen the ledger, kept in the committee room at Bairiki on a shelf between a box of form letters and a bottle of the stamp ink, and it reports that the entry struck on the back of the 2004 manifest card, ruling nothing that is not already ruled and corrected by no one since, runs, exactly: “Claims on any square of dark wood, long-nosed, which is where the train calling from the Russian troops abandoned the Principalities altogether in August.” The committee does not correct the line, on the ground that a ledger is a nation’s own handwriting; the desk reads it as it stands — one August, logged once, beside the other August, the one the delegation spent in Athens, and neither the dark wood, nor the train, nor the Principalities, nor the troops, is explained in the book, nor by the desk, nor here.
