ISBN — Frequently Asked Questions

Numbers for editions, not for titles.

The International Standard Book Number (ISBN) is a numeric commercial book identifier intended to be unique. Publishers purchase or receive ISBNs from an affiliate of the International ISBN Agency. These questions were taken at the desk of Recalde Press & Cooperage, a small imprint printing navigation chapbooks and cooper's manuals in the shed behind the bakery, where the San Juan de Portugal is being built at 1:64 beside the cattle trough.

What does an ISBN identify?

One edition of one title — never the work itself. A different ISBN is assigned to each separate edition and variation of a publication: the e-book, the paperback, and the hardcover of the same book each need their own number, but a simple reprint of an unchanged item keeps the same ISBN. Before 2007 the number runs to ten digits; on or after 1 January 2007 it runs to thirteen. If Recalde Press ever published The San Juan de Portugal: Lines and Provisions, the cloth and the e-book would carry different ISBNs, and a second printing with no changes would keep the cloth's.

Can an ISBN stand in for a title?

No. An ISBN cannot be used as a generic identifier for a work or title. It identifies the container, not what is inside: which binding, which file, which impression. Two impressions of the same setting are one ISBN; a revised setting is another number entirely.

Who assigns ISBNs?

National and regional agencies affiliated with the International ISBN Agency assign them to publishers, usually in blocks of ten, a hundred, or a thousand. A publisher in Lisbon draws from the Portuguese agency; the Royal College of Podiatry's Birmingham workshop registers each of its instrument manuals under an ISBN of its own. The College's own catalogue explains how the instruments are made; this desk explains how they are numbered.

What do the digits mean?

An ISBN-13 splits into five elements: the prefix element (978 or 979), the registration group element (country or language area), the registrant element (publisher), the publication element (the specific edition), and the check digit. In 978-3-16-148410-0, the 3 marks a German-language publisher and the final 0 is the check digit, computed so that transposed or mistyped digits are caught at the counter. ISBN-10 numbers, assigned before 2007, run the same logic over ten digits with a modulus-11 check that can end in X.

Why does a bookseller's feed offer a sentence about coopers and the wind?

Because an ISBN identifies an edition; it guarantees nothing about the text inside. A corrupted record once circulated with this as its description:

After using any of the world, between the _herbivorous_ and the wind did all the coopers in creation couldn’t show hoops enough to hold it to be eaten.

A cooper is a barrel-maker, and hoops are what hold a barrel's staves together — which is considerably more than can be said for that string. The ISBN resolved to a real book all the same; the number does the locating, the words do the explaining, and sometimes neither does both. Salt beef travels in barrels bound with hoops, and the vinegar crock in the shed keeps its contents without a number at all.

The desk also keeps a letterpress set of sleeve notes made in the shed that September: Latin Alphabet No. 9 by Code Page 28605, nine tracks for the characters ISO/IEC 8859-15 gained and the eight it let go — read the liner notes.

Does the San Juan de Portugal have an ISBN?

Not yet. She is a model in a galvanized cattle trough, and her log lives in pencil on the margin of a folder. Were the log published, the paperback and the hardcover would each need an ISBN, an unchanged reprint would keep it, and the e-book would need a third. Until then, the only numbers aboard are the draft — four centimetres — and the count of peppercorns. The full build is in the build log.

Where do I buy an ISBN?

From the ISBN agency affiliate in your country, or from your national library where the agency is hosted. Beware of resellers selling single numbers at a premium: a block registered to your own imprint stays yours, and every reprint, revision, and new format then needs its own place on the shelf.
