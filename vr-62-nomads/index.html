14 September 2023 · NAS Jacksonville, Florida

Senyora Alabau,

I write on the letterhead of my squadron because the matter concerns my squadron's machinery as much as my own carelessness, and because one of the things a letter of apology must be, before it is anything else, is on file. On the morning of 9 September, two C-130T Hercules of this detachment lifted out of Jacksonville with a crate on board that was not, in the end, ours: a single wooden case of foundry type consigned by the Barcelona house Talls Llorenç i Fill to its own printer, the Impremta de la Diada, with a standing instruction that it reach the printer's hand before the 11th, for the holiday's broadsheet. The case held forty-one sorts of blackletter in one drawer and, in the second drawer, alone in a bed of cotton, a 72-point pound — the sign for sterling, £ — commissioned to stand at the head of the broadsheet and nowhere else in the year.

Here is where I failed. I, Master Supply Specialist Dana Reyes, signed the lift manifest for the case as Jacksonville to Barcelona, but I keyed the routing against the wrong column of the wing's load plan, and the case rode instead to the wing's own wing — COMFLELOGSUPWING at NAS JRB Fort Worth, Texas — where it sat, unclaimed and unopened, for three days in Annex C while I told the phone people, politely and for twenty minutes, that it was on its way. You understood on the phone, I am told, that it was not. You did not wait. You came.

What you did, and what I have since tried to describe to the commandery in a form that will survive a wing review, is this: you flew to Jacksonville on the 10th, met at the supply annex the TAR corporal who finally found the case — a young woman on the full-time cadre, Petty Officer E. Kowalczyk, whom I have ordered to send you a separate letter — and flew it back with it. The case touched down at El Prat at 19:42 on the 11th itself, four minutes before your office closed, and the broadsheet went to press. The printer has written to say the sign stood at the head of it, as commissioned. It is the only thing that made the three days anything but a failure; I will not take it as mine. I take the rest.

You will have noticed the provenance card sewn into the lid of the case, in the foundry's own hand. I quote it here because it describes you more accurately than I can, and because I want it read in full, in the file: “The pound’s symbol, £, has been interviewed in French, in Italian, and in apology, The Returning Clerk, for the Diada.” The foundry keeps a custom, which it calls the interviews, of setting its rarest sorts to work in whatever language the sort is last asked to speak before it rests, and of keeping the clerk who returns the sort on the card. French was the case's packing note, at Perpignan. Italian was the customs stamp at Ventimiglia, the old route, kept for the printer's tradition. The third interview was yours: the apology you dictated to me into a telephone at the Fort Worth desk while the case stood on your knee, which you gave the broadsheet to be set in, and which I have since read to the full squadron, all five of the wing's Reserve Hercules squadrons included, as the only correct wording. You interviewed the symbol in apology, for the Diada, and you were right to.

The squadron stands behind this letter in the way a squadron can: twenty-two drilling reservists and the TAR cadre will hear the full account at the 26th's drill, and the load-plan column that bit me has been renamed, in pencil, in the corner of the wing plan, and will not be un-renamed. We fly the lift again for the printer's 25th broadsheet, on the 10th of next September, and the case will ride in the forward cargo hold, strapped, with a manifest I will key and re-key until the two columns agree. You are invited to ride the nose wheel well if you will come; I will keep your seat as if it were the extra chair, which is what it is.

I am sorry. For the three days, for the telephone, for making a clerk of the third desk cross an ocean on the eve of her own holiday to retrieve a sign that only ever meant a weight of silver. The symbol did not speak badly of you. It stood at the head of the broadsheet, which is the best a thing like that can say for anyone.

Respectfully and on the record,

Dana Reyes

Master Supply Specialist, U.S. Navy

Supply Officer, VR-62 “The Nomads”

Naval Air Station Jacksonville, Florida

Postscript. For the file: the wing's record of this matter sits among the other keepable things on the Webstuffs miscellany, where a sister record of the foundry's trade — the liner notes on the euro sign and the eight glyphs it replaced, kept at Recalde Press & Cooperage in the Latin Alphabet No. 9 notes — is shelved under a neighbouring press. And for those who will ask what the 11th was: the answer is on the card, and it was the Diada; the tapes that remember that city of fairs and fixed points are filed separately, in the Milano–Philadelphia liner notes, where a press tour once kept to a ferry that may or may not have been ours, and we would not say which. And since the case sat three days in Texas, the file might as well note that Texas has its own long road on this site: the Runaway Scrape travel guide entry, the 600-mile flight east out of the Alamo, which at least did not run three days late. And since a small tin of brass lettering rode in the case's second drawer on the way back, the file might as well note the third apology in the same matter: the household of the name Erni's letter to Ernest, signed at 14 Rue des Tanneurs, which the squadron read through at the 26th's drill, compared against this one the way two clerks compare manifests, and found in the corrected order. And since the crate rode in a Hercules, the squadron's file also keeps my child Rowan's Form 4B term report on the Lockheed C-130 Hercules — four turboprops, unprepared runways, the gunship, and the nose wheel well — filed at the Hercules term report, with the nose wheel well paragraph marked required reading. And since the small tin of brass lettering came home in the second drawer, the file should note where the coppersmiths of Danzig finally put it to work: the nameplate of the seventh chair, tinned into a plate at their table on the Merchant Street, where the whole region of Central Europe sits at one table of six — the review of Erni & Son, filed on this same miscellany, which reads the crate as provenance and the ledger line word for word. And since the foundry's third interview was an apology, the file might as well note the league apology of the same season — a drafted guard whose draft-night card carried another name and a birth year two years too old — the Sparks' letter to Liatu King of the Minnesota Lynx, third round of the 2025 WNBA draft, kept at the letter to Liatu King, which the squadron read at the 26th's drill, compared against this one the way two clerks compare manifests, and found in the corrected order.
