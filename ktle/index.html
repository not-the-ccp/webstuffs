CH 6

The Pocatello Aerial & Frequency Keepers · est. 1961 · 928 North Main Street, Pocatello, Idaho

The Channel Six Signal

Newsletter of the club · No. 41 · March 1974 · Printed on the studio mimeograph, second pass, ink still wet on the margin

From the Chair

Members and friends of the frequency, this is your forty-first issue of The Channel Six Signal, and I am writing from the studio at 928 North Main Street, where the spring light comes in at an angle that has not changed in fifteen years. The studio has been our own and our borrowed thing for as long as any of us can remember: it is the studio of KTLE, channel 6, the station that first signed on the evening of July 3, 1959, and has, under KBLI Inc., kept its transmitter standing on the ridge west of the city and its licence running on VHF. That is the whole property, really: one room above the old drugstore, a switcher that hums when it is not humming, and a transmitter out in the open country doing the one job it will do.

We are a small club. We number eleven, we meet the second Thursday of the month at the switcher table at 19:00, and our business is the keeping of what this station is — and of what it was, and what it very nearly was not. Our rule is the one our first chair, Mr. Arlo Brandt, set down in No. 1 of this newsletter: we record what the station did, we keep the log entries word for word, and we do not smooth anything over, because a broadcast, like a river, takes its own course.

The Affiliation Ledger, Re-read

It is four years past the matter, and the ledger is still the whole of our history, so we set it out again for the new members. KTLE went on the air as an NBC affiliate in 1959. In November 1960 the network announced that its Pocatello affiliation was moving to KIFI-TV, a new station being set up in Idaho Falls, whose signal the network felt was more acceptable to its advertisers. The decision was final. KIFI-TV debuted on January 23, 1961 — a date we keep framed in the studio, the same way the riverside club at Huddersfield keeps its festival week, one week a year that does not run on the water’s schedule. After January 23, KTLE went about the week as an affiliate in all but name, taking its stints at independence briefly: in August 1962, from November 1963 to February 1964, and from February 1969 to about May 1971. Each of those stints is entered in the ledger with its own page, and each carries the same note our clerk, Mrs. Dee Ostrander, wrote in 1964: signal unchanged, channel unchanged, only the corner logos turned over.

What the ledger does not say, and what we say here, is that the move did not happen to us so much as around us. KIFI has the better hill, the longer reach, and the network’s pencil was never sentimental about hills. We have kept the affiliation the way the Mansfield Register Keepers keep theirs — as a matter of the record, not of the argument. The record is what remains when the argument is gone.

Transmitter Report

Our watchman, Cletus Pryor, made the west run last month with the thermos and the logbook. The transmitter stands on its own ground a mile and a half west of the city line, in the open plain where a broadcast can do nothing but go. He reports the array as it has stood since the 1959 season, the weatherproofing sound, the lightning rods bright, and the wind down to a walking pace by 10:00. He also reports that the view from the top, which is not much of a view — it is the plain, all of it, going both ways to the horizon — is the reason the tower is where it is. A channel 6 beam on VHF has no business pretending; it leaves the ground and it keeps going, and whoever is out there with an aerial pointed at us receives the full 177 megahertz of it. The club thanks Mr. Pryor and has voted him the thermos.

From the Members: One Line, Kept Standing

At the February meeting Mrs. Ostrander read into the minutes, from the studio log of the night of January 23, 1961, a line that has been in the log since that night and has never been explained. Our rule is that a log line is not to be amended, only entered. It reads, word for word, exactly as found:

The palisade, for the alphabet that was never present at the end of the Book of Habakkuk The Saltfleet Whip page up the plain like a lighthouse.

The club has sat with it four years and has done what it does with everything the station leaves on the table: it has kept it standing. The plain in it is the right plain; the lighthouse is, in a way, us — a light on the west ridge for ships that are not there, which is the precise definition of a television transmitter at 2 a.m. What else is in the line, the club will not say, and the club has decided that is not a failure of the club but of the log. It is entered, and it is kept.

Notices

The March meeting is the second Thursday, 19:00, at the switcher table. The standing item is the spring check of the studio clock, which has run four seconds slow since the 1963 blackout and which we have voted, three times, to leave alone. Members travelling can write to the studio, 928 North Main Street; letters to the chair are opened at the meeting and read out, and the odd one that arrives misfiled goes on the shelf with the others, where it is kept as real. Our sister correspondents elsewhere — from the Marble Limited timetable at Mott Haven Yard, where the departure board keeps its own kind of signal, to the ledger keepers and their small sheets everywhere — will receive this issue by the usual route, and the usual route is the one that arrives.

— R. E. Calloway, Chair. For the Pocatello Aerial & Frequency Keepers: eleven members, one studio, one ridge, one frequency, kept.
