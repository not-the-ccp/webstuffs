The property of E. A. Marsh · kept for the record and returned to the keeper

The Skinner Notebook

Diary of Dr. Elin A. Marsh, psychologist · Birmingham · kept 13 February – 9 March 2023, the fortnight of Case No. 2023-V-114

SEEN · CLERK'S OFFICE · GUILDHALL

Monday · 13 February 2023 · the car from Leamington

Tomorrow at ten I have to describe the discipline to a court that wants to keep a sentence. I have done it once before, last year, in front of another court and another record, and I know the shape of the hour now: define psychology, and then be asked about the thing itself. I rehearsed the answer in the car. Behavior is the evidence where no interior can be examined. The engine in question behaved once, on the evening of 6 August 2022, at 18:41:07, without operator input, and the behavior is the sentence. One response. I keep thinking it is a strange thing to put before a court, one response. A bird in a box can give thirty a minute; the dome gives one in a season.

Tuesday · 14 February 2023 · Guildhall, Victoria Square

The hearing. Renshaw presides in a way that reads as if he had already ruled and was letting the afternoon catch up. The question about the island came at the end, on the record, as I knew it would — the island the charts do not agree about, the one I have been asked to place and have placed and am now asked to place again, in front of a court that keeps its own. I will not soon forget, I said, because it is true. The custodian, Mr. Okonkwo, sat with the tension log of Exhibit 114-A in front of him like a score, and counsel, Mr. Cade, asked that the sentence be kept as a true statement and no more. I noticed he did not once ask the court to excuse the engine, and I think that restraint is what the court liked. Continued to March. Struck, I hope, not continued again.

Wednesday · 15 February 2023 · the shelf at home

Because I needed a second opinion on one response I pulled off the shelf the 1938 copy of The Behavior of Organisms that was my grandfather's, the one with the cracked spine and the air-dryer diagram still folded in the back. Burrhus Frederic Skinner. Born 20 March 1904 at Susquehanna, Pennsylvania; dead since 18 August 1990, at Cambridge, Massachusetts; at Harvard he held the Edgar Pierce Professorship of Psychology from 1948 until his retirement in 1974. He is the man who made the box. The operant conditioning chamber — the “Skinner box,” which the trade never let him keep and he never complained about — is a small room with a light, a key or a lever, a dispenser, and behind a slot a cumulative recorder, a paper drum whose pen moves only when the animal inside responds, so that the animal's whole behavior for the afternoon is drawn, without comment, onto a strip of paper. He founded the experimental analysis of behavior, and the philosophy he called radical behaviorism, which is only my Tuesday's question made into a doctrine: the behavior is what is available, so the behavior is what is counted.

Thursday · 16 February 2023 · the rate of the dome

Read on, and found my rate of response. Skinner considered the rate of response to be the most effective measure of response strength — not whether the response happened, but how often it is happening, and a rate can be kept the way a tension log can be kept. I did the arithmetic for the engine. From the evening of 6 August 2022 to the day of the hearing, 14 February 2023, is 192 days, which is 276,480 minutes, and in that whole span the diagnostic engine has emitted exactly one sentence. One response per 276,480 minutes. The dome, then, is a box whose subject has, so far, pressed the key once, and I wrote it under the margin of the book the way a rate is written down: the sentence is the key-press; the tension log of Exhibit 114-A is the moving pen; the dome is the chamber; the chain of 20 m that went over its limit by four millimetres is the only event the evening produced; and eleven seconds later it came back inside the limit, which is the only part of the evening the engine could classify, as the dream of it reads and as the dome engineer's line on the wall of the control room already said: every four, on the applause, not on the wind.

Friday · 17 February 2023 · one line of Cade's

A letter from Mr. Cade, a page, in the hand he writes in, which is rough. He has had my 1938, and on its flyleaf he has written a line that is not part of the letter but an instruction to the record, and I set it down exactly as it stands: “Melt the inner angles of the whole record — that the sentence be kept as a true statement of what the engine has a renovating effect, and less efficacious.” I read it four times before I sat down. And now I understand his rough syntax, because it is his argument, and it is the whole of the case in one line. Melt the inner angles: soften the corners where one clause of the testimony meets the next — the mountain road into the provincial cut, the provincial cut into the beach fishing, the beach fishing into the chain — so that the record closes as one sentence a keeper of records would have kept, and no hard seam shows at the join. A true statement of what the engine has a renovating effect: the sentence renewed the archive with an evening nobody had logged; the engine's effect on the record is a renovating one, and the sentence is true in the clause-by-clause sense I swore to and no more true than that, which is all a declaration can ask. And less efficacious, his concession folded into the middle of his own sentence: his brief, the best brief of the day, is less efficacious than the tension log standing beside it, because behavior alone is one thing and behavior plus its moving pen is another. So: keep the sentence true, melt the corners, and let the log carry the weight.

Thursday · 9 March 2023 · struck

Struck this morning. The application is granted on both heads, and the sentence is to be kept in the joint keeping of the control room beneath the north concourse and the archive of the venue systems, with the log, under the reference it already bears. The result of the final stands as published; gold for the Welsh gymnast on the individual hoop; the sentence overtook the applause rather than being stopped by it. I went up afterward, with Mr. Okonkwo, and stood beneath the west ring for a minute without saying anything about segment 20. The box is empty, the key is unpressed, and that is the whole of the testimony now: one response, kept. I have filed the notebook where the record keeps itself — beside the transcript excerpt of the Birmingham hearing, the message itself under error reference ERR-114, The Frizelle Message, and the island evidence in the Vancouver record it stands beside. The island I still will not name in ink; the road the sentence named is on the shelf with the rest.

End of the fortnight. The front desk of the class miscellany is at Webstuffs.
