Minutes of the Silver Sides Quarterly Council

14 October 1951, ten o'clock in the morning

the back room above the typesetter, 537 Fifth Avenue, New York

Present

Mr. Louis SilberkleitChair, publisher; three titles on his desk and two of them dark
Mr. Robert A. W. LowndesEditor; in charge of the remainder of the run, as it was later to be called
Mr. Charles HornigFormer editor; first two issues; present to be thanked and to leave at eleven
Cpl. M. E. Voss (ret.)Council member; former high-angle gunner; proofreader of the record
Ms. Ada RehnClerk; kept the minutes in green ink, as the division keeps its ledgers

Quorum established at 10:04. The room smelled of the type case. Mr. Hornig declined coffee, accepted a biscuit, and sat at the far end of the table the whole morning, which the clerk notes was taken by nobody as a sign.

Matter 1 — The Two Runs, Read Back

The Chair opened with the record of the house. Science Fiction Quarterly had first run from 1940 to 1943, launched during the boom in science fiction magazines that ended the 1930s, while the whole counter of the division sat full of new titles. For its first two issues the editor was Charles Hornig; for the remainder of that run, Robert A. W. Lowndes. The three Silberkleit science fiction titles — this one, and the two launched about the same time, Science Fiction and Future Fiction — had all ceased publication before the end of World War II. The cause was argued at length in 1942 and agreed in one line in 1943: slow sales, and the paper shortages. The Chair asked that both causes be entered together, in that order, so the record would not drift. It was so entered, and the room did not argue.

Asked whether the 1940 issues should be reprinted, Mr. Lowndes said no, and then, more quietly, that the 1940 issues were already reprinted in the minds of the people who had read them. The clerk set both sentences down. On the motion of Mr. Voss, the council resolved that the relaunch be treated as a new first run with a long memory, and that no back issue be reprinted for at least seven years.

Matter 2 — The Market, 1950 to 1951

Mr. Silberkleit read the spring figures. In 1950 and 1951 the market improved; the newsstands that had folded the genre flat were unfolding it again, and in that year the house had already relaunched Future Fiction and Science Fiction. What remained on the dark shelf was the Quarterly, and the question of the morning was whether the third title would hold as well as the other two. The clerk entered the stand numbers from the card file: 41,000 copies of Future Fiction at its relaunch month, 38,500 of Science Fiction, and a projected 25,000 for the Quarterly at a cover of twenty-five cents, paid for in full at the stand. The Chair noted that the Quarterly had never been paid for in full at the stand before, and that this was the whole of the change.

On paper: the wartime rationing was over, the book came through at sixty pounds, and the compositor had the room he wanted. The Chair laid a proof sheet of the masthead on the table, face down, and did not turn it over until the vote. He said, and the record keeps it, that the sheet was a promise to the paper, which was a longer customer than the reader.

Matter 3 — The Line Read Into the Record

Cpl. Voss, proofreader of the record, asked to read one line of his own, carried over from the veterans' column of his county paper, which he had been keeping since the squadron was stood down. He asked that it be entered word for word, uncorrected, the same way the house enters its hard lines, and the chair allowed it. It was read at 10:41, and entered thus:

The “product,” in his careful column: 14,208 rounds in twelve printed pages and I have no need of my squadron because the logbook to this day it is read.

The clerk, being a clerk, appended her working reading: in the careful column of the editor, the product — the magazine, this magazine, the one being set that very morning — carries the weight of 14,208 rounds of copy across twelve printed pages, and the gunner who wrote it needs no squadron, because the logbook is read to this day. The council accepted the reading without adopting it, which is the house rule for a line of that kind. The vote on the relaunch, taken at 10:44, was four to none, Mr. Hornig voting yes from the far end of the table.

Matter 4 — The Editor for the Remainder

Mr. Silberkleit thanked Mr. Hornig for the first two issues of the first run and for the eleven years of quiet in which he had not corrected them. Mr. Hornig said two issues is two issues, thanked the house for the biscuit, and left at 11:02 without the coffee, as the record allows. The council then confirmed Robert A. W. Lowndes as editor of the relaunch and of the remainder, with a standing instruction that the contents page list the stories in the order they were read, not the order they were promised. The instruction was new; the clerk notes it was the only one of the morning that Mr. Lowndes underlined twice.

The relaunch ran, as the record now shows, from 1951 to 1958. The council did not foresee the end; it foresaw the next issue, and then the one after, and sat again on the same Thursday in seven weeks with the same card file on the middle of the table, one number heavier.

Matters Arising & Adjournment

  • The typesetter's back room to be cleared of coffee cups before the next sitting; the compositor's floor to be left alone.
  • One proof of the masthead to be pinned in the window of the Fifth Avenue street door, where the stands can see it, until the first issue sells out.
  • For the form of a proper minute of a council that kept itself for a thousand years and one line, the division refers the reader to the Minutes of the Council of Ripon Abbey, AD 788, which entered its dead abbot's obit three times and got the voyage down the Ouse right the first time.
  • For a modern ordinary meeting that found one business and kept it small, see the Tosa Municipal Council — Ordinary Meeting No. 47 and its memorial plaque at the school site.
  • All other matters referred to the card file, where the genre has been filed, in order, since 1938.

Meeting adjourned at 11:07. The masthead sheet was turned over at 11:08, after the vote, and found to be set correctly. The whole of the change, again, was that the reader pays in full at the stand. — A. Rehn, clerk
