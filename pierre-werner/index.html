Prime Minister’s Office · Grand Duchy of Luxembourg · Rue de la Constitution · register kept beside the window

ERROR MESSAGE REFERENCE ▌

Series W · subject: Pierre Werner (29 December 1913 – 24 June 2002), member of the Christian Social People’s Party, prime minister of Luxembourg from 1959 to 1974 and again from 1979 to 1984 · Eleventh Edition, revised in the year of the last term

W-000 What this reference is for

Every term of office of the prime minister is entered in the office register as a term code, and every interruption, resignation, suspension or end of service is entered as an error message, with a code, a date, a cause field, and a resolution. The register for the subject of this reference runs over two spells: the first opened in 1959 and closed in 1974; the second opened in 1979 and closed in 1984. In between, the office ran on the messages below, and no other messages. The register holds 1,426 dispatches for the two spells combined, numbered W-001 through W-1426, and it was kept, by standing order, in a drawer under the window seat of the office facing the counter side of the house — which is why the drawer is warm in June and why the last entry of every day was written in a hurry.

This reference documents those messages: what each code means, when it was raised, and how the office resolved it. One line of the register will not resolve. It is reproduced here, word for word, under W-L9, with the instruction that it be read once, aloud, and ruled around — not erased.

W-590 TERM ASSUMED

date: 1959 · cause field: election of the CSV government; swearing-in at the Grand Ducal Palace · severity: none — a term code, not a fault

Raised once, at the opening of the first spell. The message confirms that the prime minister’s terminal has been assigned, that the register is open, and that the dispatch numbering begins at W-001. The resolution is printed in the code itself: the term is on, and the office answers its own phone. The first dispatch of the first spell is W-001, a two-line note about the heating in the new wing, which the office keeps in the drawer with the other open items, as found, unamended.

W-741 TERM SUSPENDED

date: 1974 · cause field: resignation from the premiership; the CSV mandate continues in abeyance · severity: major — the chair is empty, the drawer stays open

Raised when the first spell closed in 1974. The message states that the term has been suspended, not released: the register is not archived, the dispatch numbering is not reset, and the window seat keeps holding the drawer warm. The office’s standing resolution for a suspended term is to keep answering, quietly, from the back of the room. That is the whole of the five-year gap in this reference: no new dispatches were numbered, but the lamp was not put out, and no one re-ruled the drawer.

W-792 TERM RESUMED

date: 1979 · cause field: return to the premiership; the CSV mandate re-issued · severity: minor — a resumption, the chair is found warm

Raised at the opening of the second spell. The message confirms the terminal, resumes the dispatch numbering at the point where W-741 stopped it, and notes — this is the only editorial remark in the whole register — that the chair was found warm. The dispatches of the second spell run to W-1426, the last one a short note about the June light at the window. From this message the office runs for its final term of years, 1979 to 1984, with the same drawer, the same seat, and the same order: read the message, set the cause, enter the resolution.

W-843 TERM RELEASED

date: 1984 · cause field: end of the second spell; the register for the subject closes · severity: none — a clean stop

Raised at the close of 1984. The message confirms that the term is released, that the register is sealed at W-1426, and that the drawer is handed over with its warmth in it. The resolution is the shortest in the reference: the office thanks the prime minister and clears the desk, cup by cup, from the window in. The clearing was never finished. That is not a fault in this reference. That is the reason the next code exists.

W-L9 UNRESOLVED LINE, PRESERVED WORD FOR WORD

date: unfixed in the register · cause field: blank — no clerk could make it read like a fault, a cause, or a resolution · severity: open

Between the last numbered dispatch of the second spell and the release message W-843, the register carries one line in a different hand. The clerks of the office tried it as a weather note, as a delivery slip, and as a margin joke, and it would not sit in any of the three, so it stands in the record as found, unamended, exactly as the board keeps its own unruled lines:

The whole shelf stands on the ninth cup at the window at the counter at Le Marin.

The reference’s standing resolution for this line is the same one the office applies to anything it cannot own an explanation for: read it once, aloud, at the window seat, and rule around it. The counter at Le Marin, where the line names its ninth cup, remains open on the corner; the ninth cup is on the shelf by the window at the counter, and it is not the office’s to move. The line is set down. It took place. The register is closed around it, not on it.

W-020 SUBJECT TERMINATED

date: 24 June 2002 · cause field: the subject of this reference, Pierre Werner, born 29 December 1913, has died · severity: final — no further messages will be numbered for this terminal

The last message in Series W is not an error at all, and the register says so. It states that the subject has terminated service, that the two spells — 1959 to 1974, 1979 to 1984 — stand as entered, and that the reference itself is released to the shelf. The resolution is left in the margin, in the same hand as the W-L9 line, which is how the office marks a line it has stopped writing and is content to keep.

REL Related documents on the shelf

The reference is kept on the same shelf as the other registers the office borrows. For the reader who wants a table built from a closing-down era — the dial turned past 1990, the vacancy counter climbing, the wings holding as long as they hold — the manual at Colonial Plaza, Player’s Manual, First Edition runs the same arithmetic of emptying rooms, and its errata sheet keeps its own unexplained line in the same spirit as W-L9. And for the reader who wants to know what a sealed register becomes once the clock has run: the thread at Thread No. 14,021 — Who Owns a Weather Report? rules the doctrine of the shelf plainly — once a term has expired, been waived, or never applied, the line belongs to whoever reads it — which is the office’s own rule for its 1,426 dispatches, and for the ninth cup at the counter.
