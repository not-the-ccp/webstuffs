THAMES VALLEY SURVEY · SEASON 1975–76 · BOOK 4, PAGES 112–119

SPECIMEN LOG
NO. 88

John Boyden

14 September 1936 – 21 September 2021

executivus classicus britannicus

Observed in habitat by T. M. Quill, field naturalist, with the borrowed notebook of the Kirkcaldy man

I. First collection — Malaya, and the shop on Oxford Street

The first entries for this specimen run to a humidity I have never found in a British room. National service in Malaya, the log says, and I believe it: a young man of nineteen, 1955–56, standing in monsoon light where the air itself is a wet instrument, the kind of posting that tunes a person down to slower movements. What I have logged at length is the next habitat, because it is the one the specimen returned to for a decade: the HMV record shop on Oxford Street, where the specimen joined the staff and, in my notation, remained a resident of the glass cases. I walked that street in the rain in March and counted the display windows. The staff kept the wax side facing the light, the way a herbarium keeper turns the specimen to the sun, and the Sunday queue ran the full length of the pavement, which is a fact worth one minute in this book.

II. Richmond — Philharmonic Records and the label that carried his name

The specimen later founded its own concern, Philharmonic Records, at Richmond, on the river, where the habitat softens: yew hedges, a bridge, and the Thames going west at its own tempo. There it pressed, there it catalogued, and there it set up a second, smaller label — John Boyden Recordings — carrying the founder's own name on the label, which is the closest a business of the period came to a signature on a wing. I have kept one sleeve from the Richmond years in my tin under the bench, the corner creased at exactly the angle a hundred thousand sleeves are creased. The plant ran hot in July; the lacquer cooled on the shelves like a tide going out.

III. 1967 — Music for Pleasure, and the sub-label for the serious hour

By 1967 the specimen was working with Paul Hamlyn at Music for Pleasure, the joint venture with EMI, where it did what the best managers of the classical side do: it carved a sub-label out of the parent the way a shell splits from the reef. The sub-label was called Classics for Pleasure, and the pairing of those two words is the whole natural history of the specimen in one line — the pleasure that is the classical, the classical that is the pleasure. The 45s and LPs went out in cream sleeves with the red and black logo, and the trade press, when it remembered the specimen's name at all, spelled it with one y and no fuss.

IV. April 1975 — the London Symphony Orchestra, and the season that ended in October

In April 1975 the specimen was appointed managing director of the London Symphony Orchestra, and the habitat became the largest indoor one a business executive of the period could hold: a boardroom, a concert hall, a season of programmes, and at the podium the chief conductor, André Previn. I kept the appointment line in the ledger the day it was read aloud. What followed is logged in the specimen's own flat, cold handwriting: in October the specimen was dismissed, the same October, after a part of the board unsuccessfully attempted to remove Previn. A boardroom is a strange ecosystem — the votes are the weather, the minutes are the rainfall, and the conductor's stand is the one point where the whole room listens at once. The specimen, it seems to me, had tried to keep both the ledger and the podium, and the October decision was the river drawing back from one of them.

V. The notation of these pages — the Kirkcaldy man

Readers of the survey will ask how the margin figures are graded, the little ticks beside the dates. The system is not mine. It belongs to a retired clockmaker and lecturer from Kirkcaldy on the Fife coast, who gave a free lecture each winter on the rests in a movement — what the silence in a phrase is doing, and how you can be wrong about a silence the way you can be wrong about a bird. He measured nothing in seconds; he measured in the pauses of his own teaching, the held breaths between one explanation and the next. His notebook came to me by way of a second-hand bookshop in Kew, and I have written these pages in his hand's system, tick for tick. It is a writing system that uses a standard this fine: the minutes of stillness at the Kirkcaldy man's lectures. Against that standard, the specimen's own papers rate as very quiet, very regular, and — in the October 1975 folder — exactly one page torn out.

VI. Afterward — the writing, and the seven days

Boyden subsequently wrote, and the log does not pretend otherwise: a thin account of the season, forty-odd pages, which he left in a cardboard box with the sleeve from Richmond years in it and the April appointment letter on top. I have read it once and ticked it twice, which is the Kirkcaldy grading for a document that is mostly stillness. The specimen's last date in the book is 21 September 2021, which is a detail the naturalist keeps separate from everything else in this log, seven days past his own birthday, the way a tide finishes a little past the mark on the wall.

Filed for cross-reference beside the other field logs of this survey: the specimen log on David Lynch, likewise kept in a borrowed notebook, and the ledger of Diocletian, the emperor who also finished in a garden. The survey itself begins at the front page.
