FAULT 2240 SCULPTOR PRESENT IN KITCHEN SEVERITY: ONE SCULPTURE

Error documentation for the standing fault that opens whenever the mountain the city faces sends a sculptor into the pot’s frame, filed in white chalk on the blackboard over the stove by Chiara Zeffirelli, and read back into the thread by Aunt Deb from Beckley, one line at a time, the way the mole card, No. 114, is read at every rehearsal of Atylantos.

§2 The Subject of the Fault

The sculptor is Auguste Rodin — full name on the fault sheet, as the house rules require, François Auguste René Rodin (12 November 1840 – 17 November 1917), a French sculptor considered a founder of modern sculpture, and the only resident of this atlas whose hands are documented as being better at holding a city than a ladle. The fault sheet records his admission against the system: he was rejected three times from the prestigious École des Beaux-Arts — 1864, 1865, 1867, the three refusals filed here as ERROR 064, ERROR 065 and ERROR 067, all three closed with the same disposition, retry, elsewhere — and he trained instead at an applied arts school in Paris, the Petite École, and in various studios and workshops, the way one trains a pot, slowly, at altitude, without the drama.

The system notes that he gained public attention and individual commissions only in his late 30s, which the household finds reassuring: the mole is the same, and the mountain is the same. Rodin is known for the boldly expressive, emotive physicality of his sculptures, and the fault sheet carries four standing entries for them: the Monument to Balzac, The Kiss, The Burghers of Calais, and The Gates of Hell, a sculptural composition that includes a figure later known as The Thinker. Many of Rodin’s sculptures were criticized — the Balzac above all, so much so that the Académie des Beaux-Arts of his own city refused the monument in 1898 — and the fault sheet observes, in its drier voice, that a dish of sixteen chiles and 100 grams of Oaxaca chocolate is criticized by the aunties of Colonia Roma for precisely the same reason: it refuses to be pretty.

§4 Standing Error Entries, by Work

  1. ERROR 0840

    La Porte de l’Enfer — The Gates of Hell

    Commissioned in 1880, and the fault sheet keeps it at the top because everything else in the kitchen hangs from it. A sculptural composition, door-shaped, crowded with the damned — a city’s worth of figures, which Doña Esmeralda approved of as the only work of his that understands a table for sixteen. From it, later, came a single seated figure leaning on his fist, the one the world renames The Thinker. The sheet notes the rename with mild displeasure: the figure belongs to the Gates; the name belongs to the market.

  2. ERROR 0885

    The Burghers of Calais

    Commissioned by the city of Calais in 1884, six men of the town who pledged their lives in 1347, and the fault the work files is that it gives the group, not the hero — six ordinary bodies standing where one triumphant body ought to stand. The household finds this its favorite fault. The mole is built the same way: sixteen bowls, not one great bowl, and the city of 9,209,944 people is fed in two passes, the way everything there is fed.

  3. ERROR 0898

    Monument to Balzac

    The man in the cloak, the face almost melted out of the stone. Criticized by the critics, refused by the Académie des Beaux-Arts in 1898, and the fault sheet logs the refusal under SYSTEM REJECTED THE WORKER’S FAVORITE, then corrects itself: the work rejected the system. Doña Esmeralda's margin note, in her own hand, reads: “the mole is the same way, and I will not fix it.”

  4. ERROR 0882

    The Kiss

    The marble pair, leaning into each other over nothing, which the atlas files as a fault only because the kitchen keeps reaching for it when it means to reach for the cinnamon. Chiara’s note: “the sixteenth bowl is by the window for the same reason the kiss is over the abyss — the next person is already there, we are just the ones who have to set it.” She signed it with the opera date and the mountain, and the mountain, as usual, kept its own front row.

  5. ERROR 1917

    The sculptor departs

    17 November 1917, Meudon. The sheet does not close the fault; it moves it to MAINTENANCE, INDEFINITE, because a founder of modern sculpture is not removed, he is kept in mind, which is the family’s official word for anything that has left but keeps coming back — the same word the Noctuary uses of the 1791 doubloon, turned over twice a year, not spent, shown to the dreamer who is next.

§5 Mangled Scan — Line 274 of the Studio Manual

In the drawer of the atelier’s old manual, between the page on wax and the page on marble, the scanner of this household (the same scanner, the one the Wheeling archivist blames for eating “PhD” in the Kanawha thread) came back with line 274 ruined. The line is kept word for word, because that is the rule of this thread and the rule of this pot:

“The carcass is next divided into kitchens and the mountain the city it is believed that he left.”

Best reading, in the moderator’s voice: the manual is dividing the charred onion, the garlic and the tomato — the carcass of the first step — into the sixteen kitchens of the sixteen boroughs, and then it goes off the edge of the page into the mountain, and what the scanner heard as the city it is believed that he left was the manual’s whole argument, mangled: that the city is believed to have left its mountain there, facing the stove, as a witness, and that the witness is not to be served. The house holds both the sentence and its reading, and serves neither.

§6 Workaround

  1. Log the fault. Do not try to close it. A fault with 16 alcaldías affected closes only at the census, and the census is in 2030, and Doña Esmeralda does not keep appointments with decades.
  2. Keep the sculptor in the frame of the door, not in the pot. He is load-bearing, and the cinnamon at minute 25 is load-bearing, and if the mole is not right after the chocolate, it is the year, not the hands.
  3. Set the sixteenth bowl by the window, facing the morning. If a man with a sculptor’s hands arrives before the soup, the bowl is his and the fault downgrades by one, to severity: one less sculptor, which is the best the system has ever been able to do.
  4. Show the doubloon to the dreamer who is next. The 1791 piece stays in the mind, not in the pot; the fault sheet is its edge-marking, the way Card No. 114 is the drawing room’s.
  5. If a board in West Virginia pins this sheet, the next person is the one eating. This is not a joke. This is the disposition.
