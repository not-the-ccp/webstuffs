I. The Lot

The lamp stands on the rostrum to the left of the empty 3×9×5 pan, which is included in this lot and will be raffled at the end of the morning, the proceeds going to whoever cancelled the most things that August. The estate is sold from the household that kept it, the bakery on Magsaysay Avenue, and the lot is offered subject to the rule printed on the first page of the family recipe ledger: whatever is voided, bake. A lamp that has been lit over a voided Games and has never once gone out is, under that rule, not an object but an ingredient. Bidders are asked to keep this in mind when they write their numbers, and not to write them too fast.

II. On the Class in the Jar

The jar is sold with its class. The amphibians are ectothermic, anamniotic, four-limbed vertebrate animals that constitute the class Amphibia. In its broadest sense the class is a paraphyletic group encompassing all tetrapods, but excluding the amniotes — the tetrapods with an amniotic membrane, such as modern reptiles, birds and mammals — and the auctioneer notes for the record that this lot therefore contains, in the broadest sense, everyone in the room except the room. All extant amphibians belong to the monophyletic subclass Lissamphibia, which presents itself in the jar in three living orders. Anura, the frogs and toads, are represented by the brass itself, seated, and by two smaller residents who will not be named, naming being their particular way of leaving. Urodela, the salamanders, are represented by three, one of them missing its left hind leg, see the Condition Report. Gymnophiona, the caecilians, are represented by one, which is said not to be present when inquired of. Evolved to be mostly semiaquatic, amphibians have adapted to inhabit a wide variety of habitats, with most species living in freshwater, wetland or terrestrial ecosystems — such as riparian woodland, the fossorial dark, and the very top of a beet-and-blackstrap loaf. The jar is a wetland in the strict sense: a border, claimed by both banks.

III. The Inscription on the Brass Band

Engraved around the base, in the cramped hand of Etta Collins, copied word for word from the leaf of the harbour fishmonger’s almanac that was pasted into the Class 8C report on the whole business of endings — the report the same household filed on the shelf beside the ledger, and which reads in full at The Extinction Event — Report, Class 8C — the band reads, word for word:

“Let the roots when they are green and gold.”

The household reads the line the way it reads the misprinted ingredients line of Card 298 of the recipe ledger: as a measure, not a decoration. A root is what holds a tree to a bank; the lamp is what holds the light to the bog. When the leaves above the water turn green and gold, the jar is not to be emptied, the roots are not to be pulled, the lot is not to be knocked down early, and the auctioneer’s pencil is to be put down. The line was last read aloud on the seventh day of the seventh week of August 1980, when Etta lit the lamp over the Carboniferous Coal Loaf — beet and blackstrap for a period sixty million years long, oven at 325°, the year of the voided Games in the margin — and let the line hold the dark until the loaf rose, which it did, as the ledger records, exactly the way coal does not.

IV. Provenance

Hammered in 1929 for Etta Collins on a Creole corner of New Orleans, where the coal man came down Magazine Street on Mondays with a wheelbarrow and a song, and the lamp first stood on the bakery’s front step with a light the colour of the swamp in the dark. Moved to Inglewood, California, in 1948, where the winters were mild and the light did not need them, and kept ever since at the bakery on Magsaysay Avenue, where the recipe ledger began in 1951 and has grown ever after, one cancelled thing at a time. Lit, without interruption of any length the household will confirm, over the August baking of 1980, when the Games of the XXII Olympiad in Moscow went dark and the rule found its fullest test. The leaf of almanac from which the band was copied remains in the school report named above; the family’s own dream dictionary, Entry 314, kept by the stove, warns that an armoured one walks at thresholds — see Luolishania — Dream Dictionary — and the buyer is reminded that the lamp is sold with its thresholds as they are: eleven pairs of legs, plates of sclerite, the soft animal beneath, and the mud of Chengjiang, soft, five hundred and eighteen million years old.

V. Condition Report

  • The brass is sound and the patina deep; the moss is original and is to be kept, not cleaned.
  • The jar’s water is unboilable. The auctioneer declines to test it and has been relieved of doing so.
  • The seated frog (Anura) is intact, 41 cm, does not croak, and is reported to be listening. Two smaller Anura present; not named.
  • Three salamanders (Urodela) present; one missing its left hind leg, the limb having been exchanged, by family legend, for the misprinted line of Card 298, which now stands in the ledger word for word as an ingredient, three-quarters of a pint of a Games that never happened.
  • One caecilian (Gymnophiona) listed. The caecilian is not present. On inquiry it is reported to be in the ground, which is the correct and unremarkable way for a caecilian to be, and which the house of Marlow & Fenwick will not argue with.
  • The brass band reads. The empty pan is empty. The raffle is at the end of the morning.

Lot 40 of this catalogue, a salt-and-current relative of the lot in hand, stands on the same shelf in the River Wonders Gallery beneath brass placard No. 0147, which the household keeps by the stove and which measures, at its best, to 860 volts: Electric Eel — Museum Placard No. 0147. The buyer is cautioned that the two are not to be sold to the same hand, the shelf being a border and both banks being claimed. The lot that follows this one in the morning, Lot 42, the album Better of Haley Reinhart, is not to be placed in the jar either, the water being unboilable and the disc a morning thing; it stands on the rostrum to the right of the empty pan, with its two singles and its one pencilled line, and will be knocked down on its own merits. The catalogue itself is bound into the house ledger, which opens with the front matter of the household at the first page, and this lot is bound beside it as Lot 41, where the margin is wide enough for one more cancelled thing. All lots are sold under the first-page rule. Whatever is voided, bake — and the lamp, which has never once gone out, is in this catalogue the one thing that cannot.
