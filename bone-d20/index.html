CERTIFIED EXTRACT

The dispute: thirteen hand-turned bone dice, inked in walnut stain, of which one twenty-sided die rolls only ones and twenties, and the condition, repeated under oath, that the seller swears it is not cursed and will eat the first roll if it is. The following is preserved verbatim from the record, with exhibits cited and one order of the Court set out in full.

p. 47

The Court. We are sitting in Civil Case No. CV-1987-019, renumbered CV-2024-041, Fitch v. De la Fuente, together with the application In re the bone twenty-sided die entered as Exhibit 14. The courtroom today is the Bell Creek one-room schoolhouse. Plaintiff's counsel has confirmed, and the record shows, that the roof is still unrepaired and that two Saturdays remain unmade good, and this Court has ruled that both are to be treated as costs in the account. The plaintiff claims ownership of the full set of thirteen; the defendant claims one die. We will hear her first, because the die is on her table and it is listening.

The Defendant. Your Honour. I do not claim the set. I claim the twenty-sided die. The set is a hand-turned bone set, d4 through d12 plus two twenty-sided dice, kept for forty years in a garage in Ponsonby and now stored in a cedar box at Section 14, Bell Creek. Every die in the set behaves. Only one does not. It rolls only ones and twenties, every time, in any hand, in any country. I want it back, sealed, and I want the record to say it is not mine because it will not let me keep it.

The Court. The defendant may be reminded that the sale advertisement published in Dice & Linens — Role-Playing Classifieds, Vol. 1, No. 3, states that the seller swears the die is not cursed. The defendant, you may answer the advertisement.

The Defendant. The advertisement is the plaintiff's writing. I will say only: I have rolled that die four hundred and twelve times, and I have kept a ledger of every roll. It is one or it is twenty. I have lost a Thursday to it in the back room of the motel on Main Street, Clayton, New Mexico. I have lost a winter to it. The ledger is Exhibit 3.

p. 48

Mr. Gann, for the Plaintiff. Your Honour, the set was offered for sale at thirty-four dollars the set, serious inquiries only, and the defendant bought it. There is no clause for one die. I call the defendant to explain her alibi for the summer of 1987, when the die first began, by her account, to misbehave.

The Court. Proceed.

Mr. Gann. Ms. De la Fuente, your testimony is that in the summer of 1987 you waited out a failed alternator in the back room of the motel on Main Street, Clayton, and read Santiago: A Myth of the Far Future aloud to the owner's daughter. Correct?

The Defendant. Correct. And the cook, Esperanza Reyes, listened from the kitchen door.

Mr. Gann. And the chile she cooked that winter is the trail chile written up on the recipe card preserved at Santiago's Trail Chile — A Family Recipe? You will read the card into the record.

The Defendant. (Reading, Exhibit 9.) “Trail chile, serves eight, three hours at a bubble so slow a match beside the pot will not flicker.” I will add, for the record, that it is the campaign in that back room — Thursdays, 7:00 to 11:00 — that has run on that chile ever since, and that the first roll I ever made with the Exhibit 14 die, on the fourth Thursday of that winter, was a one. The alternator was a one. The story that night died on that one and had to be retold taller.

p. 49

Mr. Gann. Ms. De la Fuente, you understand the game the die belongs to. You will describe it for the benefit of the transcript.

The Defendant. The die belongs to Dungeons & Dragons, a fantasy tabletop role-playing game originally created and designed by Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson, first published in 1974 by Tactical Studies Rules. It was derived from miniature wargames, with a variation of the 1971 game Chainmail serving as the initial rule system, and has been published since 1997 by Wizards of the Coast, later a subsidiary of Hasbro. Its publication is commonly recognized as the beginning of modern role-playing games and the role-playing game industry, and the industry went on to influence video games, especially the role-playing video game genre. The die, Your Honour, is not an artifact of the game. The game is an artifact of the die. Somewhere in 1974, someone turned a twenty-sided die out of bone and the whole industry sat down around it.

Mr. Gann. That is not evidence, that is folklore. On the condition in the advertisement: the seller “swears it is not cursed and will eat the first roll if it is.” The plaintiff has filed an affidavit stating he is prepared to perform. Your Honour, the plaintiff requests that the die be rolled under oath, in open court, and that the condition be treated as satisfied the moment the die lands.

The Court. Motion granted, with one observation for the record: this is the first time in my nineteen years on this bench that the Court has granted a motion to roll an exhibit. W. Hartwell, take Exhibit 14 to the bench and open the case.

p. 50

Stenographer's note. (Exhibit 14 placed on the bench. The courtroom is quiet. The die is rolled once, by the clerk, at 11:03 a.m. It settles on twenty. The clerk records: “20. A twenty.” The plaintiff rises, leaves the courtroom, and returns at 11:07 a.m. with a slice of cast-iron cornbread, which he eats in the record. Mr. Gann objects that the first roll is not a bread. The Court overrules: the condition, as written, does not say what the roll tastes like, and the plaintiff has satisfied every word of it. The cornbread is marked Exhibit 15 and returned to the plaintiff, crumbs and all.)

The Court. Before we hear the remaining exhibits, the Clerk will read into the record the letter filed by Elise Moreau, Board Secretary, Waitematā Local Board, Auckland, concerning the other twenty-sided die of the set. (Reading.) “The companion die of the set was last in lawful use on Saturday 14 June 2024 in the ground-floor waiting room of the former Newton Road clinic building, 12 Graham Street, Auckland Central, in a live action exercise organized in that room, all resolution by conversation and the register. The register itself — fourteen thousand immunisations, 1962 to April 2019 — was the subject of the board's extraordinary meeting of 12 June 2024, whose minutes, WLB/2024/EM-06, are preserved and certified at Ofanaite Ana Dewes — Waitematā Local Board Minutes. The companion die rolled no number at all that day, being expressly forbidden, and we ask the Court to find that a die which rolls nothing is not a die which lies.” (Letter filed as Exhibit 12. The Court takes it as received.)

p. 51

Mr. Gann. Your Honour, in sum: the die is the plaintiff's, it was sold to the defendant as part of a set, it misbehaves, and the condition is spent. There is nothing left to curse. I submit the whole set return to Section 14.

The Defendant. Your Honour. Four hundred and twelve rolls. One or twenty, always, for thirty-seven years, in two countries and one motel room. A die that can only roll the best and the worst roll cannot be property, because it has no middle where ownership lives. I am not asking the Court to believe the curse. I am asking the Court to find that the curse is undisproven, and to seal the die where it can do no more to any of us but to the ledger.

The Court. (After a pause of one and a half minutes, during which Exhibit 14 is rolled twice under the bench by the plaintiff and twice by the clerk, all results recorded.) The Court will state its findings and orders at the close of the record. The parties will stand by.

p. 52

Findings and Order of the Court

The Court. This Court finds: (1) that the set of thirteen bone dice, inked in walnut stain, was lawfully offered at thirty-four dollars the set and lawfully sold; (2) that the Exhibit 14 die has, in this Court's own presence, rolled only ones and twenties, and that the companion die of the set rolled nothing on 14 June 2024, which the Court records as consistent with a die that lies; (3) that the condition “not cursed, and will eat the first roll if it is” was performed in open court at 11:03 a.m., Exhibit 15 having been consumed in the record; and (4) that the curse is found, on the whole of the evidence — the ledger, the alternator, the chile, the bread — not proven, and also not disproven, which is, the Court notes, the only honest verdict a twenty-sided die can take.

IT IS ORDERED:

1. That twelve of the thirteen dice shall remain the property of the plaintiff, to be stored in the cedar box at Section 14, Bell Creek, together with the ancho-chile-stained character sheet and the coffee ring across the chapter on the whale's size, both of which the Court declines to weigh.

2. That the Exhibit 14 die shall be sealed in the walnut and brass-lined box marked Lot 27 of the Hartwell & Gann auction inventory, at 1208 Thirteenth Street, Lincoln, and shall not be rolled by any hand not named in this record.

3. That claims of ownership, of blessing, of un-blessing, and of first roll, made to the sealed die after the date hereof, be directed in writing, stating the roll and the weather. Apply in writing to Luz de la Fuente, Denver. Enclose a page of the ledger, or be refused.

4. That costs be assessed as the roof, or the two Saturdays, whichever arrives first, to be reported to the Court by the plaintiff's neighbour, Mrs. Ada Kowalczyk, whose testimony this Court found the truest in the record, though she did not testify.

The Court. The hearing is adjourned. The stenographer will note that the Court, in leaving the room, did not pick up Exhibit 14 from the bench, and that the exhibit, having been left unattended for exactly one minute, was found by the marshal on his return in a different orientation. The Court declines to rule on the orientation. The record is closed.
