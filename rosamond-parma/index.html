The Council of the Law Library, University of California, Berkeley

Boalt Hall, 2150 Bancroft Way · minutes kept in the green ledger, folio 214

Minute Session No. 214 · Monday, 11 November 1946 · 2:30 in the afternoon · reading room of the Law Library

I. Call to order; attendance

The session was called to order by the Dean of the Law School, Dr. Paul L. Sayre, at a quarter past two, the bells of Boalt Hall having gone off for the faculty assembly a minute before. Present: the Dean; Miss Gladys K. Ferrell, dean of women and chair of the committee on memorials; Professor H. M. Whitcomb of the history department, representing the faculty; Mr. Oren J. Baskerville, assistant librarian in charge of acquisitions; Mrs. Ida T. Rourke, head of the reference desk; and the clerk, Mr. S. P. Ansel. Absent and excused: the University librarian, Mr. W. B. Dutton, attending the regional meeting of state college librarians in Sacramento, to which he left, in writing, a short address for the memorial, marked for the minutes.

II. The minutes of session 213

The minutes of the previous session, held 14 October in the same room and closed in the usual hour, were read by the clerk and approved without amendment, except that the entry on the return of the California Reports set of 1891–1913 from the fire-damaged annex was corrected to read 214 volumes, not 204, on the evidence of the packing lists in the strong room.

III. Memorial for Miss Rosamond Thomas Parma (1884 – October 24, 1946)

Miss Ferrell stated for the record that the council now sat to honour Miss Rosamond Thomas Parma, of this building, who died at her home on California Street, Berkeley, on Thursday, October 24, in her sixty-second year, and that the library would keep its doors open on the day as she had always preferred, because she had always said a library that closes is a house with its lamp out.

It was agreed, on motion of Professor Whitcomb, seconded by Mrs. Rourke, that the record of Miss Parma's service be read in full, as follows: appointed to the Berkeley Law Library in 1923 and the first of its law librarians to hold the post as it is held now; builder of the collection from a shelf of digests into the working library the Law School uses daily, with the gift lists of the graduating classes kept in numbered albums from the first, album one of them, in the strong room, with her name on the cover in her own hand; first woman to hold the presidency of the American Association of Law Libraries, the address delivered at the Chicago meeting in 1945, on law libraries and the law school's claim on the community, being reprinted this month by the association for its members; and holder, in the years before these minutes, of every chair the library had, from the acquisitions table to the reading-room desk where she sat at eight in the morning before the lamps were struck. The council voted, unanimously, that a bronze plate be fixed to the door of the reading room and that the plate read simply: ROSAMOND T. PARMA, 1884–1946, first law librarian of this library, first woman president of the American Association of Law Libraries.

The Dean read Mr. Dutton's written address, which ended: “She catalogued this building before we knew how to catalogue a person, and she did it better.” The clerk notes that the reading of the memorial was interrupted once, at 3:04, by the fog horn from the bay, and resumed, and that no one in the room called for order.

IV. Report on weather and on the branch campus

Mr. Baskerville reported that the fog of the morning, thick over all the bay and up the hills by eight, had kept the delivery of the Sacramento cart until midday and that the library's own weather book now shows the third class-five morning of November. On the athletics of the branch campus, the clerk, who keeps the council's margin notes as he keeps them, sets down the remark of the Dean, entered verbatim in the green ledger at the close of the item:

ISO Class 5: no more island than mine, gone 14–2 and took the election season.

The clerk adds, for the reader of these minutes who has not been at the games: the remark was made in the hour after the memorial, with the windows open, to a question from Professor Whitcomb about the Southern Branch of the university and its team; the 14–2 is the regular-season record of the Southern Branch Grizzlies of 1925–26, the season card of which this archive keeps its account on the Hardwood Emporium's centennial shelf, and the “island” is the way the campus sits in class-five fog, cut off from the town the way the island in the bay is cut off from it — on that same water, and in that same fog, keeping its own small weather, the almanac's entry no. 47 on Alcatraz Island will tell you — and the election season, which the branch took, is the autumn campaign of 1946 that Mr. Baskerville watched from the reference desk with the ballot papers of the municipal race stacked, for the day, beside the new acquisitions. The item was closed without debate.

V. Acquisitions and house matters

Mr. Baskerville reported 4,812 volumes received in the quarter, of which 311 came under the gift fund and 89 were duplicates to be sold at the winter sale on the lawn in February. Mrs. Rourke reported that the reference desk answered 1,976 questions in the quarter, the most of them, as the quarter before, from first-year students and from reporters; the least popular shelf remains the one with the international treaties, and the most, the reports of the California Supreme Court. It was resolved that the reading room be repainted in the colour it has had since 1927, the colour on the card in the paint store still labelled, in Miss Parma's hand, parma, dark, second coat, and that the estimate be brought to session 215.

VI. Adjournment

There being no further business, the Dean struck the lamp at the desk at 4:10, the session was declared closed, and the minutes will stand as written, subject to the correction of session 213 noted above. Entered in the green ledger by the clerk, and initialed by the Dean.

S. P. A. clerk  ·  P. L. S. dean, presiding
