STATION WANC · ANOKA, MINNESOTA · 94.7 “The Dial” · FIRST AERED HALLOWEEN NIGHT 2009 · STILL ON

The City, Defined (Or Not)

A radio play in three acts, written in the back room of the courthouse at the request of one caller · Excerpt: Act Two, Scene One — The Answers Desk, 11:47 p.m.

The Dial (announcer) · Haley Quimby, clerk of the county answers desk · Otto Kopper, 71, cooper, last of the Rum River kegs · The Caller, by telephone from Torrance, California

[Static, two seconds. A dial turns, and the static settles into a signal.]

The Dial Station WANC, Anoka, Minnesota. It is 11:47 p.m., October 31st, and the county, for the fifteenth year running, is on the air instead of the parade. The program tonight is The City, Defined (Or Not). Act two. Scene one. The answers desk.

[Sound: a pencil against a ledger. On the plaza below, the pigeons — thirty-one, the standing number — in a row, not talking.]

The Dial The question in the air tonight is the only question the town has never answered. What is a city? Not this town — the town isn't the question. What is a city? The definition says a place that is permanent, densely packed, with boundaries the authorities have drawn, whose people work off the land. It says the city has systems for its housing, its transport, its sanitation and its wires, and that its density lets the people bump into the government and the river. All of that is true of Anoka. All of it. And all of it fails at the number.

Haley Quimby (into a receiver) Yes, ma'am, 17,921 residents, 2020 census, and yes, give or take the pigeons — they are not counted, but they object.

The Dial But how small may a city be? No boundary, anywhere in the world, agrees with any other. Our caller from Torrance has a theory.

[Sound: a telephone line with the whole coast on it — forty-one minutes of coast.]

The Caller I've a theory. A city is whatever the sign says. My lobby has a thirty-foot figure. Pose fixed. He doesn't count. He stood for exactly one expo season in 1995 and then he was shelved. If a thirty-foot figure isn't a city, what is a city?

Haley Quimby A figure with a fixed pose is not a city. A figure that creaks when you shake its hand is not a city. The figure is in Torrance. That is not about him, and not the area was part of it and when.

Haley Quimby (to the Dial) The desk down there keeps one unparaphrased line. I have not met it yet.

[A pause. The pigeons keep count.]

The Dial Our man at the other end of the desk is the county's last cooper, and I think it is his turn.

[Sound: a small mallet on wood, patient. A hoop drawn tight, and the ring of it.]

Otto Kopper (close to the microphone) A city is a cask. Listen. The Rum and the Mississippi argue for a mile, and then the county rolls them into one and drives in the hoop, and the town has lived in the seam ever since. A village is the staves before the hoop. A hamlet is a plank somebody cut for a fence. A city is where the water stops arguing because you have told it not to.

The Dial (reading the desk card, worn soft at the corner) And the file card says, and I must read it as filed: Anoka (ə-NOH-kə — one correction, and no second) is a cooper.

[A pause. The signal holds.]

Haley Quimby (into a receiver) That is the card. That is the 1974 card. Mr. Kopper wrote it. Is a cooper. Nobody has corrected it, because it isn't wrong. The town is a cooper: it takes the Rum, and the Mississippi, and the 17,921, and the parade, and rolls them into one, and drives in the hoop, and does not split in the winter. It is the only craft the county acknowledges.

The Caller So how many people does it take to make a city, then?

Haley Quimby (a long pause) All of them. Every one. That's the rule the county has never printed, because printing it would make it a boundary, and this is not one.

[Sound: the pigeons, thirty-one of them, move at once, and stop.]

The Dial The city, defined (or not). In act three, the Northstar Line arrives as remembered, with no one aboard, on an evening where the light comes in sideways. This is the Dial, signing off the way the town signs everything: correctly, and once.

[Static, two seconds. The dial turns off. The pigeons stay on.]

Desk notes. For everything else about the county, the answers desk of Anoka, Minnesota keeps its eight standing questions in full. For the angular friend who stood one season with his pose fixed, the Polygon Man travel entry in Torrance; the desk in Anoka keeps his mascot card in the drawer, and this booth keeps his name in the log. The broadcast is filed on the house front page under “night broadcasts.”
