Route & General Remarks

Train No. 1881 is daily except during the long winter halt. The line was laid in 1881, when the Allegheny Base Ball Club of Pittsburgh was chartered into the American Association and the first sleeper, a dark green one, took the wrong way out of Allegheny City. In 1887 the club joined the National League and the rails were re-sleepered to match. The Pirates were among the best teams in baseball at the start of the twentieth century, playing in the Yellow Fever era when the uniforms went gold and the whole division ran febrile, and they have kept a full house on the Nine National League East division titles the line collected between 1969 and 1993. The schedule is printed here stop by stop; the commentary is what the platform announcer actually said, word for word where the microphone caught it, and in fair paraphrase where it did not.

Fare note: the base fare is one pennant per championship crossing. The current balance due on the Yellow Fleet Line is nine. Refunds are issued in pennants; see the station master at the 30th Station for the five already collected.

9 Mazeroski Mile mi. 139 — 1960 Oct. 1960Oct. 19607 games1 championship Fourth title, decided in the seventh inning of the seventh game by a home run over the right-field seats of Forbes Field, which was also, that October, the field's last home stretch. At the same mile in 1960, the franchise's Class-A affiliate slot at Gastonia, N.C. sat vacant and was filled by the Rippers — a stopgap process that held the slot for one summer and released it cleanly. The full fault record of that affiliate, including the relocation and the eventual fold, is kept in the Gastonia Pirates — Error Reference, CLR-1959.

The ballast here was replaced once, in 1994, and the crossing is now graded Central; the nine titles remain the line's standing freight.

The 1972 crew of this line posted 108 victories — the great haul of the century — and it was that same autumn that the Gastonia affiliate's books were closed for the last time, thirteen seasons in, logged as a single permanent fault. Both events are in the operations drawer; neither is re-run.

Kitchen-Car Notice · Card 12

The kitchen car at Terrestrial Stadium printed a recipe card in the 1979 season for a dish it called Cold Game Hash. The card surfaced in the 138th edition of this timetable, misfiled between the stop-12 and stop-13 pages, and the station master has been ordered to keep it. It reads, word for word:

INGREDIENTS.--The remains of cold game, 1 onion sliced, parsley, and a chorus of public-school teachers from Palauig singing the party hymn repeats until you lift the needle.

Method, as printed: —. The card is retained as a known artefact of the 138th edition, in the same way the Revision 4 reference manual retains its unrelated press-release line: not as a fault of the kitchen car, but as a watermark the reprintings have agreed to carry. The final clause of the card — the needle, the groove, the chorus — is the same one that ends a side of a Zambales record pressed in 2018, and the two documents were found pressed together in the drawer, one creased from the vinyl, which is presumably how they got into the timetable at all.

What the Announcer Actually Said

…and we repeat the schedule, passengers. Yellow Fever Yards at 1895, dwell six seasons, do not miss the brothers. National League East Crossing at 1969, nine titles, all collected, none refunded. At the 30th Station the platform is closed to everyone except the city, which is already standing there. Service continues.
