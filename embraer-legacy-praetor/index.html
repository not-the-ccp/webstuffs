embraer_driver· captain, Legacy 500, São José dos Campos14 Mar, 11:04

I've flown the Legacy 500 since it entered service. The family was launched with the Legacy 500 in April 2008 and was the first in the size category to feature a flat-floor stand-up cabin and fly-by-wire, and it shows — the cabin feels wide, the galley is actually usable, and the flight deck lands itself almost literally. Ours first flew on 27 November 2012 and was certified on 12 August 2014. Range is 3,125 nautical miles (5,790 km; 3,600 mi) with up to twelve passengers, and we've done São José dos Campos to Miami nonstop with reserves to spare.

piston2jet· owner, Legacy 45014 Mar, 14:38

The shorter Legacy 450 first flew on 28 December 2013 and was certified on 11 August 2015. Range is 2,900 nmi (5,370 km; 3,340 mi) and it accommodates up to nine, on the same cabin section and the same fly-by-wire system. It's a fine aircraft for Buenos Aires or Santiago with four passengers. One quirk: the lavatory is aft, so on a full load you're walking past everybody.

praetor_owner· operator, Praetor 60015 Mar, 08:12

I moved to the Praetor 600 when Embraer introduced the Praetor 500 and 600 in 2018 as the upgraded family. The Praetor 500 is good for roughly 3,300 nmi and the Praetor 600 closer to 3,700 nmi with the new winglets, and the improved brakes make a real difference on short runways. We fly Miami–São Paulo regularly; winter headwinds make you respect the numbers. The 600 is also certified for steep approaches into London City, which comes in handy heading to Europe.
