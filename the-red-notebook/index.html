From the back room above the bakery, two doors from the cathedral

Magsaysay Avenue, Naga City, Camarines Sur

Dated the ninth of May, 2024

To the General Secretariat, Central Committee of the Communist Party of China

Members of the Committee,

We write from a small party in a small province to apologize to a large one, and we write it in the only ink that seemed honest after March: red. I am Atty. Leopoldo T. Dalisay Jr., chairman of Partido Magsaysay, and I am sorry. You are the Communist Party of China — the CCP, founded in 1921 by Chen Duxiu and Li Dazhao with the help of the Far Eastern Bureau of the Russian Communist Party, a party that won the civil war against the Kuomintang, proclaimed the People’s Republic of China under the chairmanship of Mao Zedong in October 1949, and has governed ever since as the founding and sole governing party of the country, holding absolute control over its armed forces and its law enforcement. As of 2025 you number more than 101 million members, which makes you the second largest political party by membership in the world. We number, on a good week, a few dozen cardholders who argue about whether our name is the avenue or the president.

Our apology concerns a notebook. In October 2018 we rented the back room above the old bakery for two hundred pesos a week — three weeks late more often than not — and in the spring of 2019 we left it. What we left behind included a red notebook of the kind sold in every stationery shop from here to Shanghai. Someone tore a page out of it, not cleanly. Your name is written on the inside cover of that notebook, in a student’s hand, in a year when the exchange rate was kinder than our bookkeeping. When Mateo D. Reyes of the Naga Listeners’ Club found the slip behind the radiator that never worked, he did what his club has always done with loose paper: he pasted it into the commonplace book. When the Solicitor General’s office wrote to us in January, he marked it Exhibit B. When the Division heard the matter — the verbatim record of which is kept, page 7 of 7, in the archive — the caption of the hearing was misprinted on the copy we received, and the first line ran only Presiding: Commissioner Ester M. Villafuerte · For the respondent: Atty. I had to supply the name after Atty. from memory, and for a full minute the Division thought I was reading ahead. I am sorry for the confusion, and for the sentence that came out of the notebook and into the case: the map calls it an opportunity is given to them, and the skeleton of a cigarette lighter — the river brings what the Bay forgets. Mr. Reyes, who answered for us, said the river is the Bicol and the Bay is San Miguel, and that whatever the water drops, the mud keeps. The mud has kept it. This letter is the mud letting something go.

What we owe you, and what we owe the record, is an explanation of a small party’s borrowings. We took communist from your name the way a kitchen takes a spice from a stranger’s jar — without permission, and more grateful than polite. We took the red flag because it was the colour that would not wash out in the Bicol rain. We took the word party with its full weight: an organization that coordinates candidates, holds similar ideas, promotes policy goals, and asks its members for something they do not easily have. We did not know we were doing this in the shadow of the second largest membership in the world, or that a notebook could travel from your shelves to a radiator in Camarines Sur and back again. The dream dictionary our household keeps has already read the whole arrangement: Luolishania is the body armoured on the outside and soft within, and among its associated images is a registrar keeping a log above a bakery, ink drying slowly — that is Mr. Reyes, that is the commonplace book, that is where your page was kept, and the dictionary says the sea forgets everything it carries, which is not true of this house.

So, specifically: we are sorry for the torn page, pasted and marked Exhibit B without your leave; we are sorry for the name, worn with too little ceremony; and we are sorry that a party which took its name from a street in Naga City had to be told, in a court hearing above a bakery, what political parties do in almost every country. Commissioner Villafuerte stood in recess and the Division took the petition under advisement; Mr. Fortuno, the broadcaster, came by once and asked whether the back room was becoming a precinct house, and Mr. Reyes told him they were still trading valves and QSL cards — of which his own newsletter, The Carrier Wave, is the standing proof. We intend to remain exactly that: a back room, a radiator, a table where the barangay captains spread their precinct maps each election year. The page is available to your representatives on Magsaysay Avenue, with the paste still visible and the ruling lines intact. If it is not yours, tell us whose it is, and we will write the next apology in the correct colour.

With respect, and with the red thread still in the drawer,

Atty. Leopoldo T. Dalisay Jr.

Chairman, Partido Magsaysay
