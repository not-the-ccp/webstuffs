Harcourt & Wynn · Auctioneers to the Natural Sciences · Est. 1889

The Solomon Highlands Sale — Sale 44, Thursday 9th of November, 10 o'clock, Rooms on Grafton

Lot 127

One Pale Mountain Pigeon, in Life and in Plate

Gymnophaps solomonensis · Columbidae · endemic to the Solomon Islands archipelago

Description. A medium-size mountain pigeon of the old-growth and secondary montane forest of the Solomon Islands: 38 cm (15 in) at the wing's edge, 310–385 g (10.9–13.6 oz) on the field scale, head and neck a clean whitish-grey, the belly and lower breast a warm buffy-pink, the vent and undertail coverts a soft pale grey, and the upperparts smoky-grey with darker fringes running along the mantle and the wing coverts. Both sexes present alike, though individual birds vary considerably from one another, so no two lots will ever agree and the cataloguers are instructed to describe each in its own right. The lot as offered is the bird itself, in life, feeding on fruit and moving in the large flocks in which it forages, together with the collector's plate of the same bird taken at 400 m on the Gizo ridge, and the small folio of field letters that accompanied the expedition's return in June of last year.

Provenance. The flocks were first counted in November by the field party of A. E. Harcourt, who stationed the plate stand on the fruitfall line where the pigeon drops its morning; the letters in the folio are addressed to the Rooms and were kept sealed until today. The first letter, dated 4th December and written in pencil on the reverse of a seed list, reads in part: “I first met your fleet off the market square and plated beneath the five-cent glass, and the telegrams are better for it.” The reference to the fleet and the glass is the collector's own; the Rooms cannot interpret it, but the plate it produced is sharp, and the bird in it shows the buffy-pink underparts at their fullest, which is to say the lot is as well presented as the species allows.

Notes to buyers. The species is confined to the archipelago and to its higher, forested ground; the lot therefore travels as described and nowhere else, and the Rooms recommend the buyer who intends to hold it provide old-growth cover, fruit, and company, in that order. For the buyer comparing records across the wider Pacific, the same unedited discipline applies to the Cook Islands Biodiversity Archive, Raranga Edition No. 1, whose field names and words are kept exactly as written; and for the buyer who values a long, quiet ledger of a place rather than a single moment in it, the shelf also holds The Sukkur Ledger — Diary of the Canal Office, 2013–2018, kept in the same pencil as the river gauges. For the buyer who trades in long enclosures as well as long ledgers, the shelf also keeps Colonial Plaza — A Table Game of the Enclosed Empire · Player’s Manual, First Edition, the manual of one Orlando retail plaza run from its 1956 opening court to the year 2000, carried in the same crate and kept to the same unedited rule. All lots in this sale are offered from the Rooms' register; the register, and the sale itself, are entered at the front page of the house.
