The Wanters 1923

Error message documentation · Lab Ledger No. 9 · reeling of the second release print

A silent society drama · produced by Louis B. Mayer · directed by John M. Stahl · starring Marie Prevost, Robert Ellis, and Norma Shearer

INSPECTED — REEL 3 OF 6

0 · Scope of the ledger

This ledger documents every error message the laboratory raised against the second release print of The Wanters during the reeling of 14 and 15 October 1923. The picture is a society drama of six reels, one hour forty-three minutes of running time, photographed on 35 mm safety stock in three units: the house unit at 1630 Vine, the ballroom unit at the Hollywood Hotel, and the garden unit where Miss Prevost's garden scene of Reel 3 was made on the second morning of the season. Each fault is numbered, given a cause, and given a resolution. Nothing in a ledger is unexplained; a fault that cannot be explained is a fault that will not repeat, and this laboratory does not permit its errors to repeat.

The distribution is recorded under the name Associated First National Pictures, which is the name the company carried when this print left the lab. The company became First National Pictures in 1924, and the ledger's spine still bears both names, the second inked over the first in 1924 with the care the house gave to every other revision.

1 · ERR WNT-011 · EDGE BURN, REEL 2, 12 IN

> ERR WNT-011 — EDGE BURN, REEL 2, 12 IN — CAUSE: GATE FRICTION AT PROJECTOR LAMP — RESOLUTION: CLEANED, COOLANT OILED, NO LOSS OF PICTURE

A line of amber burn along the right edge of Reel 2, eleven feet of the ballroom scene. Cause: the print had been screened at a Westside house on 3 October on a projector whose gate was not trued, and the film had kissed the lamp housing on the way out. The damage is cosmetic; the picture of the ballroom itself is clean, and the dance floor reads perfectly. Resolution: the edge was thinned and the affected feet were given a darker matte line so the eye would stop hunting for the burn. A note in the margin, in the reeler's hand, records that Miss Prevost's entrance in the same scene was not touched at all, because, as the margin says, the director did not want the star's light touched by anyone who was not paid to touch it.

2 · ERR WNT-024 · SPICE WELD DRIFT, REEL 4

> ERR WNT-024 — SPICE WELD DRIFT, REEL 4, FRAME 4178 — CAUSE: CEMENT CURED COLD — RESOLUTION: RECUT BY HAND, ONE FRAME LOST FROM SCENE

The splice at frame 4178 of Reel 4, the join between the drawing-room scene and the garden unit material, had drifted a quarter of a frame. Cause: the splice had been cemented on a night the laboratory temperature fell to forty-one degrees, and cold cement cures stiff, and a stiff splice slides. The recut cost one frame of Miss Shearer's first garden scene — one forty-8th of a second, which is not visible at the projector and is recorded here anyway, because a ledger that omits a lost frame is a ledger that has made a decision it was not appointed to make. Mr. Stahl's assistant countersigned the recut, and the signature reads J.M.S. — approved, the frame may go.

3 · ERR WNT-037 · TITLE CARD SMUDGE, REEL 5

> ERR WNT-037 — TITLE CARD SMUDGE, REEL 5, CARD 9 — CAUSE: INK ON CAMERA LENS — RESOLUTION: CARD REPAINTED, ORIGINAL KEPT IN CABINET 12

Nine feet into Reel 5, the title card reading And what was wanted, was wanted twice carried a soft grey bloom across the second line. Cause: a drop of ink on the title camera lens during the card session of 29 September; the bloom sat in the same place across every strike, which is how the laboratory knew it was a lens and not a card. The card was repainted and struck again, and the smudged original is kept in cabinet 12 with the other unrecoverable cards, where it has been since 1923. Mr. Ellis's exit in the scene immediately following the card was examined and found clean; the ledger notes it in one line, the exit takes no damage from the card, which is the laboratory's way of saying the actor was worth checking twice.

4 · ERR WNT-052 · LEDGER NOTE, UNSTAMPED

Between the entries for WNT-049 and WNT-053 there is a line of pencil that does not belong to any of the three. It was written by whoever held the ledger open that night, it was never stamped, and it has been there in the ledger's own hand since. The laboratory does not alter its record, so the line stands as written, exactly, and the reeler's successor in 1924 simply ruled around it and continued:

Since the last to stamp the page, and I must set down in 2015, and it took place.

It does not read like a fault, a cause, or a resolution, and no one in the laboratory could make it read like one. The ruling around it, done in 1924, is the only entry the ledger ever made about it, and it is the most honest entry in the ledger: the line is set down, it took place, and the page was ruled and closed. What the line was wanting, the ledger does not say, and a ledger that had meant to say would have said it in the column for causes.

5 · ERR WNT-066 · REEL 6, FINAL SPOKE, CLEAR

> ERR WNT-066 — REEL 6, FINAL SPOKE — CAUSE: NONE — RESOLUTION: NONE REQUIRED. PRINT CLEARED FOR RELEASE, 16 OCT 1923

Sixty-six errors were raised on the reeling. Fifty-two were caused by the projector houses, nine by the splicing room, four by the title camera, and one by the cold. The final spoke of the ledger is left blank on purpose: a cleared print needs no resolution, and the blank is the document's one entry with a cause of none. The print left the laboratory on 16 October 1923, boxed, labelled, and signed by both the reeler and the assistant, for a run that would carry it through the year the company itself changed its name, so that a print inspected under Associated First National could be screened, from 1924 onward, under First National — the second name inked over the first, which is, the ledger's last annotation says, a change the film did not have to make, but the name did.

6 · Where the same film has been read

The ledger's caboose keeps two cross-references, in the habit the laboratory had of sending its readers onward. For the city the house unit filmed in and the Westside houses that raised the first of these error codes, see Los Angeles — The Pacific Coast Almanac, Guidebook No. 47, which carries the Silver Lake rehearsal rooms and the undeciphered chef's note, a companion case in the same genre of pages that will not explain themselves. And for the one silent-film echo the ledger's readers have asked about most, the city that kept screening these reels into their last surviving runs, see Yerevan — Guidebook No. 88: The Pink City, which notes the echo beside Republic Square. Beside the two cross-references the caboose carries one line of annotation that is not a cross-reference; it records where the ledger’s own governing principle — that an entry stands in the record as found, unamended — has been applied elsewhere, to a stainless-steel peace monument at a Santa Monica crossing, in Chain Reaction (1991), Placard No. 14, which quotes the installation manual’s photographers’ clause and the forty-one cars of its first-year site record. Both are set down here as cross-references, not causes; a ledger that confused its cross-references with its causes would be a ledger with an error code of its own. The ruling travels, the laboratory notes, in the 1889 shipping ledger kept in Cabinet 9 with the Iran–Contra safety data sheet, Revision 1992, where the same gesture — a line in brown ink, copied word for word, ruled around and closed — is applied to the ledger's own one unexplained line, which reads, exactly as found: LIVER AND PARSLEY SAUCE FOR SALMON, TROUT, &c. A third reader of the ruling, in the dining column of the Eiter almanac, has set it against a kitchen above the valley that loses one forty-eighth of a second from the plating on cold nights and keeps the frame written down anyway; the house is reviewed at Teardrops, 14 Fiddle Row, where the ruling stands, as found, and the page is ruled and closed.
