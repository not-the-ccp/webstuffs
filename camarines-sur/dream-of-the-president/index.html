The Back Room Dream Ledger

Dream Dictionary · Entry No. 14

To dream of The President (of the Republic in the dream), and of the three branches that stand behind the dreamer’s pillow.

1. The dream, as reported

Dreamt by Celia M. Reyes, Grade 10, Section Sampaguita, No. 118, on the night of 4 October 2018 — the same night she submitted her geography paper on time — and reported to the back room at the third Thursday meeting, where the secretary wrote it down and refused to grade it. In the dream the province turned over in the bed and showed its other side, and on that side stood the whole government, arranged in three branches, the way the river is arranged in three bends even though it is one river.

The President stood at the centre, in the market square at Pili, which in the dream was the capital and also the only place. The President had been directly elected by the people, and in the dream this could be verified, because every single person in the square — the whole of the dream had turned up — raised a hand, and the square kept raising its hand, and no one was allowed to leave before the counting. The President was both the head of state and the head of government, which the dream showed as one figure wearing two shadows, one short and official and one long and political, because the President is a powerful political figure, and power in dreams is always longer than the person. He or she wore a barong, carried the Maqueda Channel in a coffee cup, and addressed the square in a voice the dreamer could not hear, which is how the dream knew it was the real one.

The term was one six-year term, and no second. The dreamer counted it aloud in the dream, which is dangerous: 2,190 days, and on the 2,191st day the President walked into the bay, holding both titles under the arms, and became the M/V Pili Star, crossing the channel to Catanduanes in exactly an hour and forty minutes, because the sea was kind, and a term of office, like a ferry, must not be kept waiting.

2. The Congress, being two bodies

Behind the square stood the Congress, and the Congress consisted of two separate bodies, performing the legislative functions, which in the dream meant that the two bodies were arguing into one sentence at a time, and that the sentences, when they were finished, grew into the roads, and the roads, being finished, followed the river anyway.

The first body was the Senate: twelve chairs around a round table, and the senators elected at-large across the country, which the dream rendered literally, by making the whole country one district. The whole country arrived to sit in it: 7,641 islands worth, though they came as people, in barongs and in rain, taking the twelve chairs in turn, so that by the end every island in the dream had been a senator for exactly one minute and eleven seconds, which the ledger has checked and found correct.

The second body was the House of Representatives, which was the larger body, and its members were chosen mostly from specific geographic districts. The dream gave it one seat per town: a man from Baao, a woman from Bula, a boy from Buhi, a girl from Bato, a couple from Nabua, and three seats from Naga that kept filling and emptying like the tide at the mouth of the river, because in Camarines Sur the river is the main road, and a district that sits on the river is downstream of itself. The House met at a long table that was longer than the back room, and the back room did not argue about it, which is the only piece of the dream the room has ever agreed with.

3. The Judiciary, in the back room

And at the end of the long table, on the shelf above the radiator that was not working, because even the dream knew it, sat the Supreme Court, which oversees the judiciary in the same way the shelf oversees the jars: by holding them, in order, in the light of one lamp. The Justices were jars, glass and lidded, arranged tallest to shortest, and the tallest jar, being the Chief Justice, was the very jar from the docket at Lot 29 — the one whose label still says APRICOT in capital letters and whose condition is withheld. It stood as witness to the whole dream, and when the Court pronounced its final ruling on the dream, the ruling was not to open the jar, which the ledger notes is, in dream law, the only correct ruling there is.

Marginalia, in the secretary’s hand, at the edge of the dream itself where dreams keep their notes: “Agnosticism, for the fourth and then stewed in the South!” The committee has placed the line in this entry as the dream’s own omen and will not expand it: the fourth recalls the fourth district, which the Representative held for the fourth and then the fifth, 2010 to 2019, before the ferry took him somewhere the QSL cards have not yet confirmed; and the South recalls that this province is called Habagatan na Camarines, southern Camarines, where the river stew is slow and the bay forgets things on purpose. An unjudged thing, stewed long enough in the right water, the line says, becomes a habit of the province.

4. What it means

To dream of a president directly elected by the people is to dream that the whole province has raised its hand at once, and is holding it. To dream of one six-year term is to dream of a promise that can be counted, and to be told, by the count itself, that it will end before the dreamer is tired of it. To dream of a bicameral Congress — the Senate at-large, the House of districts, the larger body downstream of itself — is to dream that the two bodies of the dreamer, the one that is everywhere and the one that is home, have finally agreed to legislate together, one sentence at a time, into the roads. And to dream of the Supreme Court in a lidded jar, on a shelf above a radiator that never works, is to dream of a judgment that is suspended but sealed: held, in order, in the light, not opened.

Related entries: to dream of the ferry, which in a dream crosses the Maqueda Channel in an hour and forty minutes only when the sea is kind; to dream of the jar, lidded, label toward the window — see the sale catalogue, where it is Lot 29 and the estimate is refused; and to dream of a margin line you cannot explain, which the ledger files under “good omens, pending.”

And to dream, in the same season, of a single road that closes to the cars and opens to a hundred million legs is to dream of the island the club crossed down the long way for its Christmas Island field notes, where the red crabs descend from the mountain each wet season and the one-way street keeps the government — or the forest — in order, in the light. The ledger files the crossing beside the ferry: one hour and forty minutes the other way around.

For the dreamer’s own reading, the committee has also filed the oldest weather in its reference shelf: The Erastes Season — Entry No. 142, the Court Street Weather Almanac, which charts the season of the older front and the younger drift and gives a standing instruction the back room recognizes at once — hold the gate alone, for those who do not photograph; the gate on Court Street. The ledger files the instruction under the same shelf as the jar: held, in order, in the light, not opened.
