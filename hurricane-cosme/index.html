Hurricane Cosme

June 19 – June 23, 1989

Third tropical storm · second hurricane of the 1989 Pacific hurricane season

Born: June 19, 1989, from a tropical wave off the Pacific coast of Central America

Died: June 23, 1989, over the mountains of south-western Mexico

Cause of death: rapid weakening over land

Peak strength: Category 1 hurricane, winds of seventy-five miles per hour

Landfall: near Acapulco, Guerrero, during the night of June 21

Survived by: its remnant low, the season's storms still to come, and the rain gauges of Acapulco

It is with a heavy barometer that the Weather Desk announces the death of Hurricane Cosme, the third tropical storm and second hurricane of the 1989 Pacific hurricane season. Cosme formed on June 19 from a tropical wave, moved westward across the eastern Pacific, and was upgraded to Tropical Storm Cosme before turning north to meet the coast.

It intensified into a Category 1 hurricane and made landfall near Acapulco during the night of June 21, driving wind and rain into the city and the beaches of Guerrero. Over the Sierra Madre del Sur the storm rapidly weakened over land, its eye collapsing against the heights, but its moisture remained behind.

The rain fell without pause. Rivers climbed their banks, streets became arroyos, and the hillsides gave way. The flooding was deadly and destructive, and it is estimated that thirty people died in the hurricane.

A line from the bridge log of a freighter riding out the storm off Acapulco has been kept for the notice: The whole to a sail that would hold, with interruptions, until the day book. It was the last entry the keeper made before the barometer fell, and it serves as epitaph.

The rain of June 1989 is still this desk's wettest line, and the Atlantic ledger now runs to Lot 27 — The Amazon River, offered as one river system, with the mouth belonging to the water.
