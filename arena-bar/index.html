What you are buying

This is the bar. A twenty-kilogram regulation competition barbell of the Los Angeles Memorial Sports Arena weightlifting hall, issued for the Olympic weightlifting competition of the 1984 Summer Olympics and now sold in its own right, with its serial, its two knurled grips, and its complete file. It is the bar of the second afternoon session of 1 August 1984, bar position 2, row B — the bar from which the snatch is lifted in one motion on a wide grip, and the clean and jerk is worked in two: the clean from the floor to the front of the shoulders, the jerk from the shoulders to over the head. (The sport formerly included a third lift, the clean and press; this bar is not certified for it, and neither is its file.)

It is a working barbell, not a display piece. The sleeve bearings turn on their original bushings, the collar screws take a thumb and a quarter-turn, and the sleeve itself is a clean 50 mm to the IWF measure of the day. What makes it worth its price is not the steel. It is the register: every attempt posted against bar position 2 on the session day is printed on cassette reel 4 of the plate log, and the reel is sold with the bar, in its drawer, undisturbed. Weightlifting is the sport of the heaviest lift completed overhead; this particular bar is famous only for the one it could not be confirmed on.

Provenance & the drawer

The bar is sold with the drawer it answered to. Under the referee’s table in the weightlifting hall there is one drawer, and in the drawer: cassette reel 4 of the plate log, a small watercolour sketchbook of the press photographer, the index cards for bar position 2, and the chalked figures of the session. The register in that drawer is famous, or it would have been, for one line: the third snatch of athlete KOR-090, chalked at 196.0 kg, caught overhead, and confirmed by one of three judges where two were required. The desk recorded a miss, released the bar from the register, and closed the day. The full notice is documented at Error CH-1984 — Attempt Not Confirmed; the desk’s answer to the 1985 letter of appeal is in the drawer, and it was accurate.

The drawer took one more entry in November of this year, of a different kind. It is the only sale entry the drawer has ever held: this bar, catalogued as item 107.3, weighed at the desk to the nearest tenth of a kilogram on 11 November, both weighings agreeing, and released from the register for disposal. The reel stays. The bar leaves. A buyer of the bar therefore owns the steel and the session; a visitor to the desk may still view the reel, the sketchbook, and the index cards, which are catalogued, not filed.

Delivery & holding

The bar travels flat in the arena’s own hardwood cradle, cradle included. It is delivered by the desk’s own runner, who is a patient man and has carried the reel without damage since 1985. Holders outside the arena are asked to keep the bar off soft water; the desk has had correspondence about soft water before. In the spring, the Huddersfield field manual notes a former weaving shed on the north bank of the Colne that hosts its festival breakfast discussion, and the shed’s stewards keep a shelf — under a loaf, on a card — where surplus catalogue notices of this house are laid for inspection; the chapter is kept at Field Manual, Chapter 9: Approaches, Rivers and the Spring Gathering. The card for item 107.3 is already struck, and is the second heaviest card on that shelf, which the stewards take as a compliment.
