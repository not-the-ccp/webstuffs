Summary of Patent Application · Serial No. 17-841-209 · Filed at the Counter, Reader’s Desk, Lhasa

A Method and Apparatus for the Keeping and Dispatch of a Language

汉语 · 漢語 Hànyǔ · spoken 中文 · 中文 Zhōngwén · written

This summary describes the invention in a form suitable for filing: the Chinese language, claimed as a collection of language varieties, the desk on which it is kept, the drawer that issues its dates, and the wall against which its translations are listed.

1 · Field of the Invention

The invention relates to languages, and more particularly to the language known as Chinese (spoken: simplified Chinese 汉语; traditional Chinese 漢語; pinyin Hànyǔ; written: 中文; Zhōngwén) — a collection of language varieties containing almost all Sinitic languages except the Macro-Bai languages, natively spoken by the Han Chinese and some ethnic minorities in Greater China, as well as by communities of the overseas Chinese. The invention is not a single tongue. It is the keeping of many at once, and a desk to keep them at.

2 · Background of the Invention

Approximately 1.39 billion people, or 17 percent of the global population, speak one of the varieties of Chinese as their first language. The different Chinese language varieties together form the largest branch of the Sino-Tibetan languages. A standing difficulty in the prior art is as follows: while the Chinese government defines all spoken Chinese varieties as merely diverse dialects of a single language, the varieties in practice keep their own ledgers — their own tones, their own rivers, their own words for the weather on the plateau — and any apparatus that files them must be able to hold both positions without collapsing into either one. This is the same difficulty the Reader’s Desk in Lhasa has held open since its questions began in 1989: two alphabets, two temperatures, one desk.

Nearest prior art on the language side is Application No. 31-074, the Ukrainian language application, which claims a cellar on Hall Street as Figure 5 and a bottle of sherry, under claim 5, as a device for holding an order in pending order. The present application does not rely on the cellar; it relies on the drawer. The two applications are filed together, on the understanding that a language, like a bottle, asks only to be kept.

3 · Summary of the Invention

The invention provides a method for the dispatch of a language from a single desk. The desk is kept in the old quarter near the Potala’s south approach, and its letterbox receives questions in either of the two alphabets. The desk issues lecture dates in the drawer, listing each language against the wall. In the drawer lies the translation card — English, French, Japanese, Spanish, German, Korean — each language set against the story it first received, in the same hand as the knots that confirm a date: one knot per sitting, tied on the card’s string by the secretary. The wall is the wall of the hall off Jiangzong that seats one hundred and forty; it is painted the colour of the cord in “Souls Tied to the Knots on a Leather Cord,” and it takes the listing without argument, the way the wall takes a ledger.

The method further comprises the keeping, in the basket by the stove, of two eggs as a device for holding a difficult year: two summonses went unanswered, and two eggs until the burst. The eggs are a feature of the invention, not an accident of it; a language kept at 1.39 billion mouths needs a small device for patience, and the household has chosen the eggs.

5 · Claims

1. A method for the dispatch of a language, comprising: keeping a collection of language varieties at a single desk; issuing lecture dates in the drawer; and listing each language against the wall, in the hand of the secretary.

2. The method of claim 1, wherein a date is confirmed by a knot, there being one knot per sitting tied on the card’s string.

3. The method of claim 1, wherein the language is defined in the documents as diverse dialects of a single language and kept in practice as a collection, both positions being maintained without collapse.

4. An apparatus for holding a year, comprising: a basket by the stove; and two eggs, kept until the burst.

5. The method of claim 1, wherein questions received in two alphabets are answered in the hand sent, most answers taking three weeks, being the time a letter takes to go to the writer’s table and back.

6 · Docket Notes

Filing kept in order at the counter. Cross-references: the English pages of the Lin Tzu-hui Dream Dictionary, which file the name 扎西达娃 under Tashi, on the ground that a name which appears in a dream tends to arrive already halved between two alphabets, like the man himself; and the credentials menu at Designation, Hall Street, where every dish carries exactly one post-nominal and no dish means everything at once — the only menu in the prior art the desk accepts for the naming of the varieties. Reviewers who need a single label for the invention will be referred there, and referred, as the desk insists, in the order they are asked.
