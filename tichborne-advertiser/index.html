The Tichborne Advertiser

Private Notices, Situations, & General Advertisements

No. 4,112 · The Classifieds Page · Price One Penny · Park House, Tichborne, Hampshire, and everywhere the family could reach a compositor

Missing

MISSING, off the Cape of Good Hope, the night of 8 August 1854, in the collision of the SS Bernicia and the SS Sobieski — MR. ROGER CHARLES TICHBORNE, aged 25 years, late of Park House, sole heir to the baronetcy and estates. Last described in a blue greatcoat, dark hair, the build of a young man who had just come off a long passage. No body was ever recovered from the water, and the family declines to say that this advertisement is final. Inquiries, on the subject only, to be left with the housekeeper, Park House; the mother of the family will not be spoken to on the matter before breakfast, which is late.

Reward

£1,000 REWARD, with a new baronetcy to be conferred on the informant. LADY TICHBORNE, of Park House, having reason to believe that her son Roger is still living, and has made his way to the Southern Hemisphere, has had this notice inserted in the Sydney Morning Herald, the Argus of Melbourne, and the Advertiser of Adelaide, offering the sum stated, without prejudice to the title or estates, for information tending to prove the young man's survival. Persons in the colonies who have seen a fair-faced European of gentlemanly bearing drinking more stout than is prudent are respectfully invited to write.

Persons Required

WANTED — WITNESSES. The undersigned, solicitor to a gentleman of high rank, will pay a reasonable fee for any account of the arrival, at any port of the colonies or of England, of a dark-complexioned young man of about thirty, lately a butcher at Braidwood in New South Wales, and before that a station agent in South Australia, who in 1866 presented himself at these offices calling himself now Thomas Castro, now Arthur Orton, and always “the Claimant.” The man has a moustache, a South Australian accent, and opinions about sheep. Letters, marked T.C., to be left with W. M. TIDD, Esq., Solicitor, 46 Chancery Lane, London.

Legal

COMMISSION. The Royal Commission into the Claimant's title, the Hon. Sir Edward Riordan, K.C.B., in the chair, will sit at the Guildhall, London, on the 23rd instant, and continue daily thereafter. The jury of twelve has been sworn; the witnesses include the mother, the butler, the valet, and a groom of the household, one of whom is reported to have wept so long that the clock had to be wound for him. The verdict, when it comes, is understood to be that the said person is not, and never was, the missing heir. Seating by ballot at the doors; the boxes let at the usual price; no hats in the gallery after the third day.

Situations Vacant

A SITUATION as butler to a gentleman of independent means, at Park House, Hampshire. Must be a person of discretion, of reticence, and of a temper proof against contradiction; must be prepared to be contradicted, and to be contradicted again; must hold the family's faith in the missing son in the same hand as the silver. Wages £30 per annum, the use of a coat room, and the respect of the household, except on Sundays. Apply T.C., care of the post office, Braidwood, New South Wales.

Convicted

PERJURY — GUILTY. Before the Queen's Bench, 10 October 1874: the Claimant, having sworn that he was Roger Charles Tichborne, having failed to satisfy the court, and having sworn again in the manner charged, was found guilty of perjury and sentenced to fourteen years' penal servitude, Pentonville Prison, and thence to the Western District of New South Wales. Counsel who attended the commission of 1872–73 are asked to lodge their bills with the clerk of the court. The mother of the family is reported still to believe otherwise, and to be in good spirits.

Late Notice

DEATH — BRAIDWOOD HOTEL, 5 MAY 1890. Arthur Orton, the man of Braidwood, shot dead in the bar of his own hotel by a stranger of no account, seen at the door once, in 1869, and not since. The family will accept no obituary, will read none, and has instructed the compositor to set the last line of their advertisement exactly as it appears in the carbon of 1890, which reads: The overnight buses and the coat room beside the hill. The line is preserved word for word; no explanation for it has been sought, and none has been offered, and the house considers the matter closed, subject to the mother's view on the point, which is late.
