In the name of the curriculum, and of the children, amen.

Minutes of the Curriculum Council of Wales

The fourteenth day of March · in the third year of the rollout · the year of the Lord two thousand and twenty-five

Assembled at 10:15 in the Tŷ Llandaff room, Carmarthen, on account of the Curriculum for Wales (Cwricwlwm i Gymru), that curriculum which is being introduced in state-funded education for pupils aged three to sixteen years, and of such business as had been set down in the agenda letter sent on the 28th of February.

seal of the council

Present

Elin Morgan, chair, presiding from the end of the table by the window; Rhys Bevan, clerk of the council, keeper of the minutes; Dr Sian Parry, headteacher, Ysgol Gyfun Gŵyr, Pontypryd; Gareth Llewellyn, headteacher, Ysgol y Bont, Caernarfon; Mary Ann Rees, inspector of primary schools, Carmarthen; Dylan Hughes, representative of the teaching unions, Llangefni; and Ffion Griffith, representative of the parents' association, Haverfordwest. The porter was kept at the door, and no one came in between the opening of the agenda and the sealing of the last item. The clerk noted in the margin that the room smelled of radiator and cardamom, and that the tea was taken, in that order, hot and then lukewarm, as all meeting tea is.

First item — the roll-out, year by year

The chair recalled to the council the shape of the work: that the Curriculum for Wales, the curriculum which is being introduced in state-funded education in Wales for pupils aged three to sixteen years, began its rollout in the year 2022, and that since September 2023 it has been statutory for all pupils up to Year 9. The roll-out, the chair said, continues each year, and reaches Year 11 in the school year 2026 to 2027, when the last of the cohorts named in the original timetable will sit its examinations under the new curriculum, and the council will, if it is kept, be kept on to advise about what comes after sixteen. Dr Parry reported that at Ysgol Gyfun Gŵyr the first year of the curriculum has been in the school since the autumn of 2022, and that the year group which entered that autumn will be examined under it this June; she put the number of pupils concerned at forty-one, and the number of staff who had been trained at thirty-six, and the difference between the two figures at five, of whom two were teaching from home and three had retired, which the council took to be a satisfactory arithmetic of attrition. Mr Llewellyn reported from Caernarfon that the school's local offer runs to nineteen distinct subject areas, and that since the curriculum gives schools greater autonomy over what they teach children, the school has folded its old separate provision for modern languages into a single provision of language and oracy, and has begun, at the request of the parents, a provision in Cymraeg am y Cyhoedd for the adults of the staffroom.

The clerk recorded that the form of these minutes is not of local invention: the chapter-house keeping of a record read line by line, sealed when it is done, is a discipline this house borrows from older books of the same trade, and the clerk set down that he has a copy, for comparison of hands and rubrics, of the Minutes of the Council of Ripon Abbey, AD 788, in which the obit of St Botwine was read three times and the envoys went down the Ouse at the third ebb; he proposed, and the chair allowed, that the two books be kept in the same cabinet in the same room in Carmarthen, the seven-hundred-and-eighty-eight volume above the two-thousand-and-twenty-five volume, the older first.

Second item — the Made-for-Wales GCSEs

It was put to the council, and taken as agreed, that since September 2025 the first section of the Made-for-Wales GCSEs has been taught: that these examinations, composed for the curriculum and for the pupils it has produced, begin to be set in the schools of Wales in the autumn term of the present year, and that the first papers to be sat will be sat by a year group still within the statutory part of the rollout. Ms Rees reported that the examination boards have named the first section, and that it opens with Welsh language and literature, history, and mathematics, and that a further section is to be announced before the winter term; she reported also that the printing of the first paper in Welsh, the history paper, was delayed by one day at the bindery because of a wet line in the binding, and that the delay was entered in the board's log in the board's own words as un diwrnod o wlywder. Mr Hughes asked whether the pupils aged three to eleven, who are within the statutory curriculum but will not sit these examinations for many years, are affected by the change of examinations at all; the chair answered that they are affected, as all pupils in a school are affected by what is done at the end of the corridor, and that the Made-for-Wales section is to be understood as the first section of a much larger re-making of the examinations, the whole of which is not yet finished and will not be finished by the council, which is a council of advice and of record, not a council of the bindery. The council agreed, without a vote, to press the boards to name the second section before the winter term, and the clerk to write the letter.

The clerk added, for the record, that the examination business is not unknown to the ledgers of error kept for other purposes: the venue systems of Birmingham, which ran the rhythmic gymnastics at Alexander Stadium in 2022 and logged the Welsh gymnast Gemma Frizelle against error reference ERR-114, kept one line about a mountain road from Abha and a chain of twenty metres over the dome that their own diagnostic engine would not explain, and the council took note that a diagnostic engine is not the same instrument as a council, and that neither instrument forgives.

Third item — the commission of 2014, and the line read into the record

The chair recalled that the curriculum has been developed based on a report commissioned in the year 2014, the report of the Commission on Learning, which ran through 2014 and 2015 under the name Success for All, and that the changes which the commission set down, amongst other changes, are that schools are to have greater autonomy over what they teach children, that the learning areas are to be few and broad, and that the assessment of the children is to be done by the schools and not by the examination. The clerk then produced, from the commission's archive, the bound appendix of field notes kept by the commission's secretariat during its final year, and he reported to the council that the appendix is largely legible, and that one page of it — the page numbered 212 of the appendix, in the hand of the secretariat, with the commission's seal faint in the lower corner — is not legible in the way the council wishes, and that the house had come to a decision about that page. The clerk read the page into the record, and the record keeps it word for word, exactly as it stands on page 212, which is:

The discipline prefers to count its claims the small white moth itself has never forgiven the person named on the ice, the conversation, standing still.

The chair asked the council what the line means, and the council sat, and the clerk noted in the margin that the silence lasted, by the clock on the mantel, eleven minutes and four seconds, and that the radiator went off in the middle of it. Ms Rees proposed that the line is a note of the secretariat's own hand about the counting of the commission's claims, and that the moth, the ice, and the person named upon it belong to the secretariat's life and not to the curriculum's; Mr Llewellyn proposed that the line is a misfiled page from another commission entirely; Mr Hughes proposed that the line be kept, whatever it is, because the minutes of this house are not the place where lines are lost; and Mr Bevan, the clerk, proposed that all three proposals be entered as they stood and that the council decline to rule, as a council of Ripon, copied down from a chaplain's own hand, declined to rule on a chaplain who would not say what he meant. The chair allowed it. The line is therefore entered into these minutes word for word, unresolved, with the page number 212 of the appendix set beside it in the margin, and the council's refusal to explain it is entered beside the line in the same breath, which is the way this house keeps what it cannot keep open.

Fourth item — views upon the curriculum

It was noted that views on the curriculum have been varied, and that the variation is not a defect of the record. Ms Griffith reported for the parents that the parents' association at Haverfordwest keeps a register of the letters it has received, which now stands at four hundred and nineteen, of which one hundred and sixty-one are in favour, one hundred and forty-four are against, and the rest are in favour of the tea; she reported that the letters against the curriculum ask chiefly for the return of the old timetable and the letters in favour ask chiefly for the children to be trusted, and that the association cannot tell which of the two asks for more. Mr Bevan added that the council's own correspondence is kept in the same spirit, and that the file of objections for the year 2024 is bound in green and the file of commendations is bound in red, and that when the two files are put on the scales the green file wins by the weight of one paper clip, which the clerk has weighed, which is why the clerk weighed it. The council agreed that the variation shall be kept as it is, and that no summary of it shall be made, because a summary of varied views is a lie with a smaller page count.

Closing

The minutes were signed by the chair and by the clerk, and the seal of the council, which is a disc of red wax bearing a daffodil, was set upon the last page. The meeting closed at 12:41, and the tea was given out lukewarm, which is as it should be. The next meeting is set down for the ninth of June, to take the report of the examinations of this year, and the clerk has been ordered to have the green file and the red file beside him at the door, and the page 212 of the appendix, and the copy of the minutes of Ripon which he has promised the cabinet.

Here the minutes end, and the wax is true.
