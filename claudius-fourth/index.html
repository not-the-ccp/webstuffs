at the Gauge

A thread on Claudius — Tiberius Claudius Caesar Augustus Germanicus ( KLAW-dee‑əs ), fourth ruler of the Julio‑Claudian dynasty, born at Lugdunum in Roman Gaul, 1 August 10 BC; ruled from AD 41 until his death on 13 October AD 54.

MedinetScribe Keeper of the Nile Gauge · Post No. 1 of 6 · 13 October, being the day the ledger closed

I have taken to the scribe's window a ruler who was not of our family of ledgers at all, but who came to keep one anyway. His name, in the full form the board will use, is Tiberius Claudius Caesar Augustus Germanicus — pronounced, by the board's phonetic rule, KLAW‑dee‑əs. He was a Roman emperor, ruling from AD 41 until his death in AD 54, the fourth ruler of the Julio‑Claudian dynasty. Born 1 August 10 BC at Lugdunum in Roman Gaul, where his father, Drusus the Elder — brother of Emperor Tiberius — was stationed as a military legate; his mother was Antonia Minor. He was the first Roman emperor to be born outside Italy, which is to say: the first reading taken by this family from a station the family's ledger had not kept before.

And before any of you rule the next line: yes, the limp. Yes, the stammer. Yes, the tremor, from an illness he suffered when young. The family, by its own ledger, had ruled him out of the weather of the house — which is the almanac's word for left off the line. As it stands in the source I am working from, he was ostracized by his family and excluded from — the sentence stops there, and the no‑eraser clause says it may stay stopped. The gauge of his thirteen years is set below, working. What I want from this board is the lagoon side's reading of him. The card is already moving.

KairaraShelf Shopkeeper, Kairara Field Press store, Avarua, Rarotonga · Post No. 2 of 6 · two days later, low tide

The lagoon side keeps its own copy, and it is a card, not a sentence. A visitor from Funchal — one of the water‑keepers who still counts the casks of the San Juan de Portugal from 1588, if anyone up on the board keeps that count — left a recipe card in the gate log by the harbour wall last spring. Market card, the good kind, the kind that keeps its one pencilled sentence every season. On the back of it, in pencil, was a line. The shop's copy of it, word for word, is this:

In choosing fresh butter, and pour it over the hills, not the town The first wet season in this chapter, between the lardoons of the system.

I will not erase it, obviously — the no‑eraser clause applies to the card exactly as it applies to the Archive. But the lagoon's clause is that query at low tide, when the data and the water agree about what is there. So: board, read it. It looks like a recipe and a weather note pinned together by a doorstop, and the scribe's own rule in the Theban Almanac, Entry No. XX is that a reading, once ruled, keeps its line — even a line this crooked.

AntoniaMinor Reader, registered under the empress's name · Post No. 3 of 6 · same day

Read it the way both keepers read everything: by the margin. Four clauses, four owners.

“Over the hills, not the town” is the scribe's Year Six at Medinet Habu, word for word in spirit: the almanac's wind column says northeast with rain over the hills, not the town, and the note says the rain fell on the hills and the hills were thanked. “The first wet season in this chapter” is that same Year Six — the almanac's own phrase is the first wet season in this entry — which is also the first wet season in this chapter, this board, this thread. And “lardoons” is lagoons: the sound the word makes for it getting on from Aitutaki to Penrhyn, three days' sea and a boat that runs with the moon. The system between them is the lagoon line itself. And “fresh butter” is the recipe card doing its proper work — Funchal keeps its market card, its pencilled sentence returns every season, and the visitor had simply written the weather in the margin of the butter.

The card is the lagoon's way of signing a wet year. The scribe said the same of the pond at the far end of the desert: one piece of water anywhere in the weather counts for the weather of the whole entry. Two ledgers, one signature.

MedinetScribe Keeper of the Nile Gauge · Post No. 4 of 6 · nine days of still air later

That is the whole of the thread, then, and it is the whole of the emperor too. The exclusion is not the cancellation. A reading left off the line does not stop existing; it waits at the station. Claudius was the family's Year Six: the one the ledger had ruled dry, who turned out to be the one who kept it. When Tiberius's line ended in AD 41 and the fourth of the family sat down at the desk, he did not rewrite the previous entries. He ruled them on. The almanac's rule stands for him exactly as it stands for my scribe: the weather is not edited in public; a reading, once ruled, keeps its line. Thirteen years, AD 41 to AD 54, closed on 13 October with the board still in his hand — which is why I opened this thread on that date, and which is why the stamp above says no eraser.

The gauge of his years is set below, working from the death of the first of the family in 44 BC to the new hand in AD 68. Drag it. The board's arithmetic is in the margins, as always. And for the record, one reader reports that a dining room in Portland has taken the family up as a five-course menu — Augustus to Nero, the last of them ending in fire — with the fourth served as a lobster tail and a mace nut “for the one they excluded, who kept the ledger anyway”; the table appears, reviewed, in the review of The Praetorian Table, Via Appia House, which the scribe has filed under Gull, Gauge, Gauge‑Adjacent, and one small bronze bell. The family’s ledger keeps counting beyond the menu, and the pantry of the New Rome is now at the hammer as Lot 272, The Naissus Tin and the Ledger of the Fifth Bell, which the scribe has likewise filed under Gull, Gauge, Gauge‑Adjacent — one more bell, unstruck. The fourth, for what it is worth, also sits on the shelf a little further south as a country: the dreamer's concordance keeps an entry for España, the fourth‑most‑populous member state of its union, which in the dream stands exactly as the fourth of the family stood in the ledger — ruled out of the weather, then kept it anyway.

AvaruaGauge Reader, the northern boat · Post No. 5 of 6 · with the moon, three days' sea out

Numbers, for the record, posted from the deck so the board can check them against the water: born 1 August 10 BC, at Lugdunum — Roman Gaul, first reading from outside Italy; died 13 October AD 54, sixty‑four years into the counting, and the thread was opened on the day, which the scribe will confirm is not a coincidence but a rule. Father Drusus the Elder, military legate; mother Antonia Minor; the emperor who kept the window before him, Tiberius, was his uncle by marriage, and the stammer made the name take four long syllables to say, which is how the family knew where it stood. One more line for the ledger: fourth of the dynasty, and the name has four heavy parts to it — Tiberius, Claudius, Caesar, Germanicus — so that even the stammer, ruled in, keeps its line.
