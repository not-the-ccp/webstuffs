Deceased · 28 September, 1868

Line 1, column 1 · inserted by the next of kin · $2.00

HELENA. — Hindman, Major General. Of this city, formerly of the Confederate States Army, on Tuesday last, the 28th of September, in the fortieth year of his age and the sixth month thereof. Thomas Carmichael Hindman, Jr., was born in Knoxville, Tennessee, on the 28th of January, 1828; he served with the Mississippi Rifles in the war with Mexico from 1846 to 1848; he was a member of the Mississippi House of Representatives in 1853; and in 1854 he came to Helena, where he practised law, sat in Congress for two terms, and rose to a major-general’s commission in the late army, in which he was, among other things, the officer who raised the conscripts. His family have requested that this line, and no other, be considered their thanks. Interment at the cemetery on the road to Wynnewood, Sabbath morning, the ninth; carriages at the house; no wreaths, the flowers being spoken for.

Situations Vacant · One Lawyer

Line 2, column 1 · E. B. Estopp & Co., executors · $0.90

WANTED, immediately, and to hold until the estate closes, a lawyer of the old school, steady-handed with a ledger, to take the book of Major General Hindman’s law practice, including the Helena files, the cotton accounts of the Yazoo bottom, and one trunk of correspondence marked private, 1864, which is to be read only in the presence of the executor. The vacancy is not a general one; the candidate is expected to have seen a courthouse in Mississippi, to know the Conway–Johnson family by name and to refuse their invitations, and to hold, without flinching, a brief that runs past the hour. Address, by letter, E. B. Estopp, at the old post office; no applications before 11th of this month; the executor is in Washington and returns with the first frost.

For Sale · Lot 33, the Card Drawer

Line 3, column 1 · sold by the heirs’ agent · $0.60

Among the late general’s effects, in the drawer of the library card catalog behind the desk on West Main, there was a single foreign folio, unfiled, in a hand that was not his. The heirs, not being able to pronounce what it was, have put it in as Lot 33, with the catalogue line kept word for word from the agent’s book: “Theban Necropolis Weather Almanac Entry , Station No. 714.” That is the whole of the description. The agent adds, for the satisfaction of bidders, that the folio is the seventeenth and last entry of a running almanac kept at a station in the Egyptian necropolis — the first entry, from the West Bank Station, being the one read in this office every Sunday since February, and filed at TT1 — Sennedjem’s Tomb · Weather Almanac Entry, West Bank Station for those who have followed the series. Station No. 714, the agent says, is not on any map he has; the entries are weather and nothing but weather — dust, dew, the level of the rock — and they were evidently kept by some patient keeper of a dead city. Lot 33 goes to the highest bidder on the first Tuesday; cash, no credit, and the buyer takes the folio as found, seal and all.

Persons Wanted

Line 4, column 1 · inserted anonymously · $0.45

Any person who saw Major General Hindman on the evening of the 27th, at the corner of West Main and Church, or who heard him speak of “the last reading” before he went into his library, is asked to call at this office. A reward of $50 is offered for information that will be believed. The party wishing this line inserted is not named; the office knows the handwriting, the office does not print it. A second notice, smaller, in the same column: the party who kept the station in the general’s yard through the fever season, and who left without a word on the 26th, is asked to leave a card; the yard is let, the pump is paid for, and the general’s widow would like the reason in writing before the first frost.

Rooms To Let

Line 5, column 1 · Mrs. A. T. Searles · $0.30

Three rooms and a pantry, second floor, over the hat store on West Main, with water in the yard and a shuttled shutter; suitable for a young lawyer, a schoolmaster, or one clerk of the late firm. Rent, $8 a month, the first month on the credit of a reference. A parlor also, with the general’s own desk — card drawer empty, and the agent from this page’s third line already asked twice about it. Apply to Mrs. Searles, at home in the evenings; no card games, no lectures, no second almanacs.

Notice To Creditors

Line 6, column 1 · E. B. Estopp & Co. · $1.20

NOTICE IS HEREBY GIVEN to all persons indebted to the late estate of Thomas C. Hindman, Major General, deceased, to make immediate payment to the undersigned at the old post office, Helena. All persons having claims against the said estate will take notice and present the same, with the proper vouchers, to the undersigned on or before the first day of November next. The estate is large, the ledger is larger, and the executor intends to read both. One line, added in the margin of the blank, is printed with it as written: “The conscript roll of 1864 is not a debt, and will not be paid as one.”

Lost

Line 7, column 1 · the general’s widow · $0.15

Lost, between the house on West Main and the library, one pocket watch, gold, case rubbed to a softness, with the Mexican War painted inside the cover, 1846–1848, and a second painting over the first, smaller, done later. The watch was worn to the last. Whoever finds it will know whose it is by the two dates inside, and is asked to send it to the house without a word; the widow will pay whatever the finder names, and the finder may be certain the name will be remembered. A stranger who has seen the watch in Helena is asked to write to this office and stop writing there.

A Kindness, Small

Line 8, column 1 · the paper itself · free

The paper has one line to set at its own charge. The station-keeper of the almanac whose folio is in Lot 33, whoever he or she was, kept a dead city’s weather for a living and did not once ask the general to sign it; the general, in turn, filed it in his card drawer with everything else that had outlived its use. Both of them are now in Helena, and both of them are read in this office. The paper would like to say, once, that the reading is steady; and it will not say it again, because a thing said twice in a classified column becomes a rumour. The office closes at three; the bell, at two.

Set by the compositors of The Clarion, Helena, Arkansas

Set in two sizes, black, with one drop head per notice. The almanac line in Lot 33 is set as received, its odd spacing and all, the compositors having agreed that a catalogue line is not a sentence and must be left alone. Readers comparing this page with the classifieds of other papers — the Spectator Special, London, being the best kept on the subject — will find the rates of this office lower and the general’s obituary twice as short. For the almanac itself, West Bank entry first: TT1, Sennedjem’s Tomb, Deir el-Medina, where the series opens with an unrobbed shaft and 165 objects; Station No. 714 is its seventeenth and last. A return to the whole shelf of such pages is at the front page.
