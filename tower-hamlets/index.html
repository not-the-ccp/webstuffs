Notice No. 118 · Found, not lost · Registered 4 May, 06:40

FOUND — NOT LOST

Item, as Registered

Found at the corner of Whitechapel Road and Old Street, in the light before the first bus, one complete London Borough Council — namely Tower Hamlets London Borough Council, also known as Tower Hamlets Council — the local authority for the London Borough of Tower Hamlets in Greater London, England. The item arrived whole, keys in its pocket, and was brought in to the office on the first floor, where it sat in the visitor’s chair and asked, in a voice that has since been described as Whitechapel, to be kept safe until its keepers came for it.

Description

One local authority, of the usual build for its district: responsible for the provision of certain services within the borough — bins, books, baths and bylaws in roughly that order — and wearing its paperwork correctly. Under the control of local party Aspire since 2022, when the ward count came in and the party took the seats; and led by a directly elected mayor since 2010, the mayor being a figure of the council’s own making, chosen by the borough and answerable to it, who stands at the head of the table at committee. The item is based at Tower Hamlets Town Hall on Whitechapel Road, where the room at the far end of the corridor is lit even at 6:40 in the morning, and where the kettle — see the condition report below — is never far from a decision. Colour of the council: the red of the notice boards. Odour: none, apart from the coffee.

Condition Report

Examined at the desk by the clerk, M. Okonkwo, on the morning of finding, with the door of the Town Hall open and the register open beside it. The services are running, the mayor’s office is occupied, the Aspire benches are in order, and the committee room holds its shape. One defect, entered for fairness: the Town Hall kitchen had, at the time of examination, made itself useful in a manner the register records as follows, and which the clerk read aloud into the record, word for word, as it stands in the book: “Whisk the yolks with the kitchen has taken over without being asked: at a hundred and odd visitor habits, and of the card, not the bridge.” The line has been read at the desk in this manner: the kitchen has taken over without being asked, and is feeding the queue; the hundred and odd visitor habits are the ways the morning public finds its way to the desk, and are counted, because everything at Whitechapel Road is counted; and the claim, when it comes, is of the card, not the bridge — meaning the claim form, not the Tower, which the council wears on its arms and cannot be signed for by a stranger.

Particulars for Identification

The borough is that eastern stretch of the City’s rim — the old St. Mary-le-Bow, Whitechapel, Mile End, Bethnal Green, and the river frontage — and the item carries the name of it, the hall address, the mayor’s name, the year of election, and the 2022 count in its papers. Anyone claiming the council is asked to name at least two of these four, and to do so without reading them from this notice.

Claim Conditions

Claims to be made in person at the first-floor desk, Tower Hamlets Town Hall, Whitechapel Road, Monday to Friday, 9 to 5, or at the door of the committee room when the committee is sitting, which is more or less always. The claimant must produce the card, not the bridge — the council’s own claim form, countersigned by two ward members — and must agree, before the item is handed over, that the item keeps its kitchen, its mayor, its Aspire benches, and its kettle. No reward is offered; the reward is the borough itself, which is worth more than any reward the office is licensed to print. The item will be held for twenty-eight days from the date of this notice, and has, in the event, not been missed for a single hour of them, its keepers having turned up at 7:15 the same morning, 35 minutes after registration, and taken it home.

References

The office files this notice beside its Welsh sister — Lost & Found Notice No. 47, Tufton Parish: one hymn tune, Blaenwern, complete with its 1915 first registration — which was held for its claimant with the same 28-day patience and the same rule about producing the paper and not the paraphrase. For a further example of an item kept on a classifieds board rather than a shelf — the Orbit Sheet, Friday Classifieds No. 67, where a comet of the Jupiter family is held, lost and for sale, at a speed of nothing — the office notes that its own register runs the same four columns: lost, found, for sale, in memoriam — and that one London Borough Council, found 4 May at 6:40, sits in the second of them, safely, with its kettle on.
