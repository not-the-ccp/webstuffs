In Memoriam

“Untitled (How Does It Feel)”

1999 — 10 March 2024

The record died after its last broadcast, when the rotation finally let go of it. It was, to the end, very good at making people ask the question.

★ 1 · The life

The record was born in 1999 in the upstairs studio apartment at 3424 South Fairfax Avenue, West Hollywood, where D’Angelo kept the Voodoo sessions running past 3 a.m., its walls hung with Prince posters and its carpet permanently marked by the soundboard. It was written and produced by D’Angelo with Raphael Saadiq, and it was conceived as a tribute to Prince — a vintage-style homage to the Minneapolis player’s early work, with the same slow-burn groove, the same talk, and a little of the same mischief. The lyrics are a man’s direct plea to his lover, and the record has never once been shy about saying exactly what it means. When it was finished, the engineers named it Untitled (How Does It Feel), and the name stuck, which is the kind of luck an obituary writer envies.

It appeared on Voodoo, D’Angelo’s second studio album, in 2000, and was released as the album’s third single on 10 January 2000 by Virgin Records. Critics were generally kind, and the record collected a number of awards in its lifetime, including the best-of-its-year honors the year turned up. The video, a long slow take in a single room, outlived every trend it started imitating, which was its own small miracle.

★ 2 · The last transmission

On the night it passed, the record was one more time on a late rotation at a small Westside station, and the signal went out the same way it always had — down into the mud, where the low end settles and stays. The station’s logbook, which is kept in triplicate, carries the night’s line exactly as the night operator wrote it:

On the same way, word for word, in the Caribbean Sea, in Namibia, in Newfoundland — wherever the mud doing the same time.

The operators took this to mean what it said: the groove had reached the Caribbean, the Namibian coast, and the waters off Newfoundland in the same pass, and the silt in all three places was holding the note at once. It is the only sentence in the logbook that the bureau has reproduced without an editor’s note, because there was nothing to correct and everything to keep.

★ 3 · Survivors and services

The record is survived by D’Angelo; by Raphael Saadiq, without whom it would have been a demo; by the Voodoo vault, which holds the masters; by one room in a video that never leaves the room; and by a long list of radio operators who kept it turning. A memorial listening service was held at the Fairfax studio on the first anniversary of the album, and a second, unannounced, at the station that carried the final broadcast.

Other notices of this kind in the bureau’s files — for a directive that expired like a long career, and for a London literary magazine whose publication simply ceased — are kept on the same wall: In Memoriam, Directive 2019/904 (European Union), 2019–2021, and Transmission — Publication Ceased. The bureau’s full register of remembrances is returned to the Webstuffs front page, where the record’s plot number is entered in the same ink as everything else that stopped, and everything that stayed.
