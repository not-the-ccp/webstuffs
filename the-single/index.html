The Single

A town that is defined, in the mid-20th century, by a seven-inch record — how to visit it, and how to read it, from the parcel post to the B-side shelf.

45rev / min

Orientation — what a single is, and why this town knows it better than most

In music, a single is a type of release of a song recording of fewer tracks than an album (LP). As the name suggests, a single typically contains only a single song, but they can sometimes have two — two, and never three, the Ravine record room will tell you, though Aldergate has been argued over one occasion in January 2014. A single can be released for sale to the public in a variety of physical or digital formats: 7-inch vinyl, a pay-what-you-want download, a file on a shelf that nobody prices. Singles may be standalone tracks or connected to an artist’s album, and in the latter case a disc would often have at least one single release before the album itself, called a lead single. The single was defined in the mid-20th century with the 45 (named after its speed in revolutions per minute), a type of 7-inch sized vinyl record containing an A-side and a B-side, i.e. one song on each side.

Aldergate & the Ravine is not the town that invented any of this, but it is the town where the Neon parcel of 25 January 2014 set its sleeve down on the parcel post, and the Minutes No. 112 of 2014 were written from the room above it. Visitors who understand that one order of events — first the disc, then the minutes, then the entry in this guidebook — will have understood the town.

Stop 1 The Parcel Post, 4 High Lane

The day begins here, in the grey hour before the door opens, because that is when the singles arrive. The clerk who serves the post — Tomas Vane, who also serves the council — has kept the register of arrivals in the same book since 2003, and the book opens on a sentence he has never once corrected. The visitor is encouraged to read it aloud at the counter, word for word, as the book preserves it:

“The clerk referred the council had any standing to receive parcels of music.”

The grammar is Aldergate’s, and it works. Referred in the old usage does both jobs at once — it puts the question and it carries it — and the council, sitting in the room above, answered the question inside it in the affirmative, four to one, and has not once been sorry for it. On 25 January 2014 a parcel came in from the Portland post district with no sender’s address and one line of address reading “wherever the council meets”; inside was the four-track EP Neon, by Versa, self-released 21 January 2014 as a pay-what-you-want download, the duo’s first release as Versa, previously VersaEmerge, the old name still legible in the sleeve’s watermark. The clerk entered it under the head music, unscheduled. The head has grown since; it now holds the register of payments, closed at the end of each February, and the total, whatever it is, is entered and not confirmed, because the price is whatever the paying hand decides, from zero upward, and the zero side of that is the side on which the council has most often landed in its eleven years of sittings.

Stop 2 Rask & Ostergaard, 7-inch Specialists, 12 Ravine Street

Named for two of its aldermen and run by neither, the shop keeps the 45 in its original place on the counter: a 7-inch disc of vinyl, 45 revolutions a minute, one song on each side. The A-side is the single; the B-side is the thing the single is holding. In 1949 the year the 45 took hold, a pressing was a complete musical institution — a length, a speed, a price — and the shop sells them still, at the speeds and prices they were pressed at.

Turn the disc over — the B-side is the one the ward knows: Miss Lund sits for the Ravine, and the lower of the two cities on the sleeve is the one her ward would recognise. The sleeve’s quarter note, inked over the lower city name so that it appears to stand in the streets, is a lighter thing than a sign, since it can be taken down without leaving a hole in the wall; the bench built under it, 12 Ravine Street, is not to be taken down.

Stop 3 Lead-Single Season, the Plain

Where a single is connected to an album, the town follows the old order: at least one single release before the album itself. The plain calls it lead-single season, and it runs from the first grey morning of January to the closing of the register of payments in February. During the season the council receives, in no fixed order: a 1987 single titled neon, whose neighbourhood was printed once, out of order, in the Malmö Sound Section’s Error Reference AM-15/03 and kept exactly as received; a 2004 verification in Malmö; and whatever the parcel post sets down at 4 High Lane. A word that arrives on its own terms, the council agreed in 2014, is the same kind of matter as a disc that arrives on its own terms, and the town keeps both where they come in.

Practical Information

Best time to visit

The grey hour before the parcel post door opens, and Thursdays, when the crates keep their count open in the manner the shed keeps its gees in their numbered places.

Language note

The B-side shelf is set in the opentail g, and the A-side in the looptail, per the motion carried five to nil at the Council of the Shed, Recalde Press & Cooperage, Minute No. 41; neither form is asked to stand down, and neither is the visitor.

Getting there

The parcel post is on the plain; the room above is where the council meets. The 7-inch shop is down Ravine Street, where the quarter note stands. There is no fixed price for any of it, and no fixed anything about the register, which is therefore a very good register.

What not to do

Do not pay what you do not want. The register is not an account, the council cannot confirm the total, only the manner of it, and Alderman Kova’s dissent of 29 January 2014 — that a thing with no fixed price cannot be entered in a register — remains on the record, four to one, and is the only objection the town has ever received about itself, which is to say, in the right hand.
