Ten years. The programme began in June 2010 with a Network Rail announcement that it would be finished by 2017, and it is now finished — properly finished, with the electric services running the full distance between London Paddington and Cardiff — in 2020. That is what I am writing to mark, on the board, because the board is the only place I kept a running diary of it in plain language.

For anyone returning to the thread from the archive: the modernisation covered the Great Western Main Line, the South Wales Main Line, and a set of associated lines. The plans were announced at separate times, but their implementation overlapped through the 2010s, so there were seasons when you could watch three crews working on three parts of the same decade in one valley. The work was electrification, resignalling, new rolling stock and station upgrades — overhead wire and new signalling where there had been neither, Pendolino trains where there had been nothing but the old expresses, and new platforms and footbridges at the stations that had been standing since the 1930s.

And yes, there were delays. Every year a section of the programme moved, and the 2017 end date became a rumour, then a footnote, then a historical curiosity. I followed it the whole way. Which section were you on, or did you watch it from the other side of the glass?

chippenham_regular wrote on 12 June 2020, 07:15

I was on it at Thingley the whole decade, which means I watched the work I was not getting. Four sections were deferred indefinitely, and the board minutes listed them in one flat paragraph, no drama, in this order: Oxford to Didcot Parkway; Bristol Parkway to Bristol Temple Meads; Thingley Junction, near Chippenham, to Bath Spa; and a fourth section running out of Bristol westward that the minutes never quite gave a name to. Everything else got wire, new signalling and new stations. My line did not. So when you ask what the ten years bought, I will answer honestly: at my end of the map, they bought a promise that is still, technically, open. The section stays on the old arrangements for as long as my season ticket is.

bath_gazetteer wrote on 12 June 2020, 08:02

While the thread is open I will log one odd find. Last month we were cataloguing the attic of the old Bath depot — the one over the former signal room — for the station trust, and in a crate of signal-case timber we found a rolled poster, 2010 date on the back, from a university campaign about education that costs money. It has nothing to do with the modernisation, except that it arrived in the crate in the year the programme began.

Sara’s standing question is whether things found up there go on the keep list — the laminated A5 sheet, in someone’s handwriting, with forty-one items on it: the two ticket machines from before 1990, the 1984 platform notice, the signalman’s helmet, the brass plate from the old buffet, a folded 2010 works programme with four of the deferred sections struck through. The trust’s answer on the poster was short, and I am keeping it here as it was given: It is a warning about private schools rather than in the attic's keep list. So it goes in the box marked not kept, which is, I suppose, its own kind of list.

cardiff_central wrote on 12 June 2020, 09:27

I was here for the endgame, and I will defend the delays at my end of the line. The Pendolinos first reached Cardiff Central in April 2018 — two years before the programme was formally closed and a full six before the 2017 date that never came — and the extra months the slip bought us were not wasted: they bought a longer platform, a new footbridge, and a ticket hall that is actually a ticket hall. The 2020 completion is the paperwork; the electric service is the thing. I ride it every week, and when it pulls in silent from the west I do not think about 2017. I think the ten years took, exactly, ten years — June to June — and that is a rounder number than the delay was.

signalman_west wrote on 13 June 2020, 05:58

Resignalling is the part nobody photographs, and it is the part that matters. The old levers along the line went over to the new signalling box by box through the decade, and the line’s capacity to carry trains through the heart of the day roughly doubled; the new rolling stock then used it. If you enjoy a timetable as a document that has actually kept its promises, the archive keeps the Courant Line official timetable, in force since 1752 with a 05:52 departure nobody has moved; and if you want a timetable that comes with editorial commentary in the margin, the Dhaka Dawn Express route 6 runs 04:12 to 09:04 with the editor’s notes in the same column. Both of them, unlike our end of 2017, arrived on time. The rest of the archive sits at the front page of Webstuffs, and this thread is filed there too.
