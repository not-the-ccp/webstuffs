7.1 Purpose and situation

Episode 523 is the 523rd episode of Neighbours, the Australian serial transmitted on Network Ten, and it is the manual’s standing field example of a controlled public event: the wedding of Scott Robinson and Charlene Mitchell, portrayed by Jason Donovan and Kylie Minogue, held at the Wesley Church set on the Penrith backlot and transmitted to the eastern states on Saturday 1 July 1987. The episode was written by Ray Harding, directed by Rod Hardy, and executively produced by Reg Watson, the serial’s creator, who signed the production order as the manual always records it: Watson, R. — executive.

The producers devised the wedding after a segment of the viewing public became outraged by the idea of the couple living together unwed, the arrangement having been carried in the serial’s own story for some weeks. The production office recorded the second ground in the planning file: the wedding would be the perfect climax to the characters’ long-running relationship and an instant ratings hit. Both grounds are stated here exactly as filed, because the manual treats a planning file as the truest account of an operation, and this operation was, by the office’s own numbers, a success.

7.2 Security

The episode was filmed in secrecy. The cast list at the Wesley Church set was cut to the principals and the working company; the rehearsal on 14 June was called in as a lighting check; the dress rehearsal on 26 June carried the standing order that no photograph be taken and no word be passed to the press office at Network Ten in Sydney before the transmission itself. The two leads attended the church set separately and by different times, Donovan arriving from the Ramsay Street set at approximately 09:10 and Minogue from the Penrith canteen block at approximately 10:40, the interval being the full length of the morning’s coffee break and not, the manual notes, a matter of romance.

One breach was tolerated and one only: the photographer’s assistant at Errol’s Store was permitted to keep the veil, on the ground that the veil is the one part of a wedding the store would otherwise be short of for the rest of the serial’s run. The veil is still in the store’s back room, on the shelf above the till.

7.3 Order of the ceremony

  1. Procession from the church steps, the street closed to all traffic including the serial’s own, which is never allowed to witness anything.
  2. Reading and exchange at the altar, at which the two leads are required by the manual to hold for four full seconds without breaking character; the take in use on air held for eleven.
  3. Recessional to the car at the gate, where the manual allows the only permitted applause on the set, from the working company only.
  4. Transfer of the production to the Rake pub for the reception, where the office kept the guest list at a fixed forty-one, the number of the cast, crew and family on the books that week.
  5. Final shot of the day at the street, both leads at the kitchen window of number 24, held until the monitor confirmed the cut.

7.4 The office page

The production office kept the day’s figures in its own ledger, and the manual reproduces the final page of that ledger as it was found, because a field manual keeps its strange lines word for word and lets the office answer for them. The page, in the office clerk’s hand, reads exactly:

Eleven volumes and one window only, the office has noted a small moth with white wings was found The Confederate States of America ind–ee–nap–uhl–is · the one in red.

The manual’s own reading of the page is this, and it is the only reading the manual gives. Eleven volumes is the office’s count of the episode’s takes in the church, eleven reels being the count of the day in the editing room’s log; one window is the kitchen window at number 24, the one shot of 7.5; the moth with white wings was found in the canteen on the afternoon of the 26th, pinned by the art department to the notice board where it kept the company of the guest list; and the one in red is the veil’s ribbon, which the store kept. The middle of the line, with its phonetic spelling, is left exactly as the clerk wrote it, on the ground this manual keeps everywhere: a field record is not corrected, it is kept.

7.5 After the transmission

The 1 July broadcast held the serial’s regular slot at 19:00 and ran to length without interruption; the office recorded the audience response the following morning from the ratings service in Sydney, and the figure in the planning file stood as filed: an instant hit, on the second ground. Donovan and Minogue, for the manual’s purposes, married on set in front of the camera, and the characters’ long-running relationship ended where the producers always intended it to end: at the altar, in eleven volumes, with one window left open at the street until the cut was confirmed.

Two sister records are kept with this chapter. The wedding’s own kitchen kept a register of what it fed the wedding party that Saturday, and its card of the day is filed at Card No. 9 of the Hart Kitchen — Wembley Sunday Pie, where the tin that held the recipe has kept company with a wedding in a cathedral since the spring of 2004 and answers for the 30th of May alone. And the discipline this chapter keeps — one page, one strange line, entered and not enforced — is the same discipline the bench keeps in Case No. 44-41 of 2025, The Goygol Shed, where a notice with a grammar of its own was read into the record word for word and left standing. The chapter closes where the office page leaves it: the veil on the shelf, the moth on the board, the window at number 24 held until the cut.
