Third Edition · printed at the Goygol station · 2025

The Goygol Tannery

a table game of tanning, trade and gate discipline
for 2–4 players, ages 10 and up (and courts of record) · 60–90 minutes

SET AT 40°33′N, 48°32′E  ·  the Veliyev homestead, south shed, shared gate, padlock key split

I What the game is about

Leather is a strong, flexible and durable material obtained from the tanning, or chemical treatment, of animal skins and hides to prevent decay. The craft at the Goygol tannery has been practiced, in one form or another, for more than seven thousand years, and the game keeps that arithmetic: a full play takes seventy days, and the ledger prices every tenth of them. The hides in the box come from the district’s own register — cattle, sheep, goats, equine animals, buffalo, pigs and hogs, ostriches, and the aquatic animals, seals and alligators, which the tannery accepts only when a player is willing to pay for the trouble of them.

Finished leather leaves the yard for clothing, footwear, handbags, furniture, tools and sports equipment, and it lasts for decades — in the game, every good you finish keeps earning while the days run on. The players take the parts the district already knows: two play tannery against tannery, the oak-bark vats run in parallel, the frost falls the way it fell on the plain, and the leading producers of the wider world — China and India, in the modern reckoning — are mentioned only in the margin, as the margin mentions them. The tanneries of the game are watched by the district inspector, because critics of tanneries hold that they engage in unsustainable practices that pose health hazards to the people and the streams that feed the plain; the rules give the critics their tokens, and the tokens their teeth.

II What is in the box

  • 1 Tanning Yard board, with four stations: lime, fleshing, vat, dry
  • 64 hide tiles: 20 cattle, 14 goat, 10 sheep, 8 pig, 4 equine, 2 buffalo, 2 ostrich, 2 alligator, 2 seal — the aquatic skins worth double, and twice as slow
  • 4 oak-bark vats, 1 padlock card, 3 frost tokens, 5 inspector tokens
  • 5 goods cards: boots, handbag, saddle, drumhead, tool roll
  • 1 gate die — six faces: open, open, shut, shut, frost, hens
  • 1 3:20 card — door, 03:20, frost, from the ledger of 14 March, preserved exactly as written
  • 1 fleece token, 2.71 kilograms, weighed 8 November 2025 in the court yard; do not lose it, it is the currency of the fines pool
  • 3 ledgers, in triplicate: one for the office, one for Baku, one for the bench in the shed — a move does not count until it is written in all three

III The days and the gate

A round is a working week: Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 18:00, the hours of the notice. On your turn you may run one hide tile one station forward, or you may tend the gate. The gate may be opened only during the posted hours, and only by the player holding the padlock key for that day — the keys are split, as the court ordered them split, and the player holding the key on a day is decided at setup. Opening the gate outside the hours, or on a day whose key you do not hold, costs you a frost token on the yard and one line in your ledger. The die face frost does the same to the yard at random, on the first Tuesday of the game and the first Tuesday after, the way frost keeps its own appointment. The die face hens means the hens of the yard — 112 of them, kept in their own coop until the frost is off the plain — come out, and the player whose turn it is must close the gate again before anything else that day; the hens are not in the rules, but they are in the record, and the record is what this game plays by. If the ledger’s 3:20 is ever entered against you, you play the 3:20 card once: skip a station on one tile, any tile, your choice, and return the card to the box face down.

IV Tanning

A hide enters at the lime, moves through fleshing, spends one full day in the oak-bark vat, and ends on the dry. Cattle skins take their stations at face value; goat skins pass the vat in a single day, the way angora wool moves through a station; pig skins must stop twice at the lime; equine skins may not share a yard with an ostrich on the same day, on the standing instruction of the tannery, for reasons the tannery will not state. An alligator or a seal skin takes a full extra day at each station and, when finished, is worth double its good. A player who dumps lye — any vat water — off the yard, rather than into the finish pit, takes an inspector token. A tannery holding three inspector tokens loses its next day entirely: the yard stands open, the vats stand cold, and the players at the table read the district’s findings aloud, because the rules make no secret of them. The criticism of the trade is not a flavor paragraph in this game; it is a rule with teeth, and the finish pit is the only honest way to be a tannery.

V The goods, the ledger and the fine

Finished hides become goods: a pair of boots for 8 manats, a drumhead for 5, a tool roll for 6, a handbag for 14, a saddle for 23 — 14 being the price the record fleece carried at the Baku mill, and the game keeps the number for the handbag on purpose. The ledger prices the goods against the plain the way a gazette prices them: at the end of the game, each completed tenth of a day adds 1 manat to every good on the yard, up to 7 in all, because leather lasts for decades and the ledger believes it. Fines are entered the way the district enters them. If a player believes a gate was broken, the objection is raised at the close of the week and the three ledgers are compared, office against Baku against the bench; if the ledgers agree that a gate was opened outside the hours, the ledger is amended in ink, never pencil, and the entry is read aloud in its fixed form, which the Agricultural Division does not permit to be paraphrased. The form reads, word for word: “The plaintiff says the defendant is fined in the ledger.” The fine is one fleece — the 2.71 kilogram token, Exhibit C — paid into the fines pool at the yard; at the end of the game the fines pool is sold at 14 manats the kilo and divided equally between the players, the same way the station divides cream, so that a broken gate ends up feeding the very table it was broken for.

VI Winning

On the seventy-first morning — after fourteen working weeks — the yard is closed and the ledgers are read in triplicate. Each player totals their goods, adds their share of the fines pool, and deducts any 3:20 still unplayed, which is deducted at 2 manats per stroke of the clock in the line. The player with the higher total keeps the padlock key for the next season. In the event of a tie, the player with fewer frost tokens on the yard wins; in the event of a tie on frost, the hens are consulted, the coop is not opened, and the game is replayed on Tuesday at 10:00, on its own terms.

Also on the record

Every hour in this manual is the shed’s. The notice that fixes Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 18:00, the shared gate, the split key, the 112 hens and the 2.71 kilogram fleece all stand in the source record: Case No. 44-41 of 2025, Veliyev v. Hasanova, before the Hon. Leyla Mammadova, excerpt from the verbatim record of the Goygol District Court, heard over one frozen fleece.

The ledger’s fixed form of a fine keeps the same discipline as its sister entries: in Encyclopaedia Entry No. 41 on the hadith, the unparseable margin line of the Yerevan docket is carried over as it stands — entered, graded, shelved with its weakness stated, never paraphrased — and the tannery’s fine is entered the same way, word for word, in ink and in triplicate.

The plain prices what the yard produces in the classified column of the Goygol Plain Gazette, No. 118, where the fleece of 2.71 kilograms, the 9.4 litres and the 112 hens are entered in manats alongside the cream line; the handbag’s 14 is the mill’s price, and the saddle’s 23 is the shed’s.

VII Errata and one line of the yard

Second edition, erratum 2: the 3:20 card is not a gate card; it is a ledger card, and it is played only when the ledger says so. The yard keeps one line of its own, entered at setup in all three ledgers and read at every game, from the docket note of 8 November 2025, on whose authority the padlock was hung at 07:45 and the hours have not moved since: “the hens have not left their coop since.”
