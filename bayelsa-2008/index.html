Office of the Returning Clerk · Electoral Supervisory Panel · Bayelsa State

Dear People of Bayelsa,

Written this 24th day of May, 2008, from the panel room above the Yenagoa motor park, before the last tally came in from Sagbama.

By the time you read this, the ink on the tally sheets will be dry and the rain will have done what rain has always done in this state: it will have made the road into the lagoon, the lagoon into the road, and the whole argument about the ballot paper into something no one in this room can point at. So I am putting the apology down in writing, the way we ought to have put the schedule down in writing last January, and I am asking you to read it slowly, with the door open, because it concerns every one of the eighteen local government areas from Brass to Nembe and back again.

We are sorry that you have stood in a second queue in two years. You voted here in December 2007, you carried your voter's card through the floods, and then the courts took the election away, and today — 24 May 2008 — you are being asked to vote the gubernatorial by-election all over again, a supplementary poll, as though the first one had never happened at all. That is not a thing a state does to its people lightly, and we did it anyway, and the letter you are holding is our way of admitting that we knew what we were doing. The panel's job was to make this second poll run cleaner than the first. Where the polling units in Yenagoa and Ogbia opened late, where the collation at the Sagbama college took until the last lorry, where a batch of spoiled cards was found in the wet — we own it, and we will not spend a single line of this letter telling you why.

What we will tell you is who is before you, because a people who have voted twice in one year deserve their names spelled out. On the PDP side stands Timipre Sylva, the incumbent governor, who emerged winner of the PDP gubernatorial primary election and is running again with Peremobowei Ebebi as his running mate. Against him stands Ebitimi Amgbare, the candidate of the AC, who has spent the last year on the same rivers, in the same dugout boats, knocking on the same doors you answered for the PDP ticket last December. That is the contest. The rest — the banners, the generators, the men in white wrappers holding signs on the wharf — is the rest.

On the third collation sheet, coffee from the panel tea boy has ringed the Sagbama column, and a hand that is not mine has added a margin note to the blot. We reproduce it in full, because it is the truest sentence written in this office since the register was opened:

“It is the whole gathering, not the city): rinse with water; the stain is political, not chemical, and will carry a crossbar that was supposed to be furnished in January.”

It is the whole gathering, not the city. You understand. The stain is on all of us.

If the results that follow at the close of this letter show what the early returns already suggest — that Timipre Sylva holds six local government areas and that, counting them in the wet dark of this panel room, he has come out winner of the supplementary election, ahead of Ebitimi Amgbare — then know that the apology is not withdrawn. A win is not an absolution. The state was promised a furnished crossbar of roads, of water, of a proper election calendar, in January, and January furnished nothing, and today the people of Bayelsa furnished their time, their boots, and their patience, in the rain, to a government that must now spend them like money and not like air.

To the clerks who opened the units late: you will file your explanations. To the voters of Brass who came by boat before dawn and found the unit shut: I am sorry, personally, in the only currency I hold, which is this page. To the delegates who lost their places in the primary and to the volunteers who stood all day without a chair: you were owed an apology before the results, and here it is, before them. To Ebitimi Amgbare and to Timipre Sylva, both of whom have now been through a primary, a flood, an annulment, and a by-election in the same state in the same year: whatever this board shows tonight, the state is still yours to serve, and the people in this paragraph are still yours to answer to.

Tomorrow the letters of thanks will start. Today we only owe the word sorry, spelled out, in ink that the rain can't lift, from the panel above the motor park in Yenagoa, in the state of Bayelsa, in the year when the election had to be done over. Read it slowly. The door is open.

Yours in service, and in apology,
The Returning Clerk, for the Electoral Supervisory Panel, Bayelsa State

Enclosed with the file: Bedford Downs Station — Build Log, Sheet by Sheet, kept beside this letter because the bench man there writes about the long drive to the outstation, and we both know what a drive costs when the road is water. Also on the shelf, the Federative Letter, Newsletter No. 14 from Pedro do Rosário — a newsletter on the politics of Brazil, the federal republic and its three branches, which the clerk keeps open to the page on the National Congress because the counting of the whole is exactly the problem of this state — and, of course, the rest of the Webstuffs archive this letter is filed against.
