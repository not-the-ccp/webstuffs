SUPERIOR COURT OF THE STATE OF ILLINOIS · COUNTY OF COOK · LAW DIVISION

THE CITY OF WINDY CITY,

Plaintiff,

v.

THE WIND OFF LAKE MICHIGAN (NORTHERN REACH),

Defendant.

Case No. 83 C 11,204

Before the Hon. Doris K. Whelan, of the Court

Trial day — 11 June 1993, 9:04 a.m.

Courtroom 9, Richard J. Daley Center

Counsel: Ms. R. Halvorsen, Assistant City Solicitor, appearing for the Plaintiff; no appearance for the Defendant, the wind having declined service. Stenographic reporter of record: T. M. Okafor, RPR, No. 118.

Witness: Mr. Michael Lee Capel, relief pitcher, presently of the Milwaukee organization, formerly of the Chicago and Houston organizations.

CERTIFIED TRUE EXCERPT
Clerk's Office · Tr. 14–19

[ Tr. 14 ]

CLERK BRENAN: All rise. Case Number Eighty-Three C Eleven Two-Oh-Four, the City of Windy City against the wind off Lake Michigan, northern reach, is called. The bench is present.

THE COURT: Be seated. The Defendant remains, as it has all term, unrepresented and largely unarrestable. Ms. Halvorsen, the City may proceed with its remaining witness.

MS. HALVORSEN: Your Honor, the City calls Michael Lee Capel.

CLERK BRENAN: Mr. Capel, approach, and please state your name for the record.

Q. MS. HALVORSEN: Mr. Capel, state your full name and date of birth for the record, please.

A. CAPEL: Michael Lee Capel. Born October 13, 1961.

Q. MS. HALVORSEN: Your occupation, and your occupation as the major leagues understood it?

A. CAPEL: A pitcher, ma'am. Professional. I pitched in the major leagues for the Chicago Cubs, the Milwaukee Brewers, and the Houston Astros — that is the whole of it, and it is the whole of the career on the ledger.

[ Tr. 15 ]

Q. MS. HALVORSEN: How did the leagues acquire you?

A. CAPEL: The Philadelphia Phillies selected me in the 24th round of the 1980 major league draft. I signed. In college I was a starting pitcher, and for parts of my minor league years I was a starter too. Then, in Chicago's minor league system, they sat me down in a room at the Peoria clubhouse with a coffee and a card on the table, and told me the rest of my career would be delivered out of the bullpen. So I converted, and I have been a reliever ever since.

Q. MS. HALVORSEN: Your height and weight as carried on the league roster while you were under contract?

A. CAPEL: Six feet one inch. That is 185 centimetres to a metric bench. One hundred and seventy-five pounds; 79 kilograms.

Q. MS. HALVORSEN: We come to the matter the City most wishes on record. State your complete major league pitching record for the court, in the order the box scores keep it.

A. CAPEL: Forty-nine career games. Sixty-two and one-third innings pitched. Forty-three batters struck out. A win–loss record of three and four. An earned run average of 4.62. That is the season, all of it, in three and a half years of service, and I would rather it were longer, but the ledger is the ledger.

THE COURT: Noted, and entered. The court will say for the record that a 4.62 earned run average over 62⅓ innings is a small record and an honest one. Ms. Halvorsen, continue.

[ Tr. 16 ]

Q. MS. HALVORSEN: Your Honor, we come to the defendant. In your forty-nine games, in the three parks where the lake was within shouting distance of the outfield wall, how many times did the wind off Lake Michigan affect your delivery?

A. CAPEL: Every time it blew, ma'am. It took the fastball two ticks off the board and carried the changeup back toward the plate when it should have dropped. You learn to throw a little harder than the number asks for, and you learn to leave a pitch in the glove on the second pitch of the at-bat. In the bullpen the discipline is: hold the pitch, and throw it only when the count earns it. The wind never once earned it early.

Q. MS. HALVORSEN: And those 62⅓ innings — how do you account for the third, for the record?

A. CAPEL: The one-third is one batter reached and not retired. One out on the clock, the plate left warm, and the record keeps it exactly as the ledger keeps it. I have no better explanation than that the game was still going.

THE COURT: Before we proceed, the City calls Exhibit C, the minutes of the Lake Committee for the afternoon session, folio 7, the stretch 10:42 to 10:44. The clerk of the court, you may read what is there.

CLERK BRENAN: (Reading from Exhibit C, minutes, folio 7.) “The verbatim record — the car that is not touched; the paddy — the future Mali, the future Mali, the future Mali, the future Guinea, and the minutes closed.”

[ Tr. 17 ]

THE COURT: The court has read that line, and the court does not construe its meaning. It is entered into the record word for word, as it stands in the minutes of the Lake Committee, and it is struck from the evidence. The court notes, and will confine itself to two observations: the car, as stated, was not touched; and the minutes, as stated, were closed. As for the paddy, the court will say only that the court does not rule on paddies, and the future of any future Mali or future Guinea sits with whoever keeps that committee's minutes, which is not this bench. The clerk will leave the line on folio 7, where it lies, marked and untouched. Is that entered?

CLERK BRENAN: Entered, Your Honor. Word for word, with the three “the future Mali”s and all, and the strike entered beside it in red.

Q. MS. HALVORSEN: Mr. Capel, for the record — as a pitcher of 49 games and 62⅓ innings, do you recognize the rhythm of that minutes?

A. CAPEL: I do, ma'am. In the bullpen you learn to hold a pitch and not throw it, to keep the ball in the glove while the count goes around. The car that is not touched is the same discipline, and a line that is read and struck and left where it lies is a ball held and not thrown. I recognize the rhythm. I would not call it a wind.

THE COURT: The court will enter the witness's analogy without ruling upon it. Ms. Halvorsen, any further questions?

Q. MS. HALVORSEN: None, Your Honor. The City rests its witness, and asks only that the forty-nine games, the 62⅓ innings, and the 4.62 be held against the wind and not against the man, the man being six feet one inch and 175 pounds and having thrown everything he had for three and a half years.

THE COURT: So held. The court will take the record under advisement, and this excerpt of the proceedings is closed at 9:41 a.m. The witness may step down. Mr. Capel, a clean 43 strikeouts is a clean 43 strikeouts, wherever the wind came from. Adjourned until the court says otherwise.
