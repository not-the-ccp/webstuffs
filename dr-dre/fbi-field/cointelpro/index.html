I. The Subject

COINTELPRO, which the student has spelled out correctly as a syllabic abbreviation of Counter Intelligence Program, was a series of covert projects conducted between 1956 and 1971 by the Federal Bureau of Investigation, the Department of Justice's domestic intelligence and security service. The projects aimed at surveilling, infiltrating, discrediting, and disrupting American political parties and organizations that the Bureau perceived as subversive. The director of the Bureau through most of that period was J. Edgar Hoover, and the work went out of 935 Pennsylvania Avenue NW in Washington, the same address the Bureau still occupies, so that the building the student drew in the margin of page two stands in the right place and does not need correcting.

II. The Targets

The student's list is full and in order, and the examiner signs it: feminist organizations; the Communist Party USA; anti-Vietnam War organizers; and the activists of the civil rights and Black power movements, named in the report as Martin Luther King Jr., Malcolm X, and the Black Panther Party. Student organizations came next — Students for a Democratic Society (SDS) and its weather of offshoots — and the student has been right to observe that a government which keeps a list of more than two hundred federal crimes does not stop at the list. The report's phrase for the method, the whole thing before the part, is the student's own, and it is the right phrase: the program watched the movement as a movement first, and the member second.

III. How It Came to Light

In June of 1971 a young man in Chicago, G. N. McIlwain, then of the congregation at the Church of All Peoples, opened a steel safe in a storage room at 25 East Palmer Street and found, among the files of the Bureau's Chicago field office, a sheaf of documents stamped Top Secret describing the program. The documents were copied, the safe was closed, and the papers were returned to their places, and on the thirteenth of July the New York Times carried the story and the Bureau had to answer for what the papers had said. The United States Senate, in the inquiry later known as the Church Committee, sat through 1975 and 1976 to hear what the program had done and what it had cost, and a second committee of the House, the Pike Committee, sat with it. The student has been told that the program was formally shut down in 1971, and has been told also that a program of this kind is shut down the way a record is set down: the needle lifted, the side finished, the whole thing still in the sleeve.

IV. The Examiner's Note, and the House Note

The examiner gives this paper a distinction and files it, as the house files everything, in whole. The report sits on the shelf at 2234 NW Flanders Street beside the agent's copy of Field Manual FM-14, Section 3 — Surveillance and Cover, the manual of the same Bureau, whose rules of the game are the inverse of the subject of this paper: the Bureau in that manual does not flip, counts its ten names in order, and keeps the file in its hand before it knocks, while COINTELPRO is the record of what a Bureau does in the dark, without a file, and without knocking. The same house holds Placard No. 48 · Dr. Dre · Gallery 3, The Listening Wing, and the Los Angeles field office's Case 1989-LA-047 on that street, and the reel counter on attic unit No. 2 has kept running through the reading of both without once being asked to, while the drip over the workbench kept its forty seconds over the mounting of the shelf.

On the fourth page of this report, in the margin, in a hand the examiner has not matched, one line has been preserved word for word. It reads:

“Dissolve the soda in the dark, which is really marvellous, considering that I did not write.”

The report has not attempted to solve the line, and the examiner has not attempted to solve the line; the last clause does the work, the way the house does the work of a line the house did not write. The dark is the same dark the reel counter runs through; the soda is dissolved in the sleeve of a record that has already finished; and the line, being the line of a hand that did not write the report, is filed under the case with the rest, in ink, while the engine stays cold.

V. For the Spring Term

The student is to bring the report's reading to the Senate record of the Church Committee, to the testimony of the Church of All Peoples, and to the small ledger the house keeps of lines kept word for word. One part per meeting. No flipping. The whole thing before the part.
