THE CLERK: The Court of the Trophy is in session. The record opens on the date of the subject's birth, 24 June 1987, Rosario, Santa Fe, and has been open ever since. The State calls its ledger first.

JUDGE FERREYRA: Proceed, Mr. Roldán. The bench has read the ledger twice and will read it once more without prejudice.

MR. ROLDÁN: Thank you, Your Honor. The State's count runs as follows. The subject, Lionel Andrés Messi, an Argentine professional footballer who plays as a forward for and captains the Major League Soccer club Inter Miami, is widely regarded as one of the greatest players in history. He played for the Argentina national team for twenty-one years. During his career he has set numerous records for individual accolades won, including eight Ballons d'Or, six European Golden Shoes, and having been named the world's best player by FIFA eight times. He was named the Laureus World Sportsman of the Year in 2020 and 2023, and was named the All Time Men's World Best Player by the IFFHS in 2025. He was among Time's 100 most influential people in the world in 2011.

JUDGE FERREYRA: The defense?

MR. ROLDÁN: There is no defense, Your Honor. The subject has never once contested the count, which the State considers the only true objection ever made to these figures.

[Page 47 · the witness, Sr. Oyola, is called to the stand]

MR. ROLDÁN: Sr. Oyola, you have kept the cabinet above the courtroom for thirty-one years. The cabinet holds the trophies, the certificates, and the two ledgers — the working copy on the desk, and the cabinet's copy on the shelf behind the brass lock. Correct?

SRO. OYOLA: Correct.

MR. ROLDÁN: In the working copy, as found after the 2010 move, the eighth Ballon d'Or is present but the line for the office itself is missing. The State offers you the cabinet's copy, Exhibit 12. Is it your habit that the missing line appears there?

SRO. OYOLA: It is the only habit the cabinet and I share, sir. The office was set down in the cabinet's copy. In no other ledger, in no working copy, in no desk. The office — the trophy office, with its eight shelves, its six brass hooks for the Golden Shoes, and its one door that opens only from inside — is entered once, in the cabinet's copy, in the keeper's own hand, under the heading where the count is kept when the count is not to be argued.

MR. ROLDÁN: No further questions. Your Honor, the State rests its exhibit. The line in question is read to the court verbatim, and it is entered in the record as it stands: “The office was set down in the cabinet's copy.”

JUDGE FERREYRA: Entered. The court has checked the line against the cabinet's copy, through the glass, at 9:26 this morning, and it stands. It is not to be moved, and neither is the cabinet.

[Page 58 · the bench rules from the record]

JUDGE FERREYRA: Having heard the count of eight, the count of six, the count of eight again in a different trophy, the two counts of 2020 and 2023, the finding of 2025, and the single line entered verbatim from the cabinet's copy, the bench rules as follows. The record of Lionel Andrés Messi is found to be complete, unobjected to, and in permanent session. The subject played his twenty-one years for Argentina, captained Inter Miami across the Atlantic, and kept every trophy where the keeper said a trophy should be kept. There is nothing in the file that argues, and the court will not appoint counsel to it.

JUDGE FERREYRA: Cause No. 1987-L is not closed. The court does not close causes of this length; it simply stops reading them aloud. The next scheduled reading is the subject's next birthday, 24 June, at 9:00 a.m., Room 3, and the usher will bring the working copy up from the desk and the keeper will bring the cabinet's copy down, for one hour only, so that the two ledgers may be seen to agree. They will agree. That is the purpose of the office.

THE CLERK: So ordered. This excerpt ends at page 61, mid-sentence, as all excerpts of this cause do. For the keeper's practice in the adjacent building — the room where a found item is held until the right name is called, where a coat of five globes was once filed intact and legible — the court directs readers to Claim No. 1967-D, Hall C Concession Desk and Lost Property Office, which keeps its own verbatim discipline. The court further observes that Lot 88 of the Trophy Room Sale — the 2002 World Cup consignment of Korea and Japan, with the keeper's brass ledger of 128 goals — went down at 9:26, the same minute the court checks its own line through the glass, and the court finds this either coincidence or habit, and is content with either. And for the small bench a sea away that reads a book in nine days and keeps its green ledger word for word — The Innes Bench, Newsletter of the Magsaysay Crime Fiction Society, No. 41 — the court notes that its rule of asking first would have spared this courtroom a decade. Filed under the almanac's open day, at the front of the general record, where the door is not locked from the inside.
