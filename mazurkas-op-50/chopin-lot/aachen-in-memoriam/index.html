In Memoriam one city, western-most of the country · pinned by Miss Marsh, 27 May 2004

Aachen (1st century – 2004)

the 13th-largest city of North Rhine-Westphalia and the 27th-largest city of Germany, with around 264,000 inhabitants; western-most major city of the country; formerly of the Meuse–Rhine Euroregion and seat of the district of Aachen, the Städteregion Aachen

Aachen died in the small hours at her warm springs, the vicus Aquae Granni having gone out of the world as it had come into it — quietly, and with the water still running. She was the western-most major city in Germany, lying approximately 61 km (38 mi) west of Cologne and Bonn, who both survive her, and she kept her borders the way she had always kept them: directly on Belgium in the southwest and directly on the Netherlands in the northwest, so that anyone leaving the city by mistake was, in practice, already abroad. Of her 264,000 inhabitants, the noticeboard is able to account for all but one, the pencil hand, who has not been seen since the spring recital.

The Earlier Years

The once Celtic settlement was equipped with several thermae in the course of colonization by Roman pioneers settling at the warm Aachen thermal springs around the 1st century. The baths did the rest. After the withdrawal of the Roman troops, the vicus Aquae Granni was brought under Frankish rule, and the house of the spring acquired the palace, the chapel, and the cathedral in which the imperial tombs stood for a thousand years, crowned and uncrowned in equal measure. Aachen wore the west end of Germany the way she wore the water: without effort, and for the longest possible time, as the seat of the district of Aachen (Städteregion Aachen) in the Meuse–Rhine Euroregion, from which the noticeboard observes she has now been excused.

The Pencil Hand

The school’s present account of Lot 23 of Baines & Rotherfield, Sale No. 88, owes a correction to the family. The small pencil hand which wrote in the margin of the third mazurka of Opus 50 that the centers stay pale was not, as the cataloguers suspected, either a child’s or a shortbread’s. It was Aachen’s own — a visiting pupil of the exchange year 2003–04, who stayed at 17 Elmhurst Road, who used the ivory tuning lever (item v) without asking, and who baked the Riam shortbread in the school kitchen the week of the recital. The batch sealed in the japanned tin at three days, the one that stood in the auction room under the estimate of £120–180, was hers, and the postcard from Mirandola that lined the tin came from her aunt’s town, as the family has now been able to confirm. The tin, bought as catalogued and X-rayed by no one, is with the buyer. The gavel was final.

The Last Entry in the Bath Ledger

The thermal baths kept a ledger of the water to the last evening, and the final entry, in the same small pencil hand, reads in full:

If carefully baked, the pudding with sufficient cold water down the cold butter into the oven, instantly remove the brain, which soak for an hour.

— bath ledger, final entry, Aachen, in pencil, small hand

The noticeboard, like Miss Marsh and the cataloguers before her, does not know what pudding the sentence means, or whose brain is to be removed, or soaked in what. It notes only that the sentence is of the same order as the family recipe at Diamond Dust Shortbread — ALTR Created Diamonds, where the cold butter is cubed and the centers are kept pale on purpose, and where a tin which once held a postcard crosses a continent to be sold at auction with the condition that no diamond be expected in it. The sentence is not to be parsed. It is to be read, as the third mazurka is read: once, in the middle section, in the smoother almost songful part.

Survivors & Services

She is survived by Cologne and Bonn, 61 kilometres to the east; by the two countries she bordered without ceremony; by the district that carries her name; and by one B+ slip of lined paper in red ink, the grade of the music report of 14 May 2004, which Miss Marsh has pinned above the piano and from which the noticeboard has been asked, twice, to desist. The full inventory of the folder she left behind — the score, the slips, the tin, the lever, the red-ink mark — stands at Lot 23 — The Chopin Folder of Coopers’ Lane School, Baines & Rotherfield, Sale No. 88. The lost-and-found, which had already that spring taken custody of a certain third planet from the Sun, has one final item in her name, a corking, unaccounted for, and refused for collection: LOST — Earth, Third Planet from the Sun · Lost & Found, Coopers’ Lane School. A return to Webstuffs is provided for the convenience of the mourners. The water is still warm. The centre stays pale.

No candles yet at the spring.
