Situations Vacant

Editor.

THE CHAIR OF THE EDITORSHIP, one, at the magazine that has been published every week since July 1828. Duties: politics, culture, a word on the current affairs, and the keeping of the arts pages. The office is conservative in tone and, as a matter of record, has often been a platform to high office in the Conservative Party in the United Kingdom; its past editors include Boris Johnson (1999–2005) and the late Ian Gilmour, among other former cabinet members, and applications from the young are invited on the strength of that history alone. Salary: the position itself, plus the standing of the desk. Reply to Letter E.

One (1) Critic of Opera.

MUST attend the performance, be in the house before the orchestra tunes, and file the verdict by Wednesday. Books, music, film and television also required; the arts pages are full, and the pages are under no impression that they shall be reduced. A reference to a reviewer of soufflés will be looked on as an advantage. Reply to Letter A.

Candling Assistant, dark room.

WILL be wanted, days four, nine and fourteen, to hold the vessel to the warm light and read the vascular map off the surface without breaking the seal. The full specification is on file with the patent summary of application 16/728,441, The Most Perfect Thing, and the assistant is expected to have read it; the examiner's line — the light tells you where the vessel is, but the rock tells you the story — is pinned above the door. One candle, an hour; the thumb over the large end. Reply to Letter K.

For Sale

Shell Drawer, one (1), the inventor's.

LANCASHIRE, complete with the entire improvement history of the most perfect thing, kept in order of the week in which each specimen was found — every variation, every failed hatch, every perfect one. The candling room is still warm and the buttermilk is in the dining room where the keeper sits when the light is lit. Priced down to make room for the soufflé; particulars in the patent summary, Serial No. 16/728,441. The drawer is offered with its reputation and without its eggs.

Receiver, second-hand.

FROM a Class 4B pupil of Coopers’ Lane School, who has since learned that a satellite tells you where you are and the rock tells you the story. Works; was pointed once at a constellation of over thirty satellites from a project of ten billion euros, and once at the coast. The term report on Galileo, Positioning & Navigation accompanies the machine and was marked B+ in red ink. The rock is not included; the rock is there. Reply to Letter R.

Strip, clipped, one (1).

OF the classified columns of the Lagan Ledger, from Entry No. 27 of the weather almanac, weather-bitten, in which the weather has nothing to do with the weather and which reads from the top of the strip to the bottom without a pause. This office will exchange the strip for a barometer reading from the valley and an open day at Stormont; see Entry No. 27, Arable Land. The exchange is proposed in the spirit of the columns, which are the only columns anywhere that do not say what they say.

Subscription, 107,812 of them.

THE average circulation as at December 2023, Australia not being counted. A representative for Australia has applied for the number, and the application is pending at the back of the drawer, where it has kept since the count was taken. One more subscription will make the number round and the magazine will not claim that the rounding is in the reader's favour. No; the magazine will claim nothing.

Lost & Found

Lost: one (1) air cell.

SELF-READING, its volume growing as the water content of the albumen evaporates through the pores, a hygrometer visible to the naked eye when the vessel is held to a candle. Last seen at the small end of a hen's egg, day eighteen, drawing a bead of air against the inner shell. The finder is asked to return it to the vessel, from which it was never intended to be separated; reward, a candling, days four, nine and fourteen as the reader prefers.

Found: the first number, July 1828.

UNCLAIMED for one hundred and ninety-seven years. The paper notes, for the benefit of any competitor who may be reading, that it is strictly and to this day the oldest surviving magazine in the world, and that the find is therefore not a copy but a fact. It is kept in the archive, where it does not read, and it is offered here for viewing by appointment, one reader at a time, the thumb over the large end.

Lost: one (1) opinion, conservative.

ON the state of the nation, three hundred words, last seen at the back page between the arts pages and the crossword. It was a good one and it is wanted back. The magazine, which is politically conservative and has never said so more clearly than in this notice, will pay in reprints. If the opinion should turn up in Australia, the same rule applies as to the subscription: the representative has applied, and the application is pending.

Found: the candling room itself.

WHICH had stood in the dining room of the field office at Lancaster, and which the inventor's counsel has since relocated, as the pendency of the application made the old room inconvenient. The new venue is the kitchen column below; the light remains the same, which is the only point of the method. The summary of the specification from which the method was drawn remains on file at Serial No. 16/728,441, and the class that argued the other side of the same argument remains on file at Coopers’ Lane School, Class 4B.

The Kitchen & Small Claims

The Candling Room Dining, a soufflé.

FROM the inventor's dining room, the room where the keeper sits when the light is lit. The vessel of the specification, emptied of its occupant on the twenty-first day; the whites whipped to soft peaks; the yolk folded over as prior art; the acid bath reduced to a drizzle, the dry dock preheated, and the whole put up under the warm light at the small hours and left to stand without being opened, in the manner of a seal. Five minutes’ notice; the oven does not wait on the examiner. What is claimed as novel, over the soufflé, and _serve it immediately_. No improvements will be filed: the prior art is 115 million years old, and the soufflé is one use only.

Notices.

OLDEST SURVIVING MAGAZINE IN THE WORLD — enquires; the enquiry is respectfully referred to the archive, which holds the first number and will not argue the point. Politics and culture remain the principal subject areas; alongside the columns and features on current affairs, the magazine contains arts pages on books, music, opera, film and television, and the classifieds are run in the same house style, small type, and no apology. Clippings exchanged: the Lagan Ledger, the field manual chapters, and the term reports of Coopers’ Lane School, whose candling room has been kept on the exchange list since the B+ was marked. For the general file, see the front page.
