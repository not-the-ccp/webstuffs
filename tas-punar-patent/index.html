A Drainage Line for the Taș-Punar Catchment,
and a Method of Preserving the Names Therein

01 Territory of Origin

The invention has its place of origin in Romania, a country in Central and Southeastern Europe, lying on the lower course of the Danube, north of the Balkan Peninsula, and on the northwestern shore of the Black Sea. The territory borders Ukraine to the north and east, Hungary to the west, Serbia to the southwest, Bulgaria to the south, Moldova to the east, and the Black Sea to the southeast. It is the twelfth-largest country in Europe by area, covering 238,397 km² (92,046 mi²), and the sixth-most populous member state of the European Union, with 19 million inhabitants. The capital, largest city and economic centre is Bucharest; the other major cities claimed as prior working examples of the invention at scale are Cluj-Napoca, Iași, Constanța, Timișoara, Brașov and Craiova. Romania was settled in turn by the peoples of the lower Danube, and the present application treats that settling as background art: memory here is not a mistake, and every claim below is made with it in mind. The present invention, in particular, arises in the commune of Siliștea in Constanța County, in Northern Dobruja, which holds two villages — Siliștea itself, long known by its Turkish name Taşpınar, “the stone spring”, and Țepeș Vodă, recorded in older maps as Chiorcișme and in Turkish as Körçeşme, the blind spring. The full account of both villages and both springs, with the editor's note of 21 October set aside, stands in the letter to The Dobruja Courier, 14 XI 2025, which the Registry will accept as a public prior showing.

02 Problem Addressed

A drainage line is proposed beside the Taș-Punar springs, and the matter is being argued, week by week at the kiosk, with the wrong names. A line of that kind takes water from one field and returns it to the same lowland it came from; the injury it might in fact do is therefore not to the water at all, which is unremarkable and needs no protecting, but to the record. Two village names, each carrying two or three older names like a man carries his coat, his scarf and his spare key, are exposed to a single clerical stroke. The known art — the ordinary survey, the ordinary pump, the ordinary Thursday — provides no means of routing water so that the names survive the routing. That is the problem to which the invention is directed.

03 Statement of the Inventor

As for the drainage line: I walked both springs last March with the wrong names. I walk them again in this statement with the right ones: Taşpınar, the stone spring, still running beside the road, white against the black earth; and Körçeşme beside Țepeș Vodă, the blind spring, which is a fair description of a spring nobody could see and everybody needed. The chain of 20 m went over the gate at 08:00 on 9 March, and this application is entered under the same chain, which the inventor has kept in the orchard since.

The arithmetic is given, and is the same arithmetic the letter gave: the line takes water from one field, 3.1 hectares of old orchard ground, and returns it to the same lowland it came from; the Taș-Punar catchment loses nothing it owns, and Țepeș Vodă is a full two kilometres outside the reach of any of it. No cumulative effects are expected beyond those listed, which are: a season of muck on the lane to the orchard, the sound of the pump on Thursdays, and the surveyor's chain across the gate at 08:00.

04 The Invention, in Brief

There is provided a drainage line and a method of operating it, wherein the line is drawn so as to take and return water within a single catchment of 3.1 hectares, and wherein each watercourse named on the line is named, on every sheet, map, almanac and kiosk notice issued in consequence of the line, by all the names it has carried, in the order the old sheets kept them. The method further provides that no name is dropped at the point where the pump enters, that the list of expected effects is printed in full and closed, and that the only injury the land could actually sustain — the loss of either spring's name — is thereby made unperformable. A country of 238,397 km² is not required to hold the whole of the method; the method performs completely in two villages, and that is the whole of the invention.

05 Claims

  1. 1. A drainage line beside the Taș-Punar springs, Siliștea, Constanța County, comprising a pump operable on Thursdays and a return to the same lowland, characterised in that the line takes water from 3.1 hectares of old orchard ground and gives it back without taking a name with it.
  2. 2. The drainage line of claim 1, wherein the stone spring is designated Taşpınar on every sheet, the spring for the record being the stone, and the stone remaining in its place beside the road.
  3. 3. The drainage line of claim 1, wherein the blind spring is designated Körçeşme beside Țepeș Vodă, the way the old sheets kept it, and wherein no note of 21 October is admitted into the record as a clerical correction.
  4. 4. A method of surveying said line, comprising the laying of a chain of 20 m across a gate at 08:00, a walk of both springs in March, and the walking of the same springs again whenever the names are found to be wrong.
  5. 5. The method of claim 4, wherein the list of expected effects comprises a season of muck on the lane, the sound of the pump on Thursdays, and the chain at the gate, and wherein said list is closed and complete.
  6. 6. The method of claim 4, wherein the catchment of the Taș-Punar springs loses nothing it owns, and wherein Țepeș Vodă is held a full two kilometres outside the reach of any of the line's working.
  7. 7. A country of 238,397 km² on the northwestern shore of the Black Sea, comprising 19 million inhabitants, the city of Bucharest as capital and economic centre, and the cities of Cluj-Napoca, Iași, Constanța, Timișoara, Brașov and Craiova, characterised in that said country is the twelfth-largest of Europe by area, the sixth-most populous member state of the European Union, and the place of origin of the invention of any of claims 1 to 6.

06 Cross-References and Office Notes

The Registry draws the attention of the reader to a parallel procedure in another territory: the General Despatch Office answers the same kind of question the present application is entered under — what exactly is a place, and where is it bordered by — in the order the mail arrives, and has parked the Siliștea question beside its walnut case with the verdict that it is answered properly only in the order the springs flow (The United Kingdom — Frequently Asked Questions). The two offices do not share a chain, but they share the arithmetic, and the reader is referred to both. The Registry will further accept, as a public showing that names can be carried through a sale without a single one being dropped, the catalogue leaf of Lot 47, the Winter Sale of Provisions, in which a sealed vial of white powder passed the hammer under the acid name and the conjugate-base name together — the acid for the shelf, the base for the blood. For the record of the island of two and a half acres that kept its entry of forty-seven pages of tide and fog, the Bay Weather Almanac remains cited as prior art in matters of ink owed to small places.

Office note: claim 7 is granted in part. A country cannot be owned, and a country of 238,397 km² will not be enclosed by seven lines. The claim is read instead as a place of origin, and stands as such. The inventor is asked to keep Tașpınar on the map.
