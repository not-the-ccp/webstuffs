LOT 714

Angela Eiter

The papers, chalk, and tape of Angela “Angy” Eiter, of Arzl im Pitztal — one of the strongest competition and sport climbers of her generation, offered whole from the keeping of Station No. 714.

Description

Angela Eiter was born on 27 January 1986 in Arzl im Pitztal, at the head of the valley where the station keeps its observations. The lot records a career of unusual evenness: in competition lead climbing she took the IFSC World Cup three times in succession, 2004 to 2006, and four IFSC World Championships in the same discipline. The 2011 season is documented page by page, and it is in that season that she recorded her 25th World Cup win and her 42nd podium — the station log for the afternoon of the 42nd reads, in the old hand, clouds clearing, excellent friction, name stands. The lot carries her further down the wall: in 2017 she became the first woman to climb a route graded 9b (5.15b), La Planta de Shiva, in a season the almanac marks as hotter and drier than the mean, and in 2020 the first woman to complete the first free ascent of a 9b route, Madame Ching. A climber of that line is a climate in herself: sudden, inclined to hold its line, clear by evening.

Comprising

  • The Pitztal Papers, 1994–2021: three ring-binders of season notes, in which the 2004–2006 World Cup titles occupy a single neat shelf and four World Championship programmes, spines uncracked.
  • One competition chalk bag, Imst, with the original 2011 label still legible; contents as described, not weighed, and not to be shaken before the rostra open.
  • The red tape of the 42nd podium, 2011, coiled in its box, plus the 25 wins' worth of it before, sorted by season and tied in threes.
  • A weathered entry from the station almanac for 5 September 2003 — her first route at eight, her first 9a at seventeen — filed against the observation 12 km/h north-east, nineteen degrees, dry rock, excellent friction, the almanac in full at the front shelf of the Eiter Almanac.
  • Two route cards for the 9b season: La Planta de Shiva, Fontainebleau, 2017, and Madame Ching, first free ascent, 2020, the latter with the first day's tape still adhered inside the back cover, which the catalogue advises bidders to leave alone.
  • One pinch of cinnamon, measured, from the tin that travelled west in 1894 — the almanac's own tin — kept in its paper for the thunder-cake, since the cake and the record, the editor held, are both things that must not be baked twice.

Provenance & Condition

From the keeper of Station No. 714, in whose farmhouse the name first appears, Tyrolean parish register, 1723, beside the note that the hay was brought in dry. The papers were kept in the same room as the station's glass plates, and the binders carry the faint smell of chalk and nitrate that the house gives to everything left in it past a decade. The binders are sound; the chalk bag has one repaired seam in saddle leather; the tape, as tape does, has begun to curl at its oldest ends. The station's verdict, entered in the ledger before the sale: condition of the rock, excellent. The consignor has asked that the lot stay complete, on the ground that a career, like a roof, is held up by everything in it.

The same consignment sends a second lot from the same tin. For a novel that once held up a schoolhouse roof: Lot 27, the Bell Creek Township Papers, from the tin that travelled west in 1894. It is catalogued separately, at the Bell Creek Township lot, where the carpenter's whale book cost a Nebraska district two Saturdays, and where the tin's westward journey of 1894 is traced to the door of a Burt County schoolhouse.
