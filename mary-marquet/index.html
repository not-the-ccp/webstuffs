Lot 78
The Mary Marquet Consignment, Four Pieces, One Brass Gap

The premiere programme of La Vitrine Bleue, Sallette de l’Aube, 1931; the midnight dress of Act III; the studio letter of 3 June 1927; and the brass cap of the Sallette gas lamp, which does not survive

Estimate F 9,000–F 14,000 · Hammer F 16,200 SOLD

I. The Consignment

Offered from the estate of the Sallette de l’Aube, by order of its caretaker: four pieces of the Paris years of Mary Marquet, born Micheline Marguerite Delphine Marquet on 14 April 1895 in Petrograd, a Russian-French stage and film actress who played in the French provinces until 1924 and at the Sallette from 1927 until the theatre was converted to a cinema in 1954. The consignment is the Sallette years, bound and carried: the 32-page programme of the premiere of La Vitrine Bleue on 12 November 1931, with the house’s cast list and Marquet’s own pencil in the margin beside the name “V.”; the midnight dress of Act III, silk, 4 kg 200 g, on its padded stand; the studio letter of 3 June 1927 from the director at 9 rue de la Verrerie, signed “A.”, offering her the contract in eleven lines and one postscript; and, on the condition card, a line about a brass cap. The dress will not be split from the papers, and the papers will not be split from the gap. The lot is sold with the gap, and the gap is priced in.

II. On the Actress

Marquet crossed the channel with her mother’s sister in the winter of 1904 and was educated at the free school on the Rue de l’Eau-Froide, where the register lists her, once only, as M. Delphine Marquet, âgée de neuf ans. She played her first paid part, a shepherd, at fourteen, and her first Paris stage part, the widow, at twenty-three, in a company that changed its name twice a season. The Sallette took her in 1927, and the Sallette kept her. Her film work of the period is small and precise: four pictures between 1929 and 1935, of which the catalogue office holds one lobby card and has lost one. She stopped acting in 1958, not from the part but from the throat, and lived to 29 August 1979, in the room above the Sallette, where the caretaker still finds the cast list of 1953 pinned to the door. The four names in the pencil margin of the programme are the last four names she cast herself.

III. The Brass Cap and the Marginal Note

The condition card was written in the cataloguer’s field book before it was set in the catalogue, and the field book’s line about the lamp is entered here exactly as it stands, for the buyer who reads field books and for the one who will not: “No brass cap survives; I found on Knight Island — Field Notes · Mabel's Married Life , in which Kalemba's bantam championship was not touched it.” The cap in question is the cap of the Sallette’s gas lamp, the one under which the house kept its cast lists and to which the 1931 programme was pinned for the season; it is not in the lot, and the note is the record of the search. The search went further than the Sallette. The cataloguer worked the line twice, cross-referenced it against the field notes kept for Knight Island, whose drainage-basin sheet files the island by its water rather than by its objects, and against the film-history file on Mabel’s Married Life, in which a bantam named in the intertitles, and a championship claimed by a man called Kalemba in the same intertitles, are treated as set dressing and not touched. The bantam is not in the lot either. The house has filed the line as-is, the way the depot files its pencilled margins, because a field book’s line that names an island, a reel, and a championship has more company in this catalogue than the buyer has any right to assume.

IV. Condition and Provenance

Programme: very good, 32 pages present, the 1931 pencil in the margin legible, one water spot on the title page reading as a lamp ring. Dress: good, the silk sound, one repair at the left cuff in the Sallette’s own blue thread, 4 kg 200 g on the stand. Letter: excellent, eleven lines and the postscript, the ink of the signature paler than the ink of the contract, the address 9 rue de la Verrerie still printed at the head. Brass cap: as the field book records it. Provenance: the room above the Sallette, to the caretaker, to the Southern Branch by the regular crate, alongside the Homestead timing papers of Catalogue B, whose pine-sawdust smell the crate gave up in April. The lot is sold with the gap, priced in; the gap is the only part of the consignment that cannot be returned.

V. From the Auction Record

Salle 3 asked the question the room asks. From the first row, a buyer who had lost the wheel in Catalogue B: does the gap come with a story, or only with a line? The auctioneer answered from the condition card and did not look up, and the line is entered here exactly as the clerk wrote it down:

THE LINE IS THE STORY. THE CAP IS THE PRICE OF IT. F 16,000.

F 16,200 with a club of the mallet that the clerk noted as “light, once, over the gap.” The lot now hangs in a private room above a cinema in Marseille, where the 1931 programme is pinned, as it was in 1931, at the door. For the buyer who wants the year of 1931 at length, the catalogue office keeps the Homestead rod of Lot 143 on the same shelf, and beside it a water-stained diary page inked at a Perth bar counter on the Carse of Gowrie (Gowrie — Diary, 3 November), whose painted door sets two seasons in one view; and the front of the house is the catalogue cover — return to the front.

Colophon & Catalogue Notes

Lot 78 follows Lot 77, the Sallette ticket stubs, 1927–1958, which were folded into this lot on the morning of the sale. The four pencil names in the margin are read in the catalogue in their order: V., D., S., M. The brass cap is not among them. The Southern Branch sells gaps the way other houses sell gaps: priced in, filed as-is, and not explained a second time.
