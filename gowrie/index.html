Diary · Tuesday, 3 November, 5:40 a.m. · The King's Arms, Perth

Gowrie

Weather: mist on the Carse by nine, gone by ten; rain from Dundee after four. Wrote standing up at the bar counter.

I woke before the light and could not sleep on it, so I have taken the ink to the place instead. We are in Gowrie — Gobharaidh, the maps in Gaelic, the bar bill in English — which is to say we are standing in one of the oldest pieces of Scotland still on the ground, one of the original provinces of the Kingdom of Alba. A province. The word does most of the work for me. We have a provincial capital, which happens to be the city of Perth, and the city has not quite decided whether it wants to be told this, because Perth is proud, and proud cities take provincial history as a mild slur. I will take it as a compliment. The province covered the eastern part of what later became Perthshire, sat to the immediate east of Atholl — the moors I walked on last Tuesday, where I have a splinter still — and it originally included all the ground around Perth, Scone among it, the old royal site where the crowns were kept and the kings were buried. That eastern country was later peeled off and named Perthia on its own, which I can only report as a matter of fact: the province grew a district the way a house grows a lean-to, and the district now calls itself independent.

What has stuck to Gowrie, in the way that sticks in places, is the Carse. The Carse of Gowrie is the low country south of the Sidlaw Hills, running east from Perth to Dundee, and it is the whole argument for the drive. At eleven I walked the length of a green lane between the barley stubble and the river bend and watched a kestrel hold itself motionless above a fence that I could not afterwards find. The Sidlaws were a blue-grey wall at the northern edge of the whole view, and beyond the wall, on the far side, I could have sworn the hills were already in spring, heather fresh and light, while the Carse below me was turning its November gold. Two seasons, one window. It is exactly what the painting in the bar says.

There is a painted door behind the bar at the King's Arms — a hand-painted view of the Carse, mist doing its best, a burn running out of the frame — and under it, in a small gold hand that the landlord says is older than the licence, is written the whole explanation of the place, word for word:

Set in its view — northern spring, southern autumn.

I had read it on the way in and understood nothing. Standing in it, I understand it entirely. The painter did not mean a view of a season; he meant the view the Carse keeps set in its frame all year, the same two seasons stacked, the north green and the south gold, the hillside out of time with the plain. The landlord, a tall man who pours as if the whisky were a witness, told me his grandfather kept a card in the door with a bet on it: north first or south first. “Nobody’s won,” he said. “That’s the trick. You’d have to be in two places at once.” He is right, and I have put my name to the back of the card.

The bus back goes east, past the old road signs, past the bend where the river turns to look at its own province. Scone is south-west of Perth and I did not get to it; there is a bell tower and a shadow of a palace and I have already decided to come back for it, which is a provincial kind of ambition — to keep a queue of ancient things in the east of Perthshire — but I would do it again. Gowrie will keep it out of the weather. It has been keeping things out of the weather since before there was a name for the keeping.

The bar test — five questions, ink optional

The landlord insists every traveller who touches the card answers five questions before a second pour. He keeps the scoring in pencil. If the page loads its pencil (JavaScript), the questions below will mark themselves; if not, the answers are all in the diary above, in order, and you may keep the sheet.

  1. 1. In Scottish Gaelic, Gowrie is…

    Gobharaidh — the maps say so, and so does the bar bill.

  2. 2. Gowrie lay to the immediate east of which province?

    Atholl — the moors I walked, and the splinter.

  3. 3. Which ancient royal site was originally inside Gowrie?

    Scone — crowns kept, kings buried, queue formed.

  4. 4. The area around Perth was later detached as…

    Perthia — the lean-to that calls itself independent.

  5. 5. The Carse of Gowrie runs south of which hills, east to Dundee?

    The Sidlaw Hills — the blue-grey wall with the spring on the far side.

Pencil not yet touched. 0 of 5 answered.

From the Carse road: the reserve at Out of the Woods sends its mist north on October winds, and we send our gold south to join it; and for the year of the door-painting in full, the Southern Branch keeps Lot 78 — the Marquet consignment on the same shelf, gap priced in. The whole road home is posted at the front of the site.
