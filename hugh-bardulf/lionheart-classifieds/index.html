Royal Notice · To be read once and obeyed twice

THE KING IS ABSENT. His Majesty Richard, styled the Lionheart, or C&oeur; de Lion (in the Old Norman French, Quor de Lion), because of his reputation as a great military leader and warrior, King of England from 1189, Duke of Normandy, Aquitaine and Gascony, Lord of Cyprus, Count of Poitiers, Anjou, Maine and Nantes, and overlord of Brittany at various times during the same period, has ridden off to chase his own name, as his father before him rode off to chase his. He was the third of five sons of Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine and was therefore not expected to become king, but his two elder brothers predeceased their father, and by the age of sixteen he had taken command of his own army, putting down a certain count of Anjou who had begun to speak too plainly. The household in the island is left in the hands of the wardens and the king's justices; the ransom account of one hundred and fifty thousand marks, discharged in 1193, remains pending with God; and all writs run as before. Nothing in this notice is a cancellation.

To Let

CASTLE, CORRIDOR, ONE COLUMN, PENDING. Kenilworth, Warwickshire: the gate ledger keeps the same corridor in the dream that the glossary keeps for its justice; the lye was cured in advance and whatever was corrosive in the autumn has already been ruled in solution. Let to a steward with three reigns in the cuffs of his robe. The museum placard for the place is No. 1067, and the annex will show it to any dreamer; see the entry kept at Kenilworth Castle, placard No. 1067. Rent in service, payable in bell-strokes; see also the entry below, Wanted, and the shelf where the tenant is shelved at the Dreamer's Glossary, Entry 53.

For Sale

CYPRUS, ONE LORDSHIP, FRESHLY RE-TAKEN. Recaptured 1191 and held ever since; title descends by the sword, which is the only title this sheet has ever trusted. The lord of it rides on a deck somewhere between the Levant and the ledger, and the island is advertised accordingly. Serious inquirers only; the seller is a great military leader and warrior by reputation, and the reputation is load-bearing. No haggling at the gate; the gate was counted in the ransom.

Lost

AN ARMY, TAKEN INTO THE FIELD WHEN THE MASTER WAS SIXTEEN. Answered to the name of its own; put down a certain count of Anjou in 1173, then carried its master through the eyre, the circuit and the coin-counting. Last seen mustering south of the wall of its own name. Any person or province in possession of it is charged to return it at once, as all provinces eventually are. The reward is a title, which will be struck, mailed, and delivered by overlord; the overlord of Brittany will be the first to know.

Found

ONE SENTENCE, WASHED UP IN A BOTTLE, QUAY AT AIGUES-MORTES. Found 12 of this month, floating in with the tide of a drove that nobody has billed for. The clerk's gloss stands as follows: it is the bill of a drove of whales, set down above the deck by the States-General in some A.D. of their choosing, for two strangers who were crossing and have not yet been named. The paper will not translate it and the sea will not amend it; the sentence is kept, word for word, in the ledger's fourth column, pending the naming:

If two strangers crossing the deck was not till the drove is set down above as Duodecimoes are infallibly whales, by the States-General in A.D.

Any person able to name the two strangers may collect the bottle at the counter, counterweight included. The drawer it is filed in survives the night; the name once entered is not erased.

Wanted

A STEWARD, OR A JUSTICE, OR THE SAME MAN, WHO IS ONE BELL-STROKE FROM BEING BOTH. The terms are the whole of the terms: hold a charge of office, be named a traitor for it by the younger brother's rebellion, and be restored before the stroke finishes falling. The post was filled in this reign by a man of tall build who served three kings, and the post is still held; the vacancy advertised here is for the next time, which the glossary promises will come. The candidate's name is Hugh Bardulf, also Bardolf; the column in his ledger is open on purpose, and the claims lodged against it are the ones below. No application necessary. The application is the serving.

Carriers & Couriers

FIRE, FOG, MIDNIGHT. A resident audio technician at the Lodge at the Gate receives letters in the fog months, October to March, and keeps the trail register for the middle mile: forty-six speakers, thirty-two repeats, one horn at its fifteen-second interval, matched beat for beat. The field diary where the books of the place are kept, the Lodge at the Gate's field diary, Entry No. 14, files under the number fifty-three the loose leaf that names our steward; the two of them keep the same books, this sheet has ruled. Postage: one repeat, or one loop, held in fog, without being finished.

Notices

TO THE VASSALS OF THE YOUNGER BROTHER. John has rebelled against his elder brother Richard, and this notice is the only notice. The denunciation falls on the vassal and not only on the rebel; in this reign it has cost exactly one bell-stroke, and the writs were countermanded before the bell finished its fall. The court went back to the coin-counting. Do not test the countermand; it is the one column of the kingdom that closes of itself. A sister paper keeps the same practice on the other side of the sea, at The Cairene Ledger, No. 9,813,000, where a ledger line is also kept word for word and a cow named Moti is also pending.

In Memoriam

HENRY II, THE FIRST KING OF THE CORRIDOR, d. 1189. Father of five sons, of whom two elder predeceased him and the third now reigns; served by the same steward, in the same corridor, at the same ledger. In the glossary's reading, the first king never left the corridor, the second has already passed through, and the third is served well or not at all. His coin is still counted in the chamber behind the hall. The obituary is short because the column was never closed; this is not an apology.

Rates for Insertion

Notices royal, the width of the banner; notices of vassals, one column, one bell-stroke; sentences from the sea, kept word for word, free of charge and pending forever. Advertisements are set in the type of the kingdom, which has not once missed its hour. The name once entered is not erased. All accounts, including the ransom, remain open on purpose.

To the reader: if the King is absent and the column is pending, serve the third king well. The bell-stroke will come; the countermand will come first, as it did for the steward in Entry 53, and the corridor will open on schedule.
