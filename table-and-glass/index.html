YEREVAN MUNICIPAL COURT
CHANCERY OF THE HRAZDAN
14 MAR 2025

Yerevan Municipal Court  ·  Chancery of the Hrazdan  ·  Third Division

Case No. 1726 of 2024

Mkhitar AGHAJAN, plaintiff & keeper of the table,
versus
Hagop PETROSYAN, defendant & diner of Table 9

Excerpt from the verbatim record — the matter of the law of reciprocity, commonly called the Golden Rule, heard in open court on Friday 14 March 2025, at 6:40, and adjourned at 7:15 the same morning, before the Hon. Siranush Davtyan.

PLAINTIFF
Mkhitar Aghajan, of “The Pink Table,” corner of Tumanyan Street and Mashtots Avenue. Represented by himself and by one glass of coffee, still warm.

DEFENDANT
Hagop Petrosyan, of the Edendale lot, and a regular at Table 9 since 1984. Represented by himself and by one empty plate.

IN EVIDENCE
Exhibit C — a card pinned to the menu board for eleven years, read into the record word for word.

THE CLERK. The court is in session; the record is open. Minutes are kept, as is the local custom, by the Naga Listeners’ Club, and the ledger of this matter is kept at the usual speed — sixteen frames per second.

THE COURT. I will state the matter for the record. The plaintiff sues under the law of reciprocity — the rule in its positive or directive form, that one is to treat others as one would like to be treated, and in its negative or prohibitive form, that one is not to treat others in ways that one would not like to be treated. The charge: that on the Friday morning of 10 January 2025, at 6:40, the defendant left the plaintiff’s table without paying, having taken coffee and a piece of lavash and no khash. The plaintiff claims one bill, the dignity, and the garlic. Mr. Petrosyan, your plea?

THE DEFENDANT. Not guilty, Your Honor. I treated him exactly as I would like to be treated.

THE COURT. And yet you left without paying, Mr. Petrosyan.

THE DEFENDANT. Yes, Your Honor. His lavash was a brick. It was 6:40 on a Friday, the pot was still on the stove, and he gave me cold bread at the edge of the city while the city was still cool. I treated him the way he treated me.

THE COURT. That is not the law of this court. The ethics of reciprocity is an injunction on conduct: one is to reciprocate to others how one would like to be treated, and it is not a licence to return to them exactly what they have actually done. The measure is what you want for yourself, not what he did to you. Tit-for-tat is a different and older custom, and it is not before us this morning. The plaintiff may lead.

THE PLAINTIFF. I call Anoush Grigoryan.

THE CLERK. The witness is sworn.

THE PLAINTIFF. Ma’am, will you identify the card at Table 9?

THE WITNESS. I will. It was pinned to the menu board for eleven years. It reads — I will read it word for word — “Table and glass Eat khash on Friday mornings — cow's feet simmered all night, eaten with garlic and dried lavash while the city is still cool.”

THE COURT. This is the plaintiff’s house rule, in writing?

THE WITNESS. It is, Your Honor. And on a Friday at 6:40 that card was the whole city. The brandy house down the street was still closed, the fountains of Republic Square were still silent, and the only people on the street were the marshrutka drivers and my husband, who used to wait for bit parts on the Edendale lot for forty years before the lot became a garage.

THE DEFENDANT. Ma’am. Was the khash simmered all night?

THE WITNESS. All night.

THE DEFENDANT. And the garlic?

THE WITNESS. As much as he wanted.

THE DEFENDANT. And yet on the morning in question you will not deny —

THE WITNESS. — that the lavash was cold. I will not deny it, and I will not pretend otherwise. It is a fact of this record.

THE COURT. Both parties may have the record. The plaintiff first.

THE PLAINTIFF. Your Honor, he has sat at Table 9 every Friday for forty-one years. The rule runs both ways: what he wishes upon me, he wishes upon himself. I ask the court to hold him to the full bill.

THE DEFENDANT. Your Honor, I have paid that table in kind for forty-one years — with my chair, with my silence, with the stories I left on the menu board. On 10 January I paid in lavash. Cold bread is the oldest currency in the city, and it is the only one that was on the plate.

THE COURT. It is not the currency of this court. I have heard the rule in both its forms, and I find both of you in default. The defendant left a table where he would not wish to be left unpaid; the positive form does not allow it. The plaintiff served a cold bread he would not wish to be served; the negative form forbids it. The defendant is fined in the amount of one khash, and ordered to return to The Pink Table on Friday 21 March and eat the khash as the card directs, with garlic, while the city is still cool. Is that the record?

THE CLERK. It is the record.

THE COURT. The court is in recess.

Docket note, 21 March 2025: the defendant returned at 6:35, ate three plates, asked for the lavash by the kilo, and left the garlic plate with the marshrutka drivers. The ledger is closed at sixteen frames per second.
