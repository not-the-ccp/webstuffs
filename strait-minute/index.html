NO. 14

The Two Shores Club · est. 1962 · Kuala Lumpur (seat 1) · Kuching (seat 2)

The
Minute Book

Straits Circular · No. 14 · quarter beginning 1 July · set in Kuala Lumpur, sent to Kuching by the Thursday boat

From the Secretary's Desk

We write this circular from the second floor of the club room in Jalan Ampang, the windows open on the heat, the fan doing its three rotations of the argument, and we ask the Kuching table to read it slowly, because the quarter has been full and the ink is still going on the last page. Our club, as the new members now know and the old members pretend to know, keeps one country in its minutes the way other clubs keep a garden: Malaysia, that federal constitutional monarchy of thirteen states and three federal territories, divided by the South China Sea into the two halves we hold in our two chairs. The Peninsular table sits on Mainland Southeast Asia, with its land and sea borders with Thailand, Singapore, Vietnam, and Indonesia; the Kuching table sits on the island of Borneo, with its land borders on Brunei and Indonesia and its water borders on the Philippines and Vietnam. Two shores, one book, that is the whole rule of the club, and this quarter we kept the rule exactly.

Kuala Lumpur, the national capital and the largest city, remains the seat of the legislative branch and therefore the home of the book itself; the federal administration lives in Putrajaya across the water, and the members of the Peninsular table walk the stretch between the two every second Saturday, which is why the secretary's shoes are two sizes of tired.

The Quarter's Reckoning

  • 13 states, 3 federal territories, 2 shores — counted each opening, as is the rule
  • 6 meetings held, 1 postponed for the rain of 3 June
  • 2 new members: Salmah binti Omar (Kuching) and Mr. Edwin Chuah (George Town)
  • 1 walk, 1 letter to the archivist, 1 minute book signed

Treasurer's note: subscriptions to the year end stand at forty-one names, forty-one full payments, no arrears. The kettle, however, is behind.

On the New Minute Book, and One Line From the Printer

The old minute book, bound in 1974 by Mr. Idris at the bindery on Jalan Raja Chulan, has closed its last leaf, and the new one has come in from the printer at Petaling Jaya. It is bound in dark green vellum, stitched in the double order, and it carries the club's two chairs on the cover, one facing the other across a printed sea, which the bindery says it has never done before and which we commissioned exactly. The delivery note that came with it bore, in the clerk's small hand, one line that the committee read aloud at the 3 July sitting and entered into the record, and which we set here word for word, because a line set down in a club's minutes keeps its line: “Developed for the Minute Book was signed for.”

The committee's reading, which the chair then ruled carried with six votes to one, is this: the type had been developed for the Minute Book — the letters cut, the plates made, the green ink mixed to the 1962 strength — and the finished order had been signed for at the door of the bindery by the treasurer, who was not there, because the signature on the note is the signature of the clerk, who stands in for every absence in the club. The one dissenting vote was the Peninsular motion that the line be rephrased into a sentence anyone could read in a hurry. The chair ruled that a line signed for is a line kept, and the matter stood, and it stands here.

The Walk of 14 June — Peninsular Table

Forty-one of us walked the old harbour road at Port Klang before the rain closed in, in the company of the fish carts and one very patient goat, and the walk ended, as it always does when the quarter is honest, at the water, where the South China Sea makes the whole argument between our two shores plain in one minute: same tide, same book, two tables. Mr. Chuah, walking for the first time, kept the count of the states out loud under his breath — thirteen, he said, at the end, and no more — which is the closest the club comes to a cheer, and which we record as one.

Correspondence & the Outside Table

The Kuching table writes that the rain on the Borneo side has come early and the boat has run three times on the one tide, and that Salmah binti Omar's first entry in the minute book, dated 2 June, is one line long and signed twice, once with her name and once with her hand, and that this is permitted and is now the rule. We also send the reading of the quarter to the two tables that keep their books the way ours does, across a border or a sea and not without ceremony: the Bellman corner at No. 817, where a ledger line written on the back of a programme is read into the record every season, keeps its frequently asked questions, including the strange line No. 217, and has been good to our club for thirty years; and the book of the Dhaka Dawn Express, where a commission takes its decisions in daylight on a moving train and a margin note is signed by the lower berth, files its timetable and the notes set down in the margin of the book for us to compare each quarter, as we do ours. Both books keep the same rule this club keeps on the water: the whole of the line, in order, and not one more than the whole. The circular closes here, as No. 13 closed, with the kettle on and the fan on its third rotation. See you at the second Saturday, on your own shore.

Set down at Kuala Lumpur, read over at Kuching · M. R. Ibrahim, Secretary · D. P. Loke, Kuching Reader
