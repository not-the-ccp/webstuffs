2 Hazard identification

Cressa is classified as a Category 7 absent material: a documented entity with no verifiable residue at ground level. The principal hazards are epistemic rather than physical — confident speculation, excavation at the wrong grid square, and the slow corrosion of a name by a neighbouring village that adopted it.

  • H204 — Site unlocated; ground-truthing attempts have recovered no masonry, no keramic, and one contested boundary stone.
  • H307 — Prolonged engagement may cause attachment to a city that does not, so far as survey shows, sit where claimed.
  • H411 — Harmful to cartographic certainty: three competing sites have been proposed within eleven kilometres of each other since 1923.

WARNING  See Section 14 for transport and Section 16 for the survey team's field annotations, including lodging conditions.

4 First-aid measures

In case of inhalation of the founding story (sudden certainty that Cressa "obviously" stands at a named headland): remove the affected reader to open ground, administer a counter-map of the Ilıca district, and let them argue the question at least three times before conceding. If contact with the shoreline (the actual Black Sea, not the city): rinse with fresh water; the delta mud is fine-grained and will find any gap in waders. If swallowed (a 1960s guidebook listing Cressa as a fixed point): seek a librarian.

5 Fire-fighting measures

There is nothing to extinguish. In the unlikely event that a reputation fire breaks — a popular book naming Cressa's site with a straight face — do not feed it. Withdraw. Let it burn out. Historically, fires of this kind have twice moved the proposed site up to four kilometres.

6 Accidental release measures

If the city's name escapes containment into a school textbook, do not attempt to scrub it. Cressa is a good name and will keep moving. Contain the coordinates instead: any latitude-longitude pair attached to the name in error should be struck, not footnoted. The two coordinate proposals of 1974 and 1986 remain struck on the Office's master map and are reproduced, struck, in every reissue.

7 Handling and storage

Store the name Cressa in a register of Paphlagonian settlements, away from direct comparison with Sinope and Amaseia, which do have streets. Keep in a cool catalogue. The material is stable in print and unstable in the field: each visiting season, one new villager is found who swears the ruins are "up behind the olive rows," and the rows are different every time. Log such claims; do not chase them past the second day.

8 Exposure controls / personal protection

No occupational exposure limit can be set for an absent site. Field staff should wear standard surveying kit — boots, waders, sun cover — and one additional item: a notebook, ruled, in which claims and rejections are recorded in the same hand. The Office's standing rule is that the notebook, not the map, is the true record of a Cressa season.

10 Stability and reactivity

Stability: the name has outlasted its city by two millennia and shows no sign of fading. Unstable conditions: proximity to a village that wants a Roman past, proximity to a grant deadline. Incompatible materials: definitive map legends, the words "certainly" and "precisely", and any tide table taken without a year.

11 Toxicological information

The city itself is non-toxic. The conquering episode in the dossier carries the only real toxicity: Cressa was taken by Ziaelas, king of Bithynia, whose eastern expansions into Paphlagonia in the second century BCE redrew borders on a coast whose cities changed hands with the seasons. Handle the Ziaelas entry with the caution appropriate to a conquest: the victor's records survive, the city's do not, and the balance of evidence is therefore the shape of the victor's pen.

12 Ecological information

The actual ecology of the shore in question is rich and indifferent to the city: reedbeds, wintering waders, the Halys delta running out into a grey sea. Nothing here mourns Cressa. If a future survey locates the settlement, the finding should be treated as an ecological disturbance in the reverse direction — the ground disturbed by the question — and the site closed for one full season of bird passage before any open excavation.

13 Disposal considerations

There is nothing to dispose of. Struck maps, retired coordinate proposals, and superseded foundation narratives are retained, not destroyed: an absence is only useful if it has been witnessed. The Office disposes of nothing; it merely keeps the witness list current. The same principle of retention appears, in a less severe register, in the imprint's liner notes for Two Hemispheres, One Shot — a record of the annual reformulations of the influenza vaccine, 1999 to 2025, in which a lineage not isolated since March 2020 is kept on the programme out of caution, an absence held in the catalogue the way the Office holds Cressa: useful only because it has been witnessed.

14 Transport information

Cressa, in the form this sheet certifies, may be transported by post, by hand, or in memory. No quantity restrictions. For researchers preferring a sea route to the Paphlagonian coast, the Office recommends the regular Ilıca landing and notes, for the record, that the nearest reliable ferry schedule has been reprinted with annotations in the same office as our San Juan de Portugal build log — a long provisions list, a launch, and the general wisdom of anyone who has ever carried a coast on paper. For a comparable exercise in classifying a whole region as though it were a substance — hazard classes, first aid, and all — see the California safety data sheet, which the Office keeps as the gold standard of the genre.

15 Regulatory information

The site is regulated under general antiquities law with the unusual rider that no site is yet designated. Permits therefore cannot be applied for; the Office's advice to permit-seekers is to apply, be refused on grounds of absence, and use the refusal letter — which must by statute describe the location — as a data point. Twelve such letters have accumulated since 1988. They are the largest single body of evidence in the dossier.

16 Other information

Origin of this sheet: first drafted 1961 by the Office's predecessor desk; each revision appends, never deletes. The Meriones foundation story is retained in full because a city without a founder is harder to keep from being found in the wrong place — the story pins the name to a generation, and a generation is smaller than a guess.

Field annotations, season 2019 (lodging): the survey team was quartered that year in a new resort at the head of the bay, a decision the field report does not pretend to defend. Its closing entry reads: "The luxury feels wrong after Dhaka, but I'm not complaining." The annotator — who had spent the previous season assisting on the Asia campaign covered in the Mile Jedinak assistant coach diary — added beneath the line, in smaller ink: "We still walked the creek at 06:10. No wall. Mud, gulls, one stone that argued with us for forty minutes."

Review cycle: annually, or upon the discovery of a wall, whichever first. The Office keeps, as its one instructive counterexample, a place that is entirely on the ground and entirely on paper at once — the Bell Creek Township Papers of Burt County, Nebraska, offered as Lot 27 in the Hartwell & Gann auction catalogue, where a 1854 plat still describes the creek and a carpenter's whale novel once held up a school roof.
