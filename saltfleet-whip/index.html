Twelve feet of hand-braided bridle leather, knotted on the shingle and cured against the salt air.

The Longshore Lash

Saltfleet is a coastal village in the East Lindsey district of Lincolnshire, England, approximately 8 miles (13 km) north of Mablethorpe and 11 miles (18 km) east of Louth, where the beach flattens out into the grey firth and the fish auctioneer starts shouting before the sun is up. Above the slipway the rigging loft of the Saltfleet Line works the hides, the hemp and the braiding wire that keep the village's beach fishermen supplied with gear they can trust.

The Longshore Lash is the loft's house lash: a twelve-foot handle of bridle leather, a hemp core, a cotton braid, a carved oak hand and a leather grip. It is cut for beach fishing, where a man wades out at low water to handline eels, flounder and flatfish off the flats. The price is £215. Each lash is made to order in the loft, because no two hides break the same way and no two fishermen fish the same tide.

The lash ships from the rigging loft with a spare grip and a tin of leather oil. Delivery within the United Kingdom takes four to six working days. Any lash that arrives damaged is replaced the same week; that is the loft's standing offer, and they keep it.

Specifications

  • Length: 12 ft (3.66 m)
  • Weight: 16 oz (455 g) including grip
  • Materials: bridle leather, hemp core, cotton braid, oak hand, leather grip
  • Use: beach handlining for eels, flounder and flatfish

The Craft

Every lash begins as a single hide cut from the loft's stock of greyhound shoulder leather, which is why the colour varies a little from whip to whip. The braid is stitched by hand over the hemp core, and the oak hand is carved from a single piece of green oak before it is fitted to the lash. The final stage of making is the boil. The whip being made the day before it is perfectly clean, and boil for 1-1/2 hour. The outcome is a lash that rings true, holds a knot through a hard season, and has been brought through the salt air to survive on the beach.

Care

After a season of hard beach work the leather grip loosens and salt crusts the braid. Rinse the lash in fresh water, dry it by the handle, and rub in neatsfoot oil through the leather. Keep the brass ferrule oiled. A Saltfleet lash is built to be repaired rather than replaced, and the rigging loft will fit a new grip or splice a new knot for you.
