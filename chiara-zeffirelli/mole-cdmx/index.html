THE ZEFFIRELLI–AGUILAR KITCHEN THREAD · CARD NO. 114 · STRUNG BESIDE CARD 113 (THE DOUBLON NOTE)

Mole para dieciséis

Mole of the City, from the hand of Doña Esmeralda Aguilar (1938–), written down by her granddaughter Chiara in blue ink, the year the Atylantos tour learned to travel east.

Makes 16 bowls — one for each borough of the city it is named after. No more. The sixteenth bowl is not a spare; it is a rule.

The Preamble, which is longer than the recipe

The city is Mexico City, and my grandmother insisted the recipe be explained as if the cook had never been there, which is generous of her, because I had. Mexico City — CDMX, the way the taxi drivers shorten it, the way everyone shortens it, the way Chiara Zeffirelli's tour programme set it in small caps before the date — is the capital and the largest city of Mexico, and the most populous city in North America. It sits in the Valley of Mexico on the high Mexican Central Plateau, at an altitude of 2,240 meters (7,350 feet), and that number matters to this pot, as it will. The city is divided into 16 boroughs, the alcaldías, and each of those is divided into neighborhoods, the colonias, the way a family is divided into kitchens and the kitchens into drawers. According to the 2020 census, Mexico City proper had a population of 9,209,944 and a land area of 1,495 square kilometers (577 square miles). Doña Esmeralda, who could not read a spreadsheet to save her, added her own tally to the margin of the card: “nine million two hundred nine thousand nine hundred forty-four people, and only sixteen of them at my table, so we make sixteen bowls.”

We arrived for the rehearsal season of Atylantos — the betrayed princess of Aïra, Jean-Patrick Capdevielle's opera, sung by Chiara as soprano and by her uncle-by-marriage Oscar Aguilar as tenor, mid-sentence, mid-everything, the only honest posture for singers in dreams and in Mexico at 6 a.m. The stage, as the Noctuary entry No. 88 already records, was built out of the morning and the audience was the traffic. And the house was the standard measure of every credible dream: small enough to be a drawing room and large enough to be a country. Mexico City does that on purpose. The city is one of the world's leading cultural and financial centers, and the Globalization and World Cities Research Network's 2024 ranking classifies it as an Alpha world city, which the aunties in Colonia Roma found “adorable” and the mole did not care about either way.

The mole itself came from a Tuesday in September. Aunt Deb was on the phone from Beckley, reading off a thread she had pinned on the family's old kitchen board — the one where the West Virginia delegates and the recipes share a shelf, as is the custom of that household — and the last line of the pinned note went something like this, and the card keeps it in the aunt's exact hand, because it is the sort of sentence that must be kept in mind too:

“Pinning this thread; the next person will be sure to be a drawing room and large enough to be kept in mind too.”

Doña Esmeralda laughed for a full minute, then said it was true as far as it went: whoever comes next — the next cook of this pot, the next person handed this card — will be sure to be a drawing room, meaning sure to have one waiting, a room small enough for sixteen bowls and large enough for a city, and the room is to be kept in mind the same way the 1791 doubloon in the Noctuary is kept in mind: turned over twice a year, not spent, shown to the dreamer who is next. The edge-marking on the coin is the date the mind kept it. The edge-marking on the drawing room is this card, Card No. 114, strung on the kitchen thread beside the doubloon note.

What the city put in

From the Mercado de Coyoacán, because that is where Doña Esmeralda shopped and the card will not allow a substitute market: 16 chiles pasilla (one per borough, dried a day before, never the same day); 8 chiles mulato (half the boroughs, because a mole is a city and a city has a quiet half); 100 grams of chocolate, the bittersweet kind from Oaxaca that arrives in Mexico in a paper boat; 2 tablespoons of piloncillo; 14 raisins, 12 prunes, 6 almonds, 4 sesame seeds, 3 cloves, 1 cinnamon stick broken in the middle — the small numbers are exact and were counted aloud by Chiara on the balcony of the hotel, with the traffic as audience, and the mountain the city faces keeps its own front row, as Pachuca de Soto does when the tour travels north; 1 onion, 6 garlic cloves, 1 tomato, all charred until they can be peeled with a thumb; 1 cup of toasted Pepitas; 4 tortillas torn, not sliced; and the chicken of two hens, poached first and returned at the end, because a city of 9,209,944 people is fed in two passes, the way everything there is fed.

The Method, at 2,240 meters

  1. Blister the 16 pasillas and 8 mulatos over flame until they blister and go black at the shoulders. Soak in warm water 40 minutes. The water goes green and serious; keep it, it is half the city.
  2. Char the onion, tomato and garlic under the broiler until the kitchen is smelling of a September that is not this September. The smoke is not an error.
  3. Toast the Pepitas, the almonds, the sesame and the tortillas in a dry pan, separately, in that order, each set aside on a clean cloth. Do not combine them until the pot tells you to.
  4. In the big pot — the one that holds a drawing room's worth — fry the charred onion and garlic in lard or oil, 3 minutes, then add the chiles and their green water, the tomato, the piloncillo, the raisins, the prunes, the cloves, the broken cinnamon, and 1.5 liters of the poaching broth.
  5. Here the altitude speaks. At 2,240 meters the water boils at about 92 degrees instead of 100, and the pot will look to you as if it is not cooking. It is. It is cooking gently on the high Mexican Central Plateau, the way the whole city does everything — slowly, at altitude, without the drama of a boil. Simmer 45 minutes, uncovered, and do not lower the flame in fear.
  6. Blend the pot with the Pepitas, the almonds, the sesame, the torn tortillas and the chocolate, in batches, through whatever the kitchen allows. The chocolate goes in last, when the pot has cooled to the temperature of a held note.
  7. Return the blended mole to the pot, thin it with broth to the thickness of a river coming down a street — the Rhône in Avignon, the Río de la Piedad here, they are the same water in the dream — and add the poached chicken pieces.
  8. Simmer another 30 minutes. Taste at minute 25. If it is not yet right, it is the cinnamon; if it is not right after the cinnamon, it is the chocolate; if it is not right after the chocolate, it is the year.
  9. Sixteen bowls. One for each alcaldía. The sixteenth bowl is set by the window, facing the morning, and is given to the next person who arrives, whoever that is, drawing room or no drawing room.

Margin notes

“The 1791 doubloon is not an ingredient. It is kept in mind, not in the pot. — E.A.”

“Where the pot gives more than 16 bowls, the extra ones are counted as weather and discounted. — C.Z., in the concordance's hand”

“If a board in West Virginia pins this thread again, the next person is the one eating. — D.H., Beckley”
