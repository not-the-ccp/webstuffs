Estuary Survey of Rates & Accumulations · Station: back room, Dona Luzia’s pensão, Rua da Paz, Pedro do Rosário

Field Notes on the Calculus

kept by H. M. Quaresma, naturalist of rates and accumulations, in the corner chair, 28 June – 5 July

FILED UNDER: REPEAT VERIFIED BY EAR IN A TAP

Entry I 28 June, 05:42 — The Habitat

Arrived in the back room at half past five and took the corner chair, the one that sits between the short-wave set and the tin of prime old train oil on the second shelf. I had come to Pedro do Rosário to study what the clerk of the Council of the Single Link calls the festa ledger, and I found instead a living territory of change. Calculus — the branch of mathematics that studies continuous change, the principal precursor of modern mathematical analysis, once called infinitesimal calculus or the calculus of infinitesimals — does not live in a lecture hall here. It lives in the tide, in the bread, and in the rope. The tide, the clerk told the air at four minutes past, has not yet finished turning, and I wrote it down as my first observation, because a thing that is not yet finished turning is a creature of the limit: it approaches, and approaches, and the mathematics it approaches toward is the well-defined limit that the whole field stands on.

Habitat: six rooms, one fan (fixed), one tin of oil (not for the stove). The fan is fixed and I am allowed to believe it.

Entry II 06:10 — Specimen No. 1: The Turning Tide Limes itapecuruae

First branch, differential calculus: the study of instantaneous rates of change and of slopes of curves. The Itapecuru at dawn is the clearest curve I have ever measured. I took the water mark against the quay post at 05:40 and again at 06:10: up 11 centimetres in half an hour, and yet at any single second the tide is doing a smaller, faster, more precise thing that the clock cannot hold. That thing is the derivative — the slope of the curve at one instant, the tangent line touching the tide where the tide is. The clerk was teaching the festa four arithmetics at the same hour, and four hundred and eighty reais divided among seven farinha houses came out sixty-eight with four left over, 480⁄7, a rational number that weighs true in the market. The tide does not divide so neatly; it only trends, and the trend is what the derivative is for. In the instrument below, slide the tide and the tangent follows; at mid-channel the slope is flat, and as Seu Benedito says, a flat thing sounds like a tin bucket.

Observed also: fourteen jangadas off the flats, seven snapper, seven tainha — a whole-number fact in a continuous place. The arithmetic holds as long as the things counted are not cut up. The tide cannot be counted; it can only be differentiated.

Entry III 07:05 — Specimen No. 2: The Baker’s Accumulation Summa crumbarum

Second branch, integral calculus: the study of accumulation of quantities and of areas under or between curves. The baker’s order-slip of 12 March is an integral wearing a ledger sleeve. “How now in very fine bread crumbs, 1⁄2 lb.” A half pound is an accumulation: an infinity of crumbs added so finely that the pan cannot tell the difference. The Council’s procedure has five steps — identify, exploit, subordinate, elevate, and repeat — and the third line of the slip, the one that will not file itself, is the line that goes on the repeat step. A procedure that keeps adding the same small lift, batch after batch, is precisely a sum of infinitesimals; the whole morning’s farinha d’água, white and fine as salt, is the area under the curve of the pan from the first lift to the last. The baker issues no proof. The baker issues bread, which is the only verification this kitchen has ever issued, and I have recorded that on the authority of a kitchen, a verification is a fact with its ears closed.

The full letter of the third line is read into the record in the gazette’s correspondence on P versus NP, where it is entered, word for word, in the ledger of this paper, and where the clerk asks whether the line is a solution or a certificate. From the corner chair it looks like a procedure — an instruction that runs, and runs again — and a naturalist has to say the running is the specimen, not the result.

Weight check: 1⁄2 lb. of crumbs, 480⁄7 reais per division of the ledger, 68+4⁄7 and the four that cannot go in a basket. The four are the infinitesimals; the basket is the limit they will never quite reach.

Entry IV 07:50 — Specimen No. 3: The Rope of Twenty-Seven Circumferentia non-fractionalis

The drumhead is a circle, twenty-seven centimetres across, and Seu Benedito cut its rope by ear. The rope’s true length is π times twenty-seven, and π is three point one four one five nine two six five and does not stop. But it stops everywhere, and that is the marvel: an infinite sequence of better and better numbers — three, 3.1, 3.14, 3.141 — each one a straight edge laid against the circle, converges to a limit that is well-defined, a single number, the rope that would be exactly right if it could be cut. Archimedes did it with 96 straight edges; the figure below is the same animal with twelve. Infinite series and infinite sequences, the textbook says, are the machinery by which calculus reaches its limits, and out here the machinery is a drummer’s ear and a coil of rope on a chair, and the chair complains. I measured the shell twice. Twenty-seven. Twenty-seven. The drum does not argue, and neither, it turns out, does the limit.

Entry V 08:15 — The Linking Pair Theoremata fundata

The two branches are related to each other by the fundamental theorem of calculus, and in the back room the theorem is the door the Baker does not open. Differential: the rate at which the bread is filling the pan, the slope, the instantaneous, the clerk teaching the festa out loud to a short-wave set that is not plugged in, by house custom. Integral: the whole of the pan, the accumulation, the 12 March, the repeat step oiled with the vintages. The theorem says these are the same animal from two doors — that the slope, integrated, gives back the height; that the height, differentiated, gives back the slope; that what the tide does in a second and what the baker does in a morning are one fact with two names. The driver by wire, in Back-Room Arithmetic, called the repeat step the string the next wingtip will fly off of, and I can only confirm from the chair that in nature it is always the repeat step, and the repeat step is an integral, and the integral is a sum, and the sum is the batch holding.

My instrument count for the morning: one tide, one pan, one rope, one theorem. Population of the habitat: six people and one fan. The constraint, Dona Luzia, was observed twice and interviewed never. She is the constraint and she does add, and when she adds, the ledger closes.

Entry VI 5 July, after the tide — The Board by the Fan

On the morning of the 5th I went up to the second shelf and the tin was where it had been, under the vintages, and on the board nailed by the fan, in the air’s own hand, was one line, which I enter here word for word, as the record travels with the notebook:

A prize in the Air · Pedro do Rosário — anyone been?

A prize, in the amount of one million dollars, is indeed in the air over this municipality, attached to the question of whether the class that solves in polynomial time and the class that merely verifies in polynomial time are one animal or two; Dona Luzia has said twice that if it is ever paid, the second cold shower comes off the proceeds, and the Council recorded her as a constraint in the process. The rest of the line is a call to the field: anyone been, the board asks, to the territory these notes describe? I have been, in the corner chair, for one tide and a fraction, and my answer to the board is the standard answer of the survey: the drums do not take letters, but the notes take whatever the tide leaves. The reading of 28 June stands on the air, the four arithmetics are taught, the fifth line is filed under repeat, and the fundamental theorem is doing its quiet work between the pan and the quay. Next tide, 12 July. I keep the chair.
