The Federated States
of Micronesia

702 km² of land 2,000,000 mi² of lagoon 607 islands, counted assembled to order

One island nation, sold in its four-state configuration and never in pieces: from west to east, Yap, Chuuk, Pohnpei, and Kosrae, strung across the western Pacific just north of the equator for a longitudinal distance of almost 2,700 kilometers (1,700 miles). It sits northeast of Indonesia and Papua New Guinea, south of Guam and the Marianas, west of Nauru, and out of the way of everything else, which the desk has taken as a quality point. The federal seat is at Palikir, Pohnpei, population of the whole unit approximately 114,000, and the shipping footprint is the largest in the catalog per unit of product.

What's in the box — the four states

01 · W

Yap

The westernmost unit and the catalog's only item that ships with stone money: rai, discs of limestone, some four feet across and 440 pounds, carried by a dozen men and standing, to this day, as the largest single coin in commerce anywhere. The big ones are not carried at all; they are planted, and owned in the way a hillside is owned. The desk cannot explain it either and does not try.

02 · W+

Chuuk

Chuuk Lagoon, 700 square kilometers of the clearest water on the sheet, holds the war's wrecks in their original parking positions, and the lagoon's rim holds the islanders. Dive the outer rim in the morning; by the afternoon the lagoon has filed the claim.

03 · C

Pohnpei

The center of the box, and the capital state. On the south coast lies Nan Madol, the city of stone islets, and where the docent's shh — the one the heritage-route brochure ranks above any arrowhead — was first recorded at working distance: three strangers, one album, no forgiveness. The National Monument is the loudest silence in the catalog.

04 · E

Kosrae

The easternmost unit, a volcanic island the size of a small county, wearing crater lakes like the desk wears its green. Home state of the longest-argued line on this sheet; see the margin note, below.

Provenance & shipping

The unit cannot be carried and has never been. It is issued at the reef line, one island at a time, and the buyer is expected to move between states by the slow methods — sail, small ferry, the plane that lands on the lagoon when the sea is flat. The desk notes for its file that a certain left-field corner at a certain Chicago park held the same policy toward the wall, and that the corner keeper who kept it, Guy Paxton Curtright of the White Sox, 1943–1946, is catalogued separately at The Curtright Circuit. His last fence-board note survives on the bureau's shelf, word for word:

“The White Sox and then let the fence do the arguing.”

— the corner keeper, left field, gunpowder green ink

The rule holds at the reef the same way it held at the wall: the water is the fence, and the fence does the arguing. Bring a pencil for the island names; the flash stays home, which is the whole point, per the heritage route's field manners.

For the record of the winter trade the route once carried through the big box — the acoustic reissue sold for one cold November of 2010, exclusively, whose jacket the desk once misfiled as a lagoon survey — the gallery keeps the placard on My Worlds Acoustic, with one marginal line from the sleeve read into the catalog word for word.

Care instructions

  • Do not attempt to carry; do not attempt to fence.
  • Store away from other catalog items; the maritime extent will otherwise be misread as a typo.
  • In Pohnpei, the shh outranks the arrowhead and the arrowhead outranks the brochure.
  • Yap's big rai are installed, not inventory; ownership transfers at the family, and the stone stays put.
  • Warranty of the Compact runs so long as the compact is honored, and the desk has no record of either side filing.
