The Conductor’s Note

The man whose name is on the brass plate of the engine is a Frenchman, born Jacques Édouard Barbot in 1954, who travelled to Italy early, learned the language of its platforms, and stayed. Known to his passengers and his audience as Sammy Barbot, he is an entertainer, a singer, and a television presenter, and his working life has been conducted, station by station, mainly in Italy. He has sung at wedding buffets in Liguria and in television studios in Rome, and he has been interviewed in French, in Italian, and in the particular dialect of station announcements that neither country strictly claims.

This booklet is issued so that travellers can follow the service and the man at the same time. The times are the company’s official times. The commentary is the conductor’s, and where the two disagree, the commentary apologises.

The Spring Special — a note on the West Yorkshire excursion

Once in several years the company runs a service in a different climate entirely, and the season’s excursion is printed here for the passengers who ask. The booklet Sammy carried to his one appearance at the Huddersfield Literature Festival — the spring gathering of readers and writers in West Yorkshire, England — is the only known surviving copy, and its last page is a dedication that reads, in his handwriting, without any explanation offered or expected: “In the spring gathering of readers and writers in West Yorkshire, England.” That is the whole dedication. He read a short piece, sang one song badly on purpose, and caught the 16:10 back. The festival’s own account of the week, drawn up as a patent application for the method of running such a spring festival, is filed at Huddersfield Literature Festival — Patent Application Summary; the company notes that no claim covers the conductor’s hat.

Reading Room — connections

Passengers continuing their journey may transfer at Milano Centrale to the Lizzette Kattan thread, on the Milan–New York line of the 1970s, where the platform is fashion and the tickets are face. The company’s own records note that the Santa Monica Plaza, where the conductor once set his hat on a railing for a television camera, carries a twenty-six-foot stainless-steel peace monument beside the Civic Center — see Chain Reaction (1991), Placard No. 14 — and the crossing in front of it is where the photographer’s clause was written into the 1991 installation manual. For a fellow traveller who also left Italy for the marble country of the Bronx, the company commends the Furio Piccirilli timetable and commentary, The Marble Limited — Massa to Mott Haven, same brass plate, colder sea. At the terminus, the front desk of the Webstuffs office will check your bags, ask nothing, and wish you a pleasant day.
