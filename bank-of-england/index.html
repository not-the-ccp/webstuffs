About this registry

This registry records the errors found in the standing record of the Bank of England: the central bank of the United Kingdom, established in 1694 to act as the English Government’s banker and debt manager, and still one of the bankers for the government of the United Kingdom. The Bank is the world’s second oldest central bank, after Sweden’s, chartered in 1668, and is counted among the “Big Four” central banks of the world, which is a fact the registry does not verify but which the record repeats, line for line, without complaint. The model is the point. Most modern central banks were built on this one, in the way a bore is built on a bore: the first line is the hard line, and every later line is a correction of it.

The Bank was privately owned by stockholders from its chartering in 1694 until its nationalisation in 1946 by the Attlee ministry; in 1998 it became an independent public organisation, wholly owned by the Treasury Solicitor on behalf of the government, with a mandate to keep the price of things from moving for no reason. Each entry below carries its code, its timestamp in the registry’s own reckoning, its observed value, its expected value, and its disposition. Entries are acknowledged, not erased: an old bank keeps its old errors the way it keeps its old ground — below the street, in the same line, where the water is.

ERR-1694 severity: historical ts 1694-07-27 · entry 0001
fieldfound.established
observed1694 — the charter year, taken as the founding of the Bank as a central bank, which it was then not, being the English Government’s banker and debt manager, with a loan of £1,200,000 on the strength of the Exchequer’s credit
expected1668 — the founding of the Riksbank of Sweden, the older central bank, by which the Bank of England is, on the record, second and must remain second
resolutionrecord retained as observed; the second-place standing is confirmed annually and is not in dispute

The registry notes for the file: a central bank is what a central bank does, and the doing here began with lending to a government that had spent its credit on a war and wanted it back as an institution. The stockholders were private; the debt was public; the arrangement outlived both its reasons.

ERR-1734 severity: migration ts 1734-07-12 · entry 0002
fieldbank.address
observedLothbury — the original room, where the money of 1694 first lay, and where the records began to be kept in rooms that the street would not grow around quickly enough
expectedThreadneedle Street — the present street, taken in 1734, the building begun and the record moved bodily, drawer by drawer, which is the only kind of move a ledger should ever make
resolutionmigration completed; the Lothbury record is retained as provenance, and the Threadneedle Street building is the one that has since become the whole street

The registry notes: when a bank changes street, the error is not in the street. The street is where the bank says it is.

ERR-1844 severity: split ts 1844-08-25 · entry 0003
fieldbanknote.authority
observeda single authority issuing banknotes and lending in the same hand, as the Bank had done without a break since its chartering
expectedtwo authorities, as required by the Bank Charter Act of 1844: one to issue, one to lend, separated in the books even where they could not be separated in the street
resolutionrecord split into the Issue Department and the Banking Department; the split stands in the record to this day, which is longer than most splits last

The registry notes: the split was made after the Bank Notes Act was written to close the door on notes that had been printed in a hurry and were not worth the paper. The door has been closed since 1844. The registry keeps the hinge record.

ERR-1946 severity: critical — ownership ts 1946-03-01 · entry 0004
fieldbank.owner
observedstockholders — private owners of record from the chartering in 1694, whose shares were the quietest shares in the country and whose dividend was the interest on the national debt
expectedthe nation, by operation of the Bank of England Act 1946, the Bank being taken into national ownership by the Attlee ministry on 1 March 1946, the stockholders’ shares being purchased and the record closed against them
resolutiontransition completed; the entry is left open by rule, because an ownership change of this size is the only kind of error a central bank should ever have made, and it is worth keeping the before on the page

The registry notes: from this line forward the Bank answers to no shareholder, which is why the following entries are policy instead of profit. The change was not a correction of a mistake. It was the mistake the record wanted, made on purpose, on the day the war was over.

ERR-1998 severity: advisory — off-by-one ts 1997-12-01 · entry 0005
fieldpolicy.mandate
observed1997 — the operational independence of the Bank, granted by the Bank of England Act 1997 and taking effect on 1 August 1997, the base rate being set from that date by the Bank’s own Monetary Policy Committee
expected1998 — the year by which the standing record dates the independence, the Act having been in force long enough by then to be true in the books
resolutionoff-by-one accepted and filed; the record quotes 1998 because the record is allowed one rounding, and the rounding goes to the side of the policy. From this line the Bank is an independent public organisation, wholly owned by the Treasury Solicitor on behalf of the government, and the mandate is written down in full, once, in this entry

The registry notes: a mandate is a sentence a bank agrees to be checked by. The registry keeps the sentence. The policy statement, when issued, is this registry.

ERR-2377 severity: unparseable — line retained ts 2024-11-14 · entry 0006
fielddrainage.cross-reference, forwarded from the New Madrid Township archive, drainage box 1958–1964, one card, one hand
observeda single sentence from the township’s own record, set down here word for word because the channel office could not split it without breaking it:
The township’s drainage is mostly open channel are both open Thursdays, and the Bank twin bore, 4½ ft.
parsefailed at token “are”; three sentences braided into one line, the third of which reaches across the Atlantic and back to the Bank’s own twin bore
resolutionretained unparseable. The 4½ ft. bore of the sentence is the same 4½-foot gauge the Central London Railway drove through the ground of London, twin bore, to the door of this street, and it is recorded in the sale catalogue of Hollis & Pankhurst, Sale No. 37, Lot 41 — The Central London Railway, which opened 30 June 1900 at this very terminus. The drainage clause is the township’s; the channel stays open Thursdays, as the township’s term report, Form 3B, Room 214, reads it eleven times and gets the same eleven answers. The middle clause is a schedule, not a place. The line is filed, whole, under both offices.

The registry notes: not every line is sensible, and a registry is a place where the sensible lines keep the unparseable ones company. The one that is filed is filed whole.

Registry protocol is the same as the one kept for the city on the Witham, in the error log LINC-ERR: a record is corrected in place, the before is kept beside the after, and a line that cannot be split is filed whole. For the discipline of reading a strange line word for word before reasoning about it, the channel office keeps open the township file at Form 3B. The ledger at Threadneedle Street is closed; the registry is not.
