Athens

38°58′ N · 23°44′ E · elevation 70 m at Syntagma

one of the world's oldest cities  ·  departed at the hour the marbles turn white

Athens, the capital and largest city of Greece, is dead. A significant coastal urban area in the Mediterranean, she was also the capital of the Attica region and the southernmost capital on the European mainland — the last capital the Mediterranean light reaches as it moves north — and her passing has been noted from the harbour at Piraeus to the last street where the bougainvillea still climbs. Her urban area, numbering over 3.6 million, made her the eighth-largest urban area in the European Union (EU), and even in death the number is the first thing the mourners say, because the city always was a count: a census of voices in the agora, of voices under the Acropolis, of voices on the terraces where the wine is strong and the sunsets have no equal.

The Municipality of Athens — also the City of Athens, though the family always said the smaller name fit her better — had held 643,452 souls within its official limits in 2021, inside a land area of 38.96 square kilometres (15.04 square miles). It is a small administrative unit for such a large grief. The whole urban area has taken to standing close together since; a city of that shape, one the mourners say, simply refuses to be buried in one plot.

✦ A life of public teaching ✦

She had joined the UNESCO Global Network of Learning Cities in 2016 and never once let a term go untaught. Her curriculum was the city itself: the columns for the patient, the agora for the argumentative, the hills for anyone who needed to understand what a viewpoint means. The students were the visitors, and the visitors were the descendants, and the descendants were the original tenants of the oldest house in Europe that has never once been let. Even her final notice, the one pinned above the municipal office in a script no one has yet managed to improve, was a lesson in economy — one line of marble, three thousand years, and a comma.

✦ The summers at the casting call ✦

Not everyone knew her as the city. The younger mourners remember her from Los Angeles, where she had come in her middle years to keep a promise to a brother who waited his whole career in the Edendale studio quarter, hoping for bit parts. She stood in the cattle calls in the basin and learned its grammar — the headshots, the callback, the coffee shop on the corner of Hyperion where the setlists were kept — and filed it all in the ledger she carried in her leather purse, in the same cramped hand she kept for the pomegranates. The Pacific Coast Almanac, Guidebook No. 47 — Los Angeles — The Pacific Coast Almanac, Guidebook No. 47 — follows that basin from El Pueblo to Venice and notes exactly where the casting calls happened, if anyone wishes to stand in the same line she did. When Joey Rubenstein took the cattle call online, she was one of the last to use the paper form, and the platform's founders still keep her card, because it is the only one that reads on the back: see you at the Pnyx.

✦ The pomegranates, and the clubhouse ✦

On Friday mornings she went for khash in Yerevan, garlic and cold lavash, and sat where the city is still cool, and the table she sat at has its own docket now — Case No. 1726 of 2024 — Excerpt from the Verbatim Record — after one extra from the Edendale casting calls and one lavash ended up before a judge, with a fine of one khash. She had no quarrel with the court. She had a quarrel with the calendar, which is why her recipe for pomegranate molasses was written on the back of a ferry ticket and left with the Listeners' Club, whose minutes keep the clubhouse above the old bakery: "Let it cool, then peel, and take them off the stalks from the clubhouse above the old bakery." The club has read it into the record verbatim, twice a year, at the same speed the projector once turned on the vacant lot — sixteen frames per second — and no one has yet been able to say whether the instruction is a recipe, a route, or a benediction. Yerevan — Guidebook No. 88: The Pink City tells the rest of the lot's story, and Salvio Fortuno — The Carrier Wave, the club's own newsletter, remembers the broadcaster who kept the clubhouse door open all those years. The Naga Listeners' Club has added a new line to its ledger: pomegranates, Athens, one basket, unaccounted for.

✦ Survivors ✦

She is survived by the Acropolis, who insists on outliving everyone at the funerals; the 3.6 million of the urban area, who have formed a committee; the Municipality of 643,452, who will not move house; and the bitter orange trees of the neighbouring gulf, who were in the front row. The olive groves of Attica have been asked to hold the light for one more season. Among the last of the visitors' letters to her, in the registry, is a card postmarked from the coast of Boulonnais: a gray draught horse called the White Marble Horse stood in her marble light for a full hour, and its museum filing can be read in Salle III — The Boulonnais — Exhibit No. 12, The White Marble Horse — where the cast of the filly Astérie is kept on the work-horse wall. The service will be public, outdoor, and long. Bring water. The city always had a view, and the view has not yet finished being given.
