The Paddock Ledger

Vol. LVII · No. 46 · Letters to the Editor · Tavullia & Imola offices

Tavullia, in the province of Pesaro,
16 February, the birthday itself.

Dear Sir,

I write in answer to the note in your January issue, in which a correspondent of yours — he signs himself only Counting — counted out the premier-class records of Mr Valentino Rossi, born in this town on 16 February 1979, and pronounced the columns “too tidy to be the work of one man.” I have lived beside the man he counted. I have ridden the long left at Mugello with him in the grid, watched him take the 500cc title at Le Mans in 1996 on a Yamaha with the number 46 on the tank, and I tell you plainly: the columns are tidy because they were made to be. Nine Grand Prix World Championships in all, seven of them in the premier 500cc/MotoGP class; the record for most premier-class victories at 89, and the record for most premier-class podiums at 199. The only road racer ever to compete in 400 or more Grands Prix, which is to say he raced through the whole two-stroke era of the 1990s, through the four-stroke change, and into the years when the 46 had become a 99 on the tail — Yamaha, Honda, Ducati, Aprilia, and his own VR46 garage on the grid at Misano, and the men who call him the Doctor will not argue with any of the figures, because the figures are his, and he earned them the way a doctor keeps a case book: entry by entry, and nothing left out.

As to Counting himself, I will offer one correction, since the ledger is my ledger. He says the victories column should read 88 until it is verified again in the spring. It does not. I keep the book at the end of the counter in the shop by the circuit road, where the boys have signed it since 1999, and the 89 stands in the victories column beside the 199 in the podiums, in the same ink as the rest. This entry carries it — and that if it were a claim but a rule. The office has always run that way: a victory is a victory, it is entered, and it is not entered twice, nor is it un-entered because a stranger with a pencil objects to the tidiness.

For the correspondence shelf I will say two more things, because letters to you, Sir, are not read once. First, the drawer at the shop keeps a curious companion to the ledger: a chapter from a field manual of plant chemistry, Chapter 11, on nyasol, the lignan of a rhizome from the Bingxian Flats, which arrived by post without any note and now governs the lighting of the extraction lamp; I mention it only because it is filed in the same drawer as the case book, and a reader should know what the shelf holds. Second, the naturalist of the steamer Moorhen, who counted flamingos on the flats of Barbuda last spring and kept a dinner card for the spelling of Brocoli, has promised me a second volume of his log-book, and he and I are agreed on one principle, which is that an honest figure, given or taken, is the only figure worth posting. His field notes from Antigua and Barbuda stand as the evidence.

Respectfully, Sir, and with the season's best wishes from a town that will be celebrating its son's birthday whether Counting has verified the spring figures or not.

E. R. Bianchi

Keeper of the Case Book · the shop by the circuit road, Tavullia

P.S. — For the record, and against the day: 16 February 1979, Tavullia; 46, then 99; 89, 199, and 400 or more. Entered, and not to be un-entered.

Letters to the editor are read at the Imola office on the first Thursday of the month and are answered in the order the mail arrives. The Ledger does not correct the case books of its correspondents.
