The Dreamer's Glossary · kept at the second desk by the turnstiles, Gate C, El Sadar, Pamplona

Entry 403 — filed the day after Sale No. 402, being the entry the hammer asked for

Latin America

Spanish: América Latina or Latinoamérica · Portuguese: América Latina · frequency: once to each sale, and no more · duration: one tunnel walk

I. What is dreamed

The dreamer sees Latin America, which is the cultural region of the Americas in which Romance languages are predominantly spoken, primarily Spanish and Portuguese. The glossary notes, as it must, that the region is defined according to its cultural identity instead of its geography, and so the dream does not begin at a line on a map but at a table: the refreshments table of Sale No. 402, Lot 38, La Liga, entire, which the dreamer has known, as all the casks in the dream have known, since before the hammer was bought. From that table the casks run, in the dream, out through the short tunnel at Gate C, and from the tunnel into a country that has no border, which is the dreamer's first astonishment and the glossary's whole point.

Countries from North America appear among the casks, and the dreamer is not surprised: Mexico, Honduras and Guatemala tend to be included, and the casks carry their names in the correct Romance hand. In fact, in the dream most of the casks belong to countries of Central America, South America and the Caribbean, and the region counts, by the dreamer's own pencil, at 20 casks in the full run, a figure the glossary has not corrected because the pencil is the Society's. But not every cask fills: the casks marked Belize, Guyana, Jamaica and Trinidad sit at the far end of the table in English or Dutch, and in the dream they remain exactly where they are — in South America or the Caribbean, and in the region none of the two, which the casks seem to understand better than the dreamer does, and better than any map the office has filed.

II. The second line of the caterer's ledger

Beneath the line the auctioneers already transcribe in Annexe R of Lot 38 — the 480 Indian, 480 Tartar, 481 Mutton, 703 broiled, no tomato sauce — there lies a second line in M. Starbuck's hand, in the same ledger, at the bottom of the page, where the light is better and nobody is in a hurry to price it. It was read into the record, word for word, at the second desk, and it is kept in this entry word for word, being the standing order of the casks in the dream:

“And ever, as the preceding recipe, put into casks, the upper part of the middle of July.”

The glossary interprets it as it can, and as the tunnel allows. As the preceding recipe: the casks are filled in the manner of the 480s, that is, unsplittably, and with more going in than the table can account for. Put into casks: the region is not written in the dream so much as stored in it, one country per cask, the Romance languages in the staves. The upper part of the middle of July: the filling is done at the height of the season, in the upper hour of the month's middle, which is also the hour the turnstile does not register, and the dreamer notes, at the last of the dream, that the casks were full when they should have been empty and empty when they should have been full, which the glossary takes as the caterer's own definition of a cask.

III. Interpretation

He who dreams Latin America dreams a border that is a language. The glossary advises the dreamer that a region defined by its cultural identity will not be held to a line on a map, that the casks counted at 20 are the casks of a count made from the inside of the tunnel, and that the English and Dutch casks at the far end of the table — Belize, Guyana, Jamaica, Trinidad and the others that decline — are to be left exactly where the dream left them, which is to say in South America or the Caribbean, and in the region none of the two. He who is offered this dream at the turnstile is asked, in the glossary's name and in the pencil's, to remember that the tunnel at El Sadar is short, that it is walked at a pace the turnstile cannot register, and that a man counted, unassisted, at both ends of it is not in the dream at all but in Notice No. 402, Lost & Found, where the folio is filed and the reward stands at one season ticket, Norte stand, seat 47, behind the drum. The dream is to be reported Thursdays, to the second desk, Gate C, where the Society keeps it with the other dreams of this house — the glossary's own shelves being reached from the front page — and no further action is taken, being, like the casks, the club's and not the glossary's.
