Errors in the Smith Files

Being a register of the errors in the Adam Smith files, Adam Smith (baptised 16 June [O.S. 5 June] 1723 – 17 July 1790), the Scottish economist and philosopher who was a pioneer in the field of political economy and a key figure during the Scottish Enlightenment. The register keeps its lines by the school's standing usage — the same usage that leaves the sea comedy's helmsman's order unemended in the Form 3B term report on Ludvig Holberg and the conservator's margin standing in the handbook's Chapter IV: an error that is kept stands as recorded, and the register will not emend itself.

FILE: smith/a. (1723–1790) LINES: 7 RETAINED: 6 REFUSED: 1
ERR-1723·06 INFO calendar/1723-06 · Kirkcaldy, Fife

DATE AMBIGUITY. The subject was baptised 16 June 1723 (O.S. 5 June).

Two dates, both true. The old-style calendar ran eleven days behind the new-style in the year of the baptism, and the files carry both because neither will yield to the other. The subject was born in the village of Kirkcaldy on the Fife coast, into a household that did not keep the boy long: the father's hands were not a father's trade for him, and the education that did for it began at the Kirkcaldy grammar school and carried on to the University of Glasgow, then to Balliol College, Oxford, where the lectures failed him and the reading — the French moralists, the Scots common-sense men — made the man. The register notes for the file that the subject died at Kirkcaldy, 17 July 1790, and comes home eleven days later than it left; the drift is real, and it is kept.

STATUS: RETAINED. Both dates stand.

ERR-1759·TS WARNING moral-sentences/1759 · London

MIRROR MODULE RETURNS UNEXPECTED SELF. The Theory of Moral Sentiments (1759) resolves the actor into the man who sees him.

The first of the two classic works. The warning is that a person examined by another's eyes does not come back unchanged: the subject of 1759 builds the whole moral machine on that fact, on the spectator in the breast, on the “impartial observer” whose consent is the only court that sits. The library flagged the volume as unstable on first shelving, because a book that re-forms its reader while being read is not behaving like a book. It has behaved that way for two and a half centuries. The warning stands; the volume does not.

STATUS: RETAINED. Works as written.

ERR-1767·CPH INFO relocation/1767–1768 · Copenhagen

SUBJECT RELOCATED. The subject held a public lecture chair in Copenhagen in 1767–1768, lecturing in Danish.

By then the Lille Grønngade Theatre had long since left its green street, but the school kept its annotated copy of the sea comedy on shelf K-12, and the subject, who had made the capital his own, was not unknown in its rooms: the same capital that had taken Holberg's Bergen boy in 1686 took, forty years later, the Kirkcaldy man's lectures. The files cross-reference the two men without flattery. One built the modern Danish comedy in a green street; the other built the modern account of the nation's wealth, and both of them sat in the same city and neither of them corrected the other. The report that carries the first is at Ludvig Holberg (1684–1754), Term Report No. 12, Form 3B.

STATUS: RETAINED. The relocation is on record.

ERR-1776·WN INFO wealth-of-nations/1776 · London

DISCIPLINE INITIALISED. An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations loaded, 1776.

The magnum opus, often abbreviated as The Wealth of Nations, and the one the files weigh heaviest. The entry matters because the loading is not a book but an event: from 1776, modern economic scholarship stands as a comprehensive system and an academic discipline, the division of labour first, then the price, then the money, then the nations in their separate courses, each volume a module that the next one links against. Many readers have called the subject the “father of economics”; some have called him the “father of capitalism.” The register has twice been asked to choose between the two titles and has twice refused, on the ground that the subject would not have chosen either, and that a title a man never claimed is not a line the register may emend in.

STATUS: RETAINED. Loaded 1776; still loading.

ERR-1776·DIST REFUSED distribution/wealth-and-power

501 — EXPLANATION NOT PROVIDED. The subject refuses to explain the distribution of wealth and power.

The one error in the files that is not an error. The system asks for the distribution — who holds the wealth, who holds the power, by what arithmetic the two stay apart — and the module answers by refusing. There is no fault in the wiring; the refusal is in the text, and it is the text's own. The register's usage for a kept refusal is the same usage the court applies to a line it will not explain, the same the handbook applies to a conservator's margin, and the same this school applies to the sea line on shelf K-12: the refusal stands as written, and the questions go on asking. The old handbook would call it a debt kept out of the ledger rather than entered in it — for the usage of debts of this order, the reader is referred to Of Fealty and Its Observances, Chapter IV, where a debt recorded nowhere is a debt that can be denied anywhere. Here it is recorded, and denied in the same line.

STATUS: REFUSED, RETAINED. The register does not retry.

ERR-1722·VST WARNING inventory/1722 · Lille Grønngade Theatre, Copenhagen

VESTURE LOCATION MISMATCH. A sacred vesture reported from beneath the stage of the green street's theatre, 1722.

The oldest line in the register, and the one the school keeps for the way it was written. In the winter of 1722, the season of the comedies, the churchwardens of the parish that kept the green street's theatre found their vesture missing from the vestry; it was not missing. The clerks who went down to the pit to see the new comedies of Holberg — the mountain-dweller, the schoolmaster, the sea — went down in it, and the vesture went with them, because the parish had taken the comedies as one of its offices, as a man takes an oath. The inventory clerk's note survived, and the register sets it down word for word:

The purity of their sacred vesture, the alb or tunic, worn beneath the green street’s theatre.

Let the reader weigh that note as the handbook weighs its margins. The alb or tunic is the white vesture of the altar, the one worn beneath everything else, and its being worn is the point of the line: the vesture was not stored beneath the stage, it was worn there, in the dark, at the feet of the pit, on the nights the comedies ran. The white under the green, the altar office under the pit. The clerk called it a location mismatch, and it stands in the register as one, because a kept line stands as written, and the mismatch, like the refusal, like the sea line, is the whole of the finding. What is recorded is to be kept.

STATUS: RETAINED, WORD FOR WORD. The mismatch is on record.

ERR-1790·EOF INFO kirkcaldy/1790-07-17

PROCESS TERMINATED. The subject ended at Kirkcaldy, 17 July 1790.

The subject ended where he began, at Kirkcaldy, sixty-seven years after the baptism that carried two dates, and the files close with the note that a man who had built a discipline out of the division of labour left no instructions for the division of his own. The register's last line is therefore its first usage, and its last usage its first: the lines stand as written. The two classic works stand, the refusal stands, and the vesture stands beneath the green street's theatre where the inventory put it in 1722. For the wider archive: return to the front page.

STATUS: RETAINED. The file is closed; the lines are not.
