The Onça in the Dish

a radio play in three acts · script excerpt: Act Two, Scene 3 — “The Claim”

CastDona Iara, Controller · Aunt Marva, on the phone, line 02 · Grandfather Eamon, in the studio · Notary Senhora Vaz, on the record · the jaguar, Panthera onca, SFX only
Recordedthe night of the ninetieth day, at the Federal District lost-and-found office annexed to Studio 4, a room with one window onto the avenue and one lamp

Field markspale yellow to tan coat, spots running to rosettes on the sides; a melanistic spotted individual also reported in this litter of noise; body length to 1.85 m (6 ft 1 in); weight to 158 kg (348 lb)

CUE CARD 3 read all character names as if the person were standing beside you. The jaguar is never named. It is the breathing.

ACT TWO · SCENE 3 · “THE CLAIM”

The annex to Studio 4. A table. A notary’s green leather blotter. The dish, wrapped in a tea towel, still at the temperature of a record. Through a door: a kennel the size of a planned capital, and in it, the breathing.

[Tape. The tuning hum slides up from 98 to 101.2 and holds. The ON AIR lamp comes on with a small click that sounds, to some listeners, like a jaw.]

CONTROLLER You are listening to Federal Radio 101.2, Studio 4, Brasília, the Federal District, on the night the count runs out. For sixteen days the lamp has been on the apron, and for a further ninety, held the way my aunt holds it: steadily, and without saying which ninety days. Tonight the register opens its next notice. Notice No. 47. Found in the oven of the family supper of 21 April: one large cat, reported in a 30 by 22 centimetre dish shaped like a lake.

[A line of telephone hiss. The second VU meter lifts off the floor.]

AUNT MARVA (phone, line 02, from Lyneham, over an ocean) It was between the layers. That is the whole of the description, and the whole of the claim. In the white sauce, above the survey-paper potatoes, below the hard yellow cheese. A body of pale yellow and tan, with spots that turn to rosettes on the sides, folded the way a city folds onto its own long axis. I have held the lamp all seventy-four days of the supper and sixteen more. I am telling you now, for the record: I did not put it in the dish.

[From the kennel: one long breath. Then water, somewhere — a fountain, a river, the lake at home doing its work.]

GRANDFATHER EAMON (in the studio, to the notary) Senhora Vaz, for the record, the identification. It is a Panthera onca, the only living member of the genus that is native to the Americas, and the biggest cat species this continent has. It is the third largest cat in the world, which is to say it comes behind the two that live in the other direction from here, and it holds its place the way the register holds its place: by the measure. Up to one metre eighty-five of length. Six foot one, for the listeners in the old country. And up to a hundred and fifty-eight kilograms, written 348 pounds in the register’s own hand.

NOTARY VAZ (on the record, reading from the blotter) The register asks, at the moment of claim, how an animal of that weight claims anything. (paper) I am instructed to read the clause of the deed as it stands.

[The paper is turned once, carefully, the way a capital is turned in a dish.]

NOTARY VAZ Article 12, of the keeping of what is found. “A capital is properly left: in the neighbourhood of the bed I ran up to that effect signed and witnessed.” (paper, back on the blotter) I read it as it stands. I have read it as it stands for forty-one years.

CONTROLLER Grandfather. The studio is listening. What does that mean?

GRANDFATHER EAMON It means what the deed was drawn to mean, Dona Iara. It means a capital is properly left in the ground, in the record, and in the count of the days still running. The clause has a way of being read that way. I wrote the answer to it in the notebook in 1963, on the page above the recipe, and the deed has been catching up with me ever since.

AUNT MARVA (line 02, quieter now) Eamon. The dish was 30 by 22. I have said it to the notary. You cannot keep 158 kilograms of cat in a dish that size.

GRANDFATHER EAMON Auntie. A planned capital is a dish. It has a long axis, an end where the hills are meant to sit, and a centre where everyone sits to dinner. The dish kept the capital. The dish keeps the cat. They were drawn by the same impulse, and they can be told apart by the same field mark: one of them keeps a lamp on the apron, and so does this one, now.

[The breathing, in the kennel, stops. Everyone in the studio stops with it. A clock in the annex ticks out four seconds. Then: the breathing again, and with it a soft turning-over of great weight, a settling, and — once, barely — a low sound no one in the studio will admit to hearing.]

NOTARY VAZ The record should note the animal’s method, as the register notes everything. It is the cat with the bite that pierces the carapaces of turtles and tortoises, and, on mammalian prey, the cat that bites directly through the skull, between the ears, to a fatal blow on the brain. I note it the way I note all the others: plainly, and with the witnesses present.

CONTROLLER Senhora Vaz, the claim conditions. Do they stand as filed?

NOTARY VAZ They stand. The claimants must state, without prompting, why the capital was moved from Rio de Janeiro to the Central-West — to a more central location, and not merely farther away; hold the lamp against the apron for the days that remain; and name, unprompted, the three who drew the city in 1956. (to the studio) I would add one condition of my own, for an item this size: the lamp is not to be turned off. The method is not ours to invent. The register that keeps three battleships by a name, a date, and the count of those who did not come back has the keeping on record; the recipe that keeps a lake the same way has the lamp on the apron; and the family that keeps both keeps this, too.

GRANDFATHER EAMON (answering, all at once, for the record) To a more central location. Lúcio Costa, Oscar Niemeyer, and Joaquim Cardozo. And the fourth man, the president who founded it on 21 April 1960, stays in his own place, because this family keeps people in their places.

AUNT MARVA (line 02) And the lamp. I have it in my hand. I will not say which ninety days. Neither will the studio.

[A pause the length of a breath. The tuning hum, holding at 101.2. From the kennel: the breathing, steady now, at the temperature of a record — not hot enough to change, and not cold enough to forget.]

NOTARY VAZ The claim is accepted on the record. (paper) Notice No. 47 is found. The item is released to the family, on the condition written above the clause: the lamp is on. The register proceeds to No. 48.

[The ON AIR lamp goes out. The tuning hum slides down the dial and is gone. In the annex, behind the door, the onça turns once more, settles, and is kept.]

— Excerpt ends. The full broadcast continues at the claim conditions of Notice No. 46, one planned capital, filed from Brasília, the Federal District, and is held in the Webstuffs papers the way the dish is held: not hot enough to change, and not cold enough to forget. On Thursdays the lamp is on; tonight, on the ninetieth day, the lamp is on.
