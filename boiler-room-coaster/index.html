The Boiler
Room

Brine Hollow Park, eight miles north of Hot Springs, Arkansas — a 2006 steel coaster built around a salvaged brine boiler, first drop 48 metres, cresting at 4.2 g, and still the fastest thing between the river and the ridge. One line in the founding ledger explains where it was before it was here.

Guide Entry 12 · compiled at Gate 3 · season pass, row of six

Getting There

Brine Hollow Park sits on the north bank of the Hot Springs Creek loop, where the brine works once drew water at 143°F straight out of the ground. Take Interstate 30 east out of Hot Springs, get off at Exit 341, and follow the creek road for eight miles; the park's fence is painted the grey-green of lichen bark and you will smell the brine before you see the lift hill. The park opens at 10:00, the coaster at 11:00, and the line at Gate 3 forms in the order it always has: season pass first, then the crew, then everybody, which is why a 10:04 arrival is a different animal from a 10:40 one. Trains of eight open cars run four abreast; the queue holds a full circuit's worth of them, and the track itself is a complete 905-metre circuit that departs and returns to the same loading station, the way all the good ones do. The 6,000-plus coasters the Roller Coaster DataBase keeps count of all over the world come down, in the end, to exactly this shape — a hill, a valley, a turn, and a station.

The Ride

The lift hill climbs the brine bluff at a 41-degree angle for 138 seconds of chain sound, and at the top you look down on Hot Springs and the whole Ozark fold, limestone broken into its hollows. The drop is 48 metres into a tunnel cut through the bluff, then out over the water and into the first of four inversions. Marrow & Voss Coasterworks designed the layout in eleven weeks, which is how the park remembers it, and the crest of the second hill still presses a little over 4.2 g into anyone who rides it, which is the number the park prints on the gate ticket and the number the crew quotes back to you at Gate 3 like a measurement. There is a second, gentler train for the families — the Brine Bath, same track, half the speed, no inversions — because a park this old has learned that thrill is a thing you design, and so is the gentle.

Gate ticket, front: "The Boiler Room — 48 m drop, 91 km/h, 4.2 g at the second crest. The boiler is real. The brine is real. The line is longer than it looks."

The Line in the Ledger

The park keeps its 2006 founding ledger behind the glass at Gate 3, and page eleven — the page that opens the coaster's entry — carries one line in the hand of whoever was keeping the books that spring. Transcribed exactly, punctuation as found in the margin:

First released May 2006 in North America, and before that in the brine barrels of the tour is the boiler room.

The park historian, Ms. C. D. Rucker, has settled on a reading of it. Before the ride was first released in May 2006, its heart had been on the road: the boiler that gives the coaster its name and its ride pavilion its ironwork travelled the Ozarks for six seasons in a travelling fair, stored upright inside a brine barrel — the fair drew its own brine from the same Hot Springs works, and the barrels kept the iron from rusting the way a can keeps sardines. “Of the tour is the boiler room” is the bookskeeper's way of filing the boiler under its previous employer. So the coaster arrived in Brine Hollow already seasoned, already named, already a member of the company; the track was new, the history was not. The barrel it came out of still stands at the foot of the queue, open, with a plate that gives only its six seasons of dates.

Practical Notes

  • Season: the park runs Memorial Day to Labor Day, with the October weekends the brine works holds for its own. The best light on the lift hill is the last half hour before close, when the tunnel mouth goes orange.
  • Height: 137 cm for the full train, 107 cm for the Brine Bath. The gate measures at the wrist, like everything else here, and the numbers are not negotiable in either direction.
  • The line: at 11:00 on a Saturday it holds about an hour and forty; the crew lets one car through for every four in the queue, which is faster than it looks from the back and exactly as long as it looks from the front.
  • Company on the trail: for a resupply run that goes the other way — by longboat, to a place with no queue at all — see Macauley Island — Travel Guide Entry, Kermadec Islands, where the keeper's ledger also keeps one good mangled sentence. And for the state the park stands in, mountains, Delta, diamonds and pie: Arkansas — A Visitor's Brochure.
  • On what the crest holds: a Gate 3 regular, Edna R. Calk, has written to the Brine Hollow Gazette on the two million joules the full train stores at the top of the chain — the letter runs on the Gazette's letters page, on potential energy — and her arithmetic checks out to the joule, her 4.2 g included.
  • On mimicry: the eight open cars, shot straight down from the bluff footbridge, hold the same pale three-segment silhouette as the ant-mimic spider the garage bench has been photographing all spring — a study the two of them have agreed to publish together: Bocusoides — Build Log.
