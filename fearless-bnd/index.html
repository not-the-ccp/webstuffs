Commentary

Fearless BND took the long way to We Are Fearless. The record was made across two winters and one long summer, mostly in a converted boathouse with a leaking roof, where the drip from the beams became a percussion instrument and the singers moved the rehearsal into the kitchen because the sanctuary was being repainted.

The album is the sound of a band refusing to hurry. The lead single and title track was tested on the last train out of town, then re-tested on the same train home. The boathouse also yielded a choirbook leaf of Renaissance polyphony — four independent voices, one pitch held against the others — now offered as Lot 47 at Flanders & Flanders: The Flanders Te Deum — Auction Catalogue Lot 47. The chorus asks the gathered to stand; the bridge asks them to sit down and listen for the quiet parts.

"I said about the size of a presser or lid introduced into this noiselessness came Ahab alone from his head towards the kitchen, and, in addition to stewed veal, &c."

The note was scrawled on the back of a take sheet during the quiet hours of the final mix, when the meters fell so low that the engineer wrote that the room was full of noise. The sentence wanders from the size of a shipping lid to the mad sailor Ahab, from the kitchen table to the stew pot, and then trails off with a flourish. Read as worship, it argues that the smallest vessel — a presser, a lid, a heart — can hold what cannot be measured. Read alongside Melville, it turns the band's own stubbornness into a voyage. The album gathers both readings and lets the congregation decide which departure board is real.

Released on 24 February 2017 by Fearless International, We Are Fearless closes with a train that arrives before it leaves, which is how worship albums usually try to feel and rarely manage. The timetable above is invented with affection, but the hush between the final chord and the next amen is not.

A companion placard in Gallery 4, Contemporary Worship Music — Museum Placard, traces the genre from Jesus-music coffeehouses to the modern worship band — including Fearless BND's own place in it.
