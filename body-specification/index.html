Body and Method for the Measured, Affirmed, and Broadcast Configuration of the Human Torso

Inventor: Megan Jovon Ruth Stallion, of Houston, Texas (of said district) · Assignee: 1501 Certified Entertainment and 300 Entertainment, Houston, Texas · Filed as the third single of the album Good News

ABSTRACT

A method for the self-authored affirmation of the human torso, performed by the subject upon herself during a quarantine interval of spring 2020, and an apparatus for broadcasting the result to a public audience of at least the size of a debut studio album. The method comprises the steps of (a) attending to the subject’s own figure as the primary prior art; (b) concentrating the specification upon two designated regions — the waist and the breasts — while leaving all other regions to read as dependent claims; and (c) vocalizing the configuration at a tempo of one hundred and forty-four beats per minute until the affirmation is complete. The apparatus comprises a studio in the said district, one writer with authorship intact, and one release date of 20 November 2020, coincident with the album day of Good News.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

1. The invention arises from a period of enforced stillness. During the quarantine interval of March through September 2020, the inventor, who is by trade a rapper-songwriter of Houston, Texas, found the usual apparatus of movement unavailable to her and turned, in consequence, to the one configuration that remained in the room with her: her own body.

2. The inventor reports that the creative process was inspired by her own figure, and that the specification was drafted with additional concentration upon the sizes of the waist and the breasts. The inventor does not claim these regions as novel — the prior art is public and has always been public — but claims the method of looking: the measurement taken by the subject for the subject, without an intermediary, without a reviewer’s note, and without an allowance for shrinking.

3. Prior art of the known and unsatisfactory kind includes every catalogue of figures that has ever required a woman to file her body in triplicate and wait for clearance. The present application is filed in the name of the body itself, and it names no one else. The Office keeps exactly one prior reference on the prior art of a figure dismissed: Max Wilhelm Dehn of Göttingen, whose congruence was cut, examined, and found unreassemblable by his own institution in 1935, and whose old building has since written him an apology in full from the custodian's desk, kept at the letter to Max Wilhelm Dehn, Göttingen, 1951.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method by which the torso is measured, affirmed, and broadcast as a single act of authorship, and an apparatus for carrying that act from a kitchen table in Houston to a release date of 20 November 2020. The method is body-positive in its claim set: each claim asserts a figure as it stands, and no claim is drawn against a figure it does not include. The apparatus is supported on the same day as the debut studio album Good News, from which it issues as the third single, through 1501 Certified Entertainment and 300 Entertainment.

It is an advantage of the present invention that it performs well under review. The critical observers — those who examine applications of this class for the public record — have reported the result affirmatively, praising the lyrics for being body-positive and empowering for women, and noting that the specification reads with an authority the prior art lacks. Where a prior filing of this district describes a figure at a distance, this one is written in the first person, in the present tense, and in full.

CLAIMS

  1. We claim: a method of affirming the human torso, comprising the steps of attending to one’s own figure during quarantine, drafting the specification upon the waist and the breasts, and releasing the result on 20 November 2020 as the third single of Good News.
  2. The method of claim 1, wherein the tempo is 144 beats per minute and the key of the affirmation is the key in which the body stands.
  3. The method of claim 1, wherein a field trial is conducted in a music video, the trial operators comprising Taraji P. Henson, Blac Chyna, and Jordyn Woods, among others.
  4. The method of claim 1, wherein the affirmation is examined by critical observers and passes, the result being recorded as body-positive and empowering for women.
  5. An apparatus for broadcasting the affirmed torso, comprising a writer of Houston, Texas; the labels 1501 Certified Entertainment and 300 Entertainment; and a release date coincident with a debut studio album.
