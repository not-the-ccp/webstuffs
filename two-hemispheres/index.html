Annual Reformulations of the Influenza Vaccine, Sessions 1999–2025

COLD-LP-99 Two Hemispheres, One Shot

Sleeve Notes

Every February, a committee meets in Geneva and argues about next winter. Every September, it meets again, in effect a second time, because the Southern Hemisphere is six months out of phase with the first — a continent where Christmas is summer and where the next flu season is already being guessed. Since 1999 the World Health Organization has issued these two annual recommendations, one for the Northern Hemisphere and one for the Southern, and since 1999 the working assumption has been that last year's vaccine will not fit this year's viruses. The flu drifts. The record has to be re-cut.

The original programme was trivalent: three strains, an A/H1N1, an A/H3N2, and one B lineage, chosen by vote from a long shortlist of viruses collected in the field all year. In the 2012–2013 season the committee added a fourth strain — a second B lineage, B/Yamagata — and the album gained a side. That fourth groove has been the subject of some of the most contested liner discussion in the imprint's history, not least because of where this record ends: the B/Yamagata lineage has not been isolated in the field since March 2020. When the pandemic's mask-wearing, school closures and distance rules emptied the virus's audiences, Yamagata simply stopped being heard. The committee kept the track on the programme for years out of caution, the way a label keeps a song it cannot yet bring itself to delete.

What you are holding, then, is not a concert recording but a habit: twenty-six years of the same session, run twice a year, with the guest list changed each time. It is the closest thing the medical world has to a live album — re-recorded every season, never repeated, and still, somehow, in demand.

Pressing Notes

The last master was cut on a Tuesday in November, when Plant 3 had only the control room lit. The room's skylight stands open in winter for the presses' ventilation, and on the console has sat, since the label's founding, the glazed crock used for the operator's instant coffee — a habit Lindqvist defends on the grounds that no session has ever been compromised by it. That night, through the yard glass, a single-axle delivery truck had parked for the night directly opposite the skylight, and when the test pressing was laid down on the reference turntable, the two shadows fell together across the platter. The sound operator’s log notes that, in the crock’s shadow so the single-axle truck sits in the record. Lindqvist approved the master as it was. The crock stays on the console; the truck, of course, drove off at dawn, and its groove is the only one on the LP that cannot be re-pressed.

Other Recent Pressings on the Imprint

The label runs its liner notes the way the committee runs its seasons: the format is fixed, the contents are re-examined, and nothing is thrown away. Readers who want the imprint's other habits of record-keeping can begin with the safety data sheet for Cressa — a city documented in full and located nowhere, held in the catalogue the way B/Yamagata is held on the disc, an absence that is useful only because it has been witnessed. For a longer account of what it feels like to be on the other side of a re-cut programme, there is Mile Jedinak's assistant coach diary, 2023–24: a whole season of preparations that end in a final you lose, filed honestly in a blue folder. Both are reissues of earlier pressings. For the week the label's own sound operator spent on the far side of the cold chain — an FDA audit of the Malmö vault and a flight to Rockville, Maryland, ending in one doubled fax line no one on the fourth floor could quite explain — there is the Rockville Diary, October 2025, kept in the same hand as the session logs and bound here as an unnumbered supplement to this sleeve. Further east of the cold chain, in a drained hollow in East Frisia where the ground lies at minus 3.44 metres and is not allowed to go lower, the Krummhörn Ditch-Watch & Pump Society keeps a mark of its own in The Pump & Polder, Newsletter No. 47: a single sluice logbook sentence, transcribed word for word and left unfinished, held pending further notice like B6.
