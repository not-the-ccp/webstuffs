The Object, as Listed

One complete Philip Parris Lynott, in his original sleeve and with all four seasons of the box inside. The musician who, out of a working-class Dublin childhood, built a hard rock band — Thin Lizzy — that the archive classifies under Dublin, England, and the West Midlands at once, because he was born in the West Midlands of England and grew up in Dublin with his grandparents. He remained close to his mother, Philomena, throughout his life, and the archive has kept her address card from 1952 in the sleeve with the rest. He fronted several bands as a lead vocalist, including Skid Row alongside Gary Moore, before learning the bass guitar and forming Thin Lizzy in 1969. What you are buying is the frontman: the voice on top, the bass underneath, and the pen that wrote the lot.

Contents, Four Seasons

The box is built in four seasons, and each season holds its window on the man and the band: Spring — the West Midlands, the boarding-school line, the move to Dublin, the grandparents' kitchen; Summer — Skid Row and the Gary Moore years, the singing before the playing, the pubs where the frontman was tried and found wanting and then not wanted; Autumn — Thin Lizzy in formation, 1969, the pick going on the bass, the first working-class tales written down; Winter — the characters, the Celtic figures, the records at the height, and the quiet 1986 that closed the window. The seasons are not a review. They are the way the archive keeps one man in one window without letting him walk out of it.

How to Read the Liner Notes

The liner notes run to four seasons, and the archive is precise about how the buyer should take them: “We are not read it four times with commentary on social and political controversies — four seasons, one window.” That is the printed instruction, kept word for word as it stands in the sleeve, and it reads as a rule of the house: the notes are read through once, season by season, in the single window of the box itself — Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter — and then set back on the shelf. No second pass with a marker in hand; no argument added in the margins about the controversies the years carry; the man is kept in his window, and the window is kept whole.

Provenance & Filing

Filed by the desk beside its 1986-and-after companions, because the year of the close decides the shelf: the box sits next to the liner notes for ‘One Ninety-One Chairs’, The Shed’s Paper Box, the long following of 1948–1989 kept under the same rule of one box, one window, and no commentary added after the fact. For the record of what Dublin had already done with a frontman by the time the window closed, the desk also keeps entry MF-1958, Marie Fredriksson, formed with Per Gessle in 1986 — the same winter the present catalogue number went quiet, filed two streets over in a different language.
