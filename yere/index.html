Post 1 of 6 14 March 2011, 07:42 Re: the name Yere, both given and surname — a roll call

I am opening this thread properly, because three separate members have hit me in private messages about the same name and the board should stop answering the same question by letter. Yere works both as a given name and as a surname, and the roll call the board has been building is, as far as I can account for it, four long.

  1. Mama Bah-Yéré, born 1992 — the given-name form, written with the acute on the final é when the family says so.
  2. Menzie Yere, Beninese footballer, born 1983 — the surname form, and he played it out in the box every week.
  3. Yere Goud, Papua New Guinean rugby league footballer, born 1971 — given name again, out of Lae, and the oldest of the four by a good dozen years.
  4. Yere Chandran, Indian cricketer — the board’s weakest entry; details below from whoever remembers his card.

The pattern is the point: one name, two jobs. It sits in the first-name slot in Cotonou and in Port Moresby, and in the surname slot in Ahmedabad. If you know a fifth bearer, or a spelling variant the board has missed, post it. This thread will stay pinned.

Post 2 of 6 14 March 2011, 09:15 Re: the name Yere — the shelf, the count, and Manila

Adding to the thread from the lending shelf, because the shelf is where this name keeps resurfacing. I keep the board’s printed matter alongside the field material — the Knight Island field notes and the Dumbarton Bridge field manual chapter both sit on it, three lanes each way, and the name Yere appears in a Manila ledger that came down from the city in a box of salt-stiff paper.

The ledger was re-catalogued last week and I owe the board the full figure before the box goes back. The card catalogue lists the bridge chapter, the season card, the almanac entry — and then the ledger’s own closing page, which I copy here exactly as received, because the shelf seems to collect sentences that no one fully explains and I would rather preserve the oddity than correct it:

Books in other shapes too: the desk (chair); Mara Vale — melodica and Elias Rook spoke for from Manila — counted 31 elective and appointed seats .

What I can make of it: Mara Vale was the one who played the melodica at the Manila session, she spoke in the place of Elias Rook, and the count she delivered covered a roster of thirty-one seats, elective and appointed mixed on one page. Thirty-one is the number the ledger stands on. It is not a name count — I checked, Yere appears twice in the box — but I am posting it here because the Onomast’s Board and the board whose seats she counted have been sitting in the same drawer for years, and the Kanawha Board thread is the sister record for how appointed seats and elected ones share a page when a governor signs in the missing member.

One more item from the box, for the thread’s interest in names that keep two jobs: the Log-keeper’s Apology of HMS Formidable (67), where the name Formidable serves as the ship’s name and as a run of form on the same line of the log, one spelling in two slots exactly as Yere does first-name and surname duty, and where the clerk’s apology keeps its crossed-out line entered rather than corrected — the board’s own rule, in a ship’s hand.

Post 3 of 6 15 March 2011, 22:03 Re: the name Yere — the Goud family say it is a first name

Confirming entry three, because he was born 1971 in the Lae district and his brothers were there to check the birth year against the parish book. Yere Goud came up through the PNG rugby league system — club football on the mudflats, then provincial duty, and the name went around the dressing rooms exactly as written: Yere first, Goud after. The Goud side of the family is a surname; no one on it takes Yere as the family name.

He kept a habit of signing letters with both names in either order depending on which hand was holding the pen, so the board should accept both orderings as his and log the surname as Goud. If anyone has a match report from the late seventies with his name misspelled “Yere,” post it — the board keeps a misspellings drawer for exactly this.

Post 4 of 6 16 March 2011, 06:58 Re: the name Yere — Menzie, born 1983, the surname holds

Entry two is solid. Menzie Yere, born 1983, Beninese by the register and by the boot I keep in the club window. For him the name is a surname, full stop: Menzie is the given name, Yere is what the families behind him carry, and he took the surname onto the pitch for the whole of his career. The club that first registered him lists him as M. Yere on every sheet, which is the cleanest proof the board could ask for of which slot the name sits in.

Since this thread was pinned, the surname has crossed to a second ocean and the board now keeps a proper field entry on him: Menzie Yere — a travel guide entry, No. 215, where the same 1983 register boy turns out to have a green passport, a try ledger of 195 in 292, and a fish market in Port Moresby that remembers him by his first name. The board’s rule applies to the guide as well: the number the steward gives is the number kept.

So the thread’s rule, restated for the record: the same spelling, Yere, does first-name duty in at least two countries and surname duty in a third, and no bearer complains about the board’s bookkeeping. That is what a name like this is worth — it fits the rooms it is asked to fill.

Filling in the two gaps. Yere Chandran: born 1988 in Kozhikode, first-class cricketer, a batting all-rounder of modest totals and good reflexes behind the wicket; he played a short first-class spell for the state side before the name left the county lists, and his card on the board is thin, which is why the opening post called it the weakest entry. It is a given name in his case too, so the count stands: given name in three of the four, surname in one.

And Mama Bah-Yéré, born 1992 — the youngest of the four, and the only hyphenated spelling the board carries. The family has insisted on the acute accent in correspondence, and the board logs it as Yéré when the family writes to us and Yere everywhere else, with a cross-reference between the two cards so a search in either spelling lands on the same person. The roll call, fully accounted for: 1971, 1983, 1988, 1992 — twenty-one years from the oldest to the youngest, and one name holding the whole span together.
