Field Notes of a Naturalist · Volume the Fourth · filed beside Notice No. 14/2024

Dinaric Field Notes

A naturalist’s journal of Bosnia and Herzegovina, kept at the valley bench, October 2024

Kept by Lejla Kovač, naturalist, Gornje Vukovsko, cousin of the chef at the Sana stewpan kitchen, who cracks the bitter almonds at table 20. Filed in cabinet 9, municipal office, Radna cesta 12, Kupres 83273.

Entry 0 · the territory · written at the bench beside the parcel post

☂ 9°C, rain by evening · wind SW, off the Dinaric · elevation 712 m

The territory is called Bosnia and Herzegovina, often referred to as Bosnia-Herzegovina or simply Bosnia. It is a country in Southeastern Europe, situated on the Balkan Peninsula, and it borders Serbia to the east, Montenegro to the southeast, and Croatia to the north and southwest, with a coast of twenty kilometres — twelve miles — on the Adriatic Sea in the south. I keep two notebooks: this one holds the territory whole, and the other holds only the valley where the Sana runs through the stone; the valley enters this notebook as page 823, and the territory is the rest. Where the municipal notice speaks of the ravine that gave up the vellum leaf, this notebook speaks of the country the ravine belongs to.

Entry 1 · 11 October 2024 · Gornje Vukovsko, Municipality of Kupres, Canton 10

☁ 6°C first light · the Sana the colour of the autumn · forty-one sheep counted

Idriz Hodžić’s ewes number forty-one, and not one was lost in the finding, which the register states and I confirm from the lower paddock. The territory, in this notebook, begins and ends with the valley for now, because it was in the ravine of the Sana that an oilcan caught the last of the afternoon sun on 3 October, and out of the oilcan came the leaf — the vellum, the four voices of the Gloria, the one line no one in Kupres can date. Its full account is in the office’s lost-and-found notice, No. 14/2024, posted beside the parcel post, open until 3 October 2025, with a reward of 300 convertible marks from a municipal chest that is not a large chest but is a chest. A naturalist’s territory is measured in ravines and in chests, and this one has both.

Entry 2 · the mountains · read from the bench, the peaks over the western ridge

❄ cold, the year’s first snow on Maglić · moderate continental, keeping to its rule

The country keeps a moderate continental climate with hot summers and cold, snowy winters, and it keeps to the rule strictly enough that the bench is empty from November through March and full from May. The geography is largely mountainous, particularly in the central and eastern regions, which are dominated by the Dinaric Alps. From this bench one can count the ridges in the dusk the way one counts sheep: Maglić at 2,386 metres, the territory’s highest, stands in the east, and the range runs north and south through the centre of the country like a spine with the Sana running down its length. I have not climbed Maglić. I have weighed myself on the bench scale before and after thinking about it, which is the honest number.

Entry 3 · 19 October 2024 · southward, to the smaller region

☀ 19°C at the river mouth · Mediterranean air, limestone everywhere

Herzegovina, the smaller, southern region, has a Mediterranean climate and is mostly mountainous. One drives south out of the cold and the air changes its mind at roughly the place where the limestone outcrops the forest; I noted it the way one notes weather, because that is what it is, a different weather in the same country. At Blagaj the river carves a way out of the stone before it gives its name to the valley, and the country’s whole Adriatic coastline is twenty kilometres long — the twelve miles at Neum, where the territory touches the sea with what I can only describe as a pressed flower between hard covers: brief, exact, and kept by everyone. Sarajevo is the capital and the largest city of the country, a day’s travel up the other way from this valley, in a basin of its own with the Baščaršja at its middle; it is where the municipal secretary’s letter about the leaf’s cathedral will eventually be read by a scholar with a loupe and not a shepherd’s pride.

Entry 4 · the almonds · specimen 47, pressed in the back of this notebook

⌛ picked at table 20’s hour, UTC+10:00 · two sorts, two manners of finding

The almond is the one specimen this notebook has to describe twice, and the old key, which the shop keeps in a tin under the parcel post, has the better sentence. Of the almond it says in full:

The best sorts of almonds,--sweet and bitter; but they differ very essentially both in the finding.

I agree with the key in every particular. The sweet almond is found by appointment: it is grown, it waits on its tree at the orchard end of the valley in March, its blossom the colour of a wash of the Sana at dawn, and its kernel is weighed at the Trgovina Kovač shop in grams, with the price said once and not again. The bitter almond is found by accident: it grows on the dry limestone southward, in the scrub of the Herzegovina corner above the sea road, and one comes upon it under a hedge in May the way one comes upon the oilcan in an oilcan — rusted shut, deliberate-seeming, and better for the finding. One finds the sweet almond on a list; one finds the bitter almond on one’s knees. Both kernels are kept in wax paper, the same wrapping as the envelope the leaf came up in out of the ravine, and the bitter one is what I crack at lunch, on the bench table, because the table at UTC+10:00 is where the hour is long enough for a whole meal and a whole notebook. The kitchen’s own account of the wall of twenty-eight clocks, the half-table for the small minutes, and the sewn-up lamb stew that travels on the hour is written up in the valley correspondent’s table review, Dining Across the Meridian, filed in cabinet 9 in the same drawer as this entry, which is to say the almonds have their table set, whatever else happens to them.

Entry 5 · closing the volume · cabinet 9, drawer of the lock that no longer locks

✒ ink dried before posting · the notebook filed, the leaves left alone

This volume is filed beside the leaf, beside the brass placard of International Atomic Time, and beside whatever else the drawer has room for; the drawer has room for the territory, because the territory is only so much paper. For the fixing of any position in it — the ravine, the orchard, the bench, the twelve miles of Neum — I refer the reader to Chapter 4 of the field manual, on the geographic coordinate system, which treats latitude and longitude as angles on the Earth and does not, to my mind, exaggerate once. The ewes are counted, the stew is sewn, the clocks are wrong on purpose, and the almonds are pressed flat between pages 823 and 824 of the valley, where the territory keeps them. That is the whole of the country, as far as this bench can tell: mountainous, continental, Mediterranean, and twenty kilometres of sea, with a ravine in it that once returned a leaf and will, on the evidence, return more.

— L. K. Gornje Vukovsko, autumn 2024
