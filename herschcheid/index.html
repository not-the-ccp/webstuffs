Editor’s notice: the Leppe is low this month; the planked footbridge at Herbeck is dry to the fourth post, and the crossing clock at the tower on Kirchplatz is keeping true time.

Verloren & Gefunden lost & found

LOST No. 0412

One small arachnid, grey-brown, ant-shaped, 8 mm; first pair of legs held out in front, head angled to one side; answers to the name zhaoi; last seen 6 June on a lichen boulder at Kiersper Berg, 7:40 a.m. sharp, where she is, evidently, every morning. Not for sale, and never was, which is the kind of fact that does not go in a column. Reward: a cork ramp, four centimetres tall. Her current pose, and frame no. 214 of her record, are kept in the Bocusoides build log at a garage bench in Auckland, and the owner there is willing to compare frames by post.

FOUND No. 0413

One leather lash, hand-finished, three knots, faintly salt-weathered, left on the bus bench at Lippeweg on Tuesday. It suits none of our boats and none of our hands, but the loft above the beach at Saltfleet keeps a catalogue that sets it down, stitch for stitch, as the Saltfleet Whip, hand-tied coastal lash. The bench remains until claimed; the catalogue remains where it is.

IN MEMORY No. 0414

The planked footbridge at Herbeck, 1962–2024. Sixty-two years of cattle, of children’s bicycles, of one stopwatch. Replaced this week by a steel span, six metres, no planks, no fourth post. The crossing is now two seconds, and we notice. Flowers are not required; a time, honestly kept, is accepted.

Zu Verkaufen for sale

A BOOK No. 0415 · new

Herschcheid in Three Pages, by Pfarrer J. Kruse, 1971. The whole of Herbeck, the Hillebach, the school, the bakery, the cemetery, and the Leppe itself, in twelve printed pages and one map, with every place you can actually stand named exactly once. The blurb on the back cover, kept verbatim by the printer and by us: “The crossing lasts three seconds, and neither should a guidebook.” He timed it on a stopwatch at the Herbeck bridge and kept the tempo for the whole book; a reader has reported walking the Leppe bank while turning pages and arriving at the bakery precisely as the bread came out. First copy with dust jacket, twelve marks; a second copy, soft and creased at the map, eight.

A PUMP No. 0416

One polder pump, cast iron, 437 kilograms, decommissioned at the Freepsumer Meer. Its former society keeps a fine account of the drained lake at minus 3.44 metres and of the sluice logbook of 1971; see the waterkeepers’ newsletter, No. 47, which also preserves one sentence they never finished. The pump is for sale; the sentence is not included, and cannot be. Delivery at the buyer’s risk and on the buyer’s trailer.

TWO STOOL SEATS No. 0417

Beech, from the counter of the bakery, 1958; one seat carries a ring in the grain the size of a teacup. Sold as a pair only; the bakery insists the counter was never meant to be three. Five marks. The bread from the counter is still good, for the record.

Gewünscht wanted

WANTED No. 0418

One (1) deposition officer, standing-committee references desired, patient with scrubber logs and with diamonds the size of a rough crystal. The committee’s recent minutes, No. 217, R.A. Riam Group reactor 3 at 1,520 °C, are on file and may be inspected by appointment: the council minutes keep the whole account, letter from Iba included. Apply in person; the committee does not correspond about its own minutes.

WANTED No. 0419

A compositor who works quickly. The pastor’s third edition of the three-page book is in hand and the map needs one new steel span. The setter must compose, proof, and pull the sheet before the clock hands on the tower at Kirchplatz cross at noon. No explanation is to be printed in the margin. Three seconds.

PERSONALS No. 0420

G. (H.) — to the one who leaves the lens case open on the ridge walk: she is not your spider, the cork ramp is, and the frames are being compared in Auckland. — B. (Herbeck), to the bridge: two seconds, honestly kept, is the whole of our gratitude.

A COMPAREMENT No. 0421

Our field desk keeps a return of twenty-two wards, counted overnight at a town hall across the water: the Wirral return of 1994, FM-7 Chapter 9, struck 19:42 to 02:15, 40 to 22 with the remainder printed rather than hidden. One clerk in that chapter wrote a margin note nobody has been able to explain, and we suspect ours, at Kirchplatz, could read it. Proposals for the comparement at the counter; three seconds, honestly kept, per visit.
