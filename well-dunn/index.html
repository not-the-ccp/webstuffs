A. BELTS & CHAMPIONSHIP

FOUND

One PNW Tag Team Championship

Found, 1991, in the back of a dressing room after a house show in the Pacific Northwest, leaning on a locker with its plate scratched by a knee. Pacific Northwest Wrestling held it first for the team of Rex King and Steve Doll, who wore the names Timothy Well and Steven Dunn while they wore the team name Well Dunn — a play on the term “well done,” as the announcers were never tired of saying. Two titles were won under that arrangement; this one is the heavier of the two. Call the front office at 555-0117 after the bell. No middlemen, and no referees.

FOUND

USWA belt, brass cold, Texas heat

Recovered from a garment bag in a Tulsa arena where the United States Wrestling Association ran its card and the air conditioning ran its last honest month of the year. The belt has seen the South Central States and one stretch of highway that no map printed. It was held, per the book, while King and Doll were still trading places with their real names. Claim by description of the scratch on the “A.”

LOST

WWC title — San Juan, one night

Lost, or left on purpose, in the locker room of the World Wrestling Council coliseum in San Juan. The belt came off a shoulder and onto a bench. The bus left without it, or with it, and the two accounts differ exactly the way two accounts should. A reward of one win and one handshake is offered to the man who knows where the bench is now.

FOUND

Music City belt, Nashville

Found, still warm, in the back row of a building in Nashville that booked the Music City Wrestling card — the fourth belt, the last one of the four, the one they carried north into the big promotion. It hums faintly if you are close enough to the window, which is either a feature or a complaint. Owner will consider it settled by a demonstration match, best of one.

B. HIRE & WORK

FOR HIRE

Two men. One name. Four belts.

WELL DUNN — also booked as THE SOUTHERN ROCKERS — are two professional wrestlers, Rex King at “Timothy Well” and Steve Doll at “Steven Dunn,” who compete as a tag team and invoice as one. The team name is a play on the term “well done”: split the words and two men stand under them, which is exactly how many you get. Their record: championships in Pacific Northwest Wrestling, the United States Wrestling Association, the World Wrestling Council, and Music City Wrestling, and then the World Wrestling Federation, 1993 to 1995, where the team faced the promotion's top tag teams on the big card and were contenders for the tag team championship, including a title shot at a cold Hartford show in February when the pyro went off and the crowd came over to the wrong side of the barricade. Rates: one match, two matches, a tournament bracket, or the whole road through the South with the van listed below. They will work Tuesday. They prefer Thursday.

Booking through the front office only. The name must be spelled Well Dunn, two words, or the men do not walk the ramp.

WANTED

Road manager, must count

Wanted: a road manager for two wrestlers and four belts, one who can drive a 1993 Ford E-350, keep the drum kit level, and count in the dark. The job is mostly waiting, which is the whole job. Applicants should be able to keep a promise made at 2 a.m. on a service road and still shake hands at 7 a.m. Send the price of your sleep to the front office.

C. STORMS & ODD LOTS

STORM SIGNAL — ARENA LOT

Weather desk standing order, No. 7

The weather desk at the arena has issued a standing order for storm nights over the dome, and it is kept word for word, because the order itself is the number. When the flash comes over the lot and the thunder follows in, the pyro board runs the count: Count the seconds between lightning and thunder and divide by the politician, who appears in the country. The country, for this purpose, is the one on the back of the tour van, and the politician on it counts as three. The quotient is the number of pyro cues to cut from that night's card, and the balance is the number the men are paid per cue in shakes of the hand. In 1995, on the Hartford night, the count came down at twelve, the quotient came out clean at four, and the crowd never once learned it had been arithmetic.

The desk is quiet. When it speaks, it speaks in seconds.

ODD LOTS

Second names, two

For sale or for keep: the two second names, “Timothy” and “Steven,” still warm from the program book. They came in as a set with the team name and they are offered only as a set, because separately they are just names, and a man is worth more as half a pun than whole. The original, unsplit “Well Dunn” goes with them at no extra charge, and has already.

D. VEHICLES

FOR SALE

1993 Ford E-350, “the long bus”

One 1993 Ford E-350 cutaway, the road bus that carried Well Dunn from the Pacific Northwest to Nashville and then north, 118,442 miles, four belts' worth of weight, one drum kit bolted flat. Smells of rain, road salt, and one February of pyro. Engine knocks in a key the men say they can wrestle to. First to see it buys it, or at least buys the window seat on the left, which is the good one.

FOR RENT

Back row, two seats

For rent by the night, the back row of the arena, the two seats under the weather desk. From them you can see the count, the quotient, and the politician's van. Reserved for the man who kept the ledger in 1994. Price: one true story, told standing.

E. PERSONALS

PERSONAL

Southern

To the promoter who read this page on the night of the Hartford card and stayed: we are the two men under one name, and the pun is not a joke, it is a contract. We would be well met to do it again. — T.W. & S.D.

NOTICE

As in well done

To everyone who has ever read “Well Dunn” as one word and been corrected: the correction is the point. Two words, two men, one done thing. The arena thanks you for the argument; it has never had a better one, and it books more of them.

F. LEDGERS & FIELD NOTES

READ

The retreat that kept its distance

For the man who files the night fight: Prairie Grove — Field Thread No. 191, the December 7, 1862 night fight where the rearguard held the ground in the dark and the retreat went to the Arkansas river on its own schedule. The Well Dunn book keeps this thread in its drawer because a team that has faced the top of the division and lived to bill itself again has read it twice, once standing.

READ

A county of 10,371, measured

For the man who keeps the small places: Cadillac, Michigan — Field Notes of a Naturalist, No. 27, seat of Wexford County, 10,371 souls at the census, the Clam River into the Muskegon watershed, and one split wreck with a single plate — the plate being the lesson, the wreck being the decoration. Filed beside the Hartford program because both were counted in the dark.
