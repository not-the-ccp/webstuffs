Republic of the Philippines

Commission on Elections

Second Division · Office of the Clerk · Intramuros, Manila

In re: Partido Magsaysay

Case No. COMELEC-SP-23-0417 · Petition for Cancellation of Registration and Accreditation · Heard 14 March 2024

Petitioner: Office of the Solicitor General  ·  Respondent: Partido Magsaysay, through its chairman, Atty. Leopoldo T. Dalisay Jr.

Presiding: Commissioner Ester M. Villafuerte  ·  For the petitioner: Atty. Sandra L. Ponce  ·  For the respondent: Atty. Leopoldo T. Dalisay Jr.  ·  Sworn witness: Mateo D. Reyes, secretary, Naga Listeners' Club, Naga City, Camarines Sur

Comm. Villafuerte: The record will reflect that we resume the hearing on the petition to cancel the registration and accreditation of Partido Magsaysay, which first sought accreditation in Camarines Sur in the run-up to the 2019 elections. Counsel for the petitioner, you may call your first witness.
Atty. Ponce: Thank you, Commissioner. The petitioner calls Mateo D. Reyes, secretary of the Naga Listeners' Club.
Comm. Villafuerte: Mr. Reyes, you are under oath. State your name and address for the record.
Mateo D. Reyes: Mateo D. Reyes. I live on Magsaysay Avenue, Naga City, Camarines Sur. I keep the register of the Naga Listeners' Club, which meets above the old bakery, two doors from the cathedral.
Atty. Ponce: Mr. Reyes, in late 2018 did an organization calling itself Partido Magsaysay use space in your clubhouse?
Mateo D. Reyes: It did. The back room — the one with the radiator that never worked. From October 2018 to May 2019 they paid in cash, two hundred pesos a week, sometimes three weeks late. Their chairman was Atty. Leopoldo T. Dalisay Jr., who said the party took its name from the avenue, not from the president. I remember it because Salvio Fortuno came by once — the broadcaster, the former Representative for Camarines Sur's fourth and fifth districts — and he asked whether we were turning into a precinct house. I told him we were still trading valves and QSL cards.
Atty. Ponce: Did you find anything in the back room after they left?
Mateo D. Reyes: We found part of a notebook. Someone had torn a page out, but not cleanly. The slip was tucked behind the radiator, ruled like a notebook page. I pasted it into the club's commonplace book, the same way we paste clippings and receipts, and I marked it Exhibit B when the Solicitor General's office wrote to us in January.
Atty. Ponce: I offer Exhibit B into evidence. Mr. Reyes, please read what is written on that slip.
Mateo D. Reyes: [Reading from Exhibit B.]
"The map calls it an opportunity is given to them, and the skeleton of a cigarette lighter — the river brings what the Bay forgets."
Atty. Dalisay: Objection, Commissioner. That is not evidence. It is a sentence without an author, without a date, without a precinct.
Atty. Ponce: It has a map, and a river, and a lighter reduced to its skeleton. What more does a case need?
Comm. Villafuerte: Objection overruled as to form; the weight is for the Division. Mr. Reyes, do you know what the sentence means?
Mateo D. Reyes: I do not. "The map" — every election year the barangay captains spread precinct maps across our clubhouse table, red lines and blue. "An opportunity is given to them" — the voters, I suppose. Every party offers voters an opportunity, or says it does. The skeleton of a cigarette lighter — we found an actual lighter in the Bicol River last July, all rust and no spark. And "the river brings what the Bay forgets" — San Miguel Bay is downstream of us; whatever the water drops, the mud keeps.
Atty. Dalisay: The witness is speculating about poetry. My client is a political party: an organization that coordinates candidates to compete in elections and to participate in governance. Its members hold similar ideas; it promotes policy goals. That is what political parties do in almost every country, and the registration of one cannot turn on a scrap of paper found behind a radiator.
Atty. Ponce: Except that the scrap names no candidates and no platform. It names only a map, an opportunity, and a lighter. The petitioner submits that Partido Magsaysay exists on paper only — no chapters, no conventions, no members on oath, a back room above a bakery, and a sentence that no one can explain. A party that cannot account for itself ought not to appear on the ballot.
Comm. Villafuerte: Counsel will address the Division in writing by 28 March. The record will also reflect a correspondence from a naturalist working the marshes of the Napa River in California, who copied the same phrase — "the river brings what the Bay forgets" — into her field notes from Knight Island. Coincidence or not, the Division notes it. We stand in recess until further order.
[The excerpt ends here. The Division took the petition under advisement.]
