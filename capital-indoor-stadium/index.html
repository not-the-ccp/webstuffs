Capital Indoor Stadium

Changping District, Beijing · Entry No. 11 of the Guide · set down for the season of 2022

The building has been open since 1968 and carries the scar of the 1990 flood in its floor plans; it was refloored for 2008 and re-iced, with a new ribbon of LED seating and a roof that lifts in four panes, for the Games of 2022. It is a large, polite, white hall, and on the first Friday of the second week of February it is the loudest place in China. The women's singles of figure skating was skated here on 15 and 17 February, thirty skaters from eighteen nations, and the result is short enough to say once and long enough to say twice.

The ice, and the hours

Arrive by the north gate for the short programme on the 15th; the hall fills by six in the evening and the sound it makes when it is full is a single low note, like a train about to arrive. The short programme runs to the code — twenty minutes, no more — and is judged the way a doorway is judged: quickly, and for good. On the 17th the free skating follows, four minutes and eight seconds of music per skater, and the hall holds its note for the whole of it. The ice is 3,000 square metres; the boards are the only part of the building the skaters touch, and they touch it often enough to have worn it.

The field that mattered here was small and Russian, and the medal table was read from it. Anna Shcherbakova, of the Russian Olympic Committee, took the gold; Alexandra Trusova, also of the Committee, the silver, on the back of a free skating of 155.10 — four quadruple jumps, the first ever landed in a women's free at the Winter Games — while Kaori Sakamoto of Japan, who skated the shortest programme in the competition on 80.29 and then the most complete free of any skater not from the Committee, took the bronze. The hall clapped for the Japanese skater through the entire programme, which the television pictures of the day mostly did not show.

The margin

The competition was overshadowed by one fact that the timetable of the event does not repeat: Kamila Valieva of the Russian Olympic Committee, seventeen, had tested positive for a banned substance in the pre-Games period, and the Court of Arbitration for Sport, acting on an application filed in the hours before the short programme, ruled that she be allowed to compete pending a formal outcome of the investigation. She led the short at 81.05, fell twice in the free on the 17th and finished fourth on 155.47, and was stripped of the standing by the Court of Arbitration in November 2022. The Guide does not take a side on the affair; the Guide records that the hall, on the morning of the 18th, emptied by the north gate in the ordinary way, and that the ice was resurfaced by ten.

It notes, too, that the entry was first written — in pencil, in a hand that has since faded — by the late Ivan Kirill Morozov, a skating instructor of forty years who kept the old ice at this address through the nineties and the hundreds, and who taught the first of the Guide's own skaters her back flip in the winter of 1996. He died on 4 November 2014 · 9:04 a.m. The Guide keeps the hour because it is the same minute at which the 257 arrives at Khulna at the end of its morning working, and because a guide is obliged to keep the whole of its facts in order, and not one more than the whole; see the timetable, The Dhaka Dawn Express — Route 6, where 09:04 is the arrival of a train and nothing else, and where the two are filed under the same rule.

Practicalities

From central Beijing, the subway to the stadium stop is twenty-two minutes in the off-hours and takes no interest in what has happened on the ice. The north gate has a small shop that sells the programme, the medal replica, and a blue slip of card on which the hall's only house joke is printed; the slip is sold, not given. Tea is hot at the south concourse and wrong at the north. For a wider reading of the city that the stadium sits in — the Yellow Road, the banquets, the ten-person table — the Guide refers its reader to its own earlier entry, The Celestial Empire — A Visitor's Brochure, Canton & the Great Road.

One last note, for the keeper of this leaf. The hall's book of unclaimed property is small but not empty; the Guide holds, among other things, a record of the missing-descent notices kept for the basement desk at 3400 N. Willamette, Lost & Found — Notice 4172, on the theory that a free skating and a gradient are the same object seen from opposite directions, and that both of them end, eventually, in a place that is flat.
