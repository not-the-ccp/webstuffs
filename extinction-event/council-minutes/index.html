Council of the Ledger · Coopers’ Lane School · in session in the kitchen above the bakery, Magsaysay Avenue

Minutes No. 231

On the Last Day, and the Loaf — being an account of expectations of the end of the present age, human history, or the world itself

The third Tuesday of Term 3 · called to order at 18:42, nine minutes of bread still in the oven · the tide bell has not yet rung

Quorum

Chaired by
Mr. Alistair Delgado, Natural Science and General Knowledge
Clerk
Etta Collins, grandmother of the house, keeper of the ledger shelf
Reporter
Mabel E. Collins, Class 8C, seat 22 — author of the report on the five endings, filed at The Extinction Event — Report, Class 8C
Exhibitor
Tomás Reyes, fishmonger of the harbour, admitted by proxy in the leaf of his almanac pasted into the report, the page that says with meat baked in ovens, they are wanted, and in season from June to August
Witness, nonvoting
The starling who kept the rigging above the bakery company from 1998, counted twice, once by the window and once by the radio, as she is always counted
Absent
The great auk, in perpetuity, since the last confirmed pair sat on Eldey in the spring of 1844; and the Council of the Marble Hall, which kept its own minutes No. 230 in another room and sealed them with a gold wafer at 19:11

The quorum was found to be the oven, which is always full, and the two of us at the table, which is always read. The council is constituted.

Item 1 — The word

The chair defined the subject for the record in its Greek: eschatology, from “ἔσχατος” (éskhatos), ‘last’, and the usual ‘-logy’ — the science of last things. It concerns expectations of the end of the present age, human history, or the world itself: the end times, which several world religions predict, and which teach that the negative events of the world will not merely continue but reach a climax. The clerk observed that the last letter of the subject is the first letter of the argument, and that the council has never yet lost an argument it started at the last letter. The council concurred and moved on.

Item 2 — The report of the last things

Mabel Collins presented her report, Form 12, nine pages, in which the end of the world is not a single day but a wall with five dark bands in it — the Ordovician–Silurian, the late Devonian, the end-Permian, the end-Triassic, and the K–Pg at 66 million years ago — and which carries one page pasted from the harbour fishmonger’s almanac. The council took note that belief that the end of the world is imminent is known as apocalypticism, and that over time it has been held both by members of mainstream religions and by doomsday cults; the council found no distinction between the two in the rock, and declined to make one. It further took note that in the context of mysticism the term refers metaphorically to the end of ordinary reality and to reunion with the divine, and that the household’s ordinary reality, for purposes of this minute, ends at 19:11 on the third Tuesday of every term, when the tide bell rings and the kitchen light goes red.

Item 3 — The cult of 1980

Mr. Reyes exhibited, from memory and from the harbour wall, a calendar of the imminent end dated to the year 1980, posted in a hand that the family still declines to identify. The doomsday had come and gone without the world, and the house had answered the way the house answers everything, which is by baking. Etta Collins moved that the answer be recorded as the answer, and moved further that a loaf be set against the prophecy: Card 298 of the ledger, the beet-and-blackstrap Carboniferous Coal Loaf, filed at Carboniferous Coal Loaf — A Family Recipe, baked at 325°F the August of 1980 and every August since. The motion was carried unanimously on the smell. The clerk entered that the prophecy has been answered once per August, in the same oven, at the same temperature, and that the council has yet to see an apocalypse survive a draught of kitchen air. For the account of the carboniferous itself, the period the family bakes, 358.86 to 298.9 million years ago, the council referred to the reporter’s coal section, where the oven is held to be a kind of boundary the family keeps on its own terms.

Item 4 — The prophesied, and the kept

Many religions, the chair reminded the council, treat eschatology as a future event prophesied in sacred texts or in folklore; the ledger, it was agreed, is a third instrument of the same kind, and the household keeps all three on the same shelf. What the texts prophesy, the rock testifies; what the rock testifies, the ledger keeps warm. The neighbouring registers were cited for the record: the patent office’s summary of Specification No. BA-1899-64407, the acetylsalicylic acid tablet whose batch number is entered in the same hand as this minute, filed at Acetylsalicylic Acid (Aspirin) — Patent Application Summary; and the register kept by the broadcaster at the clubhouse above the bakery, Salvio Fortuno — The Carrier Wave, whose rigging is kept for its own beauty because the ship never left the harbour, which is the council’s favourite sentence in any prophecy it has ever read.

Item 5 — Filing of these minutes

The chair reminded the council where things live. These minutes, No. 231, go to the ledger shelf in the kitchen above the bakery, Magsaysay Avenue, in the hand the household uses for its dates. Mabel’s report on the five endings, and the harbour almanac page pasted into it, are the two neighbours of this minute. Both records are filed one shelf above this one. The council noted the arrangement with approval: the account of what has ended sits above the account of what is expected to end, and the bread sits below both, at 325°, in the only heat in the house that the family trusts. The minute is filed beside the report at The Extinction Event — Report, Class 8C, and the whole shelf may be reached from the front of the house, two doors in past the oven.

Resolutions

  1. SEALED That the end of the world be held, for purposes of this house, a future event prophesied in sacred texts or in folklore, and therefore a matter of record rather than of panic.
  2. SEALED That the five endings, from 444 to 66 million years ago, remain boundaries and not conclusions, and that the black seam of the Great Dying, the worst ratio in the record, be carried as the ledger’s standing caution: whatever is voided, record.
  3. SEALED That the oven at 325°F be held as the council’s own end times, and the Carboniferous Coal Loaf as its prophet, which has been right about August every time and wrong about the world none of them.
  4. SEALED That these minutes be read twice, as everything in this house is read twice, and that the second reading be the one the registrar out of the yard of Magsaysay Avenue holds to the light one day and reads the shape of us through.

Adjournment

The minutes were read a first time at 19:09, a second time at 19:10, and the tide bell rang at 19:11, twice, and the kitchen light went red, and the ordinary reality of the house, for the purposes of mysticism, ended on time. The council found that no world had ended, that one loaf had, and that the record was complete: whatever is voided, record; whatever is recorded, bake.
