MERIDIAN & COLD STORAGE · MRC-114 · STEREO · 33′/3 RPM

The Limestone Sessions

Field Recordings of the B. Altman and Company Building

Pressed at 355–371 Fifth Avenue · New York, New York · November 1978 · All rights reserved

Liner Notes

These recordings were made between October and December 1978 in and around the B. Altman and Company Building, the department-store block that occupies the whole city block between Fifth Avenue, Madison Avenue, 34th Street, and 35th Street, directly opposite the Empire State Building. Its primary address is 355–371 Fifth Avenue, and its facade, made largely of French limestone, is what this album is really about. The building was designed by Trowbridge & Livingston in the Italian Renaissance Revival style; most of it stands eight stories tall, but the Madison Avenue end rises to thirteen, and it is that thirteenth story—the part of the facade where the limestone gives out—that gave the sessions their longest takes and their only argument.

Side A was cut at street level, 06:00 to 07:15, before the crosswalks began. The microphone sat in the Fifth Avenue doorway facing east, so the Empire State Building arrives in every track as a low, unbroken pressure of glass and steel, and the limestone is closer than it has any right to be. “Pilotis & Pilasters” (track 1, 7:42) is simply the building breathing: set-back shadows at the ninth-floor line, the ironwork of the balconies, a water main the size of a man’s arm doing its slow daily work. Track 3, “Gondola,” is the maintenance platform crossing the Madison Avenue face in the rain; the rain stopped at 06:47 and the track does not, which is why it ends where it does.

Sides B and C went inside, on the floor that had been the women’s dress department, where the plaster still carries the old ceiling rosettes in their original stucco. The room holds a quarter-note reverb that the engineers at Cold Storage declined to equalize out; every recording from that floor has a tail on it, and the reverb is in the album jacket, if you look at the way the limestone photograph dissolves at the right margin. On side C, “Escalator Study, No. 4” (11:03) is the building’s own metronome: two flights, one moving, one stopped, the moving one exactly one beat behind.

Side D is shorter than the others and it is not a field recording at all. On 14 December 1978, the Meridian & Cold Storage offices in the building’s basement rented a tape recorder at a courthouse across the 34th Street line, and the clerk on duty permitted us to record a short proceeding concerning the stone of the Madison Avenue end—whether the upper stories were to be read as limestone or not, and who, if anyone, was entitled to the difference in value. The recorder ran for nine minutes and fourteen seconds. The session’s only page of the official record, which we reproduce here in the note page of the original jacket, reads:

Present: Eeva Laaksonen, court clerk; Pekka Rautio, senior counsel, for the back of a small dry leaf, which is disputed.

We do not know what the leaf was. A dry leaf had been carried into the basement in a courier’s coat pocket on the 12th, no one had claimed it, and the clerk entered it in evidence as “an object of pale, matte surface, roughly the dimensions of a palm,” which is exactly the dimensions of the limestone sample the two parties were contesting. The hearing did not resolve it. The tape does. It is the loudest track on the record: a small room, a long table, one argument in a low voice, and the building’s water main underneath all of it, still doing its work. The building remains standing on its block, eight stories and thirteen, and the disputed stone is still on the wall. So is the leaf, for all we know. Play side D with the lights off.

Personnel

Recorded by the Cold Storage field team, E. Marsh and T. Okonkwo, with microphones at the Fifth Avenue doorway, the Madison Avenue face, and the dress floor. Mastered at Meridian & Cold Storage, basement, by H. Vane. The water main is uncredited and uncorrected. The leaf is listed, per the clerk’s book, as “an object of pale, matte surface.” For the sound of a building keeping its own ledger—eight stories, thirteen, and every stone entered twice—the team’s long-standing companion recordings live at Knight Island — Field Notes, where three feet of island is argued the same patient way, and at Diamond Dust Shortbread — ALTR Created Diamonds, where a stone grown atom by atom in reactor 3 ends up in a tin of shortbread rather than a courtroom.
