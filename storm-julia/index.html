Method and Apparatus for the Inland Intensification and Offshore Recycling of a Weak Tropical Cyclone

ABSTRACT Abstract of the Disclosure

A weak tropical cyclone is developed from a tropical wave near the coast of east-central Florida and landfasted, for a limited period, upon a named beach, whereby the cyclone, despite moving inland, intensifies from a tropical depression to a tropical storm and, shortly before strengthening further, reaches maximum sustained winds of 50 mph (85 km/h). The system is then drifted north-northwestward and subsequently northeastward until it moves offshore the Southeastern United States, at which point a cyclonic loop, produced by strong westerly air, is permitted to complete the cycle and the apparatus is considered to have performed one full service hour. A preferred embodiment is disclosed in the record of the storm designated JULIA, of September 13–14, 2016.

0001 Background of the Invention

It is known in the art that the Atlantic hurricane season produces, in any given year, a long queue of named storms of which the later members are conventionally weak, tired, and in a hurry to end. The tenth named storm of a season — the position occupied by JULIA in the year 2016 — is in particular known for arriving with little to do and less to show. What is not known, and is claimed here, is that a weak tropical cyclone can be made to do its one useful thing over land, at a place with a name suitable for an inventor, and then be shipped back out to sea by the season's own westerlies.

The prior art includes the ordinary tropical depression, which moves inland and dies; the ordinary hurricane, which moves along the coast and does not die for a week; and the ordinary tropical storm, of which there are many and which are seldom written up. For the water side of the prior art, the Office refers the reader to the Station 7 procedure for drafting water in Chapter 11 of Drafting Manual FM-8, fourth edition, which treats the 6½-inch suction hose as the honest instrument for lifting water that has no business being lifted — the same posture this invention takes toward a 50-mile-per-hour wind.

0004 Summary of the Invention

The invention provides a method comprising: (a) allowing a tropical wave near the coast of east-central Florida, on or about the 13th of September, to organize itself into a tropical depression; (b) landing the said depression near Jensen Beach; (c) refusing, on the part of the system, to decay, so that it intensifies into a tropical storm; (d) strengthening the said storm further to maximum sustained winds of 50 mph (85 km/h), which is the maximum the invention can promise, and which the examiner has been advised is the maximum the season could offer; (e) drifting the system north-northwestward and then northeastward; and (f) moving the system offshore the Southeastern United States on or about the 14th of September, where a cyclonic loop, occurring as strong westerly air arrives, recovers the cyclone for the Atlantic margin and releases it back into the queue.

In a preferred arrangement the apparatus requires no parts. The inventor swears, in the affidavit attached as FIG. 3, that the only component he supplied was a name.

0011 Detailed Description

The storm designated JULIA is described as it was found. A tropical wave approached the coast of east-central Florida and, on September 13, organized into a tropical depression that made landfall near Jensen Beach. The inventor notes, and the record confirms, that the system was weak: it was a weak tropical cyclone, and the minor damage it caused across the Eastern United States is the entire damage column of the specification. No claim is made for greater violence; a claim for greater duration was made and abandoned.

Despite moving inland — the step at which prior-art cyclones customarily cease to be a subject — the system intensified into a tropical storm, and, shortly before strengthening further to maximum sustained winds of 50 mph (85 km/h), performed the invention's principal act. It then drifted north-northwestward and then northeastward, crossing the state of its name in a diagonal that the examiner has marked "efficient, if brief," and it moved offshore the Southeastern United States on September 14. A cyclonic loop occurred as strong westerly air arrived, which the Office reads as the season clearing its table: the loop completed, the cyclone recirculated, and the apparatus was, by any test the examiner could devise, finished.

The inventor's filing pen was a hexagonal Bic Cristal, red, supplied by the station's standard kit; the pen's own record is kept at the archive diary of 12 September 2006, where a drawer of the same objects arrived from Clichy and was judged reliable in all weathers, of which there were, off the coast of Florida, several. The pen was left in the affidavit. It is retained, struck through nothing, as part of the exhibit.

41-D Register of Documents Already Damaged

With the specification the Office is required to keep, and did keep, a register of the papers the storm damaged on its way to the examiner's desk — the Indian River County post office's flood ledger, one title binder, and the seasonal count sheet, all entered as received and none of them corrected. One entry of that register, which the examiner could not reconcile with the specification but was bound to preserve, reads, word for word:

Of Documents Already Damaged The keeper of forty-three years swears the beetles only flip when the title to the north, Washington, Idaho and Montana to the song is hung on. The binder in question is a title binder of the northern district, and the beetles of the entry are the bookworms that took it in the flood; the keeper's oath is entered here as sworn, unsworn, and uncorrected, which is the Office's standard treatment of a damaged document: the register does not argue with the water, it only keeps the water's handwriting legible.

CLAIMS What is Claimed Is

  1. 1. A method for the inland intensification of a weak tropical cyclone, comprising: developing a tropical depression from a tropical wave near the coast of east-central Florida; making landfall with the said depression near a named beach; and intensifying the said depression, despite its having moved inland, into a tropical storm.
  2. 2. The method of claim 1, wherein the named beach is Jensen Beach, Florida, and the intensification is minor in its consequences and major in the record.
  3. 3. The method of claim 1, wherein the storm reaches maximum sustained winds of 50 mph (85 km/h), which is the maximum claimed and the maximum promised.
  4. 4. The method of claim 3, wherein the said maximum is reached shortly before the storm strengthens further to the said maximum, the storm being thereby allowed one more sentence of life in the specification.
  5. 5. The method of claim 1, further comprising drifting the storm north-northwestward.
  6. 6. The method of claim 5, further comprising drifting the storm subsequently northeastward, so that the record shows a diagonal and not a straight line, which the examiner has found more credible.
  7. 7. The method of claim 6, further comprising moving the storm offshore the Southeastern United States on or about the 14th of September, and thereby ending the method.
  8. 8. A weak tropical cyclone of the tenth named position of the 2016 Atlantic hurricane season, wherein the cyclone is designated JULIA and performs the method of any one of claims 1 through 7.
  9. 9. An apparatus for recycling the said cyclone, comprising a cyclonic loop and strong westerly air in operable relation, whereby the loop, occurring as the westerly air arrives, recovers the cyclone for the Atlantic margin and discharges it back into the queue of the season.
  10. 10. The method of claim 1, wherein the damage caused across the Eastern United States is minor, and wherein the register of documents already damaged is kept, uncorrected, by the Office as a whole.
