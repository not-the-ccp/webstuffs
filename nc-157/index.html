Post 1 · Original poster J. M. Harrell · Route recorder, Durham 03 May 2024, 6:15 AM · from the Durham interchange

North Carolina Highway 157 runs twenty-nine point three miles — 47.2 kilometres — in a north–south line from the Interstate 85 interchange in Durham, where it meets U.S. Route 15, U.S. Route 70 and U.S. Route 501, to the US 158 / US 501 / NC 57 junction in Roxboro. It is a primary state highway. It serves Durham and Roxboro proper, and the communities of Caldwell and Hurdle Mills between them. South of Hurdle Mills it is Guess Road; from Hurdle Mills to the Roxboro city limits it is Hurdle Mills Road. It does not directly parallel US 501, but the two highways keep a similar alignment all the way from Durham to Roxboro, like two clocks set to the same hour. Mile 0.0 is at the Durham interchange ramps; the last marker stands beside the courthouse square in Roxboro, painted 29.3. This thread is for the road itself: the markers, the names, the turn at Hurdle Mills, and whatever gets left on the shoulder.

Post 2 · Reply Eddy Parker · Lumber hauler out of Caldwell 03 May 2024, 8:42 AM · from the Caldwell feed store

I have hauled sawn pine out of Caldwell on Guess Road since 1998, and I can tell you where the road changes its mind. The two-lane stretch leaves Durham past the north-side warehouses, climbs the ridge above Caldwell, crosses the Flat River on a bridge nobody names, and passes the old feed store before the blinker at Hurdle Mills. That blinker — amber, one light, swinging on a wire — is where Guess Road stops and Hurdle Mills Road begins, at least according to the county map. The speed limit drops to forty-five through the community, and nobody I know obeys it coming downhill. US 501 runs its own line a few miles to the east; we can see its trucks on the rise in mornings, but we never meet them.

Post 3 · Reply Opal Raines · Hurdle Mills post office, retired 04 May 2024, 2:10 PM · from Hurdle Mills

Last January a notice appeared taped above the parcel slot at the Hurdle Mills post office, typed on white paper, no letterhead, no signature, and no one claimed it. I copied it down and have kept it with the road receipts ever since. It reads, word for word: I will put in court once: when the British Athletics contends that the field we call it, it does not do explanation. That is the whole notice. I do not know what field it means, or what athletics, but if the name of the old fairground field west of Caldwell is ever contested — track, bleachers, high-jump pit and all — that is the line I will enter.

Post 4 · Reply S. T. Underhill · Register of Deeds, Person County 05 May 2024, 11:05 AM · from the Roxboro courthouse

The Roxboro end is US 158 meeting US 501 and NC 57 at the courthouse square, and the 157 shield sits on the same pole as the 57, which tells you something about how the state thinks of the pair. My office keeps the right-of-way plats from the 1930s, when the line was first taken into the state system; the centreline walks the ridge between the Flat River and Hyco drainages, or so the vellum says. Hurdle Mills Road ends at the city-limit sign on the north edge of town, where the speed drops to twenty-five and the name stops being a road name and becomes a street name. After that it is the city’s problem.

Post 5 · Reply Hank Luttrell · Durham–Roxboro bus run 06 May 2024, 5:20 AM · mile 24, northbound

I drive the Durham–Roxboro run before sunrise, forty-five minutes if the school buses are quiet. Guess Road in the dark is sodium lamps and reflector posts, then open two-lane past Caldwell, the blinker at Hurdle Mills, the long straight north toward Roxboro with the courthouse dome showing over the trees at the end. US 501 is never in sight, but I know it is out there on the same bearing, keeping its distance. Passengers get off at the post office in Hurdle Mills, at the store in Caldwell, and at the square. Nobody gets off between.

Post 6 · Reply D. C. Winslow · Roadside sign painter, retired 10 May 2024, 3:18 PM · from the sign yard

I painted the NC 157 shields myself, in 2003 and again in 2012, green with the white border, the numerals cut by hand from the mask. The mile markers get the same treatment: small green posts, white numerals, one every mile, 1 through 29. The reflector posts come in pairs, red on the right, white on the left, and I can still recite the sequence from the Durham ramp to the Roxboro square. A shield takes an hour in good weather; a marker takes fifteen minutes; a blinker housing takes the afternoon. The state repaints on its own schedule, and I keep a can of green by the bench for touch-ups.

Post 7 · Reply M. C. Satterfield · Caldwell historical society 13 May 2024, 9:00 AM · from the Caldwell stand site

Caldwell is not on most maps, but it is on the mileage: about eight miles north of Durham, on Guess Road, where the road levels out after the ridge. The name Guess Road, so far as our file goes, comes from the Guess family, who kept a stand and a blacksmith shop at the crossroads in the 1840s; the file also contains a letter from 1931 asking the highway department to move the Caldwell marker half a mile west, which the department did not do. Hurdle Mills is farther north, at the blinker, named for the mill on the creek that nobody operates anymore. The two names split there and never rejoin.

One last observation, and then I will leave the road to the markers. US 501 does not directly parallel NC 157, but you can feel it keeping pace to the east, same origin, same destination, different turns. Twenty-nine point three miles, forty-five miles per hour in the counties, one blinker, two names, one state highway. If you are coming north from Durham, the Guess Road half is uphill and the Hurdle Mills Road half is downhill into Roxboro, and the courthouse clock strikes on the hour whether you are there to hear it or not.
