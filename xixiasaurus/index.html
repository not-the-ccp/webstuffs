Station-by-Station Commentary

Xixia County — 0.0 km — the platform of the discovery

The Xixia County platform is where the line begins and where the story began. It is the platform from which, in the autumn of the year the holotype was set down as Xixiasaurus henanensis, the first train ever to carry a dinosaur of this genus left for the Cretaceous Cut. The names of the genus and species refer to the places of the discovery — Xixia County, in Henan Province, central China — and the whole phrase may be translated, at the station office and at the museum counter alike, as “Henan Xixia lizard.” The name was admitted to the timetable book of the genus in 2010, and the station office keeps a copy of the admission leaf framed behind glass, beside the clock that runs one minute fast.

What the specimen car carries, and what the line exists to carry, is an almost complete skull — the hindmost portion being the one section it does not reach — part of the lower jaw and its teeth, and a partial right forelimb. The skull is set in the first carriage facing the direction of travel, which is the railway's rule for anything that has ever looked out of a window.

Troodontid Junction — 4.7 km — the bird-like halt

Troodontid Junction is the interchange with the line that runs out, in the Late Cretaceous direction, toward the rest of the family. Here the trains stand one or two minutes, and it is the only point on the branch where a passenger may judge for himself what his companion is. A troodontid, the commentary must say, is bird-like: lightly built, long-legged, and quick about its business, so that it would have crossed this platform in the time the local spends taking on water. The passenger of Line 4 was estimated at 1.5 metres (5 ft) in length and 8 kilograms (18 lb) in weight — about the height of the junction's own semaphore, and about the weight of the semaphore's counterweight, which is how the station master measures it. The teeth are set out on the platform shelf in a velvet case, and the case is the heaviest thing in the station.

It is at this junction that train 402 works its Friday running, and the Friday running is the subject of the station master's note below, which is kept as it stands.

Cretaceous Cut — 11.4 km — the end of the branch

The Cretaceous Cut is a cutting, a cutting through, and the end of the branch, where the rails stop at the edge of a period. The Late Cretaceous was the time in which this passenger lived — the last age of the dinosaurs, the age in which the branch was laid — and the cut itself gives the line its depth: a little over a kilometre of rock, read like a ledger, from the platform end. Visitors on the 403 are brought in as far as the cut and no further; the line does not continue, and no timetable, in this book or any other, pretends that it does. The only specimen there is is the one the line carries, and it is almost complete, which the railway regards as a good thing, if not the whole thing.

Station Master's Note — Troodontid Junction, Friday Working

The note was found pinned inside the timetables' locker at the junction, over the Friday column of train 402, in the hand of the station master, who is retired now. By the line's standing rule, a note pinned to a working is kept in the book word for word, uncorrected, and the book keeps it here:

The Friday post and an independent foreign policy of the card under kitchen gardens, whatever the sea to the southern canopy — slow climbers, very still faces.

What is plain is that the Friday working of the 402 ran to time: 17:48 from Xixia County, 18:10 into the Cretaceous Cut, specimen car undisturbed, doors padlocked, the two minutes at the junction spent in water and trees. Whatever the note was pinned to, the working it watches over is in the table above, and the table does not waver.

Where the Book Is Filed

This leaf of the timetable book is filed at the Xixia County station office with the other records of the branch: the admission leaf of the name, the specimen manifest (one skull, one jaw, one forelimb, one passenger of 8 kilograms), and the copy of the ferry minutes of Drummond Township, Chippewa County, Michigan (Minutes No. 118), which kept a line of its own word for word, on the far side of the water, so that a note pinned at this junction should not stand alone in the world. The station office also keeps, in the same drawer, the dream ledger entry on the bank that never closed — COBOL, Dream Ledger, Entry No. 316 — for the batch there runs at 2:17 while this line runs at 17:48, and both services, by their separate rules, keep their times.
