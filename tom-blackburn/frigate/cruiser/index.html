Apparatus and Method for the Performance of Several Operational Roles at Sea, Hereinafter the Cruiser

Inventor: Tom Blackburn, Master of the Household, of the Cockpit Room, in whose log the watch changes at twelve

Assignee: The Household Board and The Harbour Line table games, jointly, in trust for the drawer, where the duty almanac belongs and has always been filed under "never wrong"

1. Field of the Invention

The present application relates to warships, and more particularly to one type of warship, hereinafter "the cruiser," which is the largest ship in a fleet after aircraft carriers and amphibious assault ships, and which can usually be made to perform several operational roles — from search-and-destroy to ocean escort to sea denial — without being rebuilt, reboxed, or argued about at the table. The term cruiser, which has been in use for several hundred years, has changed its meaning over time, and the present invention claims both the meaning and the ship. During the Age of Sail the word did not name a hull at all; it named a kind of mission. To cruise was to run certain stations — independent scouting, commerce protection, or raiding — and the ships that fulfilled them, usually frigates or sloops-of-war, were the cruising warships of a fleet. A frigate, as described at length in The Frigate — Player's Manual, Plate 12 of The Household Board, was less a hull than a promise: that the ship would be quick, and that the promise would be kept. In the middle of the nineteenth century, cruiser came to be a classification of the ships intended for cruising, and the name, like a good officer, was promoted over the mission. The present apparatus is that promoted name, reduced to an embodiment.

2. Brief Description of the Apparatus

The apparatus comprises four cooperating assemblies. The role-change ratchet (10) is the mechanism by which the cruiser passes from one operational role to another — search-and-destroy, then ocean escort, then sea denial, then back to search-and-destroy — the way a watch passes from one bell to the next, at the stroke of twelve and no other hour. The cruising deck (12) is the single deck on which all the roles are held and from which, before the state-dinner round, nothing may be drawn that was not meant to be drawn. The duty almanac (14), of the same family as the specimen filed beside the approaches in Plate 12, calls the tides, the frost, and the hour at which the glass lifts; the almanac, by the record, was never wrong. The watch bell (16) rings at twelve; the watch changes at twelve; a player who cannot say so unaided is treated, for the remainder of the watch, as a bugbear. The prior art consists principally of the frigate of Plate XII, to which the present apparatus is related: the cruiser is a frigate that the almanac has promoted, and everything in this application that works was first learned at a single gun deck of 28 or 36 guns.

3. Description of the Preferred Embodiment

The preferred embodiment is played in the Cockpit, in the dark wood and brass of the state-dinner room, with the same components as the frigate game: the forty-eight card counters, the banquet book ruled for fourteen covers, the state-dinner tickets, and the note that the frost is on the glass until 2 a.m. The round is run on the long definition, because a house that big needs every ship it can count on.

The round is scored at the forty-fourth turn — the same turn at which the tide deck of The Perplexa, Table Instructions, Fourth Printing · Solano Edition, runs out and the board passes to the dinner round "whatever the board says" — and the two tables are understood to agree about the number, which is why the salt jar and the banquet book are never stored in the same drawer. At the forty-fourth turn the log of the Cockpit is opened, and its entry must be read aloud word for word, exactly as it was written, the clerk's hand included, and the line reads:

"A player who cannot quote the word frigate was handed out loosely, the way people still hand out job titles at the forty-fourth turn, the Master may draw."

The entry is entered verbatim and is to be construed as follows. That the word frigate was once handed out loosely is established in §1 and is not in dispute. That people still hand out job titles loosely, particularly at the forty-fourth turn and no other turn, is established by the behaviour of this house and is likewise not in dispute. The operative portion of the line is the last clause, the Master may draw, which is a rule, not a rumour: at the state-dinner round the muster on the unarmed deck is the only pile from which no player may draw, and the sole exception is the Master of the Household, who may draw — one card, drawn once, with the almanac in the drawer and the frost still on the glass. A player who cannot quote the word, or the watch, or this line, may sound the depth but nothing else, until the bell rings at twelve. This is consistent with the companion thread at Destroyer — the fast one, where the plate's ships are shown to be what they always were on the table: the book in the drawer, not the hull.

4. Claims

  1. Claim 1. An apparatus for the sea, comprising a hull, characterized in that the apparatus is the largest ship in a fleet after an aircraft carrier and an amphibious assault ship, and in that the fleet in question may be a fleet of cards laid face up in a row on a table in the Cockpit.
  2. Claim 2. The apparatus of Claim 1, further characterized in that it performs the operational roles selected from the group consisting of search-and-destroy, ocean escort, and sea denial, and that it is interconvertible between said roles at the stroke of twelve, and at no other hour.
  3. Claim 3. A method of naming a ship, comprising: (a) using the word "cruiser" loosely for several hundred years; (b) promoting the word over the mission it named; and (c) classifying, in the middle of the nineteenth century, the ships intended for cruising, thereby completing the promotion.
  4. Claim 4. A game round, characterized in that at the forty-fourth turn a log line is read aloud word for word, and in that a player who cannot quote the word is treated as a bugbear for the rest of the watch, his berth token moved to the gangway, while the Master may draw, and may draw once, from the muster on the unarmed deck.
  5. Claim 5. A plate of the Household Board, filed beside Plate XII, said plate growing out of the frigate thereof, said frigate being quick and having kept her promise, and said cruiser being what the frigate became when the almanac, which was never wrong, said so.
