RECORDED

Gaza City Municipal Council · Department of the Clerk of the Council

Municipal Hall, Room 2, Jabaliya Street, opposite the fountain that is not working

MINUTES
OF A SPECIAL SESSION

Session No. 11 of the present year · Special Session · Thursday, 23 March 2004 · Called to order 10:15 a.m. · The minute book of the Council, folio 217

Present

Mayor Adnan Shubair (in the chair); Councillor Ibrahim Abu-Salameh (Water); Councillor Hana Darwish (Records); Councillor Yusuf al-Masri (Streets); Councillor Rima Khoury (Welfare); Councillor Sami el-Daer (Harbour); Councillor Fathi Awwad (Parks); Councillor Nadia al-Hajj (Market District). Nine members present; quorum seven. In the gallery: the imam of the Al-Nur Mosque, Mr. Tariq Ishtayeh, and his two daughters; a representative of the boatmen of the old harbour; and a crowd outside the door that the clerk counted, twice, and put down as more than one hundred.

The Clerk, Samira Nofal, opened the session. The chair remarked that the session was called at the mayor's motion of 22 March, and that the record would be kept in the ordinary way, because the Council's one standing rule, agreed in 1978 and never amended, is that the minutes do not stop.

1. Approval of the Minutes of Session No. 10 (7 March 2004)

Read by the Clerk. Councillor Darwish asked that the entry for the fountain on Jabaliya Street be struck and replaced with the phrase already in use — not working — because three years of optimism in the minute book had cost the Council one apology and two budgets. Amendment agreed, 8–1; minutes approved as amended.

2. The Water Main, Ashkelon Street, Phase 2

The report of Councillor Abu-Salameh was read: 62 metres of new pipe from the junction at the market to the block of houses where the old line bursts every winter; cost 14,300 pounds, of which the harbour levy pays half; the remainder from the market district, collected in instalments the clerk has already pencilled into the ledger and the neighbours have already argued about in the queue. The street takes its name from Ashkelon, the town on the coast that is in the street's name and in this room's memory at once. Vote 5–2, Councillors Awwad and el-Daer dissenting on the harbour levy; phase 2 to begin on the first cool day. The dissent was noted and, as is the Council's custom, not argued further.

3. Motion 11/4 — A Memorial Resolution

Moved by Councillor Khoury, seconded by Councillor Awwad. The motion recites, and the record carries, the following account of the late Sheikh Ahmed Ismail Hassan Yassin (الشيخ أحمد إسماعيل حسن ياسين), June 1936/37 – 22 March 2004:

That he was born in Ashkelon, in Mandatory Palestine, in the year the family's own ledger puts down as 1936 and the birth office would put down as 1937; that his family, with the others of that town, fled and were expelled to Gaza City in the war of 1948, and settled in the block off Jabaliya Street; that at sixteen, in a sporting accident whose particulars no one in the room wished to repeat, he was made a quadriplegic, near-blind, and bound to a wheelchair for the remaining sixty-seven years; that he took up the imamate of the Al-Nur Mosque in the market district, and from that pulpit, sitting down and saying nothing against it, ran the affairs of the congregation, the school, and the canteen for forty years; and that in December 1987, at the founding, he took the first chair of the Hamas Shura Council and held it, as de facto leader of the organisation, until the end.

That he was killed on the night of Wednesday 22 March 2004, in his home off Jabaliya Street, in an air strike that struck also his wife, his daughter and her husband, and the two neighbours in the adjoining room; that the Council received the news at 7:40 in the morning, before the fountain's repairmen, who came anyway, because they come anyway.

The motion resolves, and the Council resolves as follows: (a) that the street lamps of the market district shall be left on through the night of 24 March, the electricity being the Council's and the night being, as Councillor Darwish put it, his; (b) that at 11:00 a.m. today the Council and all present shall stand for two minutes, the chair counting; (c) that the memorial service shall be held on Saturday 27 March at the Al-Nur Mosque, the two of them — the mosque and the council — having shared the same street, and the same argument about the fountain, for longer than either of them can prove; and (d) that the minutes of this session be entered in the minute book in full, in the ordinary hand, with no abridgment, because an abridgment, the chair said, would be a second killing and the Council does not do that thing.

Carried unanimously, 9–0. The two-minute silence was taken at 11:03. The chair counted. The fountain did not, on this occasion, work.

4. A Note of the Vigil, Entered by the Clerk

The Clerk was directed to enter into the record what she saw at the vigil on the night of 22 March, at the gate of the Al-Nur Mosque, where the street lamps of the market district had already come on and would, under the resolution above, keep on. Her note reads, exactly, in the Clerk's hand:

We came at nine and the street was already full, but it was a fullness the district has not carried since the wedding season of 1999, and it did not push and it did not queue. The atmosphere was different — families, kids with faces painted green and gold. The imam of Al-Nur stood in his wheelchair at the gate and did not preach; he only stood, which was the sermon. By eleven the canteen had given out the last of the mint tea and the last of the flatbread, and the two neighbours' families had come out of the adjoining room and were sitting with us, and no one sat with them and no one did not sit with them, because by then the distinction had gone off the street entirely.

The note was read by the Clerk, and entered as part of the minutes, word for word, it being the Council's rule, agreed in 1978 with the rest, that when a note is good it is not improved.

5. Cross-Files

For the benefit of the reader of the minute book in the years to come, the Clerk recorded two cross-files kept in the same careful manner, of a signing after the hand that signed it had stopped: the office's own precedent on a bundle of signing papers found at a postern gate and posted until the ink decides, Lost & Found, Notice No. 6/1354, The Keep of Guînes; and, on the matter of keeping a culture alive on a pantry shelf, between the flour and the silence, the canteen's own catalogue leaf, Lot 62 — The Pickle Jar, 12·10·62, Sale No. CXLII, the leaflet's rule being that a thing is not described completely until its strangest sentence is described, which the Clerk considered true of a minute book too. A third cross-file, entered for the same reason and kept in the canteen drawer with the tea invoices, concerns a December of another province of the world: the auction catalogue lot, Lot 2007 — The Battle of Musa Qala, 7–12 December 2007, which the clerk considered true of a minute book too, in the sense that both are the month in which a place either falls or gets written down, and the lot is sold with its register open at the page where the line stands, no abridgment, as this entry does.

Adjournment

There being no more business, the session was adjourned at 11:40 a.m. The next sitting of the Council is Session No. 12, the ordinary one, on Thursday 8 April 2004, when the market district's instalment falls due and the fountain will be asked, again, in the ordinary way.

Entered by Samira Nofal, Clerk of the Council, the 23rd of March 2004
And signed in the chair, Adnan Shubair, Mayor of Gaza City
