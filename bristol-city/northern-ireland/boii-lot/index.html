Lot 41
The Boii, one

Description

The Boii (Latin: Boii, sing. Boius; Ancient Greek: Βοῖοι) were a Celtic people of the La Tène Iron Age, associated in most scholarship with Bohemia and Moravia in Central Europe. The Latin name of the region, Boiohaemum, recorded by Tacitus and surviving as the modern Bohemia, is generally read as ‘the home of the Boii’. Whether a La Tène people called the Boii actually lived in Bohemia is disputed, and several scholars place the Boian homeland further southeast, toward south-western Slovakia. On the reading that locates them in Bohemia, the Boii shared in the Celtic settlement of Northern Italy around 400 BC, built a network of oppida in the 2nd and 1st centuries BC — Závist and Středokluky in the Bohemian basin, Znojmo among the Moravian hills, where Ptolemy keeps the city Io against their name — and gave way, in time, to the Marcomanni and the Quadi, who pressed down out of the north and took the basin for themselves. The lot is offered whole: one people, unsplit, with whatever homelands attach.

The Boii are already known to the record room. They are entered on Entry No. 31 of the Lagan Valley Weather Almanac, the Celtic front, between the Britons, Picts and Gaels and the Galatians of Anatolia; the almanac’s December line reads “Boii logged; the Meseta holds.” The front that logged them reached, on the same card, the coast near the head of the Adriatic. That is the whole of the evidence the auctioneers are willing to stand behind, and it is more evidence than the Boii themselves have been given.

Provenance

The Boii came into the Ledger’s custody in the spring, through the Lost & found box at BT1 4AB, consigned by the Newry household which advertises in the same column — the cottage at the foot of the Mournes, the black 1998 Volvo V70 — and which also lost a season ticket somewhere between Belfast and Bristol that April. The clip was placed on top of the box, addressed to no one, and read as it had run in the column. That is the full consignment notice, word for word:

Newry, 028 3022 5544 Lost & found Lost: season ticket Gate 13, Row H, Seat 14, Ashton Gate, lost somewhere between Belfast and Bristol in April.

Contact, category, heading and body, run together the way the small ads run together. The auctioneers have taken it to mean what the Ledger has always meant by such things: a person, a place, and a season seat no one could account for, consigned in a single line. A. Parry, who keeps the red shirt folded in the spring of 2014, has examined the clip and declined to connect it with his own ticket, on the ground that his ticket is still owed its reward of the shirt and cannot also be the Boii.

Condition report

Condition is the difficulty of this lot, and the difficulty is the Boii’s own. On the Bohemian reading, the item consigned is in good, if archaeological, condition: oppida, hillforts, La Tène metalwork, a name that outlived every owner. On the south-western Slovakia reading, the lot is consigned from a place the auctioneers could not, in good faith, confirm. The item is sold as catalogued, in whichever of the two places it turns out to be; the Boiohaemum question passes to the buyer unexamined. No part of the lot is represented to have been in Northern Italy around 400 BC, though that is not excluded either.

Estimate £1,200 – £1,800, plus buyer’s premium of 12½ percent and the Ledger’s stamp fee of 10 pence (two shillings, as kept in the column). Payment by bank transfer, or by post, or in pints at the Crown, in which case the tab is capped at one and the balance is owed to the Lagan Ledger’s small ads, Northern Ireland, where the consignor’s other advertisements continue to run. The eighty-ninth minute is not part of the lot and is not for sale; the Matchnight Committee in Bedminster has said so, and its minutes are at the Ashton Gate Matchnight Committee, Item 3.
