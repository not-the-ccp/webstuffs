Ciudad Bolívar, Bolívar State

Re: “Court Hours” (Side B) · the field notebook entry of 19:41 · the sleeve

Dear Mr. Kelly,

I am writing to apologise properly, in ink and on paper, for what the record did with your words. In “Court Hours,” on Side B of Gymnelia gemmifera (Ocelote Records OCE-014), your testimony before the municipal court in Ciudad Bolívar — Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 18:00 — was pressed onto amber vinyl and carried across two oceans without a word sent to your post box at the crossing. The field notebook, too, bears your name and the crossing at 19:41, written in the recordist’s own hand, and neither of us asked you whether it should. I am sorry.

I write this from Caracas, in the republic of twenty-three states, the Capital District and the Federal Dependencies — ninety-one thousand two hundred fifty square kilometres of continental landmass and Caribbean islets, an estimated 31.8 million people in 2025, bordered by the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean, by Colombia, by Brazil, by Guyana, and by Trinidad and Tobago — and by the Orinoco itself, whose blackwater tributaries run past San Félix and up into the gallery forest where our lamp hung over the stern. You know that water better than any of us: you carried it for eleven years, Tuesday to Saturday, and the court needed your hours to move a witness list across it.

Forty men in one pass, the moth left deliberately unillustrated — the gemma , a bud, a jewel; -fera , bearing. I set that into the sleeve notes because I could not draw the insect, and because Francis Walker had already named it in 1854: gemma, a bud, a jewel; -fera, bearing. A bearer of little jewels. But the jewel I took without asking was yours — your voice, your hours, your crossing. Forty men in one pass of your ferry, and I took one man’s testimony for a record about a moth.

The same discipline binds the Cook Islands Biodiversity Archive and this record: the no-eraser clause, under which a collector’s note about a survey boat stays exactly as written, because a memory is not edited in public. We kept your words unaltered; we did not keep them private. If you wish, your name can be struck from the next pressing, or left exactly as it stands. The choice is yours, and I will honour it either way.

The lamp at San Félix is still burning at 33&frac13, and the passenger moth — smaller, unnamed, catalogued only as “the passenger” — still crosses the beam at 19:41 on the reissue. None of that was yours to give, and I took it. For the hours the court never returned, for the crossing named without your leave, and for the moth left unillustrated while your name was printed: I apologise.

Yours, with the lamp unanswered,

The recordist
Ocelote Records OCE-014 · Gymnelia gemmifera · the Orinoco basin, Venezuela

A copy of this letter travels with the Archive’s next query-card printing, so that the no-eraser rule reads both ways: nothing added, nothing removed, and an apology where one is due.
