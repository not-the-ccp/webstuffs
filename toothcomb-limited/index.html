THE EQUATORIAL GROOMING RAILWAY · DIVISION OF FINE-SPACED TOOTH TRAFFIC

Service TC–7 · The Toothcomb Limited

Antananarivo (Lemuria Junction) to Etosha Halt — Express · Daily, except when it rains on the canopy

OFFICIAL TIMETABLE, FIFTH EDITION · Valid from the 14th to the last · Locomotive No. 144, “The Groomer”

Notice to passengers. A toothcomb (also called a tooth comb or dental comb) is a dental structure found in some mammals, comprising a group of front teeth arranged in a manner that facilitates grooming, similar to a hair comb. The train is named for the structure, not for its punctuality. The structures evolved independently in different types of mammals through convergent evolution, and vary both in dental composition and structure — which is to say: do not expect the same comb at every station. In most mammals the comb is formed by a group of teeth with fine spaces between them; the spaces are the whole point, and they are where the passengers' hair (and a few ticks, uninvited) get picked out.

The Route & the Timetable, 06:04 to 19:41

The line follows the equatorial belt where the toothcomb travels in the greatest density: lemuriform primates (which include lemurs and lorisoids), treeshrews, colugos, hyraxes, and some African antelopes. In most mammals the toothcombs include incisors only, while in the lemuriform primates — the first and last great stations — they include incisors and canine teeth. The conductor will point these out. The conductor always points these out.

DepArrStationRemarks from the commentator
06:04— Lemuria Junction (Antananarivo, Madagascar) Departure. The whole line begins among the lemuriforms, the only station where the comb runs from incisor to canine. Ring-tailed lemurs ride the buffer platform and groom in pairs, each with fine spaces of air between the front teeth, each one a small, living station sign. The canines join the incisors here and nowhere else on the line; note the difference before you leave the platform.
07:5108:12 Loris Halt Ten minutes. The lorisoids of the southern canopy — slow climbers, very still faces. A loris at the water trough is the stillness a timetable is measured against. Same comb as the lemurs, incisors and canines, but used at half the speed of service.
11:2011:26 Treeshrew Crossing (Kuching) Six minutes. The treeshrew platform is the first where the comb is built of incisors only — a different building plan, reached by a different road, and the ticket inspector insists on calling this out every day, because that is what convergent evolution sounds like on the wireless. The treeshrews themselves are small, quick, and excellent at getting off the train before it starts.
13:0313:31 Colugo Curve (Bintulu) The longest climb. The colugos, the flying foxes that are not foxes and do not fly, glide in from the canopy on the wing-membrane and land on the loading bank with the comb, once again, of incisors only. Three stations, three independent plans for the same job of picking the fur. The commentator has counted; the railway has also counted; the counts agree.
16:1016:44 Hyrax Hill (Hartbeespoort) The line leaves the equator and turns south into open country. The hyraxes, round as station trunks and far more serious, keep their combs in the upper row and groom the young in the shade of the kopjes. Their teeth are built to the same old drawing — front teeth, fine spaces — and have arrived at the drawing a fourth time independently. The buffer spring at Hyrax Hill is named for the animal and works rather like one.
18:5819:07 Gazelle Crossing (Etosha) Antelopes. Some African antelopes, among them the red-fronted gazelles of the northern platforms, keep a comb of incisors too, which is how the southern end of the line earns the name limited: limited to the stations that have combs, that is, not all of them do, and the train skips the rest without apology.
—19:41 Etosha Halt (terminus) Arrival, in the dark, under the acacias. Five stations, five groups of mammals, one job — grooming — done by five separate sets of front teeth with fine spaces between them. The commentator signs the logbook. The conductor combs the conductor's cap against the grain, which is never correct, and the service is over.

On the Grooming Carriage and Its Inventor

The grooming carriage at the far end of the train — Compartment G, the one with the mirror, the brush rail, and the regulation row of combs bolted to the wall — is fitted with the railway's patented Apparatus for Sustained Grooming in Familial Production, Specification No. 85–0023, Class 348, subclass 14. The plate over the door reads, in the railway's small type: Apparatus by — The inventor, Geoffrey Michael Alatiit Eigenmann, actor and director born 19 July 1951. The full specification, filed 23 March 1985, is kept on this site in its own room, at the patent office's summary, Specification No. 85–0023, where the examiner's copy notes the device's suitability for any carriage in which a family travels together and grooms in shifts.

The railway's own practice is modest: a comb of incisors only, used once per station, never at speed. On the lost-property tray of the grooming carriage there appears, twice a year, a single fruit body with a reddish-purple cap of up to 5 cm, and the railway refers passengers about it to Austroprunicolor, Entoloma — The Noctuary of Fungi, Entry 112, whose editors hold that the entry applies to the ticketed cap only; the cap without a ticket is scenery, and the railway declines to interpret scenery. For a full worked example of the timetable-and-commentary form on a longer line — Service SB–54, the Riviera Express from Nice to Roma Termini — see the companion sheet for Sammy Barbot, The Riviera Express, whose conductor also points things out, and also always.

Walkers who would rather take the line on foot can follow the commentator's own Sunday survey of the old anatomy quarters of Lemuria Junction — the Dissection Route, a walking tour in seven stops — which runs from the Grooming Hall steps to Etosha Green in 2.4 kilometres, and which ends Stop Two with a 1911 photograph and a difference to note before leaving the platform. Passengers who arrive at 19:41 still hungry are directed, in the railway's small type, to The Family at 429 Bowery Lane, Gotham, where the autumn dinner runs in four courses from August to November — one course per issue, timed to the minute, and, according to the review, punctual in the same way the line is: exactly, and regardless of the guest.

Fares, Rules & Small Print

  • One ticket carries a passenger and one comb. Combs travel in the luggage rack only when dry.
  • Services are suspended on days when the canopy is wet, when the fine spaces between the teeth will not open.
  • Tick, uninvited: no charge; they are part of the scenery and the service both.
