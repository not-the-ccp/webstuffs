Minutes of a Special Meeting
of the Matchnight & Trophy Business Committee

Bristol City Football Club · The Robins · Founded 1894

Held in the Room Above Gate 13, Ashton Gate Tuesday, 6 November, 7.45 pm, after the matchday press had gone Minute reference: AG/TC/2024-11

In the chair: Cllr. Whitfield. Absent: the crossbar at Elland Road, which has not answered its summons in thirty-one years and is not represented by counsel. Apologies were received; none were excused without a note.

1. The competition, its numbers and its cans

Cllr. Whitfield opened the meeting by reminding the Committee of the business at hand: the EFL Cup, that annual knockout competition run by the English Football League, open to the 92 clubs of the top four levels of the English football league system—the Premier League above, and beneath it the Championship, League One and League Two. First held in 1960–61 as the Football League Cup, it stands with the FA Cup as one of the two major domestic knockout trophies in the country, and is officially known as the Carabao Cup by reason of a sponsorship arrangement with Carabao Energy, whose red cans line the touchline in fifties.

Mr. Okonkwo reported that one hundred and sixty cans had been received for the third-round matchday, of which nine had been dented, four were found warm in the boot of the coach, and one had gone missing somewhere between Bedminster and the turnstile. It was noted that a dented can is still a can, and the Committee moved on.

2. The draw, the pots and the coach

The Clerk read out the draw. Mr. Tufuga reminded the Committee that a cup season is a season of travelling: ninety-two clubs in the pot, and every week the draw decides whether the coach points north, south, or to a ground the groundsheet cannot name. He recommended that the groundsheet be re-rolled, as it is now full of coffee, and that the Committee's own draw of 1971–72 be cited in evidence: Bristol City in the semi-final against Stoke City, beaten on aggregate, with a record Ashton Gate crowd of 33,516 turning up to watch the Robins go out—the largest the club has ever counted, and the reason the committee room has a view of the corner flag.

3. Report on the eighty-ninth minute

Mr. Parry was invited to report, and did so as follows, from memory and from the diary of Mile Jedinak’s assistant coach, in which the same minute is recorded half a world away at Al Rayyan. “It was the night Duke struck the crossbar,” Mr. Parry said. “The eighty-ninth minute. The ball came off the woodwork and back into play, and a point quietly ended. That night I told my son the Robins were finished, and the season ticket was not renewed until it had been lost, advertised in Belfast in the small ads column of the Lagan Ledger, and found again between two bottles of stout in the Crown Liquor Saloon.”

Ms. O'Donnell confirmed, as a witness from Belfast, that the advertisement had run for three weeks under For sale and one week under Lost & found, and that the reward—a red shirt folded away in the spring of 2014, unwashed by request—had remained unclaimed until the season ticket was returned. The Committee thanked her and noted that she had brought the shirt, folded, into the room, where it now hung from a nail beside the 1971–72 ticket stub.

4. Motion on the crossbar, and the apology

It was moved by Ms. O'Donnell, seconded by Mr. Tufuga:

“That the Committee records its apology to Duke for the noise made in the stand, that the crossbar be ruled innocent for the purposes of this meeting and all future meetings, and that the apology written in the letter Dear Ashton Gate be admitted as standing, with the seat renewed at Gate 13, Row H, Seat 14.”

The motion was put and passed, eight to one, the dissenting vote being cast by the absent crossbar, whose silence was taken by the Clerk, after consultation, to mean no. Cllr. Whitfield added that a thing may be silent and still be against you, and that the Committee would not be lectured by a length of steel.

PASSED  8–1

5. The cans, the cabinet, and the next meeting

It was resolved that the nine undented cans be placed in the trophy cabinet beside the Western League shield of 1897–98, that the dented ones be reserved for the Committee itself, and that the missing can be treated as retired from service, wherever it is. Cllr. Whitfield reminded the Committee that a cup competition is, at the end of the day, a competition that ends: ninety-two names in the bracket, one name left standing, and everyone else home by December at the earliest, which is how the cup works and why the fans stay anyway. The first FA Cup round is in two weeks; the draw is on a Thursday; the coach leaves at six.

Cllr. Whitfield noted, for the record, that the 8:20 and 8:21 closing of the meeting had already been recorded, at the Crown Liquor Saloon, on Side A, Track 4, of The Northwestern Fringe (Lagan Records LL-04), and that the Committee’s declination to comment is documented in the liner notes in full, with one dented can retired from service on account.

There being no further business, the meeting closed at 8.20 pm. The minutes will be confirmed at the next meeting of the Committee.
