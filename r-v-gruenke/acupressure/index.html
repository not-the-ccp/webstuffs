LOST & FOUND

Notice No. 45 · Filed 14 March 2024 · Expires 28 March 2024 (fourteen days, per the desk’s standing rule)

LOST: one (1) acupressure session, twenty-two minutes, unattended and uncompleted.

LOST
meridian, direction of travel: toward the goal line

1 · Particulars of the item

The session is described as an alternative-medicine treatment in which firm, static finger pressure is applied to specific points on the body, performed in conjunction with acupuncture or reflexology practice. It is based on the concept of life energy — qi — which is said to flow through channels called meridians. The session was last seen in progress on Wednesday, 6 March 2024, at 3:17 a.m., in Room 7B above The Hardwood Emporium, Sparks Street, Ottawa, where the 1925–26 Grizzlies counter card (centennial reissue) is still pinned to the door, as it has been for one hundred years, more or less.

Its practitioner, Ms. Odette Trudel of the Ottawa Meridian & Reflex Society, is a small woman in sage-green scrubs who numbers her own points in pencil and works with the same patience a registry clerk brings to a public docket. She reports that fourteen points were reached before the session left. Fourteen is the number the session keeps offering to anyone who checks on it, as if counting were a way of holding still. The desk's file also holds, unstamped, a naturalist's page on the game played on the hardwood floor below — five players to a side, the basket ten feet up, fourteen points pencilled in its margin — kept with the Varsity Council's papers as Field Notes: The Hardwood Game.

2 · Recognizing it if it returns

The session presents itself through five complaints, in this order, and in no other: nausea and vomiting (addressed at point P6, Neiguan, two widths of a thumbnail above the wrist crease); insomnia (ST36, below the kneecap, where it claims to hold the clock open); low back pain (BL23, at the second lumbar spinous process); migraines (LI4, in the web between thumb and forefinger, the point it is most fond of and the point a midwife would never allow it to press); and constipation (SP15, at the navel, where the treatment ends whenever it ends).

Claimants are asked to observe, on the record, the standing identification note of this desk: there is no scientific evidence for the existence of the acupuncture points, meridians, or qi on which the session runs. Some medical studies have suggested the technique may be effective in managing the five complaints listed above; those studies have, in turn, been found to carry a high likelihood of bias. The session is not denied for these reasons. It is simply described as it is, and claimed as it can be.

FOUND · 12 March 2024 · the intake card

The session’s intake card was found wedged in the slot of Locker 7B in this annex — the same locker whose storage register is kept, item by item, under R v Gruenke — Safety Data Sheet, SDS-PRV-263, Rev. 2. The card is the size of a playing card, ruled in pencil, and signed O.T. in the lower corner. One line of the card, which the desk is required to reproduce exactly, reads:

Solubility Soluble in solicitor–client confidence; insoluble in press releases and blanket assertions.

— intake card, line 9, found in Locker 7B

The desk interprets this as follows: the session dissolves cleanly in confidence — hand it back to the client who held it, in the room where it was begun, and it is gone, and it was never at risk. Pour a press release over it and it will not dissolve. It will sit there, whole and insoluble, in exactly the shape it left Room 7B in. So if you are holding the session and you are a newspaper, this desk will not be writing back.

3 · Claiming it

The session is claimed by the client alone. It is the client’s to claim and the client’s to waive, and no one else’s hand on it counts. Claims will be assessed one at a time, on their own facts, for the simple reason that no category of session admits automatic protection — the point of the matrix in Section 3 of the Gruenke sheet applies to treatment as well as to testimony. A blanket assertion to the effect that “all of my sessions are privileged” will be refused at the counter and noted in the log, as such assertions always are.

One claim has already been anticipated. A linebacker in black and gold, number 45, was last seen pressing at the goal line of Kinnick Stadium in a dream logged as Dream Dictionary Entry 417, seat 45A; the dream’s version of this session is transcribed at In re Buffington — Transcript Excerpt, where the linebacker, on the record, waives nothing and presses everything. The desk notes the resemblance, files it, and declines to connect the two matters further. Sister notices on the same wall: Lost & Found — Notice 4172: One Gradient Descent, last seen descending at 3400 N. Willamette, and this desk’s standing instruction that nothing so pressable should be left unattended.

Reward

One counter card, The Hardwood Emporium, 1925–26 Grizzlies season, centennial reissue — or its twin, whichever is not in Locker 7B. No questions asked at the counter; the questions are in the log, and the log has fourteen of them.

Report to the Lost & Found Desk, registry annex, +1 (613) 995-4760, ext. 7B, between the snap and the whistle. Or leave the session where the qi can find it: the bench outside, facing the well, 3:17 to the minute. The session’s fourteen pencil points were later counted out, and ruled not privileged, by the Ottawa–Cedar Rapids Varsity Council in Minutes of Session 45, which re-pinned the card’s twin to the annex wall at the height of a 65-yard line. The desk holds one sister notice on the same wall, filed by the Westside Record Bureau rather than this annex: an In Memoriam for the funk record “Untitled (How Does It Feel)”, which also pressed at a single point and asked one question on its way out. The two notices do not share a claimant; the desk declines to connect them further.
