1Identification of the substance

Product name: Yes (the debut single of the English duo McAlmont & Butler, later included on the debut album The Sound Of... McAlmont & Butler).

Supplier: Lagan Records, Box 9911, c/o The Lagan Ledger, BT1 4AB. The Ledger keeps this file beside its own community columns and the press announcement for the six-language record now on the shelf at The Northwestern Fringe, LL-04, with which it shares a pressing and a postcode.

Uses: listening, re-listening, and the quiet act of buying a second copy. Restrictions: none in force, though see Section 2.

2Hazards

Acute exposure (a full play at volume) may cause: a slowing of the pulse, a hand placed over the chest, and an involuntary nod that the listener will deny. The bridge is the point of greatest hazard; exposure at the bridge has been logged separately in Section 9, row 3, and is treated as its own incident class.

Repeated exposure over decades is not dangerous in itself. It is, however, sticky, self-renewing, and known to resurface in car parks, kitchens, and the space between a needle drop and a breath. No cure is listed; no cure is wanted.

3Composition

A soul ballad. Constituents, by weight: heart (major), one male voice and one female voice in counterpoint (minor), strings (trace), and a bass line that does not hurry (trace). The song is the first UK release of its makers and the first UK hit either had; the two facts are chemically bonded and should not be separated.

4First aid

In case of inescapable hearing, remove the listener from the room, or, failing the room, from the country. If weeping has begun, do not attempt to wipe it; it will outlast the record and the record will, at 4:11, end properly, which is the only first aid that works. Administer one pint at the register’s measure. In all cases the Office is to be notified before the listener is told the song has stopped.

5Fire-fighting measures

The substance is flammable only in the chart sense. A release in the United Kingdom in May 1995 ran to a peak of number eight on the UK Singles Chart and was not checked for three weeks. Fire crews of the period were instructed not to stand on the number, which is a figure, and to cool the surrounding singles, which are not.

6Accidental release

UK: released 15 May 1995; peaked at number eight on the UK Singles Chart; the spill remains their most successful single to date. Ireland: a secondary plume reached number 24. New Zealand: a distant reading recorded number 40, the last place the wave touched and the register closed the log.

7Handling and storage

Store the 7-inch flat, at 45 rpm in memory, in a sleeve, away from heat and from people who will not let the runout finish. The album form, The Sound Of... McAlmont & Butler, may be stacked. The single may not. Handling of reissues: wear clean hands; the 1995 pressing is the only pressing the Office considers original, and it is kept under glass at BT1 4AB.

8Exposure controls / personal protection

Hearing protection is available and has never once been used. The recommended PPE is: one pint, dry eyes, and a window looking out on nothing in particular. Exposure limits: no more than one repeat play per hour until the listener has aged past the incident; no limit thereafter. For the paperwork on hazard sheets of this kind, the cooperative keeps a California sister sheet, Washington, D.C., SDS No. 1789-07-13, and a basement that has filed its own noise complaint at Five Passes, DSR-0007.

9Physical and chemical properties

  • Row 1 Form: ballad, one voice against another
  • Row 2 Duration: 4:11 (needle time, 1995 pressing)
  • Row 3 Bridge onset: 3:47 — incident class, see Section 2
  • Row 4 Peak chart level (UK): number eight
  • Row 5 Secondary plumes: Ireland 24 · New Zealand 40
  • Row 6 Colour: silver (see Section 9.1)
  • Row 7 Odour: none, except the smell of a first pressing opened in 1995

9.1 Certification. The substance has sold over 200,000 copies in the United Kingdom, which is the threshold at which the British Recording Industry grants a silver disc. The disc is struck, marked, and displayed beside the gate of the register; it weighs, on the scale the Office trusts, exactly the same as a promise.

10Stability and reactivity

Stable for at least nineteen years. In 2014, a British weekly magazine placed the substance at number 174 on its list of the “500 Greatest Songs of All Time,” which the Office records not as praise but as a stability test passed under load: a song can be ranked among a list of five hundred and still refuse to move. It has not. No incompatible substances are known; the substance is incompatible only with lists of 500 songs made before 1995, which did not contain it.

11Toxicological information

Lethal in small doses, in the sense that one line — the line that answers a question nobody asked — has ended, permanently, whatever the listener was doing before the needle dropped. No known antidote. Long-term effects include quoting the song at dinner, defending it at dinner, and, in elderly cases, humming the bridge in a room with the lights off, which the Office files under wells.

12Ecological information

Safe for the Lagan and for the towns that press beside it. The substance degrades, over years, into background: it is found in kitchens and car parks where it does no harm and is not quite wanted, in the way moss is not quite wanted. No bioaccumulation; the listener simply becomes the habitat.

13Disposal considerations

There are no disposal instructions, because the substance cannot be disposed of. Scratched 7-inch pressings may be ground down and returned to the register; the Office keeps a jar. The song itself is non-disposable and will be present at every disposal meeting, humming.

14Transport information

UN No. 1995 (provisional), Class 9, miscellaneous. The substance is transported at 45 rpm on a 7-inch, in a sleeve, by post from Belfast to any address that answers to the Lagan. It arrived, in 1995, at number eight on the UK Singles Chart and was there delivered, in full, to the addressee.

15Regulatory information

Silver certified, United Kingdom, threshold 200,000 units, threshold met. No further regulatory action is pending. The 2014 list of 500 songs is treated as a public register, entry 174, and this sheet is cross-filed against it. The Office quotes the number and declines, on the record, to comment.

16Other information

Claim notice. Any person holding a 1995 pressing, a duplicate silver, or a receipt from the gate, is invited to lodge the claim in writing. The Lagan Ledger, BT1 4AB · reward: one pint at the register’s measure; the Office quotes the clubs and a coiled fender.
