Error AB-1932

Australian Broadcasting Corporation

abridged fault text: licence-fee notice misrouted to a public broadcaster that no longer takes licence fees

Notice no.
AB-1932, slip 1 of 1, filed 30 June 1932, re-filed third Tuesday, Vol. 7 No. 11 season
Source terminal
Carrier-wave desk, back room, above the bakery, Magsaysay Avenue
Severity
Minor. No emergency. The kettle is on.
Status
Printed, read aloud by Salvio Fortuno between records, and kept. Whatever is voided, keep.

1. Summary

The diagnostic engine of the club's carrier-wave desk flagged, at 9:00 on the 30th of June 1932, an old licence-fee notice still addressed to the Australian Broadcasting Company, the private body that since 1923 had provided programming for certain radio stations and kept its accounts in its own key. By the morning the company had been replaced: the Australian Broadcasting Corporation had come into being under the federal government, a public broadcaster across radio, and later television and the web, providing news and current affairs, emergency information, entertainment and factual programming to regional and metropolitan Australia alike. The notice could not be delivered to the address on its envelope, and the engine printed what it had instead.

2. Affected systems

One notice, one envelope, one shelf. Downstream, the notice touches: the licence-fee register, which the Corporation was initially financed by, a fee the household remembers as a single line of arithmetic that the family still sets out every 30th of June; the ledger at home, where the entry Australian Broadcasting Corporation sits between Entry 314, Luolishania, and Card 298, the beet-and-blackstrap Coal Loaf; and the harbour fishmonger's almanac at Magsaysay Avenue, whose page the Corporation has, on three recorded occasions, read the shipping forecast over before Salvio could. The Corporation's own systems — the board of directors appointed by the government, the remittance of tax revenue and grants, the small side-door of ABC Commercial — are not affected. They continue, as their charter says they shall, politically independent and accountable, which the engine notes is a state it has never once reproduced on its own.

3. Reproduction

To reproduce: take a public broadcaster founded in 1932, television from 1956, radio from the first, the web from the moment the web learned to carry weather; fund it from the purse of the Commonwealth — tax revenue and grants, with minor funding only, via ABC Commercial — and seat it before a government-appointed board of directors charged with a remit to be politically independent and accountable. Then attempt to charge it a licence fee. The engine faults. The fee is the older company's money; the Corporation has outgrown it the way a soft-bodied animal outgrows the mud that kept it. The fault is reproducible at any time of day, including between records.

4. Diagnosis

The engine's fault text ran clean for two lines and then, on the third, the type went off the platen in the way the ledger's type goes off when a month ends mid-loaf. Printed to the slip, word for word, with no dash where one ought to be:

The same shelf holds the ledger's misprinted ingredients line the family and wrote down what it carries.

Read in the household's key, the line resolves. The shelf — the one above the bakery, beside the almanac — holds two things: the ledger's misprinted ingredients line, the secret ingredient, whisked to a leaven, which the family reads as Card 298's keeping; and the family itself, with the registry at its side. What the sentence is really saying is what every entry in this archive says: the registry wrote down what it carries. The misprint is not an error of the engine. It is the engine, briefly, keeping.

5. Impact on the household

Nil, save one. Salvio read the slip aloud to the room at the third-Tuesday sitting, and Mr. Alistair Delgado, patron, who keeps the five endings from 444 to 66 million years ago and everything else on the same wall, ruled that a body which has broadcast for ninety-four years, has carried emergency information through cyclone season and has sat before its board without ever once taking the government's place, is the closest thing to a soft body the household has ever met — no shell, only a signal — and ruled it a neighbour of the Ediacaran number in spirit, whatever its age. The ruling is kept with the ruling. Mabel, editor, filed the slip beneath the Ediacaran number on the shelf: The Ediacaran — The Stratum, Vol. 7 No. 11, where it lies beside the entry for the animal that walks eleven pairs of legs through Lower Cambrian mud, Luolishania — Dream Dictionary, Entry 314, and beside Salvio Fortuno — The Carrier Wave, the broadcaster behind the bakery to whom the desk belongs.

6. Resolution

The licence-fee notice was voided, the envelope turned over, and a fresh slip addressed to the Corporation in the name of the Commonwealth. The fee line in the register was struck through, not deleted — the register keeps what it carries, and the 1932 fee, like the company, is a body without a grave. The engine was asked to re-run the delivery. It delivered, and printed nothing, which is the best a machine of this house can do. The Corporation, for its part, did not hear the fault at all. It kept broadcasting: news, current affairs, weather for the ships off the eastern approaches, and, on the night the carrier wave opened widest, the harbour forecast that the almanac now prints as its own. Whatever quarter the wind holds, the signal holds. The oven at 325°F, as ever, holds what it is given. The same post carried a copy of the slip, one more shelf down, to the boat-keepers of the Kennet and Avon waterway, who file it on the ledger shelf at Lock House 13 in the manner of the 87-Mile Boatmen & Bargees’ Club, whose The Barge & Bell, Newsletter No. 12, is kept open at the page of 105 locks.
