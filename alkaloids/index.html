Waitematā District · Graham Street Community Room, 12 Graham Street, Auckland Central 1010

Herbarium & Culinary Council

Ordinary Meeting — Minutes on the Alkaloids

Date: Wednesday 10 September 2024, 7:00 pm — 9:14 pm

Minutes no.: HLC/2024/OM-15  ·  Agenda item: 4, the Alkaloids

Room note: the same room, the same table marks. The squares were counted aloud, starting from the mints, and the habit had taken; nobody noticed the twenty-fourth, which was the point

1Present and apologies

Present: Dr. Hana Kovač (Chair), Renata Okafor, Solomon Beretta, Priya Anand, Mireille Fastré, and, as guest member, Tiana Ngāwati of the Waitematā Local Board. In attendance: Elise Moreau, acting secretary, who had kept the Local Board’s books again that week and had learned, at last, to keep the two sets in two different coats.

Apologies: Mariam Farah, of Burao, whose letter was read by the Chair — see item 6 — and the seat of the fishmonger Yusuf Qassim, which the council continues to keep empty, as the club in Berbera kept its twenty-third square for its first member.

2Confirmation of previous minutes

The minutes of the extraordinary session of 21 August 2024 (HLC/2024/EM-14), on the order Lamiales, were confirmed as a true and correct record, with no corrections proposed and none made. The Chair reported that the olive had been planted on the first frost-free morning, 26 August, at the south side of the gate, where the motion said it was to stand, for at least a century; the council agreed the planting had been on time and on the record. The squares were then counted aloud, one per family, starting from the mints at the corner nearest the door and ending on the twenty-fourth, and the count was carried into these minutes as a line of its own, in the manner of the Pacific Health Collection register.

3Declarations of interest

Renata Okafor declared, as at the last meeting, that she sells lavender cuttings on the third Sunday of the month from the back of her car; the declaration was noted as husbandry rather than commerce, which is to say the note was made and the argument was not. Solomon Beretta declared that he has taken a glass of quinine water every Friday for eleven years, on the harbour road in Berbera, at the table of the Thursday club; the council noted the declaration, and the eleven years, and allowed him to vote on the motions in item 4, which he had been held back from at the last meeting, the foxglove matter being now two years old and his. Priya Anand declared that she has kept the extraction bench at the Waitematā pharmacy since 2019 and knows the smell of an acid-base workbench in the dark; the declaration was noted, and the council asked her to chair the report, which she did, and which follows.

4The Alkaloids — report and motions

Report: Priya Anand, the council’s pharmacologist, laid the matter on the table. The alkaloids, she said, are a broad class of naturally occurring organic compounds that contain at least one nitrogen atom; some synthetic compounds of similar structure may also be termed alkaloids, a liberty she noted the council would not grant to lavender. They are produced by a large variety of organisms — bacteria, fungi, plants, and animals — which is to say, she continued, they are made everywhere the council has ever kept a kitchen, and in two more places it has never been. They can be purified from crude extracts of these organisms by acid–base extraction, or by solvent extractions followed by silica-gel column chromatography; she said the last phrase slowly, and then once more, slowly, because the council had never met it before, and because, she said, a name a council has not met is a name it should meet at the table and not on a shelf. As to what the substances do: antimalarial (e.g. quinine), antiasthma (e.g. ephedrine), anticancer (e.g. homoharringtonine), cholinomimetic (e.g. galantamine), vasodilatory (e.g. vincamine), antiarrhythmic (e.g. quinidine) — a wide range of pharmacological activities, as the literature puts it, which is to say, as the council puts it: a fever on one coast, an asthma on the next, a heart that has to be persuaded, and a memory that is asked to stay.

The Chair added the line the minute-taker is required to record: the Cinchona, of the family Rubiaceae, and the Vinca, of the family Apocynaceae, are both members of the order the council counted last month, the mint order, which is to say that at least two of the twenty-four squares in the bed already hold plants that hand nitrogen through the gate — the cinchona in square twelve, the vinca in square twenty-one — and that the order of the month and the substance of the month were, in this particular room, never properly separate. The council took note, and did not say so twice.

Motion 4.1 (Anand, seconded Ngāwati): that an extraction bench be established in the back room of the community room, comprising two glass flasks, one graduated cylinder, one column of silica gel 60 standing on the windowsill where the Shed Council counts its thyme, and the acid–base kit of the pharmacy, on the understanding that every substance worked at the bench be entered in the register one line at a time — the substance’s name, its nitrogen, and the day of extraction. — Carried 7–0.

Motion 4.2 (Beretta, seconded Okafor): that one cinchona seed and one cutting of Vinca minor be entered in the register as the first two lines of the nitrogen book, and that the vinca cutting be potted in square twenty-one of the bed, in the corner nearest the door, where the mints stand, on the ground that a vasodilatory plant is a plant the door should open easily. — Carried 6–1; the dissent was Mireille Fastré’s, who objected to anything in the bed that smelled of a dispensary; the Chair ruled that the vinca, on the bench and in the literature alike, is recorded as smelling of nothing, and the dissent was retired with its nose in the varnish.

Motion 4.3 (Ngāwati, seconded Kovač): that a jar of quinine tonic, for the table, be set on the weighing scale in the front room and weighed once each season, the weight read aloud at the meeting, in the manner of the Waterkeepers’ count of their thirty-one wells, one number a season, a number the town keeps. — Carried 7–0. The first weighing is appointed for the meeting of 8 October; the acting secretary will note it in both sets of books, in two different coats.

5Silent minute

A minute of silence was observed for the foxglove leaf of the roadside, which was eaten by a boy and lived, and with it for the Friday glass of quinine water of the harbour road in Berbera, eleven years running, which the Thursday club counts as one line in its ledger — one line, not eleven, on the arithmetic of the club, which the council continues to find kinder than its own. The silence lasted the length of a strip of cinchona bark, forty-one seconds, which the Chair timed by hand and set down, and was then called off.

6Correspondence

Correspondence received, read by the acting secretary in the order it lay in the tray, came to four items. The first was an invoice from the nursery at Saltfleet for one cinchona seedling, which had arrived by post in a parcel the size of a candle, with two olive cuttings beside it in the same box; the council accepted the seedling, accepted the cuttings, and paid the invoice in full, noting for the record that Saltfleet had sent the postmark once and would not be asked again. The second was a letter from the Council of the Shed at Recalde Press & Cooperage, confirming that the thyme on the windowsill stands at one plant, counted in both books, and that a silica-gel column of foreign manufacture resting on the sill is not thyme, is not counted, and is welcome on the sill for so long as it does not bloom; the council accepted the column’s welcome and thanked the Shed Council by minute. The third was a note from the Al-Jawf Date & Waterkeepers’ Society, recording that the olive of the south of the gate had been entered in the books of the order, as the surviving branch of a country now at rest; the note is filed with the obituary in which the country is remembered, in the obituary of the Arabian Peninsula, where the Chair’s line on the olive has already been read exactly as it was laid down.

A fourth item, unsigned, was a sheet of the cousin’s paper from Burao, its heading torn off in the wash, leaving one line in ink. The acting secretary read the line as it stood, unamended, word for word: "Nor were the same room, around the hills.""The rest," said the Chair, "is the cousin’s paper, and not the minutes’." The cousin from Burao, asked to read the line, read it twice and identified it as the foot of her July letter, which had been written on the hill road above Burao, where the fuel store stands, in the summer when she and Mariam carried the Thursday-club ledger inland — the line meaning, in the cousin’s reading, that she and Mariam were not in the same room, that they had split the ledger between them, and that each had walked the hills around the town with half of it, which is to say the ledger was safe, in two places, in the hills, and the paper had simply lost its beginning in the wash. The council resolved that the line be kept in these minutes exactly as it was set down, that the sheet be pinned to the noticeboard where the heading had been, and that no motion be made to rewrite the minutes, which is to say, to rewrite the letter, which is to say, to mend the paper — the third time the council has so resolved this summer, and the acting secretary has begun to keep a column for it.

The council further noted, without numbering it as correspondence, a small line in the Muscovite Ledger, No. 217, 1613, where a chest of pale bark from the Indies lies noticed to the apothecaries of Kitay-Gorod, its name not yet coined in that town but held in the column for later; the council read the line with respect, being of the same bark, the same bitter, and the same habit of keeping a number counted aloud, and the two books — the Ledger’s and the council’s nitrogen book — now keep the cinchona between them, one per line.

The next ordinary meeting will be held 8 October 2024 at 6:00 pm in the same room, around the table marks, and the first weighing of the quinine jar will be read aloud, in the front room, on the scale, in both books.

The meeting closed at 9:14 pm.

Hana Kovač, Chair  ·  Elise Moreau, Acting Secretary of the Meeting
