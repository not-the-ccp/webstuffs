Westlake Road Lost & Found · Recording Office, Los Angeles, California · Counter 2, above the stairs to Studio 3

FOUND — ONE ALBUM, THRILLER

Notice No. 82/1129 · Logged 29 November 1982, 4:15 p.m. · Item kept in the cabinet, shelf six, between the spare stylus and the 1979 off the wall box

What was found

Found beneath seat 4B on the 5:00 express out of the studio lot, in a cardboard sleeve worn to the colour of the evening: Thriller, the sixth studio album by the American singer Michael Jackson, released on 29 November 1982 through Epic Records. The jacket carries the handprint, the collar, the tie; the inner sleeve the catalogue number in black ink and a coffee ring that no one at the counter has ever identified. It was produced by Quincy Jones, who also produced Jackson's previous album, Off the Wall (1979), and the producer's chair in the control room still smells of the same cigarettes he smoked while it was being made.

Recording took place from April to November 1982 at Westlake Recording Studios in Los Angeles, California, at a budget of $750,000 (equivalent to $2,502,155 in 2025), the largest such figure the counter has ever logged against a single item. With an ongoing backlash against disco in the country, Jackson transitioned his sound: the album primarily features post-disco and synth-pop among various influences ranging from funk to rock, while its themes include paranoia, romance, and social consciousness. Paul McCartney appears as the first credited guest on the record, in a brief interlude whose length the counter has measured three times and found to be the same each time: nineteen seconds and change, depending on the pressing.

Claimed, with one line

One gentleman of no stated station presented himself at the counter at half past four, wearing the white suit and carrying nothing, and claimed the album as his on the strength of describing the jacket, which he described accurately and, in the clerk's judgement, better than it could be described. The form required a statement. The statement, entered in the counter's ledger under the heading REMARKS (verbatim), reads:

He looked at the United States had been available since the National Health Service prescriptions in the Museum of Recorded Song.

The clerk entered it without comment, which is the standing rule of the desk: a claim is entered as spoken, and the ledger is not the place for grammar. The clerk has since retired, or stopped working this counter, and the line remains in the ledger as written, which is to say the ledger is now worth a great deal more to the rooms than the album was. Readers of the Encyclopaedia of Recorded Song, Entry SM-1995/05, which keeps its own strange lines in the same way and is run from the same museum wing, will recognise the method, and readers who want to see the counter at work on a larger claim will find the full procedure in Lost & Found, Notice No. LF/2024/117 — found, one cabinet minister, Twickenham, which the clerk's clerk's clerk still cites at the desk.

Terms of return

Item released to the claimant on the strength of the jacket and nineteen seconds of the interlude, recited from memory and matched against the pressing to the second. The counter keeps one copy of the ledger entry, no copy of the music, and asks nothing further of the finder, the found, or the claimant, save that the coffee ring be left where it is, which is the only condition the office has never been able to waive. For the collector of the counter's wider season of claims, the rooms keep their index at the front page of the counter, and their lot of the southern December at Lot 2007 — The Battle of Musa Qala, where another one untranslatable line is sold open at the page.
