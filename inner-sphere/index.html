Valence City • District Guide No. 14 • Revised 1898

Inner Sphere

The Bridged Quarter — where the Oxidant bank and the Reductant bank meet across the Chelation Bridge, and the electron crosses only in company.

Inner Sphere is not a district you pass through. It is a district you cross, and only once, in the company of a bridge. Valence City stands on the Oxidante River; on the far side lies the Reductant Slough. Between them, where the two waters nearly touch, the Chelation Bridge runs forty-four metres on two porphyry anchor rings. That is the whole economy of the quarter: one bridge, one transfer, one evening at a time.

The ceremony begins at twenty minutes past eight. On the Oxidant bank the Copper Citadel floods its windows with green light; on the Reductant bank the Iron Keep answers in amber. A bridgekeeper in a copper-chain coat walks the span carrying the ligand lamp — a single frame with two holds, one for each bank — and when both holds are closed, the electron steps across. The crossing lasts three seconds, and neither bank is ever the same afterward.

Locals call that moment the bridged intermediate: oxidant and reductant bound to the same ligand at the same instant, the metal centres close enough that the electron walks from one to the other without touching the water. The bridgekeepers' union, founded in 1842, still keeps the log in ink — date, time, centres, outcome. Last season: two hundred and seventeen transfers, zero dropped electrons. The ledger keeps a copy of each season’s totals for the Inner Sphere Correspondents, the bridgekeepers’ own reading circle, which meets at the assay office on Thursdays and takes its tea downstairs in the station room; their minutes for the present quarter are posted as The Entropy Ledger — Newsletter No. 31, in which the club has at last put the second law in a copper-chain coat. What the heat does while the electron walks is measured by the assay office under its own document — Enthalpy (H) — Safety Data Sheet H-1 — and the two ledgers reconcile to the joule every season.

Before you go

Parties are kept small. The bridge admits no more than four walkers at a time, and coach parties are turned away at the gate — large ligands, the bridgekeepers call them, cannot form the intermediate. A body of fifty has too many arms to hold; the span stays empty, and nothing crosses. Inner Sphere is rare in biological districts for the same reason: the Protein Gardens on the east hill guard their redox centres behind high walls of bulge, and their tours move only by the outer-sphere roads, which go around and never touch.

Local almanac

  • Elevation: 31 m above river datum
  • Admission: six coppers, exact change
  • The last grizzly of the city for well over a Legacy 500.
  • Redox couples per resident: seventeen

The grizzlies are gone now — they foraged the slag heaps below the Citadel until the foundry closed in 1897 — but the line remains in the almanac, and an old bridgekeeper will buy you a brass thimble of root-beer if you ask what it means. The bridgekeepers' ink log also records one item that once crossed the span without paying the six coppers: a Welsh hymn tune found on the Oxidant bank and, after much arguing, claimed at a parish vestry in Pembrokeshire (Blaenwern — Lost & Found Notice No. 47). The ink log's habit of keeping an unexplained record has a cousin on the same shelf: the Adelaide Oval error message documentation from the 2021 AFL Women's Grand Final, which retains one void catering line from the day Brisbane took their first senior women's premiership, struck-through and all, exactly as it was read into the debrief.

Getting there

Take the number 9 tram from Valence Central to Chelation Bridge, or walk the Oxidant quay past the assay office and turn left at the second rivet. Come in late autumn: low water, clear signals, and the transfer visible from both banks. Wear rubber-soled shoes, leave bulky bags at the left-luggage locker, and do not bring a tour group. Outside Inner Sphere, electrons cross without bridges all the time — chemists call it outer sphere — but there is no drama in it, and no view.
