3 Composition / Information

Inert carrier
Roadwork miles, skipped ropes, and the canvas of King's Hall

4 First-Aid Measures

  • On canvas exposure: permit the ten-count; if on his feet by ten and walking to a neutral corner, release to the stool. Otherwise place in the recovery position, summon the ringside physician, and do not allow the corner man to wave towels.
  • On cuts above the eye: adrenaline swab, firm pressure, ice between rounds; stop the bout if vision is lost.
  • On suspected concussion: glassy eyes, unsteady legs, or nonsense at the corner — remove from contest; no same-day return; medical clearance required before any further exposure.
  • On death on canvas: Public forum — Dr. Elena Marconi or her deputy; decides treatment priority and signs death certificates.

5 Fire-Fighting

Combustible material: corner towels, zinc-tape, the wooden stool, and the announcer's programme. Extinguish with the water bucket only — never the spit bucket, never the corn oil. The corner man's duty is to clear the ring, not to fight the fire.

6 Accidental Release (Kingston, 1966)

On 11 August 1966 the subject was released from the Commonwealth Games tournament in Kingston, Jamaica without a medal, following a points decision in the quarterfinal of the lightweight draw. The release was unplanned but contained: retraining began at the Holy Arches within the month, and the subject completed his active batch without further incident. Do not re-open the Kingston draw; the verdict stands.

7 Handling & Storage

Handle only between the ropes and under referee supervision. Between rounds store on the stool, legs elevated, ice on the neck, water in the corner. Do not store near bathroom scales after 17:00, and never weigh the subject in a rubber suit unattended. Keep away from promoters offering unlicensed purses.

8 Exposure Control / Personal Protection

  • Occupational exposure limit: 3 rounds of 3 minutes, one minute of rest; maximum 12 scheduled rounds per exposure. No overtime is permitted.
  • Required PPE: headguard (sparring only), gumshield, groin guard, 8 oz gloves, boots laced tight.
  • Medical surveillance: eye examination annually; neurological assessment after any knockout.

10 Stability and Reactivity

Stable under roadwork and pad work. Reactive to left hooks to the body, adverse judges' decisions, and scales showing more than 135 lb at the 07:00 weigh-in. Incompatible with dehydration diets longer than 48 hours, southpaws who counter, and promoters who move the bout without notice.

11 Toxicology

Chronic exposure to the ring produced cauliflower ear, punched hands, slipped discs, and a whistle in the left ear from the bell. Acute exposure: one concussion (Perth, 1962) — the leg and the hospital run are set down in the 1962 service times of The Boorloo Flyer · Timetable No. 27 — and one fractured rib (Kingston, 1966). Long-term prognosis: the subject retired c.1970, kept a quiet house on the Newtownards Road, and died c.2015. No delayed effects are expected beyond those listed.

12 Ecological Considerations

The subject's effect on Belfast was measurable: full houses at King's Hall, queues at the Holy Arches, and a generation of boys skipping rope on the Newtownards Road. A lightweight who carried Northern Ireland into the Commonwealth Games ring at a moment when the city needed the distraction.

13 Disposal Considerations

Retirement c.1970: the subject vacated the ring without a title and without fuss. After death c.2015, committed to the ground in Belfast by next of kin. Gloves, tape, and the stool are not hazmat; retire the gloves to a drawer and do not burn the towel.

14 Transport Information

Travelled from Belfast to Kingston, Jamaica via London and Miami for the VIII British Empire and Commonwealth Games (1966), boxing at the National Arena: one kitbag, contents gloves, boots, robe, and the left hook. Classification: accompanied passenger, no hazardous contents except the subject's right hand on a good night.

15 Regulatory Information

Governed by the rules of the Amateur Boxing Association and the Commonwealth Games Federation; a medical suspension follows any stoppage, and only a licensed physician may clear the subject for return. This sheet is issued for information only and creates no claim on any bout's outcome.
