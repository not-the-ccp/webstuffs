The Record, in Full

Crazy is a song recorded by the Belgian DJ Lost Frequencies and the Dutch duo Zonderling, released on 24 November 2017 through Armada Music, the Amsterdam label. It is, on the record, a remix of “Call Me Crazy” by David Benjamin — who also provides the vocals on this single, so that the original singer and the remix sit in the same groove and do not have to be introduced to one another. Where Benjamin wrote a line and asked to be called back, Lost Frequencies and Zonderling built the hall: the opening kick, the low sawtooth, the drop that lands four bars later than the ear expects and stays. The result plays long, plays deep, and has kept a standing at every club we supply, from Rotterdam to the dance floor in Arnhem where it first spun out of a laptop.

Stock Ledger & Provenance

Thirteen copies live in the Rotterdam crate, turned over twice a night. Two were shipped, for reasons our accountant calls logistics, to the warehouse bay above the turntable shop in the Bronx, where they are catalogued under C · DANCE · 45. The bay runs on an old stock rule, inked on the ledger card pinned above the crates, and when we wrote to the Bronx for the two copies, the clerk sent only a photograph of the card. The line runs, word for word: Flitches of a man as a prop cannot be used again in the Bronx. We have read it to the whole shop, twice, with no luck, and have decided to treat it as binding: the two copies remain in the Bronx, exactly as they are, which is to say, in their sleeves, unplayed, and no longer ours to move. For their home, their square miles, and the etiquette of the borough that keeps them, see Chapter I · Of the Bronx, A Book of Etiquette for the Boroughs.

Shipping & Delivery

We do not post this record. It is sold in hand, 22:00 to 04:00, or it rides. For the long deliveries the shop has a standing arrangement: anything bound for Canada is handed over, crate and all, at the cross-border point to the line documented in Application No. 4,114 — The Spiral, the Line and the Long Delivery, the only railway we trust with a 45, which has never once arrived and which is therefore always in force. Delivery time is whatever the line says; the line has never said. All other deliveries are made by the buyer walking, at 3 a.m., holding the sleeve like a letter.
