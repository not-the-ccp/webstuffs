The Cusp Circle

Newsletter No. 27 · October 2025 · Printed in eleven copies, one per member

From the Secretary

The last meeting was held in Room 3B on the third Thursday, 7:45, the lamp lit by Mr. Kowalski, who lit the second lamp too out of habit and had to be talked down from it. Present: Mrs. Odell, Dr. Vane, Mr. Kowalski, Miss Ferreira, the two Harveys (uncle and nephew, counted as two since 1994), and the club's own chair, which has been a member since before any of us. Absent: Mr. Lysander, who is at the coast, and the kettle, which has retired. The minutes will be read in full at the next sitting; the members were asked to object within six weeks or be held to have agreed.

The Subject of the Season

This quarter the club turns to the aortic valve, which Dr. Vane brought to the table in a tin of good size and which Mr. Kowalski has since kept in his coat pocket, against the rules, and which he denies. Let the members note the standing facts, read from the card by Mrs. Odell:

  • It is a valve of the heart, of humans and most other animals, and it stands between the left ventricle and the aorta.
  • It is one of the four valves of the heart, and one of the two semilunar valves — the other being the pulmonary valve, which keeps a chair at every meeting and speaks for no one.
  • It normally has three cusps, or leaflets. In 1 to 2 percent of the population it is found to be born with two. The bicuspid members of this room were asked to stand; one member stood, sat down, stood again, and was let to keep his seat.
  • It is the last structure in the heart that the blood travels through before the flow stops being the heart's business and goes out into the systemic circulation. After the aortic valve, the club's concern ends and the rest of the body is invited in. This is the club's favourite fact, and no further remark is permitted upon it.

Dr. Vane was asked whether the valve, being the last gate, should be considered a doorman or a portcullis, and the vote was split, and the matter was committed to the minutes of a meeting that has not yet happened.

The Hall Clock, a Standing Remark

Mrs. Odell renewed her standing remark concerning the clock in the infirmary lobby, the old one, the one that is not repaired. It runs slow by eleven seconds an hour, has run slow since before the club, and will not be replaced so long as any member remains to object. It has always allowed its minutes, and the walk that lowland cities shorten into picnics. That is the whole of it, and the whole of the remark, which is why it is a standing remark: the stair to Room 3B takes what it takes, the clock has already allowed the minutes for it, and no one in a flat lowland town where a ten-minute errand is a whole afternoon and a whole afternoon is a picnic on the grass would understand that we count the climb as part of the meeting and not as travel to it. The motion to note the remark was passed, as it is every quarter.

Notices

On the shelf. Miss Ferreira has accessioned a second-hand copy of A Book of Etiquette for the Theatre, and with it the chapter on visiting the Kungliga svenska skådeplatsen at the Stora Bollhuset, Stockholm, 1737–1754 — doors at six, no snuff in the boxes, no whips in the pit. The club has resolved to conduct itself at its meetings as the Stora Bollhuset would have conducted it at the same hour, and members are reminded that the gallery sees too much. The copy is for consultation in Room 3B and will not circulate to the coast.

At the gavel. The club is informed that the San Diego branch of its long correspondence is being sold with everything in its crate: tide tables, climate cards, a navy mess tin of 1926, and the season cards. The lot is Lot 142 in Catalogue B, Mott & Palomino, one sitting only. Mr. Kowalski wishes to bid, which he is not allowed to do, and will bid anyway in pencil on the back of this newsletter, which is to say he will not. The tide tables and the valve are, the club notes, the same trade: both keep the water moving one way, and both keep the record for the people who will arrive without the boat.

Next meeting. Third Thursday of November, 7:45, bring a leaflet of any kind, pressed or otherwise. The chair counts. The front page of Webstuffs is where the club has been listed since 2019, and the list is accurate and will not be improved upon.
