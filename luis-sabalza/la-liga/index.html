El Sadar, Gate C, second desk by the turnstiles · Calle de San Juan 4, Pamplona

Sale No. 402 — Lot 38

Lot 38 One professional football league, entire, with all appurtenances, the refreshments table, and the title ledger

To be sold without reserve on the first Thursday of the month, at the hour the turnstile does not register · inspection each evening, the tunnel being short · the hammer to fall when the Society is patient no longer

The Lot

The lot comprises the Campeonato Nacional de Liga de Primera División, commonly known as the Primera División or La Liga, and officially known, for sponsorship reasons, as LaLiga EA Sports: a professional association football league in Spain, and the highest level of the Spanish football league system. Governed by the Liga Nacional de Fútbol Profesional, it is contested by 20 teams over 38 matchdays, each team meeting every other twice, at home and away, which is to say the lot is one season of 380 matches, and the hammer price has been adjusted accordingly. The auctioneers have examined the lot in the person of its clerk, and report it complete in all particulars, with the exception of one lawyer and club president, Luis Sabalza Iriarte, Notice No. 402, who walks the tunnel at his own pace and is not for sale, being, like the papers that concern him, the club's and not the auctioneers'.

In its present condition the league has drawn, since its inception, a total of 63 teams, and the auctioneers will be pleased to point out that no other lot in this sale carries as high a passenger list. Nine clubs have been crowned Spanish champions in the lot's history. Real Madrid and Barcelona have dominated the competition, with 36 and 29 titles respectively, and the auctioneers note that the arithmetic of those two figures is the entire basis of the lot's reserve price. Other successful clubs include Valencia, with six, Atlético Madrid, with seven, Athletic Bilbao, with eight, and Real Sociedad, with two, while the remaining two of the nine — Sevilla, one, and Real Sporting de Gijón and Deportivo La Coruña, two apiece between them in the books the office will not be asked to correct twice — are offered to the highest bidder as a consolation pair, and are considered very good value indeed.

The 38 Matchdays — a working of the hammer

The season is the lot's chief appurtenance, and the auctioneers invite any bidder to try the hammer's own reckoning before the bell: enter a club's record over the 38 matchdays — wins, draws, and losses — and the board below will strike the points at three to a win and one to a draw, and place the club where the standings keep it. The board is of the Society's make, it runs at the second desk by the turnstiles, and it does not hurry, which is the only qualification a thing needs at El Sadar.

—

Enter a record of exactly 38 matchdays and the board will place the club.

The board keeps no opinion on the club, only on the arithmetic. A record of 36, 35, or 37 matchdays is not a record this board knows how to strike.

Annexe R — The Refreshments Table, and the Caterer's Ledger

With every sale of this house there goes a refreshments table, and with this sale the table was kept by M. Starbuck, caterer of record, who held the post for three sales and one long afternoon in the tunnel, and who kept a ledger in which the auctioneers are obliged to transcribe, word for word, one line they have been unable to price:

“Presently, after many failures Starbuck contrived to escape his duty to mix 480 Indian 480 Tartar 481 Mutton, baked minced 703 broiled and tomato sauce.”

The line is filed exactly as found, with its numbers, which the auctioneers have checked against the table and report to be the table's, though they have not checked the many failures, and the 480 Indian and 480 Tartar and 481 Mutton, and 703 of the broiled, and no tomato sauce at all, are offered to the highest bidder as one confused course, unsplittable, being the lot's only item with no reserve price and, on the auctioneers' show of hands, no appetite either. It is worth recording, for the books of the sale, that the caterer's escape was counted at both ends of the tunnel, as the Society counts everything, and that Starbuck was not, on any count, found. The auctioneers further report that beneath this line, in the same ledger, lies a second line of Starbuck's that the office could not price either — a standing order of casks — and that it has been carried over, word for word, to the glossary's night table, where it fills the entry that the hammer asked for: Entry 403, Latin America, and the casks of the middle of July.

Conditions, and the Watch

All lots are sold as catalogued, the league being offered with its governing body, its full history of 63 competitors reduced to the 20 on the present board, and the understanding that the tunnel walk continues through the sale. Bidders are referred, for the lot's immediate neighbours, to the Society's own Lost & Found Notice No. 402, where the missing president and his leather folio are filed, and to the committee minutes of the same red book, reached in the house at Minutes of the Meeting of the Ecclesiastical Committee, 26 May 604, whose clerk from Pamplona signed this sale's catalogue in person, with the folio, and whose watch at the turnstile is asked to extend to any bidder who walks off the lot. The front page of the house — Webstuffs — carries the shelf this lot is laid upon. Enquiries to the second desk by the turnstiles, Gate C, Thursdays; the reward for the return of the president remains one season ticket, Norte stand, seat 47, behind the drum, which this sale regards as the best seat in the house for a man who will not sit.
