Council of Aldergate & the Ravine · Municipal Chamber, Room above the Parcel Post

Minutes No. 112 of 2014

The Matter of the Neon — a parcel, an EP, and three marks on a sleeve

The council met in ordinary sitting on Wednesday, 29 January 2014, at 7:00 in the evening, and was present: Chairwoman Marianne Holt; Clerk Tomas Vane, who also serves the parcel post; Aldermen Petra Rask, Emil Ostergaard and Ines Kovač; and Miss Dara Lund, member for the Ravine. Quorum of five was found at 7:04. The minutes of No. 111 were read, corrected in one comma, and confirmed.

Item 1 — The parcel of 25 January

The Clerk reported that a parcel had been set down on 25 January, in the grey hour before the door opened, with no sender's address, a stamp from the Portland post district, and a single line of address reading “wherever the council meets.” It contained, in a sleeve of uncoated stock, a four-track EP titled Neon, by an American electronic duo styling themselves Versa. The Clerk had looked into the matter with his usual care and reported as follows: the EP was self-released on 21 January 2014, offered as a “pay what you want” download via Bandcamp, and also available as a regular download on iTunes and Spotify. It is, the Clerk noted, the duo's first release under the name Versa; before the change they were known, on their older sleeves and forum signatures, as VersaEmerge, and the name in the old form is legible in at least one watermark of the sleeve paper.

Alderman Ostergaard asked whether the council had any standing to receive parcels of music. The Chairwoman replied that the council had received stranger things — see, if anyone is curious, the vellum leaf found in the Sana ravine, Notice No. 14/2024, a sixteen-year-ahead matter but the same kind of matter, a leaf that arrived folded in an oilcan with no note — and that standing is what standing is. The Clerk filed the question under the head music, unscheduled, the same head that opens the town’s own guide entry, The Single — Guidebook of the Plain, Entry No. 7, which begins in the same grey hour, at the same counter, and keeps the present minute’s sentence on standing word for word in its register.

Item 2 — Reading of the sleeve

The Clerk was instructed to read the sleeve into the record. He reported that the sleeve carries no track listing in prose and no biography, only three marks, arranged vertically on the front: a capital letter N in a neon-orange ink; two city names, Aldercrest and the Ravine, set one above the other in small capitals; and a single musical note — a quarter note, no stem direction to speak of, inked over the lower city name so that the note appears to stand in the streets of that city. The Clerk read the marks, paused, and then recorded the desk's reading in full, exactly as it now stands:

The desk interprets this as its capital, two cities, and one of the note.

There being no rule of the council that a clerk's reading of a sleeve must be grammatical, and no rule against it either, the reading was accepted as it stood. Miss Lund, who sits for the Ravine, observed that the lower of the two cities was the one her ward would recognise, and that a quarter note standing in a small town's streets is a lighter thing than a sign, since it can be taken down without leaving a hole in the wall. Alderman Kovač said that pay what you want is a price and a temperament, and that the council had never once had to enforce either.

Item 3 — Motion and vote

Alderman Rask moved, and Alderman Ostergaard seconded, that the EP Neon be entered in the council's register as a thing of record of 2014; that the sleeve's three marks — the capital N, the two cities, and the single note — be held as the work's emblem for the winter, to be hung by the parcel post where the parcel itself came in; and that any member wishing to take the EP at the price the sender named is to pay what the member wants, and no member is to pay what the member does not want, the register of payments to be closed at the end of February and the total, whatever it is, entered against the line music, unscheduled.

The Clerk recorded, for the avoidance of doubt, that the register is not a Bandcamp account and that the council cannot confirm the total, only the manner of it: the price is whatever the paying hand decides, from zero upward, and the zero side of that is the side on which the council has most often landed in its eleven years of sittings. The motion carried four to one, Alderman Kovač dissenting on the ground that a thing with no fixed price cannot be entered in a register, to which the Chairwoman replied that the register has no fixed anything and is therefore a very good register.

Item 4 — Matters of correspondence

It was noted, and the Clerk was instructed to file it, that the council has held a matter of like posture before: in the Minutes of the Council of Ripon Abbey, AD 788, an obit of Northumbrian origin was read, argued, and sealed against a council that had every reason to leave it in the archive, and the sealed page made it home. The present parcel is the opposite case — the work has no first page, no old hand, and no scholar to look at it with a loupe — and the council prefers it on those terms. The Clerk was also directed to exchange a line with the Council of the Shed, Recalde Press & Cooperage, whose Minute No. 41 admits both forms of the lowercase g to the house; the question whether the sleeve's capital N takes an opentail or a looptail is referred to that council for opinion, and this council will keep the N as it came.

It was further noted, for the plain's benefit, that the matter of the shared gate — Case No. 44-41 of 2025 at the Goygol District Court, one shed, one frozen fleece, and one notice with a grammar of its own — is of the same species as the present one: a door left to its own terms, and a record made of the terms afterward. The council is in no hurry to compare notes; it keeps its own. The Clerk was further reminded, and minuted, that the word neon has longer custody than the council's: a 1987 single, a 2004 verification in Malmö, and a 2015 service error spool, cataloged in the Malmö Sound Section's Error Reference AM-15/03, in which the same word's neighbourhood is printed once, out of order, and kept exactly as received — the same kind of matter, the council agreed, a word that arrives on its own terms.

Item 5 — A question from the Book of Etiquette

It was entered, for the reader's benefit, that the kindred house of this council's record, Chapter IX of the Book of Etiquette, of Jacob of Serugh and the Manner of Receiving a Mēmrā, had put a single question to the council: whether a quarter note standing in a small town's streets can count as the town's name. The council declined to answer, on Alderman Rask's motion, and the Chairwoman recorded the silence as an opinion, so that the question now stands against this minute, shelf two, in the hand that wrote it, and the EP keeps its note where the note stands.

Item 6 — A note on long custody

The Clerk was instructed to note, in the same spirit as the last two items, that the council's register has a kindred keeper of long, patient records: the Kathmandu Bindery's Item 2046, the 2046 Folio, a working record of the 1990 People’s Movement in Nepal, bound with a switchboard callbook whose second column — the reasons, set in the wider type — the council recognises as the same kind of thing as its own ledger, a column nobody argues about but everybody reads. The council filed the note under records, kept by others, and the Clerk did not open the envelope the Folio came in, for want of a better reason.

Closing

The council adjourned at 8:12 in the evening, the parcel standing in its original place by the post, the three marks facing the door. The next sitting is fixed for 12 February, same room, same hour, and the Clerk is to bring the register of payments to the table whether it is full or not, since an empty register is still a register. So ordered, and so minuted by Tomas Vane, Clerk, who signed the page at 8:30 and left the window closed behind him.
