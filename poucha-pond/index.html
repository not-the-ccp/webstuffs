Poucha Pond

The Night Recording of 18–19 July 1969 · Chappaquiddick Island, off the eastern end of Martha’s Vineyard, Massachusetts

This is the complete night, from the front door of the island club to the first grey light over the dunes, recorded with a two-track Nagra on the sandbank where the gravel track dead-ends, and transferred at fifteen inches a second in a room on Main Street in Edgartown on the morning of the 21st, with the keeper’s hands not quite steady enough to matter. The pond was black before the party started; it was black when the last car left. In between, one car crossed a one-lane bridge over Poucha Pond on its way to the ferry landing that had, in fact, been moored at the dock since 10:40 that evening.

The party is the island’s summer party, the kind with the lawn chairs in a circle and the radio under a blanket. Senator Edward M. Kennedy left at 11:15 p.m. with his twenty-five-year-old staff assistant, Mary Jo Kopechne, who was not going back to the island with the rest of the guests. He meant to take her to the ferry landing and drive back to his room at the hotel in Edgartown. Instead the car took the dirt road that runs past the dunes to the bridge — the wrong turn, the one he would name again and again — and the headlights went out over the edge into water that the ferry master has called, in the only sentence of his that the transfer notes record, deeper than it looks from either end. The Senator swam free in under a minute, by his own account, and said he tried to turn the car around and pull Kopechne out through the window. The car did not come around. She did not come out. He walked back to the party, and the party was still going, and nobody at the party knew, and the island kept its distance from the mainland for the rest of the night, which is its natural state.

We do not edit. That is the only rule of this label, and it is why Side One ends the way the night went and Side Two opens where the light came up. Track nine is the quiet one, and the one to explain: two instruments, cut into the master on the night of the transfer. One is the tide gauge on the north bank of the pond, a brass drum on a post that the ferry master reads twice a day with a pencil he keeps in the same pocket. The other is a float he keeps on the river the boats use on the mainland run to the landing, up the channel past the point. And on the river; both are on the line, meaning both are in the mix, at the same volume, the way a ferry man hears them — one water and the other water, and the boat goes in whichever way both allow. It is the same pairing, at a different scale, as the gauge pair in the Millbrook Hall notes for MG-1987-04, where the river gauge and the weaker metabolite are kept in one sentence and nobody in that room moved until the second reading was over.

The dawn patrol is searchlights, boats, the names of the guests on the telephone wire, and the sound of an island being asked to be a scene. The keeper’s field manual reads that patrol as the state arriving, in Chapter 14 — The Crime, where the night is filed as the series’ one field example and the transfer slip’s one strange line is kept word for word. We kept it all. The full listening of this record, from 11:15 to first light without a flip, is what this was made for; the Album Keepers have logged it as one of their complete-listening assignments, and their Long Side, No. 47, gives a schedule if you want to do it properly, needle down, lights out. If you only listen to one side, listen to Side One to the end. If you only listen to one track, play track five, the eight minutes and twenty-six of black water, with the door closed. And if you finish, as you will, with track eleven, the pond at rest, know that the keeper signed the transfer slip at 9:40 a.m., put the Nagra back in its case, and walked the gravel track out to the ferry in the morning sun, the way the island lets everyone out in the end. The keeper kept a diary that same morning, of the other sky the radio was carrying in from the wire — the Eagle down at the Sea of Tranquility, the three names, the only part that returns — and it is kept with the case as an addendum to this record: The Keeper’s Diary, 20–21 July 1969. A year on, in the Friday post of 17 July 1970, a folder of state ledger pages came up with no return address, and the keeper’s second diary entry — the one on the column of signatures and the line in small type it will not give up — is kept beside Chapter 14 as an addendum to the same case: The Keeper’s Diary, Entry Two: The Eugenic Ledger.

The night has one branch, and the label keeps it off the record: the Pigeonhole Board has been running a thread on the eleven minutes between the mooring and the bridge — The Divergence at Poucha Pond, Thread No. 4812, opened at 11:15 p.m. on the anniversary — and the ferry master, who reads it, allows the whole downstream a boat ride of sixteen minutes, Edgartown dock, no searchlights. He says the branch works because the scene is small, and that is his one more sentence, which these notes will not keep, because we do not edit, and a ferry’s words are his. On the anniversary night, too, the landing took in the one object the night never produced: a workshop of the old Art, left at 11:32 with its black water corked in a jar, now kept on the shed’s shelf as Notice Nº 4813, Found, where the shelf’s one rule matches this label’s exactly — we do not edit.

Credits

Recorded by the Recording Keeper of the Edgartown Field Tape Collective, from the sandbank on the mainland side of the bridge, with the Nagra 4C and a pair of ribbon mics under a canvas. Transferred and edited to nothing by the same pair of hands, Main Street, Edgartown, 21 July 1969, 9:40 a.m. Sleeve set in the keeper’s own hand; the forty-two copies are numbered in pencil on the gatefold, the pencil being the same one that reads the tide gauge. The ferry master contributed his one sentence and neither of the instruments. No guest of the party is identified by name in these notes; the island keeps that record better than we do.
