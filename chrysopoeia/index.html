Notice Nº 4813

Posted 3 July 2019, the anniversary, at 11:15 p.m. — the same minute the board opened its thread

FOUND
Item consigned
One (1) alchemical workshop, complete in itself, called by its owner “the Art” and, in the notebook, “the Knowledge” — the craft the Arabic word al-kīmīyā (الكیمیاء) gave the world, the old branch of natural philosophy practised in China, India, the Muslim world and Europe, and known in its Western form first from the pseudepigraphical texts written in Greco-Roman Egypt during the first few centuries AD.
Contents, counted on the bench
  • One athanor, lead-lined, with a single flue — one lane wide — and a fire that the notebook calls, in the Greek the owner favoured, mystic, sacred, and divine.
  • One alembic of the sort that condenses a whole night back down to what it was made of.
  • One leaden crucible, base metal in it, base metal meant to stay out of it: the common aim of the house, chrysopoeia, the transmutation, worked by purifying, maturing, and perfecting.
  • Three glass vessels, labelled in a steady hand — the blackening, the whitening, the goldening — the first one the same colour as the water it was filled from.
  • One brass tide gauge drum, unmounted, with a pencil in the case — not the ferry master's pencil, but a companion of it, kept in the same pocket, by somebody's count, on the same island.
  • One jar of black water, corked, filled at the dead end of the gravel track, labelled only: deeper than it looks from either end.
How it came to the shelf
Found at the ferry landing at 11:32 — the minute the downstream in Thread No. 4812, the Divergence at Poucha Pond, says a car with two in it and a full tank would have come off the boat at the Edgartown dock — on the anniversary night, wrapped in the canvas the recording keeper keeps over his ribbon mics, with no name on the canvas. The night it was left, no car turned onto the dirt road: the landing was empty, the ferry moored, the workshop sat in the dark with its black water keeping time on its own, and the island kept its distance from the mainland, which is its natural state. The keeper of this shelf has compared it with the other found lots on the island — the astronaut group of nineteen in Notice 19/66, which is also kept, also unclaimed, and also better recorded by the island than by us — and has filed this one beside it, on the night side of the shelf.
What the notebook says
The notebook runs to forty-two leaves — the run of the record, by coincidence, or by rule, the shelf has not argued it — and sets out the work in the old language: a matter taken base, a fire kept low, a vessel sealed, a stage passed and named, a stage begun. It names the pond once, and the gauge twice, and the bridge once, in one sentence the ferry master will not repeat because the ferry's words are his. The last leaf, in a different and faster hand, is kept word for word on this shelf, unedited, as the label keeps everything:
“Everything since — the one he would name again and again — and it is fit for work till she had succeeded in withdrawing from his mood.”
Nobody on the island has parsed it yet, and that is all right. The label's one rule applies to found objects as it applies to records: we do not edit. The line stays as it was written, the way the ferry stays as it is, moored or unmoored, and the downstream stays unwritten until someone writes it. The blackening takes the longest of the three stages, and it is the only one that looks like nothing at all. That may be what the leaf means, and that may be what the jar means, and the shelf is willing to wait a year for the person who knows which.
To claim
Come to the ferry at slack water — eleven minutes forty, by the pencil — or at the crossing against the tide, fourteen minutes twenty, for those who prefer the long way. Bring proof any one of three ways: the name of the third stage; the number eleven, which the notebook keeps coming back to for reasons of its own; or a pencil that reads twice a day and is never in its owner's other pocket. The workshop, the jar, and the notebook will be weighed, counted, and handed over in one motion, the way the boat hands over a passenger. If it is not claimed by the next anniversary, it stays where it is, which is where found things do stay on this island: kept, unedited, and in no hurry at all, the way the pond at rest is in no hurry at all.
