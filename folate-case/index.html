DISTRICT COURT OF ASHFORD COUNTY

THIRD DIVISION · CIVIL DOCKET · ROOM 4, 112 MERROW STREET

The People of Ashford County v. Aldous Bakery Co.

Case No. 44-1187 Continued hearing, morning session 6 March 2009, 9:41 a.m.
Deputy reporter: V. Quist Presiding: Hon. M. Colton EXCERPT — pp. 112–131 of the record

EXCERPT · KEPT AS SPOKEN

THE COURT: We are in session in Case No. 44-1187, the People of Ashford County against Aldous Bakery Company, concerning the flour sold as white bread in this county between the first and the thirty-first of May. Mr. Ellery, you may examine Dr. Hartmann.

MR. ELLERY: Doctor, you are a hematologist at St. Brannan’s?

DR. HARTMANN: I have been, for thirty-one years. I keep the ward on the fourth floor and the reference lab in the basement.

MR. ELLERY: I want to put the word folate on the record. What is it?

DR. HARTMANN: Folate is one of the B vitamins. It is also called vitamin B9, and in the older literature, folacin. The body uses it to make DNA and RNA, and to metabolize the amino acids necessary for cell division and for the maturation of blood cells. Because the human body cannot make it, a person must take it in the diet — it is an essential nutrient. It occurs naturally in many foods: the green leaves, the beans, the eggs, the citrus fruit.

MR. ELLERY: And the measure?

DR. HARTMANN: The recommended adult daily intake here in the United States is four hundred micrograms, from foods or from dietary supplements. Four hundred. A small number, and the reason it is a small number is that the body keeps no reserve of it worth speaking of. A shortage shows up in the blood before it shows up anywhere else.

MR. ELLERY: The plaintiff, Ms. Okafor, presented in April with megaloblastic anemia. You treated her?

DR. HARTMANN: I did. The red cells were large and immature, the maturation had stopped mid-stroke. It is the anemia the textbooks keep in the chapter on this very vitamin. I treated her with folic acid in the form of tablets, manufactured folic acid — the body converts it into folate — and within three weeks the count was turning. The reason the tablets exist in that form, doctor to reporter if I may, is that the manufactured form is more stable than the natural one during processing and storage. That stability is why it is in the supplements and why it is in the fortification of flour. Folate, in the form of folic acid, is what treats this anemia. That is the whole of the medicine, in one line.

MR. BRANN: Objection noted, Your Honor, that the last line is not in the record. I will stipulate to everything before it.

THE COURT: Go on, Mr. Ellery.

MR. ELLERY: I now call Celia Aldous, the owner’s daughter, who ran the day book at the Aldous bakery. Ms. Aldous, on the morning of the sixth of May, a delivery came to your father’s bakery. What was it?

MS. ALDOUS: Flour. Merrow Mill, up the river. The lorry came in at about half past seven, two men, one with the day book under his arm. The flour went into the big mixer room.

MR. ELLERY: Was it the same flour you used in April?

MS. ALDOUS: No. The April sacks said fortified, with the number on the panel, four hundred. These sacks said plain white. No stamp, nothing under the weight line. My father argued with the driver in the yard. The driver said the fortification was not on the mill’s ticket that week.

THE COURT: And the count of the sacks — who kept it?

MS. ALDOUS: The porter, mostly, but the man at the counter was calling them out as they went over the threshold, into the mixer room, one by one. The waiter said it aloud: three hundred of them. Three hundred sacks of plain white, in one morning, and he wrote it in the day book the way the mill’s man had dictated it: plain, 300. We baked on it until the fortieth arrived with the fortified sacks back on the ticket.

MR. ELLERY: Your Honor, the record will show that for thirty-four days the Aldous counter sold bread made on unfortified flour in a county whose daily measure, four hundred micrograms, a good many of its regulars were meeting at that counter.

THE COURT: The People may rest there. Mr. Brann, your examination.

MR. BRANN: Ms. Aldous, no one died. No child was born with a neural-tube defect in this county in the first half of that year. My client baked bread, not medicine, and he went back to the stamped flour the day it came in.

MS. ALDOUS: He went back the day it came in, yes.

MR. BRANN: The court will have my findings by return of mail.
