The Amazon River

Catalogue B · Lot 27 · One (1) river system · Subject to reserve

Description

One (1) Amazon River, whole, in approximately 6,400 kilometres of channel, rising in the Andes of Peru and running to the Atlantic: the largest river in the world by discharge volume of water — mean flow at Óbidos about 209,000 cubic metres per second — and the second-longest river system in the world, or the longest, a title disputed with the Nile. For nearly a century the Amazon basin's most distant source was held to be the headwaters of the Apurímac River on Nevado Mismi, in Peru, until a 2014 study found the farthest source to be the headwaters of the Mantaro River on the Cordillera Rumi Cruz, also in Peru. The Mantaro and Apurímac join, and with other tributaries form the Ucayali River, which in turn meets the Marañón River upstream of Iquitos, Peru, forming what countries other than Brazil consider to be the main stem of the Amazon.

The lot comprises the river with its tributaries and accounts: the Ucayali and the Marañón, the Madeira, the Negro, the Japurá, the Putumayo, the Caquetá, and the Mamoré, which the field manual's chapter on Bolivia sends from the Pantanal toward the Atlantic and out through the Amazon. Also included: one gauging station at Manaus, one at Iquitos, the river port of Belém, the island of Marajó at the mouth, and the delta's own books, which the water does not respect.

The river's discharge is greater than the next seven largest rivers combined; its basin covers roughly 6.3 million square kilometres and eight countries besides Brazil — Bolivia, Peru, Ecuador, Colombia, Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname and French Guiana — and the water has never recognised a boundary.

Condition

The river shows age and use: the channel shifts, the banks move with the wet season, and a bearing set in the dry season should be checked again when the water returns. One glass collecting jar per gauge station is included; put some at the moment they are exposed — when the falling water shows the bank, the jar goes in the same day, sealed, and labelled with the kilometre. The mouth belongs to the water, and the water does not respect the line.
