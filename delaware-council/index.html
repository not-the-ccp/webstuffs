COUNCIL FOR SMALL AND DENSELY KEPT TERRITORIES · OFFICE IN THE SMALL ROOM, STATE HOUSE, DOVER, DELAWARE

Session Nº 44 · Tuesday, 21 June 2006 · The room on the third floor, the one with the better window on the bay

MINUTES OF THE COUNCIL

On the keeping of a small state on the Delmarva — Delaware, Mid-Atlantic, South Atlantic — and such things as June put in its way

Roll call

The session opened at ten past one, with the following present: 9 members and the clerk, D. Halloway, who kept these minutes in the long hand, the one the gulf-facing room also uses. One member — Councillor A. Mid-Atlantic — sat with her hat on all session, which the council recorded and did not discuss. The clerk's slate stood at nine for the day, and the session is recorded as duly convened, because a small state keeps quorums the way a lighthouse keeps its flame: without being asked.

Item 1 — The bounds of the state

The council took up, as it does each June, the matter of where the state is. It was read from the standing record that Delaware is a state in the Mid-Atlantic and South Atlantic regions of the United States, that it borders Maryland to its south and west, Pennsylvania to its north, New Jersey to its northeast, and the Atlantic Ocean to its east — four neighbours, one of them a body of water, and the council observed that this is a fair trade for a state of its size. It occupies the northeastern portion of the Delmarva Peninsula, together with some islands and territory within the Delaware River. It is the second-smallest state, the sixth-least populous, and, which the council notes with the quiet pride proper to such a finding, the sixth-most densely populated. Its most populous city is Wilmington, and its capital is the small room's own town, Dover, which the council keeps for quorums and small states in general. No amendment to the bounds was moved. The bounds are the bounds; they have outlived the last three June storms.

Item 2 — The name, and who it is named after

Councillor T. West, of the third barony, reminded the council, and took the hat off once for the purpose, that the state's name derives from the adjacent Delaware Bay, which in turn was named after Thomas West, 3rd Baron De La Warr, an English nobleman and the Colony of Virginia's first colonial-era governor. The bay holds the name the way a shelf holds a notice: it does not let go. Councillor M. De La Warr moved that the council record its gratitude to the barony, which carried. The council observed, for the record, that a name which has travelled from a nobleman to a bay to a river to a peninsula to a state is the longest journey any article on the council's shelf has ever made without once going missing.

Item 3 — The wet first, from the gulf side

Councillor B. Big brought a copy of Notice Nº 106 from the Big Bend Mercantile, pinned 14 June 2006: Tropical Storm Alberto, No. 1 of the 2006 Atlantic hurricane season, formed June 10 in the northwestern Caribbean at 70 mph, ashore in the Big Bend area of Florida on June 13, then east through Georgia, North Carolina and Virginia, where on the 14th it stopped answering to its own name and went extratropical somewhere over the mid-Atlantic — which the council reads as this council's own waterline, and records accordingly. The mercantile's clerk log, kept in the same hand as the tariff, carries a marginal line about a smell in a wet Tuesday; the council has transcribed it, held it under the register, and declined to explain it, on the theory that offices of lost and found do not keep things that have already gone. The wet first was referred to the shelf and not discussed further. It is a June article, and it did the minimum, loudly, which is all the council asks. Under the same register heading the clerk entered one further find of the season, a single small cap washed up on the bay shore, reddish-purple, 4.2 cm across, which the council has not measured itself but has referred to the Noctuary of Fungi at Austroprunicolor, Entoloma — Entry 112, and entered in the margin simply as: entry 112, spores setting.

Item 4 — One planet, and the heading Psalms_

Councillor C. Coopers laid on the table the school office's Notice Nº 47 — Earth, third planet from the Sun, reported missing from the astronomy cabinet at Coopers' Lane School, an ocean world of 70.8% crust under water, last seen under the small white sail at the fish market, with one folded, salt-stained recipe card as its identifying mark. The council has taken the card's line — The calf being a tent spread over the fish, and mix with these vesper hymns. — and kept it in the council register under a marginal heading which the clerk has written, in the long hand, simply Psalms_, with the underscore, as the clerk writes it. It is under that heading that everything the council finds washed up on the Delaware shore — the second-smallest, the sixth-most densely kept — is now entered, with its measurements and its one unreadable line. The clerk's standing rule for such finds is entered in the minutes word for word, as follows: Ocean-worn objects with a name still on them may also be reported to the same hand as the identifying mark of the Psalms_. The council adopted the rule without debate, and noted that the planet itself has not, by every receiver at Oberpfaffenhofen, ever once refused a fix; what is missing is the cabinet's copy, and the cabinet, the council holds, is answerable for its own shelves.

Motion, vote and conclusion

It was moved by Councillor D. Dover, seconded by the Chair, that the shelf along the Delaware shore be kept open for one full season, that all finds be entered under Psalms_, and that the clerk keep these minutes in the long hand until the next June. The motion was put to the roll. It stood at 7 aye, 1 nay, 1 abstain — Councillor A. Mid-Atlantic abstained with her hat on, which the clerk recorded in the margin as (hat on) — and the motion carried. The shelf will be kept. The next session is fixed for the first Tuesday after the next wet June, in the small room on the third floor, the one with the better window on the bay.

Entered by D. Halloway, clerk of the council, this 21st day of June, 2006, in the long hand, while the tide outside the bay was doing what tides do: turning, and not turning on anyone.
