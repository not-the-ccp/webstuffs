Cognate

noun · plural cognates · clearance: field

Cognate names words that stand in direct descent from one etymological ancestor in a common parent language, inherited and never borrowed. English father, German Vater, Latin pater and Sanskrit pitár are cognate: one sound-shape, four inheritances, changed by regular sound laws. Cognates may not be obvious — language change can work radical effects on both sound and meaning — and words that appear similar, or identical, in different languages may be no more than false friends, such as English actually and German aktuell. A cognate is distinguished from a loanword: the loanword crosses from one language to another, while cognates divide when a language divides.

Procedure — establishing cognacy

  1. Record the word in the field with date, informant and place: Anoka Township, Coon Rapids Dam Road, 12 November.
  2. Compare the shape against the parent-language card-file. Reject anything that entered by trade, conquest or classroom.
  3. Test the sound correspondences. Initial p- in the parent regularly becomes f- in Germanic, so pater and father pass; anything irregular fails the card-file.
  4. Check the shift in meaning. Cognates may drift — English meat once meant any food — but the drift must follow use, not a borrowed sense taken wholesale.
  5. Enter the set in the register: parent form, reflexes, correspondences, exceptions.
  6. File the set under its station code. Anoka sets carry the river mark; coastal sets carry the quay mark.

Field caution — the wash

Pour this on the quay and the acid juice of the storey above.

The reagent strips recent loans from the page. Inherited sound survives the wash. Anything still legible at the quay and the storey above alike is cognate by the card-file's rule.

Field notes — Anoka station, night of 12 November

Informant Ruth Hammons, who kept the Shaw–Hammons house through both world wars, supplied the river set with wet hands and a saucer at each step: water, wave, the old word for ford. Recorder Petra Lindqvist, Room 214, Period 4, noted that the same card-file serves four thousand miles inland and the quay at Hunters Point, where sixteen hulls ride at anchor beneath fog that never burns off. The geometry repeats: the gap between the head of a first-storey window and the sill of the storey above, where the lexicon lets the water in, and the quay that takes what the storey above releases.

Standing orders

If cognates walk through your sleep, ask what they inherited and what they stole. Open the register. Pour the wash. Keep the card-file dry.
