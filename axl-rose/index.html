The Rose Pacific Railroad · Official Timetable No. 6 · In force from 6 February 1962

The Appetite Limited

Lafayette, Indiana ➔ Los Angeles, California — Express — One continuous journey, no transfer

Train No. 6 · Principal passenger: W. Axl Rose, pronounced AKəsəl, born William Bruce Rose Jr. · Conductor of record: the microphone

◆ ◆ ◆

The Appetite Limited is the longest single-name service the Rose Pacific has ever run: it leaves Lafayette, Indiana in Tippecanoe County on 6 February 1962, crosses the continent in one unbroken movement, and stands down at the foot of the hill in Los Angeles, where it is still standing. The passenger of record, W. Axl Rose, American singer and songwriter, is the lead vocalist of the hard rock band Guns N' Roses and has been the band's only constant member since its formation in 1985. Outlets with long memories — Rolling Stone, NME and Billboard have each, in turn, ranked him among the greatest singers of all time, the way a junction town ranks the one bridge that carries the whole county across the river. The railroad notes that his voice is wide-ranging and powerful, that it can arrive like weather and leave like weather, and that no timetable has ever had to amend for him, because he does not wait at junctions. He goes.

Card No. 6-B · Commentary from the Road Manager

Three things about this service are worth the passenger's time. First, the voice. The Railroad rates it wide-ranging and powerful, and the rating is conservative. A voice of that kind does not need the siding; the siding waits for the voice. This is the passenger's real advantage on the schedule: everyone else on the line is measured against the timetable, and the timetable is measured against him.

Second, the constancy. Since 1985 the formation has been rebuilt around him more times than the yard has switches, and he has remained the only constant member — the one wheel the track was originally laid for. The passenger lists (Hollywood Rose, L.A. Guns, and others before) are the small vehicles he rode before the long service, and the railroad keeps them in the branch drawer.

Third, the record. Over 30 million copies of the debut album, and the best-selling U.S. debut, standing unamended. The platform office at Aperture Junction prints the figure on its window and has let the glass fog over it, which is the office's way of saying the number is now part of the weather. Passengers who have heard the service described as a voice arriving like weather and leaving like weather have asked whether the weather signs its own tickets. The road manager's answer is that it does, in its own hand, at the head of the schedule.

The line passes, without calling, through the region of another stage passenger of this network — see the Chapter XIV · Of the Child Who Bows Last of the house book, whose bows are the counter-timetable of this service, the curtain's arithmetic to the track's. Fellow timetables in the office's file: The Bostock Mail, Gallipoli to Darwin, whose telegrams the office has compared with the Bunker Hill yard line, and Chapter 9 of the Huddersfield field manual, for the neoclassical Victorian station where the platform office keeps its own spring gathering. Notices to Passengers

  1. Block B is retired to the hour. Do not enquire at the window.
  2. The passenger's pronunciation is AKəsəl. Announcements should use it.
  3. The voice may arrive before the train and leave after it. This is not an error.
  4. Luggage found at Hollywood: one guitar, one bandana, one opinion. Claim within 1986.
