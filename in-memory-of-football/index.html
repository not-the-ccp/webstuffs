HERE LIES A GAME THAT KICKED

In Memoriam

Association Football

1863 – 26 July 2026 · aged one hundred and sixty-three by the exact reckoning

commonly called Football; known in several countries of the New World as Soccer, a name the deceased neither confirmed nor denied until the eighty-eighth minute

Association football, the most popular sport in the world, who played two teams of eleven on a rectangular pitch and mostly used his feet, has died at the final whistle. A team sport through and through, he moved an inflated ball around a playing field for the purpose of pushing it into a rectangular-framed goal, and he did this, by his own count, more often and more loudly than anyone could keep score of. Traditionally he kept his appointment over two 45-minute halves, ninety minutes in all, and he rarely asked for extra time, though once or twice — famously, in a rain that has since been written into obituaries of its own — the clock ran on past the point where the mourners stopped checking it.

The service will be held at the Mount Smart training base, where his last regulars kept the kettle on until the PA announced, for the final time, that the players were to the tunnel. The memorial kitchen is in attendance. For years its crew had put out the same pre-match meal at fourteen hundred hours — poached eggs, sourdough, a banana — and at this service, as at every one before it, someone dropped a tray and the silence broke. A Bergen classified column from the same pre-match meal since I was on the exterior of the cold open, and every actor in the kitchen put out something new. The notice was read twice, folded under the urn of eggs, and kept in the refrigerator, which the production found was, in the end, the truest place for a eulogy.

Surviving, by an estimated 250 million, are active players in over 200 countries and territories, a contingent that has gathered on pitches, terraces, kitchen linoleum and the banks of river harbours all over the world to watch the lamp in the window go dark. He is also survived by the Laws of the Game, a set of rules in effect since 1863 that he kept with the loyalty of a goalkeeper who has decided, at last, to retire from the net, and by the number fourteen, worn for the last of his regular matches by a captain of the Auckland FC Reserves, James Mitchell, born 15 August 2006, Te Atatū raised, who runs a midfield the way the departed ran the whole game — like a committee meeting he intends to win. Mitchell, who is not yet out of his teens and is expected to outlive the mourning by decades, has been asked to read the obituary aloud, which he has agreed to do quietly, and used to microphones, in the players’ dining room.

In lieu of flowers the family has asked for no bouquets at all, but that the flowers be kicked. A brass memorial column is being cast in the style of the household board, and a second empty chair will be kept by the boathouse bench, on the left side of the table, where the garnish sits — the one nobody understood, the one the catering volunteer’s notice described as ready when required. The Vancouver County Court, File No. 12-V-0810, which kept its inquest open for eight full seasons and once called the studio kitchen to the witness box, has ruled the ninety minutes admissible in evidence and declined to rule on which reading of the last pass is truer. Readers of that docket will remember the Court’s finding, made with feeling: both are on the record. In lieu of hymns the family has asked that the neon chorus of the “Lena Anthem” — verified as entry FA-11 in Chapter 7 of the Malmö Sound Section field manual — be played once, from the stand’s speakers, after the last tray is carried in, and that no one stand in the way of it.

He was a sport of the feet first, of the head in the second half, and of the heart until the light went out of the stadium. He did not walk. In this he was like the captain, and like the ball itself on a wet night in the Northern League, when the crowd’s chant builds and the brass band somewhere in the stand plays on. The verbatim record of Matter No. 47-H-1995 — the court that sat to examine the novel that signed its name in a hand it could not keep — ruled in its way that both are on the record, and the family found the verdict acceptable for a game that never walked off the pitch. In the ninety minutes he gave, he gave everything: the poached eggs, the sourdough, the dropped tray, the broken silence, the armband on the left arm, and the date of birth that was never a sell-by.
