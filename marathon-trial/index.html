EXHIBIT · SEVEN

The Athletics Selection Tribunal
sitting at the Old Library, Kew Green, London

Case No. BA-2021/029 · Verbatim record of the sitting of 14 April 2021 · before the Hon. Mrs. Justice M. Allwright, sitting as a sole arbitrator

In re the British Athletics Marathon and 20 km Walk Trial

The Games of the XXXII Olympiad — nomination of athletes for the marathon and the 20 kilometres walk — the Müller British Athletics Marathon and 20 km Walk Trials, held at Kew Gardens, London, on 26 March 2021

The CourtThe Hon. Mrs. Justice M. Allwright, sole arbitrator
For British AthleticsMs. Helena Marsh, instructed by the federation’s solicitors
For the claimantMr. Terence Voss, solicitor
ReporterD. S. Penhaligon, shorthand writer

The claimant, Mr. Arthur Bell, placed fourth in the men’s marathon at the trial, and contends that the circuit around Kew Gardens was mis-lapped on the morning of 26 March 2021 — the first dedicated British marathon trial since 1980 — and that the nominations to the Games of the XXXII Olympiad ought to be set aside. British Athletics contends that the trial was run under its own rules, that both the marathon and the 20 kilometres walk were held over multiple laps of the gardens, and that chip timing and the lap sheets control. The marathon events were won by Chris Thompson and Stephanie Davis, both of whom qualified for the Olympics; the 20 km walk events were won by Callum Wilkinson and Heather Lewis, both of whom finished outside their respective Olympic qualifying times.

  1. THE COURT: Mr. Voss, you have the opening. Keep it to the laps and the clock. Nothing else.
  2. MR. VOSS: Thank you, Madam Arbitrator. My client was in the second pack at the bell for lap six. He says the lap board read five, that no official raised the flag, and that he crossed for what he believed was the finish with a lap still to run. If that is so, every time recorded after lap five is wrong, and the nomination list with it.
  3. THE COURT: The result sheet has Chris Thompson first, 2:10:41, and Stephanie Davis first, 2:27:19. Both inside the Olympic standard, both nominated. Callum Wilkinson won the men’s 20 kilometres walk in 1:22:46; Heather Lewis won the women’s in 1:35:22. Each outside the entry standard, as the record will show. Is any of that disputed on its face?
  4. MR. VOSS: The times are not disputed. The laps are.
  5. MS. MARSH: Madam Arbitrator, the course was eight laps of a 5.25-kilometre circuit for the marathon, sixteen laps of a 1.25-kilometre ring for the walk, each lap marked by cones and an official with a bell. Chip mats at the start line and at the finish. The clock was started by the chief timekeeper on the gun, 11:02 a.m., and not before.
  6. THE COURT: Call the timekeeper.
  7. MR. FELIX CROWE (CHIEF TIMEKEEPER, SWORN): I am Felix Crowe, chief timekeeper for British Athletics, and I was at the gun. Eleven hundred and two. The gun went, I pressed the button, and the board clicked over at once. I stood at the finish with Mr. Dawes, the lap scorer, and we called each athlete down as they came through. Thompson passed at two hours ten minutes and forty-one seconds. Davis at two hours twenty-seven minutes and nineteen seconds. Wilkinson at one hour twenty-two minutes forty-six. Lewis at one hour thirty-five minutes twenty-two. I initialled the sheets at eleven forty-five.
  8. MR. VOSS: Mr. Crowe, on lap six the bell was rung late. My client saw the board say five when it should have said six.
  9. MR. CROWE: The board said what it said. I did not touch it. Mr. Dawes turned the numbers, and I initialled the sheet.
  10. THE COURT: Mr. Bell, you say you crossed for the finish with a lap still to run?
  11. MR. ARTHUR BELL (CLAIMANT, SWORN): I saw the clock. I saw the board. I thought it was done. Then an official shouted “one more!” and I ran another lap. My time stands either way; I was fourth. I only ask that the list be checked against the chip mats.
  12. MS. MARSH: The chip mats agree with the watch, Madam Arbitrator. Every finisher within a second of the sheet. Mr. Bell was fourth, four seconds behind the third man, and there was never a lap in doubt except in his own telling.
  13. THE COURT: Then let us see Exhibit Seven, the page my clerk pulled from the marshal’s box at the gate. Mr. Voss, your client’s solicitor marked it. Read the line that is written on it, please.
  14. MR. VOSS: (Reading) “And the front page of 1718, when the shopkeeper is open, and the plain.” That is all there is. Pencil, bottom of the sheet, beneath the lap totals.
  15. THE COURT: I do not know what the plain is, and I do not know which shopkeeper is meant, though I note there is a kiosk at the gate that keeps number 1718 over its door. The line is not evidence of a mis-lapped race. It is evidence of a margin. It stays in the record, word for word, because verbatim records do not tidy what they keep.
  16. MR. VOSS: We submit nothing further on the line. On the laps, we ask the tribunal to direct that the chip data be re-tallied before any nomination issues.
  17. THE COURT: The chip data is in evidence as Exhibit Eight, and the re-tally was done on 29 March. Nothing changed. Thompson and Davis are nominated for the marathon. Wilkinson and Lewis won the walk on 26 March 2021, and each finished outside the Olympic entry standard; the federation may deal with the quotas as it sees fit. The claim is dismissed. The record is closed.
  18. MR. VOSS: We note our exception, Madam Arbitrator.
  19. THE COURT: Noted. The sitting is adjourned.

I, D. S. Penhaligon, shorthand writer, certify that the foregoing is a true and correct excerpt of the transcript of the proceedings of the Athletics Selection Tribunal held on 14 April 2021 at the Old Library, Kew Green, London.
