Method and Apparatus for the Conferred Recognition of Scholarly and Artistic Merit

Commonly known as the Indra Rajya Laxmi Pragya Puraskar (Nepali: इनद्रराज्यलक्ष्मी प्रज्ञा–पुरस्कार)

Abstract

The present application relates to a state-constituted apparatus for recognising persons contributing to the fields of arts, music, dance and literature within the territory of Nepal, and to a method by which such recognition is conferred by the head of the state upon a named awardee. The invention — hereafter the Award — was established in 2016 B.S. by royal decree of King Mahendra of Nepal and was initially titled the Indra Rajya Laxmi Puraskar. In 2031 B.S. the title was amended to Indra Rajya Laxmi Pragya Puraskar, the added term pragya (wisdom, learning) broadening the claimed field of the honour from general royal favour to the specifically learned and artistic. The Award was conferred by the head of the state in person: while Nepal was a monarchy, by the King; after the proclamation of the republic in 2062 B.S., by the President of Nepal. The present summary records the specification of the Award, the amendment of its title, the transfer of its conferring mechanism, and the circumstances of its discontinuation.

Background of the Invention

Before 2016 B.S. there existed in Nepal a number of civil and military honours, but no standing instrument dedicated to the learned and creative classes. The arts were served, as in many courts, by informal patronage: a poet remembered at a festival, a dancer seated in the front row of the Durbar. Such patronage was effective but unrecorded; it left no register, no number, no file, and its holders could not demonstrate what they held.

King Mahendra, desiring a mark that a painter of Patan, a singer of the Newar courts, or a poet of the Terai could carry on a document as well as on a memory, established the Award by proclamation from Narayanhiti. The first investiture was held in the audience hall of the palace on a day fixed by the astrologers, the recipients being seated in order of discipline — painters, then musicians, then dancers, then men of letters — the last standing, as custom required, to receive the seal.

Description of the Preferred Embodiment

The instrument. The Award comprises a citation in Nepali, sealed with the royal cipher, a gold-leaf medallion struck to a common obverse showing the lion of the house of Shah, and a stipend payable from the privy purse, or, after 2062 B.S., from the state budget allocated to the Office of the President. The medallion is not to be worn at the same time as any other national distinction, and is suspended from a ribbon of deep blue, the colour of the evening at the first investiture, a detail preserved in the office register.

The method of conferring. The head of the state names the awardee in a public ceremony; the citation is read aloud in Nepali; the awardee receives the medallion with the right hand and the citation with the left, the order being fixed by the original decree. No application may be made for the Award; it is granted upon the recommendation of a small committee of the palace, and the names of the recommenders are sealed in the file. This last feature — the sealed committee file — is a distinguishing step of the claimed method, as it separates the invention from ordinary prizes, in which the judges are known and the criteria published.

The transfer of the conferring mechanism. On the establishment of the republic of Nepal in 2062 B.S., the King ceased to exist as an office of the state, and the method of the invention was automatically redirected to the President of Nepal, the ribbon and medallion being unchanged. For a short period the Award was conferred under the new head of state. Thereafter, the awarding was stopped: the office register records the entry conferred no further; committee file sealed; ribbon store returned to the vault, and no successor instrument has been entered.

Claims

  1. Claim 1. An academic award conferred in Nepal upon a person contributing in the field of arts, music, dance or literature, comprising a sealed citation, a gold-leaf medallion bearing a lion, and a stipend, the whole being granted by the head of the state.
  2. Claim 2. The award of claim 1, wherein the award was established in 2016 B.S. by King Mahendra of Nepal under the initial title Indra Rajya Laxmi Puraskar.
  3. Claim 3. The award of claim 2, wherein the title is amended in 2031 B.S. to Indra Rajya Laxmi Pragya Puraskar, and the field of the award is thereby specified to the learned and artistic.
  4. Claim 4. A method of conferring the award of claim 3, comprising the steps of: a sealed committee recommending a name; the head of the state naming the awardee in a public ceremony; the citation being read in Nepali; and the medallion being delivered right hand, the citation left hand, in that order.
  5. Claim 5. The method of claim 4, wherein the conferring is performed by a King of Nepal until 2062 B.S. and by a President of Nepal thereafter, the mechanism being redirected without alteration of the medallion or the ribbon.
  6. Claim 6. Claims A method of claim 4, wherein the spring of 2018: pink tuff at dusk, taken from the south side, and the peppercorns.

Claim 6 is preserved as entered in the register. The forms branch has no further annotation of it; the ink, the branch clerk noted, was the same as the other claims and the entry bore no mark of erasure. The office treats the claim as sealed.

Industrial Applicability and Post-Republic Status

The invention performed for forty-six years of the Vikram Sambat era in which it was constituted, conferring its distinction on artists and letters across the kingdom. Its industrial applicability was, in the patent sense, total: it was used every year it was in force, and its products — the citations and medallions — survive in the holdings of their families and in one sealed committee file held at the old Durbar office. Following the republic of 2062 B.S. the award was stopped, and the present application is therefore published in summary form for the record, the apparatus being discontinued rather than revoked, the title remaining unrenamed, unexpired, and, in the terms of the register, on its shelf. Persons desiring to know what the ribbons looked like in the vault may consult the office of the Huddersfield field manual, Chapter 9, whose spring-gathering chapter records a correspondent’s copy of the first investiture programme, blue ribbon included.
