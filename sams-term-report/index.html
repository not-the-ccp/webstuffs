1Situation of the school

The School of Advanced Military Studies is one of four United States Army schools that make up the United States Army Command and General Staff College at Fort Leavenworth, Kansas. The school this term ran its three programs at full strength. The larger program, the Advanced Military Studies Program, carried the 87 cadets of Class 2025-2 through four courses in the Whitman Hall building, lecture at 0800, study hall in the Harkness Library from 1700 to 1830. The Advanced Strategic Leadership Studies Program, a Joint Military Professional Education II (JPME II) certified senior service college program for senior field-grade officers, held its 26 colonels and two-rank flag officers in a seminar of one long table in Room 214, where the coffee is strong and the minutes are kept in a green ledger. The Advanced Strategic Planning and Policy Program (ASP3) supported 11 officers pursuing doctorates from civilian schools, and its fellows filed four dissertations this term, two of them on the history of the garrison itself. The school educates future leaders of the United States Armed Forces, and this term it did so without once dropping a class.

3Field component

The class's field training ran 4–7 November on the west side of the post, into the Kansas Flint Hills country past the bluestone quarries and up the creek bottoms where the sycamores hold their leaves to the last. Four hundred and twelve metres of post, nine kilometres of trail, one bivouac in frost at −3°C, and a map problem that took Cadet Ostergaard eleven minutes, which is fast, and took the man behind her eleven minutes and one second, which is the difference the school likes to talk about. Attendance: full. Equipment: accounted for. The post band was not asked to attend, and did not attend, and the field was quiet enough to hear the surveyor's pencil.

4Remark on the AMSP 302 paper

On 28 October the cadet submitted a 2,400-word paper for Civil-Military Relations on coastal communities and the state's obligations to them, built around the surviving line of a ferry ledger from the village of Zoutkamperlaan on the North Sea coast of the Netherlands, a line the class anthology keeps word for word: “The number fourteen is a city can be claimed — in the North Sea to the village has never once been cancelled.” The paper's reading of it is this: fourteen is the village's registration mark, a number a city can be claimed to hold but which the village itself kept, and the ledger's second clause is its witness — the ferry service out in the North Sea to the village has never once been cancelled, and a service that is never cancelled is an obligation that is never renounced. Major Kessler's margin note, reproduced here at the cadet's request, reads: the village is the argument; the ferry is the proof. You lost a point on citations and one on the date of the second cancellation season, which you insisted did not exist. The school records the insistence. The school does not record the score the insistence bought, which was a B+, and the cadet asks that both facts be kept together, which they are.

5Conduct and remarks

Conduct: exemplary; one entry for arriving four minutes late to drill after a library session, withdrawn when the library session was found to have ended on time. The cadet kept the class study-group ledger for the second term, balanced to the cent on 6 December, and stood in the Harkness Library window during the 12 December wind, which two classmates recorded separately and which the dean has been told about. No disciplinary entries.
