The Whittier Evening Ledger

Personal & Public Columns — Classifications, Notices & a Small Town’s Long Goodbye, Kept Open for Twenty Years

Ads received at the desk on Green Acres Avenue, paid in advance, all except one, which is still being argued about. Click any notice to stamp it HOLD.

Resignations & Vacancies

RESIGNED. — ONE (1) 37th PRESIDENT

Richard Milhous Nixon, b. Yorba Linda, California, 9 January 1913, on the family citrus ranch; R. Milhous Co. grocery and gasoline service, 6810 Green Acres Avenue, from boyhood; Whittier College, then a Navy that went to the Solomon Islands and came home. Member, both Houses of Congress, State of California, 1950–1953. 36th Vice President of the United States under President Eisenhower, 1953–1961. 37th President, January 1969, reduction of the war in Southeast Asia, the table with China, the quiet with the Soviets, the Moon of 20 July 1969, the EPA and OSHA both founded and fed. Resigned the office 9:02 a.m., Saturday, 9 August 1974, becoming, to this desk’s count, the only American president ever to resign from it. Keys to the cello and the telephone left at the townhouse. The desk regrets the vacancy and notes the 38th was already in the seat, having been the 37th’s own vice president, which the town calls the economical arrangement.

VACANT — POSITION No. 37

Chair, the White House, to be let, term limited, references from the previous occupant not required, references from no one required. Applicants are reminded the next man to wear the number 37 will arrive in 1988 and be a different person; the desk has no intention of re-listing this one. No second term will be offered with it. No third term will be pretended.

FOR SALE — 1974 PICKUP

One (1) 1974 pickup, portico orange, the exact color a Greek Revival portico is not to be painted, though the Register’s small print has never stopped a private owner. Runs on gasoline, as the family business at 6810 taught its young man to run everything. Sold, in the spring of 1994, to the last buyer who would hear the whole story. Engine turns; the engine’s owner no longer does.

For Sale & To Let

CELLO, BAVARIAN VARNISH

For sale, one (1) cello, worn on the C string, in continuous service since 1921, when a ten-year-old in a rented room on Green Acres Avenue decided the family grocery needed music and the rent, possibly, did not. Played at a small town, at a war, and at a White House where the last one to hear it clearly was not in the room. Price: what the next cello player will say it is worth. The bow is included; the sonatas are not negotiable.

TO LET — OVAL OFFICE, 1600 PENNSYLVANIA AVE. N.W.

Executive suite, one (1), one (1) desk, one (1) telephone that has not been answered, one (1) window with a view that is now merely scenic. Rent due monthly; current tenant will vacate within the hour and is, at the time of printing, in the middle of doing exactly that. The tape recorder is NOT included; see Missing, this page, and do not wipe anything with a paper clip. No leases longer than one resignation.

RANCH, YORBA LINDA — SOLD 1946

Recorded for the file: citrus ranch, the boy, the lemonade stand, 1936–1940, netting $27.50 and one lesson in Republican economics, the kind learned before the word is chosen. Sold 1946, the year the boy’s father went to Washington and the lemonade stayed. The price was always more than the lemons were worth, which is where the lesson was.

Lost

LOST — APPROX. 4,000 HOURS OF TAPE

Reel-to-reel, presidential, 1969–1973, some of it secret, all of it now, in the town’s phrase, of a piece with the weather. Wiped, some of it, by one method this desk will not name but the reader will. If found, in any reel, in any safe, in any drawer of a building that has since changed its mind about the building, bring it to the desk, which has kept this advertisement open since 17 August 1973, the night it began. Reward: one (1) appointment to the third term, which does not exist, and is therefore a sure thing.

LOST — ONE (1) ARROWHEAD, GREEN & WHITE

Marker, chevron-shaped, the sort that stands at street level in front of a listed house and points at the monument. Last seen on the Pacific–Great Lakes Heritage Route, which runs the full account in its visitor’s brochure — the 1966 Act, the copper whale of 1895, the docent’s shh — at the National Register of Historic Places, Visitor’s Brochure, Stop C, aimed at the monument. And at the river, the alley, or sometimes the road itself. The pointing is the trouble with this one; it points the way a person of the 37th pointed, with conviction and without map. Whoever finds it is asked to bring it back with its shh, believed to be packed in the same crate, and to leave the Flanders Street club out of it, which is a club and not a search party.

Found

FOUND — THE MOON, 20 JULY 1969

Found, from the ground, by a crew of three and a nation of television, Apollo 11; examined, walked upon, and left in the condition found, which is to say, a very good condition. The finder, the 37th of the adjoining column, no longer answers the telephone and declined this space, so the desk claims it instead. The successor has the keys to the White House and the finder has the keys to a library in this town, where the Moon’s photographs hang in a room the visitors pass on the way to the cello. Please contact the library, not the former White House, which is merely an oval room to let.

FOUND — AN EXIT, SOUTHEAST ASIA

Found, at last, on the desk, 1973; a way out of the war in Vietnam, one in which the American soldier comes home. The finder declines credit; the finder is the man in the first column, and the credit goes where the credit has always gone, which is to the ones who walked back. Filed here so that the found things of this administration stand in one column and can be read in a single sitting, which is the town’s whole argument for keeping a column open twenty years.

Established & Ongoing

ESTABLISHED 1970 — TWO AGENCIES

The Environmental Protection Agency and the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA), both founded this administration, both ongoing, both now hiring the word federal by the yard. The one makes the air something other than a complaint; the other makes the workplace a place a body may survive. They outlast their founder, which the town has learned is what everything that is meant to outlast him does. No sale, no lease, no resignation attaches; see the small print, which is the whole point.

Personals

DÉTENTE. — S. & C.

S. and C. (Soviet Union and China) are pleased to announce a quiet of their own arranging: S. has agreed to talk, and C., visited at the table in February 1972, has learned to bow at a distance no longer awkward. The table-tennis ball that started it is in a drawer at the library in this town, and the handshake across the street in Beijing has aged like paint on a Greek Revival portico — badly in damp, but it holds. The 37th, who arranged the quiet, is no longer the party to it; the desk notes this only so the column stays true, and so the quiet may rest on its own two borders.

ONE (1) DOCENT’S SHH, FOR SALE

In continuous practice since before 1966, said to carry more weight than any arrowhead on the Register, and posted, in its full recorded form with the second, smaller line about the almanac page, at the left-field corner of a certain Chicago park, beside the fudge stand — the corner itself fully described in the Curtright Circuit tourist brochure, seasons 1943–1946. The shh will be sold to the quietest bidder, and only to that bidder; the auction closes at dusk, when the mowers run their last loop. The buyer will also take the rule, which is that the kitchen is closed, and the correct response to the closed kitchen is silence, not a eulogy. The desk, for the record, is not the quietest bidder. The desk is not, in fact, bidding.

WANTED — PRESS SECRETARY, ONE (1)

The former was two hundred pounds, excellent at the game of no comment, and missing in action since the spring of 1973, when he took to the road and has been, ever since, in the same condition as the arrowhead in the Lost column: pointing somewhere, with conviction, without map. Applicant must be willing to be asked, at every podium, a question the answer to which is the question’s only answer. The 37th no longer takes applications. The town does.

The Twenty-Year Notice

IN MEMORIAM — RICHARD MILHOUS NIXON, 9 JANUARY 1913 – 22 APRIL 1994

The column has been kept open since 9 September 1974 and is closed on this day, 26 April 1994, the day after the last of it passed in New York, four days after his 81st birthday, and is now a record. Richard Milhous Nixon, the 37th, is buried in this town, in the library he built, where the cello is, where the table-tennis ball is, where the photographs of the Moon are, and where the 1974 pickup, portico orange, made its last trip in the spring of this year. The town’s account of him runs the full length of this page, and no account of him the town has ever written runs any shorter, which the readers may treat as the town’s opinion, offered in the only currency a small-ads column is permitted: the fact that the column is open, and that it has been open, and that it is now, finally, and only, paid in full. The shh stays. The shh was always the shh. Read the page once more, and leave the kitchen closed.

Desk note, in pencil, on the verso: the 36th and the 37th are the same man, and the column says so in two places, which is why there are two places. The 36th served under Eisenhower, 1953–1961; the 37th served until the 9th of August. That is the whole of it, and that is the whole of the column. — Webstuffs, general index of the house’s shelves, if the reader wishes the shelf this page is filed on.
