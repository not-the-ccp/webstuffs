On the Record Itself

Codeine-6-glucuronide — the chemists call it C6G, the band calls it "the six" — is a major active metabolite of codeine, and it may be responsible for as much as 60% of the analgesic effects of codeine. We spent eighteen months at Millbrook Hall trying to record that 60%. The drug is the parent; the metabolite does most of the work, quietly, downstream, in the body's own conversion. That was the whole argument of the album before a single note was played: something is swallowed, and what it becomes is not the thing you swallowed. Side one is the parent compound. Side two is what it turns into.

C6G also exhibits decreased immunosuppressive effects compared to codeine, and we took that sentence seriously in the control room. The second half of the album is a half that has let go of part of its weight — thinner strings, less tape saturation, the organ standing outside the room instead of inside it. Engineer Ada Okafor cut the vocal chain between takes on the ninth of March and never quite put it back the same way. She called it "metabolizing the room." We called it side two.

Recording ran from the first week of November 1986 to the last week of June 1987 at Millbrook Hall, a converted grain store on the north bank, with the river gauge — Station 4, brass and rusted, installed 1911 — on the window of the main studio. The gauge is the clock on the record. Whenever the water moved, the band stopped and listened, and if the water kept moving, the take was called good. Of forty-one takes laid down, the water killed eleven and approved the rest.

The lyric book printed with this pressing survives in one fragment. The sheet for the final track, The River Gauge, is water-damaged and reads, in Larch's hand: "Settlers reached the field material — and the chamber that the smallest borrowed detail — the one the river gauge." We printed it as it is. It is the smallest borrowed detail on the record, and it is the one that holds.

Credits & Persons

Personnel
Mara Larch — voice, valve organ; Tomas Reyes — guitar, brass-bent harmonics; Ada Okafor — engineering, tape; I. Fenn, N. Fenn — field percussion (the drip line, second job); the Millbrook Hall Residue Ensemble — strings, eight chairs, five occupied.
Studio
Millbrook Hall, converted grain store, north bank, Station 4 gauge (1911). Recorded November 1986 – June 1987, in order, in whole, no flips.
Label
Millbrook Hall Records. Catalogue MG-1987-04. Pressed 1,987 copies, 180 g, gatefold, lyric fragment enclosed. Mastering: Ada Okafor, the cut of 9 March left in place.
Filed alongside
The complete-listening club in Portland keeps this record in its attic rotation, and their newsletter, The Long Side, took the album's only published review — four paragraphs, one empty chair, no verdict, which is how you know it was a good one. A museum placard on the composer and guitarist Joey Rubenstein hangs two galleries down and mentions the rehearsal rooms this band borrowed in the same season; both documents are kept within reach of the turntable.

Millbrook Hall Records does not flip the record before the hush. The six is the one that holds. MG-1987-04, reissue of the 1988 gatefold, ink set by hand, all 1,987 copies.
