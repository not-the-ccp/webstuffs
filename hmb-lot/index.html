Lot 47

A Sealed Vial of β-Hydroxy β-Methylbutyric Acid, with its Conjugate-Base Name, the Assay Certificate, the Medical-Food Dossier, the Kestrel Trial Notebook, and a Pamphlet Misprinted in Two Hands

The Lot, Described

A glass vial of 100 grams, food-grade β-hydroxy β-methylbutyric acid — the acid the trade knows as HMB, and the same substance, as its conjugate base, β-hydroxy β-methylbutyrate — the stopper waxed, the wax stamped twice with the assay laboratory’s mark, one stamp a small antler and the other a chain of six links. The substance is a fine white powder, slightly granular, faintly sour on a wet finger, and it is sold here as the whole consignment was assembled: not the powder alone, but the powder with the paper that tells a buyer what the powder is for. The lot is offered by its consignor, the estate of Dr. Ada Linge of Marlow-on-Brack, who held it from the Kestrel Nutrition works at Brack Wharf, where the substance was compounded in 2011 as the active ingredient of a medical food intended to promote wound healing and to give nutritional support to patients wasting muscle through cancer or through AIDS.

What the powder does, in the language the consignment keeps for itself: HMB is made naturally in the body as a downstream metabolite of the amino acid leucine — roughly five parts in every hundred of the leucine a person metabolises become, through the hydratase step, this one branch of the valine pathway, C₆H₁₂O₃, one hundred and thirty-two point one six grams the mole. In healthy adults who take it with exercise, supplementation has been shown to increase the gains in muscle size, muscle strength, and lean body mass that the work itself would have given, to reduce the skeletal-muscle damage the work inflicts, to improve aerobic exercise performance, and to shorten the recovery between sessions. The trial notebook in the lot — the Kestrel notebook, bound in green oilcloth, forty-two pages, in Dr. Linge’s hand — records a twelve-week course of 3.2 grams daily, taken in two doses, in a panel of nineteen volunteers; the last page gives the recovery time after the standard rowing test before and after, and the difference, in the consignor’s own ink, is underlined once. For trained and competitive athletes the notebook is less sanguine; Dr. Linge writes that the evidence there is mixed, that two of the eight did not move at all, and that she is not willing to call it a success where it was not one. The catalogue commends her for it.

The Pamphlet, and Its Misprint

With the notebook, the lot includes a slim pamphlet of the Readings in the Metabolism of Leucine series, printed at Brack Wharf for the works’ staff library. Its title page was relettered by a second hand — the first hand’s plate having been damaged in the bindery — and the relettered page now reads, in full: “(Latin: Boius; Ancient Greek: Βοῖοι) were a small shelf of readings, workshops, and printing demonstrations across not fewer than the whole.” The first hand had set a title about leucine; the second hand was evidently given a title intended for a shelf — a small shelf of readings, workshops, and printing demonstrations — and the two were bound together, misprint and all, and have stayed that way. The pamphlet is valued as the misprint is valued at this house: the error is the evidence, and the sentence is kept here word for word for the same reason the assay keeps its duplicate stamp.

Provenance & Condition

From the compounding shelf of the Kestrel Nutrition works, Brack Wharf (2011); thence to Dr. Ada Linge, who kept the vial in the assay drawer, the drawer labelled in her hand not to be opened before the notebook is finished; thence to the estate, which consigns without reserve. Condition: vial sound, wax intact, powder free of lumps; dossier complete in eleven leaves; notebook with one damp corner, nothing lost. The consignor asks only that the vial, if sold to a kitchen rather than a cabinet, be weighed before use and that the two names be kept for the one substance, as the dossier keeps them — the acid for the shelf, the base for the blood.
