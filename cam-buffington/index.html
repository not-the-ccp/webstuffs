Buffington, Cam

Born June 16, 2005 · American college football linebacker · Iowa Hawkeyes · Number 45

Appearance

He appears in the dream as a linebacker in black and gold, standing at the goal line of Kinnick Stadium in Iowa City after the crowd has gone and the lights have been left on. His number is 45, though the dream renumbers him at every down, and the digits rearrange themselves into dates — June 16, 2005 — and back again. He is 6’2”, 232 pounds, and when he speaks his voice sounds like a whistle blown at the end of a quarter that never ended.

Scenarios

If you dream of lining up opposite him at the snap, you are being asked to hold a boundary you did not know you had. If he tackles you in the dream, something buried is being brought to the surface; expect news from Iowa City within fourteen days. If you are the one carrying the ball toward him, you are running from a decision that keeps its own clock. If you watch from the stands, seat 45A, you are already part of the play whether you ever stepped onto the field or not.

Numbers

Fourteen tackles in a single game is the dream’s ceiling; the waking record sits at seven, and the difference is the margin between dreaming and waking. Kinnick Stadium holds 69,250 people when full, but the dream version holds exactly one: you, in the tunnel beneath the west stands, counting the seconds between snap and whistle. June 16 is the date the dream first arrived, and 2005 is the year to which it backdates itself.

Conditions to avoid: blanket assertions of privilege, waiver, and public-interest challenges.

The archive admits the tape of this dream only under subpoena, and the linebacker waives nothing. To claim that the dream is not yours, or that it belongs to the public because he wears a public uniform, is to misunderstand the possession: the dream owns the dreamer, not the other way around.

Interpretation

To dream of Cam Buffington is to dream of the collision as a form of clarity. The linebacker does not carry the ball; he meets it. In the dream, meeting is the point. You will wake with the sensation of impact in your shoulders and the certainty that a boundary has been set somewhere west of the Mississippi, under black and gold lights, at the exact moment the clock showed 3:17 a.m.
