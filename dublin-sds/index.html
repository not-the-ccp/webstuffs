Dublin

Product identifier: Dublin, Baile Átha Cliath (IA) · state matrix, solid, n.o.s. · Sheet No. 1215-06-15 · Other names: Dubh Linn, the Black Pool; the Liffey Town; the Capital of Ireland

WARNING

1 Identification of the substance/state and of the supplier

Recommended use: Capital function for Ireland; long-term human habitation; river crossing at the Liffey; port work on Dublin Bay; stout maturation; wall walking at the measured pace of Section 14.

Restrictions on use: Situated on Dublin Bay at the mouth of the River Liffey, in the province of Leinster; bordered on the south by the Dublin Mountains, part of the Wicklow Mountains range. The bay is uncontrolled on the east side; the river is uncontrolled below the bridges. The largest city by population on the island of Ireland is this one, and it does not share the record with anyone.

Supplier: Office of the City Surveyor, City Hall, Dame Street, Dublin 2 · Emergency telephone: 999 / 112 (24-hour)

Related document: The Desk keeps this sheet beside the one for its Welsh sister capital, whose dining room bills by the gramme and settles at a weighbridge counter — The Coalhouse, Bute Docks, Cardiff · The Webstuffs Dining Table No. 41. The two matrices were weighed the same week and the funnel ruled on both.

3 Composition / information on ingredients

Substance/mixture: City matrix, heterogeneous. Not a pure substance; composition varies by side of the Liffey. The city is the largest by population on the island of Ireland; at the 2022 census the city council area had a population of 592,713, the city including its suburbs had a population of 1,263,219, and County Dublin had a population of 1,501,500. Various definitions of a metropolitan Greater Dublin Area exist; the Desk prints the three figures it can stand behind and declines the rest, for the moment.

History: A settlement was established in the area by the Gaels during or before the 7th century, followed by the Vikings, who took the name of the place and made it a kingdom. As the Kingdom of Dublin grew, it took its name from the Dubh Linn — the black pool where the Liffey widens and darkens before it gives itself to the bay — and the city kept that name the way a river keeps a pool: without deciding to. In 1169 the Norse city was on its side and the Gaels on the other of the river, and Diarmait Mac Murchada asked the king of England for help; the town wall, the charter, and the street grid that survive are of 1215, and this sheet is numbered for them. The 1 July 1690 line and the 25 April 1916 line are both recorded in the city ledger under reactivity, Section 10, and both were walked, later, at the pace of Section 14.

Ingredients above threshold: none. Trace components: nitrogen and carbon dioxide (stout, the two-gas head), salt (bay), peat smoke (seasonal), fog (harbour, morning), and one copper funnel's worth of kitchen opinion, imported, see Section 16.

4 First-aid measures

Inhalation: Move indoors. For peat smoke or harbour fog, remain in a dry room until the air clears; the city usually does, twice a day, by weather.

Skin contact: The rain requires no treatment; a change of socks is the whole protocol, and it works. The Liffey, if it has been on the hands, washes off with the same water and a little soap.

Eye contact: Rinse cautiously with water for at least fifteen minutes. Do not use the bay; it is not the water for this.

Ingestion: Do not drink Liffey water. If stout has been taken as a remedy where it was not the remedy, the treatment is water, time, and a chair that faces the room. Obtain no one's opinion; opinions in this matrix are expensive and usually delivered at the pass.

Most important symptoms and effects: A warm voice (ethanol); grey clothes (rain); the sense, in a stranger, that the city has already been discussed once, with them, some years ago.

Advice to physicians: Treat symptomatically. There is no specific antidote for a capital.

5 Fire-fighting measures

Suitable extinguishing media: Water, rain, and time, in that order of preference. The ledger keeps three great fires — 1270, 1674, and the April of 1916 — and the media are the same three in each entry.

Specific hazards arising from the substance: Thatch and peat give a slow, low fire that does not argue; timber and river give a fast one. The bay is not a fire, but it is the colour of one at dusk, and the Dock A men of the old harbour keep the distinction on file.

Advice for firefighters: Keep the river at your back and the bay in front of you, and do not follow a fire down toward the pool. The black pool is where the river stops being a river, and the fire does not have to stop with it, which is the whole of the danger.

6 Accidental release measures

Personal precautions: For any release to the Liffey, work from the upstream side; the tidal reach runs one way at a time, and it tells you which, by the water, without a vote.

Environmental precautions: Contain sediment and salt before the bay; the pool at the mouth is the last place in the matrix where a mistake can still be a mistake.

Methods and materials for containment and cleaning up: Booms of the harbour pattern; coir and straw for the banks; the old wharf ledgers for the record, which are kept complete in the city archive and are the reason the Desk trusts this matrix's numbers.

Reference to other sections: See Sections 8 and 13.

7 Handling and storage

Precautions for safe handling: Cross the river at the bridges and not below them; the three principal crossings in the walled span have kept the city in one piece for eight centuries and are not to be improvised. Store the wall walk folded, as above (P405). The weighbridge principle travels here from the Welsh sister capital: The Coalhouse at the Bute Docks prices its menu in grammes of coal and settles at the counter by the door, and Dublin has never once tried the same, which the Desk counts as a character point — the matrix bills by the pint, which is a unit that cannot be quietly inflated either, because it is the law of the room. The comparison is kept in The Coalhouse, Dining Table No. 41, where the room rates a building in crossbars.

Conditions for safe storage, including any incompatibilities: Store the matrix at the bank, as it stands. Do not store Dublin Bay in a basement. Do not store the Liffey under the city; it is already under the city, at the lower levels, and the two arrangements have never met and the city has thrived on the distance between them. Incompatible materials: brick built against the tidal line, a boat on a Sunday that means it, and a surveyor who has measured the wall once already and been asked to measure it again.

8 Exposure controls / personal protection

Control parameters: No occupational exposure limit established for the capital matrix. Rainfall is the controlling variable and it is not set by anyone; it is noted, as the field ledgers do, and never argued over.

Appropriate engineering controls: Canopies at the quaysides; the bridges themselves, which are the city's original engineering; a roof over the stout in the maturation cellar, which is the one level of the matrix that the weather has never reached.

Individual protection measures: Waterproof coat, the long kind; stout-soled boots for the wall walk; a hat, kept; a second set of socks, carried. The wall walk of Section 14 is not a race; it is a reading of the city, taken at walking speed, and the weather column is part of the record.

Hygiene measures: Dry the boots at the door. The city notices the boots before it notices anything else, and it is not rude about it.

10 Stability and reactivity

Reactivity: Stable under ordinary handling for at least thirteen hundred years; the Gaels are still in the record beneath the Vikings, and neither has been removed. Reacts, without further notice, in July 1690 and in April 1916; both events are in the ledger, both were walked afterward at the pace of Section 14, and the walk is the city's own form of re-entry.

Chemical stability: Stable under recommended storage conditions, which are the conditions the city has kept itself in; the wall of 1215 is the oldest standing arrangement and it is still the correct one, which the surveyor's office does not say twice.

Conditions to avoid: A drier century than this one; a surveyor's second measurement against the first; the Liffey above its tidal line in April.

Incompatible materials: See Section 7, second paragraph, where they are listed once and kept.

Hazardous decomposition products: None under normal conditions. A city, decomposed, gives stones and a river and a name, and the name is the last to go, which is why the name is printed first on this sheet.

11 Toxicological information

Routes of exposure: Ingestion (stout, tea, the Liffey where it is not to be); ocular (rain, bay spray); inhalation (peat smoke, malt dust, the room where it is being discussed).

Acute toxicity: Ethanol, 4–11.5% v/v in the stout range, is the only listed component with a number the toxicologist will print; the number is the percentage, and the percentage is the whole of the advice.

Chronic effects: The voice warms; the boots grey; the stranger begins, in the second week, to have been in the city before, in a life the city does not have on file but does not deny. The Desk has had two reports of this from its own staff and keeps them together.

Note on the two-gas head: The stout of the matrix is matured, in the cellar under St. James's Gate, on a nitrogen–carbon dioxide mix that keeps its head in two phases, and the maturation runs by the week, not the hour, which makes the cellar the one clock in the city the weather cannot reach. Exposure to the head itself is not hazardous; exposure to the argument about the head, conducted daily at every counter in the matrix, is Category 3, and the room, as at the Coalhouse, has a way of settling such things through the funnel and not the pass.

Target organs: Voice, boots, socks, opinion. Signs and symptoms: a warm voice, grey clothes, and the standing conviction that the rain is a policy and not a fact, held in the matrix without a vote.

12 Ecological information

Ecotoxicity: The pre-tidal Liffey of the old ledger was a listed hazard to its own water; the tidal reach now carries salmon, and the change is the single entry the city reads as a verdict on itself, rendered in fish.

Notable taxa: Liffey salmon, returning to a river the ledger once declared unfit; brown trout below the weirs; cormorant at the bridges, in numbers the harbour men keep count of; heron on the north bank at the pool; grey seals in the bay, which come in and go out the way the tide does, without notice and without a vote.

Persistence and degradability: The river biodegrades; the wall does not. The two are stored together and the city is the result, which is the point of keeping the wall, if the point is asked for.

13 Disposal considerations

Disposal methods: The municipal stream for inert material; the river, for nothing; the bay, for the tide itself, which the city does not bill. The Liffey is not to be used as a disposal route in either direction, and the wharf ledgers are the record of the century it was, kept in full, unargued, as the weather is kept.

Contaminated packaging: Corks to the cork tree at the door of the nearest public house, where they are a building material and the last word on the subject.

Waste treatment methods: Incineration under permit only; the peat in the matrix predates the permit system and is not to be treated as if it did not.

14 Transport information

UN number: UN 3267 (city, solid, n.o.s.), assigned provisionally. UN proper shipping name: Dublin, state matrix, solid, n.o.s. Transport hazard class(es): 9 (miscellaneous). Packing group: III. Environmental hazards: Mark as a marine pollutant where the wharf stream enters the bay.

The wall walk, by foot: The city's own transport of record is the wall walk of Form DB-1215, seven legs run on the line of the walled city of 1215 — the two Liffey legs under the three principal bridges, the bay leg at the mouth, and the south leg at the foot of the Dublin Mountains, where the Wicklow range keeps the matrix honest. The legs are measured in metres, and the seven together sum, in full, to 1,263,219, the middle figure of the 2022 record — the city including its suburbs, entered in the census ledger with no committee vote, as the census goes. The outer figure, 1,501,500, is the county, and the inner, 592,713, is the council area; the wall walk takes the middle and the wall walk is the city's way of standing inside the number without spending it. The surveyor's margin note on the form, which the Desk keeps word for word, reads: the legs are fixed at the middle figure, and the pace is the walking pace of the party, which is the pace at which the metres take the day the length of a day. Moreover, they were to be walked at walking speed if one keeps to the middle figure in the record without a vote, and the tour changes with it. The last clause is not decoration: when a new census is printed, the seven legs are re-measured to the new middle figure, the form is re-issued, and the old walk is filed, complete, under the year it kept, which is how the city has walked its own number for eight centuries without once walking it twice the same.

Companion measure: The method is the one the trunkline walk keeps in Michigan — one stage at a time, the distance measured honestly, the weather noted and never argued over — 362 miles of U.S. Route 23 from Lambertville to Mackinaw City in six stages: The Trunkline Walk — U.S. Route 23 in Michigan. The mile and the metre are cross-filed in the Desk's drawer, and the two long lines have been walked this year in the same weather column.

Transport in bulk: Not applicable. The matrix is already in bulk; it is the whole of the question.

15 Regulatory information

Regulations specific to the substance/state: The Local Government Acts as amended for Dublin; the Navigation of the Liffey; the street grid of 1215, which is law by standing and not by statute, and which the surveyor's office will not re-measure (see Section 7). The pint is 0.568 litre by long custom, and the room does not discuss it, which is the custom working.

Not classified as a hazardous material when transported in its intact state as a capital; the classification is reviewed, once, at each census, and the walk of Section 14 is the review.

Chemical safety assessment: Not required; the wharf ledgers serve in its place, and they are complete.
