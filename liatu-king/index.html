8 July 2025 · Los Angeles, California

Ms. King,

I write on this letterhead, in the office that ran your draft night, because the office made the mistake and the office is writing the apology. When the league read your name at the United Center in Chicago on the night of 24 June 2025, fifteenth of the third round, the program in every seat in the building carried a card that was not quite you: it gave your name as LaTara King, listed you as a point of origin of Pittsburgh alone, and dated your birth to the year 2000, two years older than the February 10, 2002 that your records carry. A draft card that gets a name wrong is not a minor thing; the card is the mark, and a mark is what a pick stands on.

What the card owed you and did not give was the second line: after Pittsburgh, the Panthers, you came to Notre Dame, and the Fighting Irish are half of the résumé the round saw when it took you. The teleprompter at the table read the card, the card read the error, and the error went around the building faster than the pick went around the board. I have taken the card's error on as the office's own, because the office keyed the card, proofed the card, and let the card stand.

The league's draft log kept one marginal line on the third round of that night, written by the table clerk in the column where remarks go, and I quote it here in full because its last clause is the whole apology in one sentence: “Turn the year the union could not agree was a speed that does the name appears on no other mark.” The clerk has said the sentence records the round's tempo and nothing more, and I will not argue the sentence's grammar with the clerk. I will only take the ending: the name appears on no other mark. It was a name meant to stand on its own card, and it stood on another one, mis-keyed and two years too old, for four minutes and the length of an anthem.

Since that night, and since your trade to the Lynx on 4 July, the office has done what it can in the way an office can. The corrected card — Liatu, born 10 February 2002, Pittsburgh, then Notre Dame, guard, the round and the number as read — has been reprinted and sent to your agent's office, to the front office at Target Center, and to the program archive, which will print the correction facing the original, the way a corrected page sits against the one it replaces. A replacement card, pressed and numbered to the round, is in the parcel with this letter. We are not asking that the night be re-read; a pick is a pick and the number stands. We are asking that the mark carry the name, which it now does.

I am sorry. For the card, for the year, for the missing second line of a college career, and for making a player taken in the third round spend the first week of her professional life correcting a typo she had no hand in making. The pick belongs to you. The name is yours, in the order it was always in, on the card that is now the only card we will print.

Sincerely,

Dana Whitfield

Director of Communications

Los Angeles Sparks
