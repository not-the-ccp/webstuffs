Small Ads — Tumbling Beetles & Kindred Notices

Mordellistena dalmatica, a tumbling flower beetle of the family Mordellidae, genus Mordellistena, recorded 1956. Home range: Austria, Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, Hungary, Italy, Slovakia, and most of the states of the former Yugoslavia. Copy at 14 words a line.

FOR SALE — VIAL OF THREE

Three Mordellistena dalmatica, pinned in spirit, collected by my late father, druggist, at Velden 1956–1959, the year the species was written up and named, from the linden hedge behind the apothecary at Am Hof 9, Klagenfurt. Blue-black, 4 mm, they flip like coins when the vial tip is knocked. Vial, label and the original prescription-card tag (“1 drop, at night”) go together, 420 pf., collection post. My father kept one drawer of the tumblers separate from the rest of the press, and I keep his drawer now. Enquiries to Anton Reber, c/o the Velden pharmacy, ask for the drawer, not the beetles. If you have bought a vial from us before you know the rule: no two tumblers turn the same way, and neither does the person who releases them.

LOST — ONE BEETLE, TUMBLING

Lost from a jam jar, last seen 3 June at the Brünn market hall, one Mordellistena dalmatica, female, 4.2 mm, metathorax dented in a shape her keeper, Mrs. Hana Šimková, describes only as “a little crooked, like the old gate.” She has set up the jar by the window again, lid loose, a drop of honey water inside, on the understanding that the beetle came from a hedge and will go to a hedge. Reward: a jar of the same honey water, refilled as often as needed. Please do not send photographs of other beetles; we have been sent eleven, and eleven are other beetles.

FOUND — TIN BOX, SEVEN STATES

Found on a bench, Kufstein side of the border, one tin biscuit box, lid stamped with the seven countries and a diagram of a beetle tumbling, contents: nine pressed Mordellistena, two pins, a folded field sheet listing Austria, Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, Hungary, Italy, Slovakia and “the rest of the former Yugoslavia, most of it,” which is honest, and a note in pencil. The note is illegible except for one line, which the bench clerk has preserved word for word, in her own hand, in the lost-and-found ledger: “What the sentence on the Milan–New York line of nonsense about printed books.” The clerk’s comment, added in smaller type: “Understood as follows: the line is Milan–New York, the subject is nonsense, and the nonsense is about printed books. Filed as is.” The box, whatever it means, is held under No. 114; claim with a story, not a key.

WANTED — THE 1956 PAPER, ONE COPY

The Bulletin library wants the original 1956 description of Mordellistena dalmatica, one complete copy, any binding, for the Mordellidae shelf, where the genus Mordellistena currently stands in for its missing ancestor, like a neighbour holding the door. We pay a fair price and we shelve with care. If your copy is the one with the margin note “Dalmatia or not at all,” we pay more, and we will not tell where it came from, because the margin note is a small thing and small things travel. Send by any post; the shelf does not check the stamp.

EXCHANGE — HEDGES FOR PINS

Collector, Zagreb side, will exchange two fresh Mordellistena dalmatica, field-fresh from the rose hedge at the Ilovo station, for two brass pins, 25 mm, or one honest photograph of a tumbler mid-flip, of which we are, it must be said, still short. The same collector keeps a ledger of every beetle he has handed to a window and watched go, 34 entries and counting, and will show it to anyone who brings a tumbler. The tumblers, for the uninitiated, land on their feet or they do not, and the ledger records both outcomes without commentary, which is the whole of its philosophy. Exchange column, box 11, Ilovo post office.

NOTICE — ON THE GENUS, IN FOUR LINES

Members are reminded that Mordellistena is a genus, Mordellidae is the family, and the species before us, Mordellistena dalmatica, was written up in 1956 and has since been collected from Austria, Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, Hungary, Italy, Slovakia and most of the former Yugoslavia, a range that follows the old hedges and not the new borders. The beetle does not know the difference and neither, on a good day, does this column. Copy closes at five; the beetles do not.

From the same week’s papers

The Velden auction house has listed a script drawer from a Munich household — the archive of a Bavarian actress and theatre director — and among the lot paperwork, pressed in a folder marked Schmörn & beetles, a tumbler of the very species now advertised: Lot 314, the auction catalogue, page 44, where the drawer is described and the beetle is described as “one, unclaimed.”

The same house has, we are pleased to report, finally listed the order itself: in Lot 400,000, Catalogue No. 40, the spring sitting at Velden, the Coleoptera goes in by the elytra, estimate “more than the building,” and our drawer 11 — the tumblers’ drawer, vial included — is held in reserve inside the tin box for the buyer. The house’s standing note is set word for word at the foot of the lot, and it is the sentence this column has needed for thirty years.

For collectors who prefer their columns to a mathematician’s: the Bergen Posten classifieds, Saturday edition, column 11, ran a lost shapefile and a case of Margaux the week before, and the lost shapefile, we are told, was of the beetle’s own range.

And a word on names, from the lexicographers who keep the card-file on the lake: a species name and its binomial stand in direct descent from the one who described them, inherited and never borrowed, which is to say the 1956 paper is the parent of Mordellistena dalmatica the way a father is a father, and this is why we are looking for it. Chapter VII, Of the Common Language Infrastructure, treats the standing order that keeps such names running on one shelf without being rewritten.

And where the tin box’s seven countries have been given a case of their own: the Rooms have hung Placard No. 0207, the Republic of Bulgaria, in Case 12 of the Gallery of Borders, where the tumbler is described as at home in the Kazanlak rose fields, the linden hedges of Sofia, the Varna harbour gate and a single hedge on the Burgas pier, the four corners of the box’s diagram, and the column’s tin box is said to be travelling there next month for the frame.

And one keeper writes from farther east than the tin box’s seven countries, from Bandar Seri Begawan, where a tumbler alighted in the istana’s hedge on 15 July and the household diary of the palace recorded it the same morning as its sultan’s eightieth birthday; the entry, with its white cottage and blue door at the foot of the Belait wharf, is filed in our papers as the wardrobe-wing diary of 15 July 2026, and if the beetle was one of ours it is now beyond the reach of the exchange column, which notes this without further comment.

And a crossing that is less small than any of ours: a Kumamoto junior-high class filed a report on Eta Funayama Kofun, the 1951 historic-site designation, and the Baekje-style bronzes that moved from a burial mound to a museum case in Tokyo — the same long transfer we practise on a 4 mm scale — set down in Eta Funayama Kofun, School Report, Class 2-C, where the class walked the route south of the second pass.
