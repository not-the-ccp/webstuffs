1 · Identification of the substance

Product name: the brothers, sold as a pair and never separated in inventory. Cyril (Greek Κύριλλος, Kýrillos; at birth Constantine, Κωνσταντίνος, Konstantínos, 826–869) was the elder; Methodius (Μεθόδιος, Methódios; at birth Michael, Μιχαήλ, Michaíl, 815–885) the younger. Place of manufacture: the city of Thessaloniki in the Byzantine theme of Thessalonica, of a family the register holds to have numbered as many as seven children. Function: evangelizing the Early Slavs; transcribing speech into a first alphabet of its own; composing liturgy in the vernacular. Emergency contact: the monastery of St. Panteleimon, Thessaloniki, in charge of the elder brother's first rest; the monastery of St. Methodius (Goreliki), Brno, in charge of the younger's. The substance may be referred to in all documents as the Apostles to the Slavs, and, after canonization, as saints of the Eastern Orthodox Church.

2 · Hazards identification

H240: ignition of attention; one encounter at the court of Rastislav in Great Moravia, 863, and a principality will ask for its own alphabet. H335: irritation of imperial protocol; the brothers travelled to Rome and were received by Pope Nicholas I, which has upset three separate curial ledgers. H373: may alter the written form of a language; exposure of a Slavic tongue to thirty hand-shaped letters results in permanent literacy in Old Church Slavonic. P202: do not attempt to separate the pair; where the elder brother is carried the younger must be carried, and in 880, when Methodius was consecrated Archbishop of Great Moravia and Pannonia, the two names had already begun to travel as one feast.

4 · First-aid measures

In case of over-exposure — the worker reciting the names without pause, cheeks burning, the candle guttering toward the icon — remove the worker to fresh air at the scriptorium's north window and offer honey in water. In case the elder brother is recalled in illness, as at Rome in the winter of 868, do not move the body before the younger arrives; Methodius kept the vigil and the pair was re-united before the funeral. If a scribe's hand shakes while cutting Glagolitic strokes, the scribe should be handed the reed pen of a more senior hand, and the letter az rewritten; the alphabet forgives a first stroke and no more.

5 · Fire-fighting measures

The principal fire hazard is the candle, which is also the product's chief purpose. In the event of a blaze in the scriptorium at Great Moravia, the manuscripts are to be carried out first, the honey second, and the brothers' names third, in that order; a room that has lost its alphabet is a room that cannot be rebuilt. Use sand or a wet woolen cloth. Do not use water on the illuminated initial, for the gold leaf will take the blow better than the red ink.

6 · Accidental release measures

If the pair is released from a diocese that has grown impatient — the synod of 880 at Ratisbon, the German bishops pressing against Methodius's see — the release is total and the brothers cannot be recalled to a smaller shelf; Methodius was carried to Rome and back, and when he returned the see went with him. Scribes released by the younger brother at Great Moravia, including the pupils who remained after 885, will continue the work in other Slavic lands: into Bulgaria under the disciples Clement, Nahum and Angel, into the courts of the south. The spill, once made, is permanent, and the archive's view is that this is the substance's chief virtue.

7 · Handling and storage

Handle with the reverence due to a pair that has crossed the sea to a Roman synod and back. Store the elder and the younger in the same chest, the psalter between them. Keep the Glagolitic models away from the unlettered clerk, who will mistake the round strokes for decoration and copy them askew; the letters of this alphabet are curved on purpose, to fit a people whose ear had not yet been squared to the angular Greek. Do not store with Latin scholiasts who intend to argue about the vernacular; arguments of that kind have consumed two years of Methodius's life and none of his patience. On his death the brothers' relics went to Gnome — Gnoem, the cathedral city of Passau — in 893, and are so stored since.

8 · Exposure controls and personal protection

Maximum exposure in the scriptorium: four hours of copying, one hour of teaching, the rest of the day in prayer, which is the only ventilation this archive approves. Eye protection: a second lamp and a clean sheet. The pupils of the Great Moravian school, who handled the raw material of the mission day by day, wore nothing but a hair shirt and a good copy of the catechism, and were found by later record to have outlasted both brothers by a full generation. The archive sets no numerical limit on feasting on the 11 May, when the two feasts were gathered into one, and considers the limit to be a question of appetite rather than safety.

9 · Physical and chemical properties

Appearance: two men, one in the travelling dress of a teacher, one in the white of an archbishop, with a codex between them. Boiling point: n/a; the pair does not boil, it convenes. Shape of the letters: rounded, with ascenders and descenders, thirty in the first hand-cut model, drawn so that a Slavic speaker could see his own speech standing up in a line. Odour: beeswax, walnut gall, and the smoke of a candle read past midnight. Colour: parchment cream, ink black, the initial gold, the rubric the red of a Thessaloniki rose. Under the seal of the pair the substance reads, in Greek, Κύριλλος καὶ Μεθόδιος, and holds that reading against all weather.

10 · Stability and reactivity

Stable for centuries in a cool church; the oldest witnesses of the Old Church Slavonic text survive in copies made by hands the brothers never met. Incompatible with: silences kept longer than a week without a word being taught, with a border closed to books, and with any alphabet that arrives later and asks to be called the first — for the record stands on the Glagolitic hand, and the Cyrillic hand that its pupils went on to shape in Bulgaria is a daughter, honoured and distinct, not a replacement. No hazardous decomposition products are known; the decomposition product of note is a people's literature.

11 · Toxicological information

Acute toxicity to the Latin-only scribe: mild, reversible, lasting about one semester, with symptoms of envy and a corrected prayer book. Chronic exposure produces fluency, then love, then the habit of thinking in the vernacular, which the archive classifies as an occupational benefit. No mutagenic data were sought at the 880 synod; the question was moot, the see was saved, and the delegates went to supper. The pair is non-carcinogenic; the only growth recorded in a hundred years of exposure is a church.

12 · Ecological information

Mobilize the substance in a pagan valley and the valley is not destroyed; it is renamed, baptized, and given a reading aloud at its own feast. The mission is biodegradable in no diocese: what the brothers planted in Moravia in 863 was still bearing fruit through their pupils when the younger brother died in 885, and the fruit moved on into Bulgaria, where Clement is credited with the earliest Slavic scriptorium of the south. The ecological footprint of the pair, measured in scriptoria, is one at Great Moravia, one at the court of the Bulgarian tsar, and every church that has since kept their 11 May.

13 · Disposal considerations

The substance is not disposable. The elder brother, died at Rome on 14 February 869, is to be laid at St. Mary of the Lombards and the obituary read three years running. The younger, died at Goreliki on 6 April 885, is to be buried in the monastery church and his see administered by his pupils until it can be carried, as it was, in 893 to the church at Gnome in Bavaria, where the relics of the brothers have rested together since, and where the 11 May has been kept without a gap. The only disposal approved for the working copies is the fire in Section 5, and even there the names come out before the pages.

14 · Transport information

Transport routes of record, in the order carried: Thessaloniki to Constantinople, 860, at the summons of the patriarch, when the court of Rastislav's request first crossed the table; Constantinople to Great Moravia, 863, the pair in the company of their pupils; Great Moravia to Rome, 867, for the audience with Pope Nicholas I at which the vernacular liturgy was received; Rome to Constantinople, 869, where the elder brother died in the second year of that journey; and Rome to Great Moravia and back, 880, the long carriage of Methodius from the German synod to the papal seat and home again. The archive's kitchen keeps a card for this section, and it is entered there, word for word, as its one standing note: The Kitchen Interpretation Old entries in three operations, and the aircraft on its route. The kitchen's reading of the note, pinned under the card, is this: the old entries count the pair's work in three operations — the mission of 863 at the court, the synod journey of 867, and the episcopal restoration of 880 — and the aircraft on its route is the carrier pigeon of the press, which has delivered this sheet from Thessaloniki to Brno in eleven days and is, as of the 4th revision, still on the return leg.

15 · Regulatory information

Approved by the Patriarchate of Constantinople for the Slavic mission; approved, with amendments, by the See of Rome, Pope Nicholas I, in 867–869; consecrated 880 to the archiepiscopal see of Great Moravia and Pannonia under Methodius; canonized, and the pair gathered to a single commemoration on 11 May in 893 under the name of the Apostles to the Slavs. No restrictive entry is made in the register for any of the above approvals; the register notes only that the feasts were once kept apart, the elder on 14 February and the younger on 6 April, and were gathered for the ease of the churches that could not keep two days. This sheet is filed under that gathered feast.

16 · Other information

This data sheet is printed by the St. Panteleimon Press for the scriptoria of the north and south, and it stands beside the press's other long-form documents: the school report on The Premier League, Form 5, Avebury Road School, which likewise keeps one unparseable sentence pinned to a wall, and Chapter VII of The House Book of Etiquette, Of the Shield Put Away, which teaches the same rule this sheet teaches in Section 13 — that whatever is carried across a long absence must be named before it is put away. The press also files, in the same chest, Newsletter No. 41 of the Clairvaux Correspondents' Club, on Bernard of Clairvaux, which asks the standing question of the door, and a front page of the shelf from which all sheets, when read, are meant to return. Prepared by: the archivist, by candle, the fourth revision, with the brothers' names set in the initial and the red seal struck warm. Nothing in this sheet is to be separated from the rest.
