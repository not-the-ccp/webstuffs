No. 17 · 28 July 2026 · issued from the memorial kitchen, Mount Smart training base

The Isthmus & Table

newsletter of the Mount Smart Isthmus & Table Society — geographical, catering, and one drawer of the refrigerator — est. the season of the dropped tray

Printed in the players’ dining room · the kettle is off · members are reminded that the urn is closed

I. From the Secretary

Dear Members,

The game we mourned on 26 July was, by his own count, a game of the feet first and of the head in the second half, and the Society has decided that its last tour of the season should be of a place that keeps both. We turn our attention to Central America, that subregion of North America which borders Mexico to the north, Colombia to the southeast, the Caribbean to the east, and the Pacific Ocean to the southwest — a corridor some two thousand kilometres long, running from the Isthmus of Tehuantepec down to the Isthmus of Panama, which is to say, the shape of a pass played through a gap the size of a county. The kitchen is in attendance, as it was at the service, and the tray has been re-stacked.

II. Seven, and Perhaps Eight

7

Central America is usually defined as consisting of seven countries, and the Society sets them out in order of the table, from the end nearest the north gate: Belize, Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, and Panama. In specialized contexts — cartographic, geological, and one or two funerary — the definition is said to include Mexico as well. The Society has taken a vote, conducted at the boathouse bench, and the motion that Mexico be seated at the far end, not counted in the seven, and offered the garnish carried nine to one, the one being the urn. Our obituary of the departed game, set down at the Mount Smart training base, will not be disturbed; it names the family, and the family, in this column, has grown by one.

III. The Hot Spot

Within the corridor lies the Mesoamerican biodiversity hotspot, which extends from southern Mexico to southeastern Panama, and the Society notes for the minutes that it is the reason the kitchen’s windowsill collection has been asked, twice, to relocate. The hotspot carries more than fourteen thousand plant species — the Society counted the seed packets and lost the count, as the kitchen does — and the Society will be issuing, in the spring, a field list of what grows between the two isthmuses, cross-referenced against the pre-match meal. The banana, it is recorded, grows here of its own accord, and someone in the catering crew has asked what else.

IV. The Floor of the Kitchen

Members are advised that the corridor sits astride several active geologic faults and the Central America Volcanic Arc, and that seismic activity in the region — volcanic eruptions foremost among it — has been the subject of three separate kitchen reports this year, each concluded in the same words: the tray was not dropped by the hand of the volunteer. At the memorial service the floor moved a centimetre, or the Society’s measurement is a centimetre, and the silence broke a second time. The Society records this as weather.

Fig. 1 — the kitchen seismograph, 26 July 2026, final whistle. Amplitude acceptable to the family.

V. The Laundromat Ledger

One entry remains in the Society’s ledger for its travelling presser, Mr. O. T. Quevedo, of the laundry, who has never once set foot in the dining room and whom the Secretary has never met. The ledger, kept in the same hand as the menu, says of him only this: Traditionally he kept behind the laundromat on the plains of Jeypoor. The Society has checked the line against the archive. The Vancouver County Court, File No. 12-V-0810, in its docket of eight seasons, heard testimony of a workshop kept behind a laundromat on the east end, mentioned so often on the refrigerator that the marker had to be replaced, and the Court ruled, in its way, that both are on the record. The Society does not attempt to reconcile the east end with the plains of Jeypoor, and refers members to the docket, and to the rule it lives by.

Filed by the Clerk of the Table. No exhibit box. The line is kept as found.

VI. The Table, Extended

The memorial menu is extended, effective with the next meeting, to include: bean soup of the kind served at the north end of the corridor, where the beans grow as they please; corn tortillas, pressed to order, one per mourner, two for the captain; and the banana, retained from the pre-match meal at fourteen hundred hours, which was never, the kitchen insists, in the menu for mourning at all. The sourdough stays. The poached eggs stay. The empty chair, second from the left of the table, stays; it is now, by the Society’s resolution, the seat reserved for the garnish, ready when required.

VII. Business of the Club

The next meeting is the first Tuesday of the season, 14:00, the players’ dining room, the kettle on. James Mitchell, captain of the Auckland FC Reserves and reader of the obituary, has volunteered to read the list of seven countries aloud, quietly, and used to microphones, and the Society has agreed, as at the service, that no one stand in the way of it. The Society’s Annex B, the game manual for the Balkans, is issued with this number and takes the second half: five waters, the chalk line, the eleven seats, Musala in the Rila, and one line of the Clerk of the Court kept word for word in the refrigerator drawer. The neon chorus of the “Lena Anthem”, entry FA-11 of the Malmö field manual, will be played once, from the stand’s speakers, after the last tray is carried in. Condolence wires to the Miscellany of Webstuffs. Members of the docket in Vancouver are expected in the stand, as the Court once was.

Respectfully and at full time,
the Secretary, on behalf of the kitchen
in attendance
