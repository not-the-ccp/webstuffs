THE HOUSEHOLD OF THE NAME ERNI 14 RUE DES TANNEURS · ONE DOOR · THREE BELLS, THE PAPER MERCHANT'S, BORROWED

An Apology
to the Name Ernest

For the file · A Modern Guide to Names & Address, Fortieth Edition, Whitcombe & Daughters, errata

❦

To the Name ERNEST,

in its full form, the form it keeps in the letter to the bank,

c/o the font, St. Vitus’ Church, Wuppertal,

and everywhere else it is written.

The evening before the fortieth edition's errata, 1931

Ernest,

I write in the name of the household I carry, which is to say I write in a name two letters shorter than yours, and I have already made the apology you were owed. The first shortening was done at the font in Wuppertal by a grandfather in the Rhineland who loved the Germanic word from which we both descend — ernst, the serious one — better than he could say it, so he said less of it. By tea the child answered to the shorter, and you were kept for the letter to the bank. That first cut we now say was forgivable. This letter is for everything after: one hundred and fifty-four years of the shorter answering the bell of the longer, and of the whole family of us, at dinners in the province, being called Ernie, Ernie dear, and once at a reception in Danzig “Erns” — the conversation cooled by exactly two degrees, which we have measured, because the household measures, and because you deserve the number.

The registers we have consulted — the baptismal book of St. Vitus’ in Wuppertal, the merchant list of the Danzig guild, and one stubborn parish roll in Piacenza — yield, in the decade around 1888, no fewer than eleven children christened Erni and three families bearing the name as a surname, and the list is not closed; the coppersmiths at Erni & Son in Danzig add names at their leisure, and the paper merchant of Piacenza keeps his own forty-third variety of paper for them, the forty-fourth shelf standing empty on purpose, for the name not yet invented. Of the eleven we claim one. The other ten — Would not, could not have been ours, and we would not say which. We would not say which not because the register is lost but because it was water-damaged in the Piacenza flood of 1899, the margin line is illegible, and because a household of our manners would be shortening a name a third time by speaking it aloud, in the wrong order, at the wrong desk. The parish priest, when asked, said only that the ink was honest and the water had been careless. We agree with the priest entirely.

For the dinner table we owe an apology as well. You know the story: the volume presented at our table on the Rue des Tanneurs, the dedication which was to be written by the giver, read by no one, and tucked where the household would see it first. We tucked it into the back cover, and there it stayed, and the musician who enters after the third line of the seating is read found, pasted there, the whole system of the house begun again from the beginning. One paste, one corner, one start. The rule was right; the corner was ours, and the corner, that evening, did not hold. The Ernis of Piacenza change the subject to the fish, and do it well; we on the Rue des Tanneurs had already eaten the fish, and had none, and had to invent one — a sea bream — and it showed.

We must also apologise where the name has touched the machinery of the squadron. In September 2023, a case of foundry type from the Barcelona house Talls Llorenç i Fill, consigned to the Impremta de la Diada for the holiday's broadsheet, rode the wrong column of a load plan into Annex C at Fort Worth and sat there, unopened, for three days. In its second drawer, beside the 72-point pound commissioned to stand at the head of the broadsheet and nowhere else in the year, there was a small tin of brass lettering that was ours: the nameplate for the seventh chair, the chair of the name itself, which the household always sets at a table of six, and no guest is to ask about, on the manners. The returning clerk, Senyora Mercè Alabau, flew to Jacksonville and flew it home; the case touched down at El Prat at 19:42 on the 11th itself, four minutes before her office closed, and the brass lettering came back in her bag, the record says, still warm from the tarmac. The supply officer, Master Supply Specialist Dana Reyes, wrote her own apology to the clerk — the Nomads' letter, filed in the same miscellany as this chapter, which the household has read in full and found to be in the corrected order — and the squadron read it at the 26th's drill. Our apology stands at that same drill, since the name was in the case. The symbol did not speak badly of the clerk; the tin did not speak badly of us, which is the best a thing like that can say for anyone who carries it.

What we have kept, and are sorry to have kept too well, is the manners: the flat pronunciation of a surname, no flourish, at the pier in Szczecin, where the ledger is kept; the seventh chair; the forty-fourth empty shelf; and the rule that a name signs as it is written, answers as it is spoken, and is filed once, correctly, at E, beside Ernestine and Enfield. We have kept these on your account, in the hope that the shorter name would one day be measured by the manners it kept for the longer one, and not by the two letters it was without. The fortieth edition of the guide has its corrections; this letter is the one that was missing.

Annex A — The Never-Shorten-Twice Checker

So that the apology is not only in words, the household has set a bench at the door of the page. Enter the name as it stands, and the shortening you were about to say; the bench will tell you whether the door stands open — permitted, with the host's nod, and only once — or whether you hold the wrong drawer, the locksmith's box, or a surname at all, which is not to be shortened, not pluralised, and not pronounced with a flourish. The bench knows the register: Ernest and Ernst in their full forms, Erni in its two natures, and the surnames the file keeps dear. It does not know the rest of the world; for those, the old advice stands, with or without the bench: measure twice, cut once, and when in doubt, ask the host.

The bench has no answer yet. It has, however, already measured one error — the reception in Danzig — and found the conversation two degrees colder and, as of the fish, recovered. It would prefer not to add another.

If the bench does not answer on this device, the rule stands without it: never shorten Erni to anything shorter, do not lengthen it, and where Erni is the surname, ask the name, pronounce it flatly, thank them, and sit down.

We ask nothing. Not to be lengthened — the key is carried in the pocket, not in the locksmith's box. Not to be shortened again — you will not. Only to be left, as we leave you, in the letter to the bank, in its full form, where the name has always been safe. The chapter that set these manners down, Chapter VII of the Guide, of the Name Erni, is filed in the same house as this letter, and the whole miscellany sits on the street; the reader is invited to take it down, open it at the errata, and leave the seventh chair exactly where it is.

Yours in the corrected order,

The Household of the Name Erni

14 Rue des Tanneurs

for the full name, and, through it, the shortened one

Postscript. For those who arrive by way of the squadron: the sister apology is on file on the letterhead, in the apology to the returning clerk. For those who arrive by way of the directory: the chapter is Chapter VII, of the Name Erni, where a directory of the sort once given to a Chaplin would, for Erni, be merely a directory, and the street itself is the Webstuffs miscellany. The brass lettering is in the seventh chair. The fish was a sea bream, and we are sorry — and we have since found it cooked, at last, in the coppersmiths’ own room on the Merchant Street, where a reviewer has filed the account of Erni & Son with the ledger line kept word for word, eleven pierogi for the eleven of the register, and the seventh chair at the head of the table of six. The fish was a sea bream, and we are sorry, and the corner held.
