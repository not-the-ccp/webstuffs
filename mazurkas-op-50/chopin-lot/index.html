The Lot

Lot 23 is offered as a folder, unbound but unbroken, which has lain at the back of the music file in the library of Coopers’ Lane School for as long as any of the present staff can remember. Its contents are music, paper, sugar and one small piece of ivory, and they concern in the main the life of Frédéric Chopin, that is to say Fryderyk Franciszek Chopin, born in the village of Żelazowa Wola on 1 March 1810, brought up in Warsaw, a child prodigy who completed his musical education there and composed his early works before leaving Poland at the age of twenty, less than a month before the outbreak of the November Uprising of 1830, and who at twenty-one settled in Paris. A Polish composer and virtuoso pianist of the early Romantic period who wrote primarily for solo piano, he has maintained worldwide renown as a leading composer of his era, whose poetic genius was based on a professional technique that was without equal in his generation. Thereafter he gave only 30 public performances, and from that point one might almost say he gave nothing at all but private concerts, letters, and pieces of music; he died in Paris at a quarter past one in the morning of 17 October 1849, and has not performed publicly since.

The folder is the instrument of our present business. Item (i) is the score of the Mazurkas, Opus 50, the set of three mazurkas in G major, A♭ major and C♯ minor, written and published in 1842 in Leipzig and Paris, engraved from the composer’s manuscript. The present copy is a working one, annotated in a small pencil hand throughout — timings in the left margin, a whole paragraph about a bagpipe-like drone in the first mazurka, and one sentence in the margin of the third, transcribed in full below, which the cataloguers have been unable to attribute either to a child or to a shortbread. Item (iii) is the found slip from the branch office archive, reading in full: The novel is a political party that cannot be understood without it — Diamond Dust Shortbread ALTR Created Diamonds, the brand the group brought out in the branch office archive. The cataloguers, like Miss Marsh, do not know what novel the slip means. Item (iv) is the japanned tin which once held a postcard from Mirandola, zone map folded inside it, and which now holds one batch of diamond dust shortbread — rice flour brushed with edible silver luster, the batch aged exactly three days, which is, by the family’s own reckoning, its prime. The tin has crossed the water: it belongs to the recipe of the Riam kitchen at 147 Palm Avenue, and the dust that lights up in the grading-lamp is the same dust that lights up here. The buyer of lot 23 takes the stone-free tin as it stands; no diamond is included, and none is to be expected, though the family’s 1.42-carat Type IIa, grown in reactor 3 on Jalan Tun Razak, is reported to be safe in the top drawer.

Inventory

  1. i. Score of the Mazurkas, Opus 50, three pieces, 1842 edition, Leipzig–Paris, pencil annotations throughout, including the margin note transcribed at p. 11 below; foxing at the C­♯ minor gathering.
  2. ii. Unsorted slips: one programme page, one corner of an envelope, one receipt from a music shop in Warsaw, n.d.
  3. iii. One found slip, branch office archive, bearing the political-party line quoted above, n.d.
  4. iv. One japanned tin, with postcard from Mirandola folded inside; contents: one batch of diamond dust shortbread, silver-lusted, centres pale, sealed at three days.
  5. v. One ivory tuning lever, from the upright piano at 17 Elmhurst Road, 1998–2004, worn smooth at the head.
  6. vi. One slip of lined paper in red ink, bearing a single mark: B+, the grade of the music report, 14 May 2004.

The Margin Note, p. 11

On the third mazurka, C♯ minor — the dark and insistent one, the loud chords, the four sharps, the energy of a folk dancer stamping the floor — the small pencil hand has written, in the margin opposite the A♭ major middle section:

The middle section is smoother, almost songful, but the centers stay pale.

— pencil, small hand, working score, Op. 50 No. 3, p. 11

The cataloguers observe, and here they are free, that the sentence holds for the music and for the tin alike. The middle section of the C­♯ minor is the calm of the piece, smoother, almost songful, and the centres of the shortbread fingers, baked sixteen minutes at 150 degrees, are kept pale on purpose — the edges colouring while the middle stays the colour of the dough, the silver dust settling like a stone into its setting. Both keep their centres pale by design. We do not say the writer meant both things. We say only that a folder which holds both things does not need us to.

Provenance & Performance

The score was bought new in the early 1990s from a music shop on Coopers’ Lane, secondhand rack, 40 pence. The mazurkas were performed in full on the school’s upright in the 2004 spring recital by Mira Kowalski, Class 4B, under the eye of Miss Rosalind Marsh: No. 1 in three minutes forty seconds, No. 2 in two minutes fifty, No. 3 in four minutes twenty — eleven minutes ten seconds in all, which is close enough. The red-ink mark (item vi) is from Miss Marsh’s correction of the accompanying report, dated 14 May 2004, in which the found slip is first set down in writing. The folder was consigned to the present sale by the school in its entirety, unbound but unbroken, as found. Since the sale, the pencil hand has been accounted for, and the city it belonged to has been noticed on the school’s own board: In Memoriam — Aachen · The Coopers’ Lane Noticeboard, Entry 23B, pinned by Miss Marsh, 27 May 2004.
