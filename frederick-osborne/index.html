I · The Product, Described

The Evans Collection is presented in a quarter-bound box of Canberra grey card, the lid lettered in gilt: OSBORNE, F. M. — EVANS, N.S.W. — 1949–1961. Inside, the seat is arranged as its owner kept it, in five terms: 1949, 1951, 1954, 1955, and 1958, the last ballot before the seat changed hands and the collection was closed. Frederick Meares Osborne & Bar, V.R.D. (20 January 1909 – 23 July 1996) served in the House of Representatives for eleven years on this one patch of New South Wales, and the box holds what the years left behind.

Before politics, the file is a file of a lawyer — the & Bar was earned, not inherited, and the early cabinet of the box is lined with briefs, conveyances, and company papers from Sydney practice. After politics, the record of a company director who sat on boards well past the last of his ministerial appointments, until the final entry in his diary on 23 July 1996. The V.R.D. — the volunteer reserve decoration — is reproduced on the spine as it was awarded, and no other ornament is used anywhere on the box.

It is a heavy product, in every sense the word allows, and it is sold as one piece: seat, files, and decoration together, as it was kept. Chifley & Co. does not break it up. We were, after all, founded the year the member first stood for Evans.

III · What Is in the Box

  1. The five election ballots, 1949–1958

    Form 6, the candidate's particulars for the seat of Evans, one for each election won, in the order they were kept. The 1949 ballot is the founding document of the collection: the one that opened the seat to its owner, in the last year of the Chifley years, and the one our own firm was founded to match.

  2. The Customs & Excise file, 1956

    Twelve months of tariff schedules, duty notes, and the correspondence a customs minister generates in a single year. The schedules themselves are the largest single item in the box and account for half its weight. One page, marked in pencil in the margin, is the one our clients ask about most; we lend a facsimile with the box and keep the original.

  3. The Air file, 1956–1960

    Four years of the Minister for Air's business: air routes, the civil aviation register, and the telegrams that never reached a minister's desk until they were urgent. Bound in blue, to match the seat's party.

  4. The Repatriation file, 1960–1961

    The final portfolio, kept to its last entry of 1961. Pensions, appeals, and the small print that carries the weight of the collection more than any other item in it; read it slowly.

  5. The company director's cabinet

    Minutes, share certificates, and annual reports from the boards kept after politics ended, from 1961 forward. The box was not closed at 1961; the record of a director continued, and the collection follows it to the end of the diary, 23 July 1996.

  6. The V.R.D., reproduced

    The volunteer reserve decoration on the spine and the inner lid, as awarded, in relief gilt. The original remains with the family; the reproduction is exact except that it cannot, of course, be pinned.

  7. A glass of water

    Every boxed set leaves the George Street counter with a tumbler of water on top of the lid, because the files are thirsty things in the Australian heat and the first thing a buyer does is read. It is the one item we have added; everything else was there.

IV · How to Keep It

The collection stands best in a reading room, not a showcase: it was made to be opened. The box takes a shelf of forty-two centimetres and asks only for a spare hour a week. If the tariff schedules come out, lay them on a flat surface and do not be in a hurry; a customs schedule read at speed is a customs schedule not read. In the dry months, keep the window a finger's width open and a second tumbler within reach.

Buyers in the country have asked how the files bear the heat. Our standing answer, copied from the correspondence book at George Street, is this: they bear it as well as anyone in the Commonwealth does. The records were kept in New South Wales; they are not afraid of it.

VI · The Record in Short

Frederick Meares Osborne & Bar, V.R.D., was born on 20 January 1909 and died on 23 July 1996. He was an Australian lawyer, politician and company director. He was a member of the House of Representatives from 1949 to 1961, representing the New South Wales seat of Evans for the Liberal Party. In the Menzies government he held ministerial office as Minister for Customs and Excise in 1956, Minister for Air from 1956 to 1960, and Minister for Repatriation from 1960 to 1961. That is the whole record, and it is the whole product; the box adds weight, gilt, and a glass of water to it, and nothing else at all.
