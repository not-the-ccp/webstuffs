GOYGOL DISTRICT COURT
AGRICULTURAL DIVISION

Goygol District Court  ·  Agricultural Division  ·  Republic of Azerbaijan

Case No. 44-41 of 2025

Elchin VELIYEV, plaintiff & keeper of the Goygol herd,
versus
Nigar HASANOVA, defendant & tenant of the south shed

Excerpt from the verbatim record — the matter of the shared gate and the wintering of the angora flock — heard in open court on Tuesday 4 November 2025, the record opening at 10:05 and closing at 11:40, before the Hon. Leyla Mammadova.

PLAINTIFF
Elchin Veliyev, of the Goygol plain, keeper of 63 Qarabagh angora goats, 41 Akkaraman ewes, and 112 free-range hens. Represented by himself and by one ledger, wet in two places.

DEFENDANT
Nigar Hasanova, of the Sheki highlands, tenant of the south shed since 2019. Represented by herself and by one notice, pinned for four winters.

IN EVIDENCE
Exhibit A — the notice, read into the record word for word. Exhibit B — the milk ledger of 14 March 2025. Exhibit C — one fleece, 2.7 kilograms, weighed 8 November 2025 in the court yard.

THE CLERK. The court is in session; the record is open. The minutes are kept in triplicate, as the division does: one copy in this office, one in Baku, and one, weather-permitting, on the bench in the shed.

THE COURT. I will state the matter. This is a case about animal husbandry in Azerbaijan, which is to say it is a case about the animals raised here for meat, milk, eggs, leather, wool, fur and fibre, for people’s consumption in the republic — though the particular dispute is narrower. It is the south shed of the Veliyev homestead on the Goygol plain, fixed for the record at 40°33′N, 48°32′E. The plaintiff says the defendant let the winter in and ruined the fleece. The defendant says the shed is shared and the gate has always been open. Ms. Hasanova, your plea?

THE DEFENDANT. The shed is shared, Your Honor. The notice says so.

THE COURT. Read the notice into the record.

THE DEFENDANT. Exhibit A. It has been pinned to the shed door since the winter of 2021, and it reads — I will read it word for word — “Open Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00–18:00, and whenever the shed gives frost and snow are seldom remarkable as faithful essays.”

THE COURT. The second half of that notice is — how shall I put it — not in a condition the court can enforce.

THE DEFENDANT. I have never tried to parse it, Your Honor. The hours are the part I keep.

THE PLAINTIFF. The hours are the part I keep, Your Honor. It is the “whenever” that froze my goats.

THE COURT. The plaintiff may lead.

THE PLAINTIFF. I call Rashad Quliyev, lead shearer of the Goygol station.

THE CLERK. The witness is sworn.

THE PLAINTIFF. Mr. Quliyev, the angora fibre is set in winter and cut in spring — is that right?

THE WITNESS. It is. If the shed takes frost in March, the fleece that comes out in April is short and weak. This year’s best ewe gave 2.7 kilograms. The record fleece was 3.9 in 2023, and we sold that one to the Baku mill at 14 manats the kilo.

THE PLAINTIFF. And the gate?

THE WITNESS. The gate was open all of the night of 14 March. It is in the ledger: door, 03:20, frost.

THE DEFENDANT. Cross. Mr. Quliyev — may I, Your Honor — is that entry in the ledger yours, or mine?

THE WITNESS. Yours, ma’m. I have no hand in the ledger.

THE DEFENDANT. I opened it for the hens.

THE COURT. The hens, ma’m?

THE DEFENDANT. The plaintiff’s own 112 hens, Your Honor. They would not roost. I opened the gate at 3:20 for the hens, and the hens brought the frost in with them.

THE COURT. The plaintiff says the hens were not in danger.

THE DEFENDANT. The plaintiff did not know. The hens did not know. I knew.

THE PLAINTIFF. The hens are worth their manats, Your Honor, but the angora lot is 63 head, and the fibre is the whole reason the lot exists. The shed is for the fleece.

THE COURT. Let the court look at the milk ledger. Exhibit B, 14 March 2025: 9.4 litres at the morning, 0.0 at the evening. Explain, ma’m.

THE DEFENDANT. I milked the 9.4 at dawn and left it on the bench, because the gate was already open and I could not get my bucket round. The morning’s milk is in the ledger. I do not ask the court to believe the bucket.

THE PLAINTIFF. The morning’s milk is the bucket, Your Honor. It is in the photograph.

THE COURT. The court will give its view. This is a case about a shed, and it is also a case about what animal husbandry in this district is for. It is for meat, milk, eggs, leather, wool, fur and fibre — that is the list in the district’s own register — and it is not for either party to freeze the other’s list. The plaintiff locked the south gate at 18:40, an hour after the notice closed; the defendant opened it at 03:20, in the middle of the night, for hens on her own information. Both are in default of the notice. And the notice, for what it is worth, grants “whenever,” which is not a time this court will enforce. The plaintiff is ordered to rehang the notice in legible prose; the defendant is fined in the amount of one fleece — 2.7 kilograms, Exhibit C, weighed this morning in the court yard — payable to the Goygol station; and both parties are ordered to share the gate on a padlock, the key split, and the hens kept in their own coop until the frost is off the plain.

THE CLERK. Is that the record?

THE COURT. The court is adjourned. The record is closed at 11:40, and the shed reopens, on its own terms, at —

THE CLERK. On its own terms, Your Honor, Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 18:00.

THE COURT. Very well. The court is in recess.

Docket note, 8 November 2025: the fleece weighed in at 2.71 kilograms; the padlock was hung at 07:45; the notice was rehung in two lines — the hours, and an apology to the grammar. The hens have not left their coop since.

Also on the record

The bench keeps its ledgers in the same spirit as its sister court: in Case No. 1726 of 2024 at the Yerevan Municipal Court, the law of reciprocity was applied to a cold lavash and an unpaid table; here it has been applied, in both its forms, to one cold gate and eleven years of a shared door.

The fix given here, 40°33′N, 48°32′E, is the same fix carried by the Coastal Survey Service in Chapter 4 of Field Manual FM-9, whose §4.1 reminds counsel that latitude and longitude are angles and that a careless minute is nearly two kilometres — which at Goygol is the difference between the shed and the plain.

The Goygol station keeps its diary in the old tea-house manner, one page a day, the same manner as the travel diary of 14 June at Ortaçal&i, where the tall hustings of the square were recorded beside a plate of tea.
