ON AIR OZARK RADIO HOUR · TRANSMISSION 67½

The Expansive

A one-act for one voice, one desk, and a ferry · script excerpt · first transmitted 12 December 2012 · from the cabin kitchen, Mount Magazine area

TAPE COUNTER 0000 · REEL A · MONO

Dramatis Personae

  • The Archivist — the voice in the booth; HR coordinator from Little Rock, recording now from the cabin. (M. Ozark, posts: 92.)
  • Cutter — Charles Ammi Cutter, Ames Free Library, Iowa, 1877. Voice, about thirty-seven.
  • The Deckhand — R. Calloway, Saint John’s, Newfoundlands. Live from the dock, on the line.
  • The Ferry — not a character. A sound.

Scene One — Ames, Iowa. The Free Library. 1877.

[A desk. A pencil that does not stop. Outside the door, a wind that has not yet decided to be a winter.]

CUTTER (reading to himself, then setting the pencil down) A man in New York has just published forty-four pages, and half the catalogue rooms in the country are already calling it a system. I have forty-four pages too. The difference is that his begin with a round hundred, and mine begin with a grudge.

[The pencil starts again.]

CUTTER A round hundred is a promise. This book belongs here and nowhere else. I have been a reader of that promise for fifteen years, and I will not make it. I make a thing that grows. A class is a seed, not a fence. Logic has children, and the children have children, and this schedule is the name of the whole growing, and I call it expansive because it means it. It is classification the way a river is geography: not the bank, but the going.

[He writes. The wind decides, briefly, to be a door.]

CUTTER And I will give my top letters away, if anyone in Washington ever asks, because a top floor is a top floor. General works. Philosophy. History. The poets. Law. It is the same order you would find in any honest room of any honest library, and the letters are only the door handles. What I keep for myself is the decimals, the argument under the floorboards, the part that out-files its own edition and has to be re-shelved without a funeral. That is the whole of the scheme, and it is bigger than any library, and I mean that as a compliment to the library.

Scene Two — The cabin kitchen, Mount Magazine area. Present.

[A radio hour on a Friday. One can of black coffee, opened. A pie-stand pamphlet holding down the 1928 abridgement, the way it has been holding it down all winter. The booth is the kitchen table; the microphone is the salt shaker with the felt removed.]

THE ARCHIVIST (into the microphone) You are listening to the Ozark Radio Hour, transmission 67½, and what I am about to read is the script the readers’ group is rehearsing. It is about a man in Iowa in 1877 whose top letters grew, against his intentions, into the top floors of the letters in Washington — the schedule the ferry now lives at tee-bee five-fifty-five. The play is called The Expansive. The group voted four to one to keep it on the hour, which is how you will know it is good.

[She opens the green book. A card slips in its pocket.]

THE ARCHIVIST There is one passage I cannot make to parse, and it is not mine. It is a dictation. The tape arrived in a pie-stand box; the group will not say from whose; the producer’s transcript, line eleven, reads exactly:

TRANSCRIPT · LINE 11 · DO NOT STRIKE

“Many of its inventors, into a reservoir, from which, as I was, and when all are more frequently served with black coffee.”

— the voice on the tape, in the kitchen, 12 Dec 2012

THE ARCHIVIST That is the line. It parses no better now than it does on the scan, and we are keeping it, which is what this house does with its lines: a salvaged sentence is kept as a placeholder, the way a surveyor keeps a broken baseline. There are two things said in its favour, and both are on the record. The first is that it names the inventors of the letters, and it is true that they are many — a chief and a clerk in Washington in 1897, and an Iowa librarian with a grudge against the round hundred in 1877, and a room full of readers since, all of them filing the same private amendment on pencil cards. The reservoir is the collection. The sentence knows more about this house than the transcript gives it credit for. The second thing in its favour is that it ends on black coffee, and precept five of the etiquette book has ruled that before anything over 300 is classified, one black coffee at the pie stand — and this line, unlike most of its company, obeys the precept voluntarily.

[The telephone on the kitchen table rings. A voice comes through with wind in it and, under the wind, the sea.]

THE DECKHAND Maggie. I’m on the dock. The harbour master has chalked TB 555 under the ice chart, and I told him, politely, that the corner was spoken for in March, and he said nothing, which in our harbour is a re-shelve. I’ve got the schedule up in the tin box with the ice-chart chalk in the lid. I have a question, and you have to hear it, because I have only got it once.

THE ARCHIVIST You have one.

THE DECKHAND If Cutter’s top letters grew into the letters, and the letters gave the ferry TB 555, then the ferry has, since 1877, been carrying a schedule that carries its own address on the cover. Six crossings a day, twelve seats, a place for one car — all of it under a classification it is a part of. I have asked the harbour master if he thinks about it. He says the tide table doesn’t ask the tide.

THE ARCHIVIST (softly) That is the whole of the play. I am sorry. I had meant for the last scene to be longer, and I was going to give it a resolution, and the deckhand has just walked in on the line with the resolution and taken it out in a tin box.

THE DECKHAND The ferry doesn’t mind. The ferry has never once re-shelved itself. Hang on — that’s the 2 o’ crossing. Say the sign-off. I’ll get the chalk down.

[The sound of the sea, briefly, whole. The sound of a pencil, elsewhere, also. The on-air lamp is put out.]

THE ARCHIVIST This is the Ozark Radio Hour, signing off from the cabin kitchen, Mount Magazine area. The 1877 schedule is shelved, in the letters house, at Z 675.3, the Expansive, before the letters, and the pencil card is pinned above it, because a house may bend its drawers so long as it tells the truth about bending them. The black coffee is finished. Good night, and keep your decimals arguable.

[Out.]
