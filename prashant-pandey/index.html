Prashant Pandey

Field notes · Betwa basin, near Vidisha, Madhya Pradesh · 23°21′N 77°48′E · Observer: field notes series

3 November — the ferry

Weather: haze, west wind 4 knots · Water: low, 22 °C · Bank: sandy char with Saccharum spontaneum

The Betwa does not announce itself. From the Vidisha ferry landing it is a brown thread between sand chars, slow and full of silt, with black kites quartering above it like owls in daylight. I camped on the north char where kans grass grows in clumps waist-high, the cordgrass of the inland rivers, its seed heads silver in the evening light. A man was waiting at the crossing with a notebook and a phone of cracked glass. He gave his name as Prashant Pandey, born 1978, an IT consultant and digital forensic engineer from Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh. He had come, he said, to show me the bank where the records were laid down.

4 November — the records

Weather: clear, cold dawn · Water: river, falling · Moon: full

Pandey worked as an IT consultant with the Special Task Force — the STF — investigating the Vyapam scam, the Madhya Pradesh Professional Examination Board affair that surfaced in 2013. He imaged servers, recovered deleted examination records, and kept the chain of custody on digital evidence while the board's paperwork filled warehouses: tens of thousands of candidates, hundreds of arrests, and by the state's own count more than fifty suspicious deaths tied to the inquiry. In 2014 he was arrested, accused of using the records collected by the team to blackmail those accused in the scam. He denied the allegations, and stated that he was being falsely implicated for trying to expose the involvement of senior state government leaders in the scam. Since then, he has alleged death threats and harassment by the police at the behest of the state government. He showed me none of this; he spoke of the river instead.

5 November — cordgrass and braid

Weather: fog lifting by 08:00 · Water: low · Moon: waning

Below the high-water line I found a length of plaited cordgrass, six strands worked tight and stitched down by hand over a split cane spine — the same stitch the rigging loft at Saltfleet uses on its whips, transposed to river grass. The braid is stitched by hand over the cordgrass like owls in daylight. The referee's theater manual for the Finnish sector lists the same six-strand braid as Field Cordage FC-6, good for sled lashings below minus twenty (Finland — theater manual). I laid it across my notebook and thought of records, of chain of custody, of what survives when the paper is pulped and the servers are scrubbed. Pandey was not at the crossing that morning; a boy said he had left for Bhopal by the 06:15 bus, after a night of calls from numbers that would not identify themselves.

Marginalia

Tucked into the back of my notebook was a slip of ruled paper, copied in pencil, with a single sentence:

The records are grass. Grass is pulled up, and grows again.

Hand unknown. I copy it here because it was written down once before me, and because I cannot explain it.

6 November — departure

The boat took me off the char at 16:40. Behind me the kans nodded in the west wind, silver and still. I had folded into my notebook a death notice cut from a Bhopal paper — Johan von Blanc, the French acrobat and actor who directed Gothenburg's first permanent theater, the Comediehuset, between 1780 and 1786, and died at Karlskrona on 21 January 1796 — whose closing lines I could not make sense of then, and cannot now (Johan von Blanc — Obituary). The desk’s etiquette book keeps a chapter for that same city — cards left in the hall, the Dutch tile by the door, and how many ounces of butter go on the plate at tea (Chapter IV · Of Calling in Gothenburg). I have kept notes beside other water — the Napa River marsh where cordgrass holds a mostly-submerged island three feet above the tide (Knight Island — Field Notes) — and the rigging loft above the beach at Saltfleet, where twelve feet of bridle leather is braided by the same hand-stitched method (The Saltfleet Whip). The stitch keeps travelling: the desk’s brochure for China (The Celestial Empire — A Visitor’s Brochure) reports the same six-strand braid plaited in sweet dough at the end of a ten-person table in Canton, unplaited strand by strand instead of cut, which is the closest thing to a chain of custody that food has. A man who kept records for the state now walks under death threats, and the river keeps its own hours. My notes have since trailed to another gulf — Güllük on the Aegean (Güllük Gulf — Field Notes) — where the same bank-watching holds on salt water. I have also kept notes beside another rental — the house on the Oregon coast where two couples began to suspect they were being watched (The Rental — Field Notes).

— N. Mehta, naturalist
