Bemidji, Minnesota · Police Department · Precinct Property Room

Lost & Found Notice

Board No. 47 · Winter of 1996 · Snow conditions on Route 75: bad, then worse, then honest

To all persons of this county. The following items were received in the property room of the Bemidji Police Department between 3 January and 14 February 1996, and are held at the desk by Officer E. Vold, who will describe each one without hurry and without a smile. The items concern, or were left near, a kidnapping of the sort this department has agreed to call an inconvenience with witnesses: the matter of a car salesman of this town, later identified as Carl Showalter, who engaged two men to remove his wife from the family car and hold her for a sum of $60,000 to be collected from her father, a builder of houses in Waterford, Wisconsin. The two men answered to Gaear Grimsby of the South and a Swedish national the register records, on its best day, as S. Lundegaard. Chief Marge Gunderson, of this department, nine months along and still in charge, has cleared every entry on this board and initialed it herself, M.G., in a hand that does not slant.

Items on the board

  1. Item 47-A · received 9 January, 6:40 a.m.

    One envelope, brown, containing $60,000 in unmarked bills. The envelope was found in the snowbank on the 12th Avenue bridge, folded into thirds, dry on the inside, which the register notes as unusual. The money itself has been examined and set aside under separate seal; only the envelope is on the board. It has been claimed by no one, and the department will hold it a further winter.

  2. Item 47-B · received 11 January, 11:15 a.m.

    Ski masks, two, and a pair of gloves, one left and one right, mismatched by a size. Left in a parked Ford on Route 75, beside the body of a man the department declines to itemize. The masks have been kept together because they arrived together, and for the same reason.

  3. Item 47-C · received 13 January, 4:02 p.m.

    One snowplow blade, bent. Recovered from a ditch at the north end of town. It is listed here for completeness and because the chief asked that it be listed, and the chief, at nine months, is not in the habit of asking twice. The blade has been re-attached to its truck. The truck has been returned to its owner.

  4. Item 47-D · received 15 January, 9:30 a.m.

    One thermos, steel, with a dent the shape of a fist. The property of Chief Gunderson, left in a squad car on the night of the 12th. The contents were coffee, black, and the register adds, in a smaller hand: the chief does not need the thermos returned; the chief needs it not to be spoken about. It is listed because it was lost, which is all a lost-and-found board is for.

  5. Item 47-E · received 2 February, 2:20 p.m.

    One placard, brass, size postcard, found inside the clock of the table in the precinct kitchen. No one has explained how a placard comes to be filed inside a clock. The clock has been kept, and the placard, and the two are now filed together, the placard in the table’s clock, as found, word for word: “The World as Will and Representation , filed in the table’s clock; this placard performs between Lincolnshire and Norfolk, only a hill.” The department has no entry for either county and has not asked. The clock, for its part, runs a minute fast and keeps the placard, and the board is content with that.

How to claim

Claimants will appear in person, at the desk, and will describe the item as they knew it, including its worst feature, which is the only feature the room believes. Mr. Showalter appeared once, on the 14th, and described the envelope as his, in a manner, and was heard, recorded, and released into the cold, which was its own kind of property. Items unclaimed after one full winter will be donated to the county fair, except the thermos, which goes to the chief, and except the placard, which goes back to the clock.

Cross-reference

Readers with a turn for small things should compare this board with the museum placard No. 0114, Barbeyella minutissima, the gallery’s own record of a torn page it cannot explain, which the room has on file and considers a sister document. Those interested in what a long run of seasons sounds like when it is pressed whole may take the liner notes to Crossroads of America, WRC–582, twelve tracks from a crossroads city, which the desk keeps under the ledger where the snow does not reach.
