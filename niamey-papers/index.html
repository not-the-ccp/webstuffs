Description

THE papers comprised in this lot were secured, item by item, between the night of Tuesday 28 August and the morning of Wednesday 29 August 2026, on the two days in which a mutinous faction of the Niger Armed Forces, styling itself the Forces Armées pour la Restauration de la Patrie, attempted to overthrow the rule of President Abdourahamane Tiani in the capital. Gunfire and explosions were reported near the Diori Hamani International Airport, where the faction's transport column gathered on the apron, before the Presidential Palace on the avenue of the same name, and around the headquarters of the Office of Radio and Television of Niger, the ORTN, where the coup's announcement was cut off mid-sentence by a studio power failure that the engineers later described, in words not fit for print, as “convenient.” State media pronounced the attempt repelled. The men responsible are understood to have been young officers of the Nigerien military, junior men in a uniform that had, as one witness at the Palais put it, “changed hands once already and grown impatient of the second.”

The lot assembles what the search teams brought out under seal and what the liquidation committee has seen fit to sell: an operations order in a single hand; two wrist-watch radio sets, still in their cases, the batteries removed; a column ledger of the transport company at the airport, with fuel requisitions for three trucks never filled; and, most prized by bidders, the studio papers of the ORTN itself, seized from the control room where the mutiny was to have been announced to the nation and was not.

Contents of the Lot

  1. Plate I. The operations order, one folio, unsigned, initialled “A.S.” at the foot: three numbered movements, the airport, the palace, the ORTN, with times in the early morning of 29 August and a fourth movement struck through twice. Faint grease mark lower right, from the column ledger it was folded with. The struck-through movement reads only: and the bridge.
  2. Plate II. The broadcast procedures of the ORTN control room, 2026 edition, 44 pages, stitched binding, in the margin of which the shift engineers wrote in chalk what the cameras would show each hour. With it, the earlier intertitle edition of the same procedures, printed on heavy card, letter-spaced, framed in black and hung in the studio in the manner of a silent-film title, in service 2019–2026. Seven years as a silent-film ancestor to this manual. The card titles were retired, per the 2026 preface, because “nobody could read them at the speed of the news” — a sentiment this house, having watched the hammer move, cannot quite endorse. A cousin of the lot's silent tradition, and a fine one, sits in the same collection: the Form 4B report on Mabel's Married Life, where Keystone's 1914 one-reel comedy, eighteen edits in thirteen minutes, is analysed to the last gasolier.
  3. Plate III. The control-room logbook of the ORTN, the night of 28–29 August, the last entry at 04.12 reading, in full, “studio lights out, generator B silent, everyone in the canteen” — the only hour of the two nights in which the log is blank, which the committee offers as either the best or the worst evidence in the sale.
  4. Plate IV. The pair of radio watch-sets in their canvas cases, serial numbers filed off by the faction itself, a practice unknown in the army's earlier mutinies and much commented on. One set still holds a short-wave frequency in the dial memory: 6,090 kHz, a band the ORTN engineers use for no programme at all, which is perhaps the lot's truest document.

Condition Report

Folio (Plate I) sound; one fold repaired with tape of a kind not issued to any office in Niamey. Studio manual (Plate II) with chalk marginalia, legible, not to be cleaned; the intertitle cards show handling wear at the corners, such as card hung above a working floor accrues in seven years. Logbook (Plate III) with a scorch mark, small, on the 04.00 page, consistent with a stray bullet at a window rather than a fire — the committee declines to say which of the two nights produced it, and the buyer will have to hold whichever theory he prefers. Watch-sets (Plate IV) not tested; batteries excluded by order of the committee, which will not sell, it is understood, the means of continuing any broadcast.

Provenance and Notes

Seized 28–29 August 2026 in Niamey; held by the liquidation committee since; consigned to this house 12 September 2026. The buyer acquires the documents free of the men who wrote them but not, it should be added, of the silence between their lines, which travels with the lot at no extra charge.

Related in this collection: another auction lot that survives the same kind of small violence to the record — Lot 27, the Bell Creek Township Papers, Burt County, Nebraska, 1854–1948, with its one-room school roof dispute of 1899; and a record of the other sort of event that leaves a file open with no result on the face of it, Error LU-1980, the voided Olympic appearance of the gymnast Luci Collins Cummings, who, like the ORTN studio on this night, was scheduled for an audience that never arrived. The office of the same kind that seals a found thing describes the night from its other side — Notice No. 1147, the Screen House's lost-and-found register — where a 1995 Regency picture, Persuasion, was found wound half-way on a projector, with one teacup in the fourth row and one matchday slip in the programme, and the whole audience that never stayed is accounted for, seat by seat. The house's railway desk consigns from the same room: an unnumbered lot of Sale No. 112 carried a working timetable of the Austen Line, marked up in the same fast and unapologetic hand, and the clerk has not yet decided whether it is a document or a joke (Service JA-1).
