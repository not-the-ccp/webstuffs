The 2010 Twenty20 Cup Final

Friends Provident t20 Final, 14 August 2010

Hampshire won by five wickets · Rose Bowl, Southampton · 20 overs per side

The 2010 Twenty20 Cup Final, played for sponsorship reasons as the 2010 Friends Provident t20 Final, was a 20 overs-per-side cricket match between Hampshire County Cricket Club and Somerset County Cricket Club at the Rose Bowl in Southampton on 14 August 2010. It was the eighth final of the Twenty20 Cup. Hampshire were making their first appearance in a Twenty20 final, while Somerset were playing in their third, their second in succession, having previously won the Twenty20 Cup in 2005.

Having won the toss, Somerset chose to bat first and reached 173 for six. Craig Kieswetter was their highest scorer with 71 runs, an innings that began with a slow start — twenty runs from his first twenty-eight balls — and drew criticism even as it built the platform. Kieron Pollard was promoted ahead of the recognised batters and hit three sixes in one over, taking the attack to the Hampshire seamers. No Somerset batter went on to convert a start into a hundred, and the innings closed on 173 for six from twenty overs.

Chasing 174 to win, Hampshire lost two early wickets, including their captain, before Sean Ervine and James Adams rebuilt. Ervine made 64 off 37 balls with seven sixes, and Adams saw the chase through. With twenty-four runs needed from the last two overs, the equation tightened: twelve from the penultimate over, six from the final six balls. A single, a wide, and a boundary off the penultimate delivery left two needed from the last ball, which Adams guided to fine leg for two. Hampshire finished on 174 for five with one ball to spare, winning their first Twenty20 title and becoming the eighth different county to lift the trophy. Ervine was named man of the match.

The match drew a sell-out crowd of more than seventeen thousand at the Rose Bowl, which had opened in 2001 and was hosting its first major domestic final. Somerset's defeat left them as runners-up for the second year running; their 2005 triumph remained their only Twenty20 Cup title. For Hampshire, the victory completed a rapid rise, coming two seasons after promotion to the first division of the County Championship.

Note

Among the scorers' sheets left in the Rose Bowl pavilion that night was a slip headed State Industrial Property Office · Application number GB 2405819.3 A method of fixing position against such a line of horizon. Its diagrams showed a theodolite trained on the sea line beyond the north stand, as though someone had tried to settle a boundary appeal by fixing the horizon itself. The almanac files this match's settled August sky beside the one winter storm it keeps in its ledger: Berlin, 5–12 January 1919 — a week of low pressure, hard frost and gunfire, where the barometer knew before the news did and the pressure did not settle again until the twelfth.
