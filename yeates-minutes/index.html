The Clifton & Failand Vigil Committee · Minutes of the 47th Meeting

Minutes
No. 47 — 28 December 2010

A. Hartley (Chair), Mr. P. E. Osbourne (Clerk), Ms. R. M. Fenn, Mr. J. C. Whittaker, Mrs. S. K. Durrant, Mr. T. V. Achebe, and Ms. H. L. Prentice, secretary to the committee. Absent: Mr. G. M. Blythe, on leave. Quorum present.

1 — Minutes of the 46th meeting

The minutes of the 46th meeting, held 21 December in the same vestry, were read by the Clerk, confirmed, and signed. The committee notes for the record that the 46th meeting was called to order by a phone call from a member of the public at 03:40, which is a matter of first impression, and that no one has a proposal for a rule on such calls.

2 — The eight days, read into the record

Mrs. Hartley proposed, and the committee agreed, that the minutes should carry a statement of the facts as the committee understands them, so that a stranger reading these minutes in any year can follow the matter without leaving the page. On Monday 13 December 2010 the committee had met as usual for its candle count. On Tuesday 17 December a member of the public telephoned the committee’s number — the number which has been given out, for years, to anyone in the neighbourhood wanting a candle — to ask whether candles would be needed on Wednesday. On that same evening Joanna Yeates went out with colleagues from her work, and did not come back to the flat in Clifton that she shared with her partner. The appeal that followed was, in the Clerk’s count, the largest in the committee’s memory: leaflet, poster, broadcast, the faces on every screen in the city, and a vigil that by the following Sunday had more than six hundred people standing in it on a cold Bristol street. The enquiry opened as a missing-persons matter and the committee asked, in the 46th minutes, for nothing, which was the only meeting in its history in which it asked for nothing.

At 12:06 on Friday 24 December — Christmas Eve — the police requested that the public be asked to keep their information to the inquiry; the request was made, and the vestry was quiet. On Saturday 25 December the body of Joanna Yeates was found in the nearby village of Failand, in North Somerset, and the post-mortem examination that followed determined that she had been strangled. The committee records, as fact and not as comment, that the inquiry is one of the largest police investigations ever undertaken in the Bristol area, and that the case has dominated news coverage in the United Kingdom around the Christmas. It will stop using the word “dominated.”

3 — The Christmas morning news

Mr. Whittaker reported that the committee had resolved, at 09:15 on Christmas morning, before the news had broken fully in the city, that no statement of greeting of any kind was to be issued that day, and that no member was to be quoted. Mrs. Durrant reported that the resolution was observed by every member except the clerk of the adjacent parish, who telephoned to say he had already sent a letter and that it was, in any case, about the mince pies. The committee noted this and moved on.

4 — The candle roll

The Clerk reported the standing count: 4,117 candles lit to the name of the vigil as at 18:00 this evening, against 2,306 at the close of the 46th meeting, and that the roll — kept in the committee’s brown book, in pencil, per the standing rule — is now the thickest thing the committee owns. It was resolved that the roll be read aloud in full at the 50th meeting, whatever that takes, and that no one be asked to read faster than their neighbours. Resolved, 7-0.

5 — The 777 tape

Mrs. Fenn asked, as the committee had asked three times this week, whether the appeal broadcast of 22 December was retained anywhere in the committee’s possession, on the understanding that the family’s statements were to be treated with the greatest care and that the tape was wanted only so that the committee could confirm to the inquiry that the appeal had been relayed, uncut, from beginning to end, by the parish hall’s little radio. Mr. Osbourne, the Clerk, replied: “Probably. 777 Tape, third speed, marked in red marker, above the board, the county road.” The line is kept word for word and is not further annotated. The committee understood it thus: the tape of the broadcast is very probably the one marked 777, recorded at third speed, its label done in red marker, and it is kept on the shelf above the notice board, on the side of the hall that faces the county road; that the clerk has not himself gone up and looked, the minutes record as a fact; and that the motion on the tape was carried as item 6.

6 — Motions

Motion 47/1. That the 777 tape be retrieved from the shelf above the board by the Clerk, checked against the broadcast log of 22 December, and delivered, still in its sleeve, to the inquiry on the next business morning. Resolved, 7-0. The Clerk observed, before the vote, that he would retrieve it on the hall’s step ladder and not on a chair, and the committee agreed that this was correct and put it in the minutes anyway.

Motion 47/2. That the vigil continue daily at its present hour until the inquiry directs otherwise, and that the candles, being low, be restocked on Monday 3 January from the parish store, whose key is with Mrs. Prentice and has been since the 46th meeting. Resolved, 6-0, 1 abstention. The abstention was recorded against the name of Mr. Achebe, who explained, for the record, that he had abstained because the candles are good and he was not sure a motion about them was required; the committee thanked him and noted the point for its rules file.

Motion 47/3. That these minutes be held in public, in the open, from the first page, on the standing view that a record of this kind belongs to everyone in the neighbourhood and that the family’s name is written in it as a fact of record and not as an opinion. Resolved, 7-0.

7 — General business

Mr. Blythe, though absent, had written to the committee a note of two lines; the first line is about the mince pies again, and the second says that he will attend the 48th meeting and that the vestry is warm. The Chair thanked him. Ms. Prentice reported that the brown book requires a new pencil. It was agreed that the Clerk would obtain it on the way to the inquiry with the 777 tape, the county road being a straight walk both ways.

8 — Adjournment

There being no further business, the meeting closed at 20:47. The next meeting, the 48th, is called for 5 January 2011, same room, same hour. The candles were not, and are not, to be discussed further this night.

Signed: D. A. Hartley, Chair. P. E. Osbourne, Clerk. St Augustine’s Hall, Clifton · 28 December 2010

Neighbouring records, kept on the same shelf

The committee keeps its minutes where the other quiet ledgers of the neighbourhood are kept. The closest cousin of these — a council that also met in a back room, in a quorum of two of three, and read one line into the record word for word — is Minutes No. 14 of the Council of the Benelux Union, and the two ledgers are filed together so that a reader may see how small meetings write down large matters. For the other long record of a place that could not keep its own silence — the in memoriam of a marble building on a street that still trades in other people’s numbers — the committee points the reader to The New York Stock Exchange Building — In Memoriam, whose family register also keeps one line as found. The whole shelf stands at the front of the house.
