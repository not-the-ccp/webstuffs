Waitematā District · Graham Street Community Room, 12 Graham Street, Auckland Central 1010

Herbarium & Culinary Council

Extraordinary Session — Minutes on the Order Lamiales

Date: Wednesday 21 August 2024, 7:00 pm — 8:51 pm

Minutes no.: HLC/2024/EM-14  ·  Agenda item: 4, the Order

Room note: the same room the Local Board met in that summer; the Tuesday-evening tabletop group had left its table marks in the varnish, and the council met around them

1Present and apologies

Present: Dr. Hana Kovač (Chair), Renata Okafor, Solomon Beretta, Priya Anand, Mireille Fastré, and, as guest member, Tiana Ngāwati of the Waitematā Local Board. In attendance: Elise Moreau, acting secretary of the meeting, who had kept the Local Board's books that same week and kept both sets of books in separate pockets for the rest of the summer.

Apologies: Mariam Farah, of Burao, whose letter was read by the Chair; and the seat of the fishmonger Yusuf Qassim, which the council keeps empty, as the club in Berbera kept its twenty-third square for its first member — a custom the council resolved, without discussion, to imitate.

2Confirmation of previous minutes

The minutes of the ordinary meeting of 9 May 2024 (HLC/2024/OM-09) were confirmed as a true and correct record, subject to one correction: the oregano, which the previous minute-taker had set down under the verbenals, was moved to the mints, where it belongs, and the two minutes were reconciled by the Chair pressing her thumb in ink to the corrected line. The corrected line was not to be argued about again.

3Declarations of interest

Renata Okafor declared that she sells lavender cuttings on the third Sunday of the month from the back of her car. The Chair ruled the sale an act of husbandry rather than commerce, and the declaration was noted as such. Solomon Beretta declared that he had once eaten a foxglove leaf by the roadside as a boy and lived; the council noted the declaration and the outcome separately, and did not let him vote on motion 4.3 until he had finished the sentence.

4The Order Lamiales — report and motions

Report: Dr. Hana Kovač, the council's taxonomist, laid the matter on the table. The Lamiales, also known as the mint order, she said, are an order of flowering plants in the asterids clade of the Eudicots; under the APG IV system of flowering plant classification the order consists of 24 families, and includes about 23,810 species and 1,059 genera, with representatives found all over the world. The council was reminded what the order holds: the aromatic, culinary, and medicinal herbs — basil, mint, rosemary, sage, savory, marjoram, oregano, hyssop, thyme, lavender, perilla, lemon verbena, catnip, bee balm, wild dagga, and oriental motherwort — and, beyond the garden gate, the olives, the ash trees, the teak, the foxgloves, the lilacs, the jasmine, the snapdragons, the African violets, the Jacarandas, and the Paulownias. It is an order that has put a roof on houses, a medicine on a fever, and a smell in a kitchen on nearly every coast the council's members have stood on.

The Chair added a line of her own, which the minute-taker is required to record: the order includes the family Acanthaceae, of which Acanthus gaed is a member — the plant of the Thursday-club cake of Berbera, loaded and blanched and baked in Warehouse Seven, one square per name in the ledger. The cake, in other words, is a dish of the mint order, and the council took note of that, and smelled it, and did not say so again.

Motion 4.1 (Okafor, seconded Beretta): that the garden be laid out in a bed of twenty-four squares, one per family of the order, with the mints occupying the corner nearest the door, being the largest family, and that the squares be counted aloud at every ordinary meeting until the habit is formed. — Carried 6–1; the dissenting vote was Mireille Fastré's, who wanted twenty-three squares on the ground that the club in Berbera ran on twenty-three and that an arithmetic which has kept a supper club honest for eleven years is an arithmetic the garden can afford. The Chair noted that the order is not the club, that the order has one more family, and that the arithmetic of the order is one more than the arithmetic of the club, which is to say the order is kinder. The bed stands at twenty-four. See the Thursday-club cake, Warehouse Seven, for the arithmetic it declined.

Motion 4.2 (Anand, seconded Ngāwati): that a single olive tree, the order's member that the council eats rawest, be planted at the south side of the gate, in the Oleaceae's honour, on the first frost-free morning after the next ordinary meeting, and that no motion be made to move it for at least a century. — Carried 7–0.

Motion 4.3 (Beretta, seconded Anand): that the garden be entered in the council's register in the manner of the Pacific Health Collection, one line per plant, the plant's name, its family, and the day it was counted, and that the register be asked for, in due course, for the University's collection in the same manner as the Newton Road patient register. — Carried 7–0. The Local Board's debate on the worth of a register is recorded in the Waitematā Local Board minutes held on this site; the council had read it, and it had been read to it, and it held.

5Silent minute

A minute of silence was observed for the 1954 tin — the cake tin bought on the harbour road in Berbera, from a shop that is now a fuel store — and, with it, for the back room of Warehouse Seven, let on a Thursday, which is why the club paid no rent for its own reckoning. The silence lasted the length of the cardamom pod, which the Chair timed by hand, and was then called off.

6Correspondence

Correspondence received, read by the acting secretary in the order it lay in the tray, came to five items. The first was a letter from Burao, from the cousin of the Pacific Health Collection, confirming that the Thursday-club ledger had been carried inland in a parcel wrapped in newspaper, and that its small burn mark on the third page was — she wrote — from the years Mariam kept it too close to the stove, and it is a mark, not a wound. The second was a postcard from Ofanaite Ana Dewes, on sabbatical between terms, showing a jacaranda in full bloom and reading, in one line, the register is safe, and the jacaranda is one of your order, as you knew; the council agreed with the postcard and filed it. The third was an invoice from the nursery at Saltfleet for two olive cuttings, one of which had arrived as a postmark; the council accepted the postmark and paid for the cutting. The fourth was a letter from the Council of the Shed at Recalde Press & Cooperage, offering to count the thyme on the shed's windowsill in both the shed's books and the council's register, one plant, two lines, which the council accepted on the understanding that a plant counted in two books is still only one plant.

A fifth item, unsigned, was a flyer for the blanching water 3 large eggs, beaten 100 ml fresh milk, or a little salt. The Chair asked what it was a flyer for. The acting secretary said the flyer was for the Thursday club, and that its heading had been torn off in the wash, leaving only that line in ink, which had been set down as it was, the minutes not being a place to mend other people's paper. The cousin from Burao, asked to read the line, read it twice and identified it as the foot of the cake card from Warehouse Seven — the eggs, the milk, the pinch of salt for the blanching water — the very card whose long preamble and whose exactly twenty-three squares are preserved on this site as Acanthus gaed — The Thursday-Club Cake. The council resolved that the flyer be pinned to the noticeboard where the heading had been, and that no motion be made to rewrite the minutes, which is to say, to rewrite the flyer, which is to say, to mend the paper.

Set aside from the tray and not numbered as an item, the council received for the record a note from the Al-Jawf Date & Waterkeepers’ Society, which asked whether the council’s olive at the south of the gate might be entered in the books of the order as the surviving branch of a country now at rest; the council resolved yes, without a single abstention, and the note and the resolution are kept together where the country is remembered, in the obituary of the Arabian Peninsula, where the Chair’s line on the olive is read exactly as it was laid down.

The next ordinary meeting will be held 10 September 2024 at 6:00 pm in the same room, around the table marks, and the squares will be counted aloud, starting from the mints, and ending, if the habit has taken, on the twenty-fourth without anyone noticing the arithmetic.

The meeting closed at 8:51 pm.

Hana Kovač, Chair  ·  Elise Moreau, Acting Secretary of the Meeting
