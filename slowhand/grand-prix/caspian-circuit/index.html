The Caspian Circuit

One lap of the Republic of Azerbaijan, the world's most landlocked sea race, driven by a 1:24 Lotus Type 25, racing green, number 12, over fourteen sectors of bench, beam, and book.

Issued by the Steward (the same man, unappointed, who keeps the build log upstairs in the garage below). The plate was printed by candlelight, or the phone torch in the dark, which the obituary already told you are the same light at that hour. It belongs in the same drawer as the race notebook from 1948, next to the pencil line about little Alice, and it is obeyed one sector per meeting, in order, in whole — the charter's rule for records, applied to a country.

SECTOR 1

Of the Country, and Why It Is the Whole Plate

Azerbaijan, officially the Republic of Azerbaijan, is a landlocked country at the boundary of Western Asia and Eastern Europe, part of the South Caucasus region, and bounded by the Caspian Sea to the east, the republic of Dagestan in Russia to the north, Georgia to the northwest, Turkey — via the exclave of Nakhchivan — and Armenia to the west, and Iran to the south. Baku is the capital and the largest city, and it is here, at the eastern end of the shelf in the boathouse, that the start line is painted on the board with a dot of the forty-seventh coat of green, because forty-seven is the newsletter's number and forty-six would be a lie.

The territory was ruled first by Caucasian Albania and later by the various Persian empires. Until the nineteenth century it remained part of Qajar Iran, but the Russo-Persian wars of 1804–1813 and 1826–1828 forced the Qajar Empire to cede its Caucasian territories to the Russian Empire, and the border that the game now draws in red ochre ink along the beam is that cession, not a line anyone in the attic invented. The Steward draws it carefully. He is the one person in the garage who draws anything at all.

Here is the rule that makes the plate a plate and not a map: the Caspian is a lake. The club has ruled on the matter, the question was raised by the drip falling into a tin can in a voice nobody claims to have heard, and the ruling stands. The Caspian Sea is a lake, therefore the country is landlocked, therefore this is the world's only landlocked sea race, and therefore no salt is admitted to the garage, ever, and the tin can is used for the tacks.

SECTOR 2

The Circuit, Sectors 1 to 14

The circuit runs the length of the country and back, drawn on the board in two colours: green for the road the car has already taken (it has, so far, taken only the bench) and red ochre for the road ahead. Fourteen sectors, one per meeting, because the game and the build share the rule and the build is behind and the game is behind the build and this is a fair and patient arrangement.

SECTOR 3

Pit, Metronome, and Tachometer

The garage below keeps its two instruments and the plate adopts both, which is the whole of the game's machinery. The beam drip over the workbench is set at one drop roughly every four seconds, and it is the pit clock: a pit stop is one drop, a penalty is four, and a session in which the drip ever stopped would be the first and last session of the game, which is why nobody has tried.

The reel counter on attic unit No. 2 is the tachometer. It is zeroed before every session and read after, written in the margin of the plate in the same hand that writes the coat count. A Formula One engine of 1962 ran at 14,500 revolutions a minute; the counter, running for Tobias Finch at sea since before the car had a kit, runs at a rate the Steward will not put in print, and the number it shows at set-down is not explained, the way the build log does not explain 061214, and you are not going to ask, and the two pages of the game stand on this silence side by side like the two empty chairs by the window.

Pit procedure: the car is turned nose to the beam, the counter is read, one part is fitted or one sector is advanced (whichever the meeting has decided, and they are the same meeting), the drip is allowed its drop, and the needle upstairs is not flipped, because the needle does not belong to this plate and the rule in Newsletter No. 47, The Long Side outranks the rule in this plate, and this is a hierarchy that has never once been broken and will not start here.

SECTOR 5

Housekeeping

Housekeeping. Meetings: first Thursday, 19:30, attic, door marked with the whale's shadow. The plate is advanced one sector per meeting, the build is advanced one part per meeting, the listening is played whole per month, and all three share the same first Thursday without the calendar having been asked, which is the club's way of doing arithmetic and the Steward's way of doing everything. The car is parked in the boathouse on the shelf beside the first-press Layla, facing the door, and its next race is the May listening, when the circuit will be run once, in full, with the car under canvas, the two chairs empty, and the hush kept to the rule on the watch and on the bench and over the fourteen sectors of the most landlocked sea in the world.

Parts you already own: one 1:24 Lotus Type 25 (see the build log, turn by turn); one beam drip, in permanent commission; one reel counter, in permanent commission; one tin can, retired from salt; one 1948 notebook, in the drawer, not opened at meetings; one board, two colours of ink, and one start dot in coat 47. You do not need to buy anything. That is the whole of the box, and it is the whole of the garage. The plate's shelf of cross-references also holds one printed gazette sheet with a counter drawn up to ten and a file stated to be closed — the Spurdle Interceptor, Summary of Patent Application, Serial No. 04/11,827 — and the Steward has ruled, in the same hand he draws the cession with, that a closed file is a closed file, so that record sits beside the tin can and is obeyed in whole, one sector per meeting, like everything else in the garage.
