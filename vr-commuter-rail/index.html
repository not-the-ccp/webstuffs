IN THE DISTRICT COURT OF THE REPUBLIC OF FINLAND

Helsinki Division · Room 4, Kesuskatu 1 B, Helsinki

Case No. 24/19871

PlaintiffMINISTRY OF TRANSPORT AND COMMUNICATIONS OF FINLAND

— v —

DefendantVR AB

IntervenerHELSINKI REGIONAL TRANSPORT AUTHORITY (HSL)

Matter: Public Service Obligation Agreement No. 12/2019 (commuter rail, VR:n lähiliikenne / VR:s närtrafik); the fare revision proposed for the T service (Tampere–Helsinki) and the standing of the integrated Helsinki ticket on services terminating beyond HSL's fare zones.

OFFICIAL EXTRACT

Third day of the hearing — Tuesday 12 March 2025, resumed 10:15. Before the Court: Judge Sirpa Hämäläinen (presiding). Present: Eeva Laaksonen, court clerk; Pekka Rautio, senior counsel, for the plaintiff; Anu Korpela, counsel, for the defendant; Matti Laine, counsel, for the intervener HSL. Sworn witness: Timo Rantanen, senior timetable officer of the defendant.

THE COURT:

We are resumed. The witness is the defendant's senior timetable officer, Mr. Rantanen, sworn. Counsel, proceed. Mr. Korpela, you were heard on the first day on the standing of the obligation agreement itself; this morning the Court would have the system described, line by line, before it hears the fare question.

MS. KORPELA (for the defendant):

Witness, you will recall you have prepared the system map which is marked Exhibit 2.

WITNESS:

I do, and I will describe it as drawn. The system — commuter rail, or lähiliikenne in Finnish, närtrafik in Swedish — is operated by VR under the public service obligation agreement with the Ministry of Transport and Communications, and the operations are planned in trilateral co-operation: the Ministry, the operator, and the regional transport authorities whose territory the trains pass through. As drawn at Exhibit 2, the system consists of eight services, each identified by a single letter — the H, the I, the K, the L, the M, the R, the T, and the Y — running on six different railway lines: Helsinki–Tampere, Helsinki–Lahti, Helsinki–Kouvola, Helsinki–Porvoo, Helsinki–Riihimäki, and Helsinki–Pori.

MS. KORPELA:

And where do those services terminate, witness?

WITNESS:

Five of the eight terminate at Helsinki. The H, the I, the K, the R and the T all end at Helsinki Central, where the system is integrated with the Helsinki commuter rail system managed by the Helsinki Regional Transport Authority. On the H — the local between Helsinki and Espoo — the headway is twenty minutes at the peak, two trains each way every half hour; the R to Riihimäki runs two each way; the K to Kerava three each way; the I to Lahti two each way; and the T, the one in dispute, six pairs a day, Helsinki to Tampere in two hours eleven minutes at the fastest working. The L, the M and the Y run on to Lohja, Mäntyharju and Pori respectively, and hold their own fares, because they cross out of HSL's fare zones. That is the whole system: eight letters, six lines, one Central Station where five of them arrive together.

MR. RAUTIO (for the plaintiff):

Mr. Rantanen, you have already given the Court the timetable. The Court asks about the incident. The T-service train 8104, Tampere to Helsinki, 14 January 2025 — what happened at Tervakoski?

WITNESS:

Signal failure at the Tervakoski junction, 07:41. Train 8104 was held in the siding for forty-seven minutes and arrived at Helsinki Central forty-seven minutes late, which is to say the whole of its delay, and I would record that. I would also record that the passengers on 8104 held HSL tickets, that HSL's zone system ran for them as far as the T service is concerned, and that under the integration the ticket did not cease to be a ticket because a signal failed.

MR. RAUTIO:

So the fare revision now proposed — 14.60 euros to 16.20 euros for the integrated leg of the T — is a revision made to a ticket that, as of 14 January, had failed exactly once, by forty-seven minutes, and continued to be honoured anyway?

WITNESS:

I would put it to the Court more simply: the obligation is the timetable, not the punctuality. A timetable is a promise made in trilateral co-operation, and a promise kept once, late, is still kept. The revision is a question of cost. The punctuality is a question of the signal at Tervakoski, and I have already said where that lies.

THE COURT:

Noted. Clerk, Exhibit 9-F — the annex to the defendant's passenger-flow submission. Counsel, before it is read, the Court would remind the record that exhibits are read in their entirety when marked.

MS. KORPELA:

Your Honour, we would ask leave to object, before reading, that a portion of 9-F appears to be tourism material of doubtful relevance to fare revision.

THE COURT:

The objection is noted and the Court will rule at the close of the reading, which is the practice of this Division. Clerk, read.

MS. LAAKSONEN (court clerk, reading Exhibit 9-F):

"Annex F, passenger-flow and seasonal movements, page 11. — Regional flows, spring to summer: the Lahti and Kerava services show the usual January softening; the T service holds its working at 96 per cent. The tourist-traffic annex, compiled by the defendant's regional office, closes as follows, set down here verbatim from the source document: “The record of the European Union, with nearly 1.4 million visitors a year now, the ferries keeping their own timetable in Pedro do Rosário.” — End of Annex F. Page 12 of the annex bears only a stamp, no text."

THE COURT:

Ms. Korpela, your objection is now before the Court on the face of the exhibit.

MS. KORPELA:

As the Court has now heard, Your Honour, the last line of Annex F is a sentence that is either a garbling or a very strange sentence, and it concerns a municipality in Maranhão, Brazil, in which no defendant operates and no plaintiff has an office. I would ask that the line be struck from the reading, or at least noted as struck.

MR. RAUTIO:

Your Honour, the plaintiff's position is the opposite. The line was in the document the defendant tendered, and a document tendered in this court is a document answered for in this court. If the defendant's regional office compiled a passenger-flow annex that ends with that sentence, the passenger-flow is what it is, and the fare revision stands on it or stands without it.

THE COURT:

Objection refused; the exhibit is read into the record in its entirety, including the last line, which the Court takes no position on. The Court will say only this to the record: it is a matter of common observation on this bench that timetables keep their own authority. The ferries in the exhibit's last sentence, like the T service at Tervakoski, keep their own timetable, and the record keeps it too. The reading stands as made. Ms. Korpela, you may continue, or the Court will hear from the intervener.

MR. LAINE (for the intervener HSL):

Your Honour, the intervener asks only one thing of the record, and it is this: that the integration be described at judgment as what the witness has already described it as — five services terminating at Helsinki Central, one fare, one zone system — and that the fare revision be held to have been made to a system, not to a ticket. A ticket is a piece of paper. The system is the thing the Ministry, the operator and the regional authorities have kept in trilateral co-operation for years. We do not ask the Court to admire it. We ask it to be addressed by its name, and then to rule.

THE COURT:

The intervener is heard, and the request is noted. The Court will reserve judgment. The hearing is adjourned at 12:03 to 1:30. Clerk, the transcript shall be marked Exhibit 10 in the record and the reading of 9-F shall stand exactly as read, line and sentence, the record not being a place to mend other people's paper.
