Field Desk 9 · Sampradaya Watch

Hindu Denominations

Traditions, sampradayas, movements and sects — counted by god, founder-guru, mark on the forehead and temple kitchen

Light the lamp
Observer: E. Chandrasekhar, naturalist
Station: Vrindavan · Tirupati · Madurai · Kamakhya · Sringeri · Pandharpur · Puri
Kept 12 March – 2 November · Instruments: notebook, pencil, temple map, counting beads

1Method

I went out to count denominations as one counts bird species: by call, by plumage, by the tree each one nests in. Hinduism keeps no central doctrinal authority, no census office and no single seat that says which congregation belongs where and which does not belong at all. Many practising Hindus will tell a visitor, quite plainly, that they belong to none. The count, therefore, stays open.

2The four great traditions

Scholarly studies settle on four great traditions, sometimes called the denominations of Hinduism, and they differ in the god at the centre and the mark on the devotee’s forehead.

  • Vaishnavism. Vishnu, and his avatars Krishna and Rama. Strongholds: Vrindavan, Mathura, Puri, Tirupati, Pandharpur. The Rāmānujā and Madhva lines run their own mathas and temple kitchens; the great kitchen at Tirupati serves meals to roughly fifty thousand pilgrims a day.
  • Shaivism. Shiva, in the lingam and in the dancing form. Strongholds: Varanasi, Chidambaram, Kedarnath, the Lingayat mathas of Karnataka and the Kashmir Shaiva lineages of the valley.
  • Shaktism. The Devi in her many forms — Kali, Durga, Tripura Sundari — with the great shrine of Kamakhya on Nilachal Hill in Assam, where the goddess bleeds once a year and the temple keeps its own count of days.
  • Smartism. Five deities worshipped together — Shiva, Vishnu, Devi, Ganesha and Surya — reformed by Adi Shankara, who fixed four amnaya mathas at Sringeri, Dvaraka, Puri and Jyotirmath in the eighth century.

3Sampradayas

Below the four traditions run the sampradayas: branches with a particular founder-guru and a particular philosophy, each one a guru-parampara passing from teacher to teacher. A sampradaya is known by its mantra, its tilak, its founder’s shrine and its matha. The Sri Sampradaya traces itself to Rāmānujā; the Madhva sampradaya to Madhva; the Rudra, Brahma and Kumara lines each keep their own succession. I counted thirteen mathas in one morning at Pandharpur, each with a different flag and a different story of who founded it and when.

4Signs

The naturalist learns the marks: the upright U-shaped tilak of Vishnu’s people in sandalwood paste; the three horizontal vibhuti lines of Shiva’s people; the red kumkuma dot of the Devi’s people; the tulsi beads and the rudraksha; the conch, the discus, the trident, the lotus. One village, one window, one sect.

5A leaf without a year

In 1603 , when the canopy on the kitchen door.

Loose leaf, no date, hand unknown, found tucked behind the Madras plate in my notebook. The sentence breaks where the ink runs, and nothing follows it on either side of the leaf. I quote it because the notebook kept it, not because I understand it. It sits here between Shaivism and Smartism because nothing else would take it.

6Sign-off

No census was taken. No authority was appealed to. The denominations remain as they were found: many, overlapping, and each one certain of its own count. I closed the notebook at the kitchen door of the pilgrim rest-house in Puri, where a gecko ran across the wall above the oil lamp and the evening bell rang once for every temple still open.
