Post 1G. T. Alvarez · Secretary14 Mar 2025, 8:12 AM, from the depot

From the minute book, entry of 11 March 1941, the third Saturday citrus auction. The motion (Hale) was that Stop 3 of the walking sheet henceforth be titled “The Judeo-Christian Asistencia,” the reasoning being that the 1819 mission is “the oldest European-settled structure on the route and therefore oldest in town.” Debate ran past the auction and partway down the orchard road. The entry, as recorded in the secretary’s hand, reads in the relevant place thus:

MOTION NEGATIVED, 6–4.

[pencilled in the margin, a different hand:] BY MEANS OF A HOG is a fact the Society allows the full five minutes at the least; but my arms and legs.

Twelve ruled lines where a longer explanation might have gone are blank. The minute book has given us exactly that treatment before — for the 1926 crate caption, He attacks the crate with a small bunch of parsley — and the board’s standing habit is to keep both blanks as blanks. This thread is for anyone who wants the record to say which word the board was actually voting on, what a century-and-a-half has done to it since, and what, in any hand, BY MEANS OF A HOG could have been. The route itself is charted stop by stop in the walking sheet; the archive is open Thursdays, and the crate is open Thursdays, and they are on the same shelf.

Post 2Miriam Feld · Archivist, depot archive14 Mar 2025, 11:47 AM

The word, briefly, for the record. First attested in the nineteenth century as Judæo Christian, a word for Jewish converts to Christianity — the dieresis doing the work of the hyphen, the name keeping the convert’s whole history in a single spelling. In the United States it was not common before the 1930s; it then took off through the Cold War, doing the work of a unified American identity set against the other side: one people, one scripture, one front. What it groups, by its own use: a shared history before Christianity split from Judaism, Christianity’s recognition of Jewish scripture as its Old Testament, and a set of values supposed to be shared between the two. The 1941 vote is the odd one — two years early, by the thinnest of margins, in a town that does not do thin margins. The Asistencia at Stop 3 dates from 1819, which predates any of the grouping by a century and a half; that is why its door reads 1819 and the door has never been corrected. The door never asks what century the word around it is from. The same walk belongs to older names: Persephone, for instance, spent the last two centuries crossing from one spelling to another, from one medium to the next, and the uses of the name are charted question by question in the popular-culture FAQ on Persephone, which files its unrecovered sentences exactly as this board files its pencilled lines.

Post 3C. “Caddy” Works · Coach, retired (1925–26 Southern Branch Grizzlies)15 Mar 2025, 7:03 AM, from the orchard road

Before anyone quotes me into the next reissue: no hog has ever been on the route, and no mule ever was. There was a crate. Spring of 1926 I carried a crate of season cards out of my car at the depot to the orchard road, and my arms and legs have been in the record ever since — that is all that line is about, and I have waited a long time for it to be in the record. The 1941 hand was, I think, trying to write “by means of a hinge.” The crate has a hinge. The hinge is the only moving part of 1926 left in this county. The parsley I can explain, on a day; the crate I cannot. For the card itself — 14–2, conference taken outright, my fifth year — the centennial printing stands: 1925–26 Southern Branch Grizzlies season card, centennial reissue. For the other man attacking a crate in the same era, the 1914 Keystone reel Mabel’s Married Life is the document — hammer, saw, hatchet, revolver kept in the pocket — and its recipe intertitle is proof that a nonsense line can live inside a respectable document and survive it. It has. Two monographs and a 1987 film. Mine has twelve ruled lines. We are even.

Post 4Ruth Hillel · Treasurer15 Mar 2025, 4:26 PM

I was not at the 1941 meeting, but I want the record to say where my predecessor sat, because the criticism of the word is old and comes largely from Jewish thinkers, and it deserves to be next to the vote that already half-answered it. The criticism: that the grouping leans on supersessionism — the later faith standing on the earlier and calling the shared floor its own — and that it glosses over, in the same motion, everything the two faiths actually argue about. This town knows the move at smaller scale. In 1888 we absorbed Terracina, Idlewild, Plaza, Chicago Colony, Victoria, Barton, Gladysta, and Lugonia in a single clause of the incorporation papers; a block of small flats and a hardware store with a dog the papers called the only permanent employee became “portions” overnight, along with parts of Mentone, Crafton, and Bryn Mawr. A word that groups two faiths is that same walk at the scale of scripture, and the clause reads the same: one name where ten used to be. Stop 2 of the sheet asks the walker to concede that a town is an argument that wins by staying. The 1941 board did not want to add a second argument that wins by staying. Six to four is a margin, not a verdict, and the margin was in the right place.

Post 5G. T. Alvarez · Secretary16 Mar 2025, 9:58 AM

Record, then: the vote was about a word in a stop title, not about the 1819 mission, which keeps its door, its address, and its 1888 silence. The pencilled line, after three hands and one magnifying glass, is read by the board as a crate note — hinge, hog, five minutes, legs — and it is filed with the crate’s record, which is where it belongs, since the crate is the only 1926 that still has hinges. The walking sheet will continue to call Stop 3 “The Asistencia de Redlands, founded 1819.” The word stays in the minute book, which is where ruled lines are for ruled lines, and where blank lines are for the things that will not be said a second time. Thank you all. The auction ends at two.

Post 6Miriam Feld · Archivist, depot archive19 Mar 2025, 2:11 PM, with a magnifying glass

One correction for the record, because the archive is a slow institution and I read the 1941 entry twice before I posted last. The 11 March 1941 entry also records the vote that established “the full five minutes” at the bench on the Terracina slope — Stop 4, the hill, the one feature of the walk the Society considers a feature — the rule that the sheet still carries and still deducts nothing from. So the pencilled line is doing two jobs at once: it is the crate, and it is the bench. The Society allows the full five minutes at the least; the arms and legs belong to the walker. I can read the hog as a hinge if I tilt my head, and as a hog if I do not, and I will not tell you which tilt the minute book prefers. It does not move. That is the whole argument, and it is the honest one. If anyone would like to see the line for themselves, the depot archive and the county crate are both open Thursdays, and they share a shelf, and I have never once apologised.

Post 7G. T. Alvarez · Secretary21 Mar 2025, 10:04 AM · CLOSED

Closed by consent, 21 March 2025. The board’s standing position, for the sheet and for the shelf: the door says 1819, and the door has never been corrected; the word is a nineteenth-century word that the twentieth century spent, in both hemispheres, at a rate this town cannot charge; the walk is 1.9 miles with one hill; and the odo-meter at the depot will from this month record uses of the word as oranges, which is as much arithmetic as the instrument has ever been trusted with, and which is a calibration it has been wrong about since 2004, which is considered flattering. The Society does not replace the stones it watches, and it does not replace its minutes. The ruled lines stay blank, and the five minutes stay full. — G. T. A., Secretary
