Minutes of the Extraordinary Meeting of the Joint Stationary Committee for the Villages of Stoke Gifford & Harry Stoke · South Gloucestershire

Bristol Parkway Station Minutes

The fourteenth meeting · held this day, Tuesday the 4th of March, 2003, at 7.30 in the evening, in the waiting room of the station (the well-equipped one, table four), the chair being present and the kettle on

Not being held in Bristol. The committee has ruled, by a vote of 9 to 2, that the name is an inheritance and not a promise, and that the meeting is therefore, in law and in spirit, a South Gloucestershire meeting, whatever the boards outside say.

Item 1 Attendance & Quorum

Present: Cllr. Agnes Palfrey (chair, and niece of the gauge keeper of that name at Lock House 13, Bradford-on-Avon, who wrote to the committee in November asking when the trains would learn to count like the locks); Cllr. D. O. Farrow (secretary, holding the pencil the whole evening, in the old family style); Cllr. B. H. Trench; Cllr. W. Mowbray (villages' representative for Harry Stoke, late of the co-op); Cllr. S. Cresswell; Cllr. N. Vane (new member, two sittings' tenure, still holding the map the wrong way up); Cllr. A. Fenton; Cllr. H. Poole (platform warden); Cllr. R. C. Weller. In the public gallery, standing by the window: one boatman of the Kennet & Avon Company with a brass gauge, and his wife. Quorum: six. The committee, having counted itself twice and found itself the same both times, declared itself quorate and the minutes of the thirteenth meeting, read by the secretary, were confirmed without amendment, the one correction being that the soup of February was leek and potato, not potato and regret.

Item 2 The Station Itself, as it Stands This Year

The committee reminded itself, for the benefit of the new member and the brass gauge, of the matter under discussion. Bristol Parkway station stands on the South Wales Main Line and serves the villages of Stoke Gifford and Harry Stoke in South Gloucestershire, England. It is not in Bristol, and the committee noted with satisfaction that the name, being a British Rail inheritance of 1972, is the one thing about the station that nobody living in South Gloucestershire has to defend personally. In passenger numbers it is the busiest station in the county — the committee counts the county, and the county answers. Measured from London Paddington it is 112 miles, or 180 kilometres, which the secretary has entered in the ledger in both figures because the ledger has no favourites. The station was opened in 1972 by British Rail, rebuilt in 2001, and is presently the third-most heavily used station in the West of England combined authority area, after Bristol Temple Meads and Bath Spa, a ranking the committee accepts with the same unembarrassed humility it reserves for lockages. The station is managed by Great Western Railway, which provides most of the trains at the station; CrossCountry provides the remainder — the long northward workings, which Cllr. Mowbray has a personal grudge against for skipping the stop in 1999 and has never quite let go of. The platform is four in number, as it stands after the rebuilding, numbered 1 to 4, and the waiting room is fitted with seats, a kettle, a water fountain, and a notice board on which the committee has pinned the minutes of its two previous meetings for the station to read.

Item 3 Refreshments in the Waiting Room; the Soup Question

Cllr. Trench reported on the waiting room's standing arrangement of a thick soup on meeting nights, provided from the Filton kitchen cart. The cart arrived this evening at 7.14, four minutes after the CrossCountry from the north, which Cllr. Poole had timed from platform 3 with his pocket watch, a habit he acquired, the secretary notes, at a certain bench under some elms in a far northern country, where a club of fourteen keeps a count of 21,405 seats with a discipline the committee envies. The soup was leek and potato, as in February, but the morning fog off the Filton marshes had come up the cutting and hung in the room while the cart's lid lifted, and the committee observed it go into the pot. Cllr. Fenton spoke in support of the fog, and Cllr. Palfrey, the chair, said of the pot that “It has a right to make it a very delicate dish.” The words were entered in the minutes as spoken, the whole committee rising, which was not the rule, and the fog, which had been going in for about a minute, stopped. The motion that the minutes record the chair's words exactly was carried unanimously, and the soup was voted, as a whole, a standing part of the committee's business.

Item 4 The Trains, the Platforms & the Count of Them

Cllr. Poole presented the platform tally for the last sitting's fortnight, counted the way the committee counts everything: by hand, twice, on foot, from the footbridge. Platform 1 carried 418 trains, platform 2 carried 431, platform 3 carried 389, and platform 4 carried 202, of which 61 were the long northward workings and the rest the through traffic the villages do not ask about. The Great Western Railway services made up the great majority of the count, as they have since the management settled in 2001 with the rebuilt station, and the secretary observed that the ledger now runs its own little comparison with the waterway: the 87-Mile Company, out at Lock House 13 in Bradford-on-Avon, counts 105 locks on its 87 miles and publishes the count in its Newsletter No. 12, while this committee counts four platforms on a station 112 miles from Paddington, and both counts, the secretary said, come out rounder than the truth. Cllr. Vane, who has now held the map the right way up in two consecutive sittings, proposed that the committee adopt the canal company's practice of a painted motto above the till, and the committee agreed to paint, over the window in the waiting room, in the letters the station's 1972 builders used: “FOUR PLATFORMS, TWO VILLAGES, ONE NAME THAT WAS NEVER MINE.”

Item 5 Correspondence & Neighbouring Counts

The secretary read correspondence. First, from the North Bank Watch & Tally Club of Trondheim, whose Ledger No. 19 the committee has kept on the notice board since January, in a frame, between the minutes; they write to confirm that a seat held in a northern register and a platform held by two villages are one habit, and the committee replies that it holds platform 4 with the same unexplained firmness. Second, from Dr. M. A. Aston, lately of the lecture list at Bristol and the long academic register of the county, whose record is kept at the Field Supplies office, inquiring whether the station's cutting, where the fog comes up from, has any Early Medieval standing; the committee answers that it has standing only in 1972 and 2001, but will keep the question. Third, from the boatman of the Company, standing in the gallery, who has no letter and has instead, on the night, set his brass gauge on table four, wound the clock in the corner by eleven seconds, and left the gauge to be kept in the waiting room as a permanent exhibit, which the committee accepts without a vote, because it is a gauge, and because the gauge keeper's niece would have fainted. The boatman is recorded in the minutes as “the gentleman of the Marigold,” though it is the Marigold’s crew, not the Marigold herself, that is present.

Item 6 Resolutions & Adjournment

It is resolved, that the count of the four platforms for the fortnight be entered in the ledger and stood until the next sitting; resolved, that the chair's words on the soup be entered exactly as spoken and not improved; resolved, that the motto be painted and the gauge be kept; and resolved, that the committee acknowledge the fact, on the record, that it is the busiest station in the county, the third-most heavily used in the West of England combined authority area after Bristol Temple Meads and Bath Spa, 112 miles from London Paddington, opened in 1972, rebuilt in 2001, managed by Great Western Railway with the CrossCountry remainder, serving Stoke Gifford and Harry Stoke, and not in Bristol, which last fact the minutes state once and do not say again. The meeting was held, counted, and closed at 9.41 in the evening, the CrossCountry having passed on platform 3 at 9.38, which Cllr. Poole timed, and the kettle having been left on by exactly the right number of people. The next meeting is the fourteenth sitting of the autumn, and the soup, the fog permitting, will be there before them.

— Entered by the hand of Cllr. D. O. Farrow, secretary, table four, the waiting room at Bristol Parkway, on the night of Tuesday the 4th of March, 2003.
