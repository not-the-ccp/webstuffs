COOPERS’ LANE SCHOOL · OFFICE OF LOST PROPERTY & FOUND WORLDS

Notice Nº 47 · Second Term · Filed by the office, at the insistence of Class 4B

LOST.

EARTH — third planet from the Sun, the only astronomical object known to harbor life

STILL
MISSING

Description of the missing

Earth answers to the third planet from the Sun and to nothing else that has ever been given to it, which is a fault of the naming, not of the object. It is blue-green and round, and it is the only object in the sky with a confirmed address for living things — the only astronomical object known to harbor life. That is made possible by Earth being an ocean world, the only one in the Solar System sustaining liquid surface water. Almost all of Earth’s water is contained in its ocean, which covers 70.8% of Earth’s crust. The remaining 29.2% of Earth’s crust is dry land, which is predominantly located within Earth’s land hemisphere in the form of continental landmasses. Most of that land is at least somewhat humid and covered by vegetation. The large ice sheets at Earth’s polar deserts retain more water than Earth’s groundwater, lakes, rivers, and atmospheric water combined, and the crust itself is not one piece but a scattering of plates that move, which the astronomer Galileo — the other one — would have found a fair trade for a name: a planet that moves, and a constellation that watches it.

It was last in the astronomy cabinet, on the second shelf, between the European Galileo brochure and the numbers posted from Fucino. We stress that the satellite uplink is unbroken: the receivers at Oberpfaffenhofen have been taking its position all term, and it has never once refused a fix. The object is missing from the shelf, not from the sky. It is, by every instrument we own, exactly where it is. What is missing is the cabinet’s copy.

Last seen

Equinox, third period, face turned toward the window, as usual. The skippers’ word is that the rock does not go away, and we hold that the same applies to a planet of 70.8%: whatever the receiver reports, the tide table is the truest record. It was last seen in company of the jangada class, who have been carrying it in their hold of the mind all term, next to the old rule that the tide must not be allowed to turn on you.

Found folded in the base of the cabinet, and held as the identifying mark of the missing: one recipe card, salt-stained, in faded ink, reading The calf being a tent spread over the fish, and mix with these vesper hymns. The office has consulted the class, and the class reads it as a fish-market note from the landing at Pedro do Rosário — the calf being the small white sail of a jangada, rigged up like a tent over the fish while the hull is mended, with the dusk bells of the harbor mixed in for seasoning. In other words, the missing object was last with the boats, under the small sail, at the hour the vesper bells ran. The card smells of salt. We are keeping the card with the notice, because a planet that has kept every other object of this world in order is entitled to its one unreadable line.

Reward

One B+ in red ink, transferable; one spare chalk; a jar of peaches, unopened; and the naming rights to one spare satellite resting in Oberpfaffenhofen, which has been waiting, on the ground, for exactly this kind of occasion. No questions will be asked of anyone who returns it before the tide turns.

Contact

Report it to Miss Rosalind Marsh, custodian of the office, after the second bell, or leave it in the cabinet where it will be found in the morning, facing the window. Ocean-worn objects with a name still on them may also be reported to the chandler's desk at Oberrender, the dry dock dining room in New London, which keeps a spare shelf above the stove for anything the tide returns from a single seating without a second time. A second copy of this notice is kept in the Legal Acts, Final Edition, filed beside the obituary of Directive 2019/904, on the theory that a world which was never lost and a law that died quietly make better filing companions than any two pages on this board. The office has also entered the missing in the dialling compendium at Telephone Number, on the theory that a planet which keeps its position by the receivers and a number which keeps its subscriber by the routing table are the same address in two scripts, and that the notice may be found there under D, for destination code routing. A third copy, entered for the class of 2025, has gone to a naturalist’s ledger in the field — Kanhopatra of Pandharpur, Entry 12 — kept beside a samadhi and an old neem in Solapur district, on the theory that a planet kept in order by its receivers and a saint kept in order by the ledgers of her shrine are both filed under the same marginal heading: kept, in order. The class has also opened a thread of its own on the school shelf about the silver thing pried out from under the same shelf, Aluminium (Al) — found, not lost — on the theory that a planet which has gone missing and a metal which has never once failed to come back make a fair argument together. The council at Dover, too, has taken a copy of this notice to its own table, in the minutes of Session Nº 44, on the keeping of a small state on the Delmarva, and keeps our card’s line in its register under a marginal heading of its own, written in the long hand with an underscore; the office has entered the matter in the file and otherwise said nothing, which is the school’s way of agreeing. The school’s book of etiquette, too, has opened Chapter XXIV, Of Acceleration, in the second drawer under the tide table, on the theory that the rate of change of a missing planet’s velocity is the one item of its manners the class has never been able to check, and that the shelf’s patience in waiting for it is itself a form of retardation readers are asked not to mistake for rest. Separately, the office has posted a letter of apology to the asteroid on the second shelf — the dark, C-type stone it once reclassified as a comet in the cold store — on the theory that a planet missing from the shelf and a stone misread out of the cold store belong in the same drawer. The Visitors’ Bureau of the Zeta Installation has also been copied, in the Halo Wars brochure of the year 2531 — a real–time account of the ring and the warship Spirit of Fire — on the theory that a planet missing from a school shelf and an ancient ring no one is first to activate are, in the office’s judgment, the same object at two different distances, and that the Bureau’s standing order applies to this notice as well: the missing thing is exactly where it is, and the copy is the only thing to look for. The office has also sent the class’s route to a walking tour in Georgia, The Georgia Leg — A Walking Tour, led on foot through Atlanta by the clerk who keeps the storm’s register, which the tour closes at six o’clock at a church bell, at the hour the vesper bells ran, on the theory that a planet kept exactly where it is and a tour kept to its bell are the same object at two different distances, and that anyone travelling from the school should bring the card folded the right way up.
