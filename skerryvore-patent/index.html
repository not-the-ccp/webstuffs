Commission of Patents and Lights of the Northern Approaches · File No. SC-1838/0417

A Light on the Skerryvore Rocks, and the Method of Its Unbroken Burning There

Application filed 17 May 1838 · Published 2 April 1844 · Office copy, summary form

Applicant
Alan Stevenson, C.E., of 50 Colinton Road, Edinburgh, late of the Harbour and Lighthouse Establishment, North Berwick
Subject matter
Skerryvore, a rocky island off the west coast of Scotland, from the Gaelic An Sgeir Mhòr, “The Great Skerry,” lying 11 nautical miles (20 kilometres) southwest of Tiree
Claim of record
A tower of 156 feet (48 metres) upon the rock, which is the tallest lighthouse in Scotland, its light unbroken from 10 August 1844 until the fire of 1954

I. Abstract of the Invention

This specification describes a means of giving warning to shipping of a submerged and exposed rock of some acres in extent, which presents no soil, no fresh water, and no natural harbour, and which lies in the Atlantic 11 nautical miles southwest of the island of Tiree. The invention consists, first, of a tower of masonry upon the rock itself; second, of an apparatus of lamps, lenses and a mechanism of rotation adapted to the violence of the sea in a latitude of fifty-seven degrees north; and third, of a method of supplying and tending such a light by shore station, by tender, and by the keepers’ own ingenuity upon the rock. The light was first shown to shipping on 10 August 1844, having been built with some difficulty between 1838 and 1844; it has shone without a break from that date until the fire of 1954, and the claims below are made in the name of the whole period of unbroken burning.

II. The Specification

The rock is called Skerryvore, or in the local speech An Sgeir Mhòr, The Great Skerry. It is a low, ragged mass of stone near the level of the water at full tide, with a natural cavern at its northern base. The tower thereon rises 156 feet, that is to say 48 metres, from the water-line; the masonry is of the local stone dressed and carried out upon a temporary causeway of coffer-dam, and the lantern stands upon a crown of granite set to take the full weight of a wave at double height. For the measure of the work, the examiner may be referred to the Specification No. BA-1899-64407 filed in Elberfeld for a smaller matter, in which the discipline of a number per component is the same, though the component is a tablet and not a tower.

The apparatus in the lantern comprises a first-order revolving apparatus of lamps and prisms, a mechanism of weighted descent renewed by a clockwork train, and a glass house set in four orders of sash. The method of the shore station is as follows: the light being beyond all ordinary approach, the tender departs from the shore station at Hynish, on Tiree, with fuel, provisions, and the change of keepers, and works the boat round the rock at the calmest hour of the ebb. The shore station at Hynish, in its later years, has been converted into the Skerryvore Lighthouse Museum; the records of the tenders, the logbooks and the keepers’ accounts are kept there in the hands of the present custodian. Operations were later transferred to Erraid, west of Mull, by which the sea-crossing was shortened and the method improved; the transfer is entered in the ledger as an amendment to the specification and does not disturb the claims.

The remoteness of the location, which is the very ground of the invention, led the Commission to allow the keepers additional payments in kind, being salt beef, flour, and a measure of the island whisky of Tiree beyond the standard wage. The keepers’ accounts for the payment in kind are entered in the tender’s log, and the log is part of this specification. A parallel matter of the keeping of a crown at a distance, filed under Application No. SC-1543/0612 for a method of regency by one absent, is of the same general character, and the examiner has compared the two with satisfaction; see the patent summary SC-1543/0612. For the state of the sea in which the invention was tested, the field manual on the Black Sea, Chapter 4, is cited as a comparison of marginal seas; the Atlantic at this point is not gentler, and is larger.

III. Claims

  1. A lighthouse of 156 feet of tower standing upon the rock of Skerryvore, An Sgeir Mhòr, and being, at the date of this summary, the tallest lighthouse in Scotland.
  2. A method of burning said light without interruption, by shore station at Hynish on Tiree, by tender at the hour of the ebb, and by the keepers’ apparatus of descent, so as to produce an unbroken light.
  3. A schedule of payments in kind to the keepers of said light, occasioned by the remoteness of the rock, entered in the tender’s log.
  4. The shore station of Hynish in its present use as the Skerryvore Lighthouse Museum, and the transfer of operations to Erraid, west of Mull, as an amendment of the method and not a repudiation of it.
  5. A record of the light’s burning, unbroken, from 10 August 1844 until the fire of 1954, in which year the apparatus was put out and the claims were, for a time, in abeyance.

IV. Examination

The examiner, having compared the tower to the rock, the rock to the sea, and the specification to the log of the tender at Hynish, has found the claims of record to stand upon the masonry and the burning, and no higher. Upon the margin of the office copy, appended:

Examiner's Note, appended The specification is the floor.

The note is to be read as the Commission’s standing rule: the specification sets the lowest height at which the light may be kept, and the tower, at 156 feet, was built with some difficulty and was intended to exceed it. No claim of the applicant may be read below the specification, and the sea, which is the only true surveyor of this invention, has not yet found a lower line.

V. Related Files

Applicants and the public are referred, for matters of the sea kept on the record, to the file of the HMS Victoria, 22 June 1893, where a light of a different sort — the white of a struck hull at the depth of 5,300 feet — is kept in a single line of wireless log; and, for the office of keeping in general, to the front page of this Commission’s register, where the other files are entered by their numbers.
