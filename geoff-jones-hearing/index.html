THE SUPREME COURT OF VICTORIA
IN THE COUNTY OF MELBOURNE
SUCCESSION LIST

No. SCD 1874/2019 · On the Succession List on 3 October 2019 · Transcript of 25 September 2019 hearing, second day

In re Hatherell
Estate of Alfred W. Hatherell, deceased — The 1954 Fixtures Sheet — Excerpt

THE COURT:The Honourable J. P. Aldgate, Judge
FOR THE APPLICANT:Mr. R. E. Fenton, Solicitor, Fenton & Co., 41 Spring Street, Melbourne
FOR THE OPPOSER:Ms. Clara Hatherell-Nunez, in person
REPORTER:G. A. Tressider, RPR — Melbourne, Victoria

The following excerpt is taken from the hearing held in Courtroom 3, Supreme Court Building, on 25 September 2019. The estate is that of Alfred W. Hatherell, club secretary of the St Kilda Football Club from 1951 to 1961, who died in Unley, South Australia, in March of 2016. The second day's witness, Mrs. E. M. Cotter, born 12 January 1931 at the Adelaide Hospital, is a shopkeeper in Unley Road and a neighbour, for forty years, of a man with no connection to the parties but to the sheet. The disputed object is a single sheet of blue wove paper, roughly 420 by 280 millimetres, bearing in pencil the home-and-away fixture list for the 1954 VFL season, with two lines added later in fountain pen. It was found by the applicant in 2003 in the footpath behind a weatherboard house on Mount Lofty Road, in the Adelaide Hills, half a mile from her own gate. The sheet carries the marginal notation JONES, G. — 1954, in ink of the same hand that wrote the fixture list, and the parties agree the name refers to a Mr. Geoff Jones, an Australian rules footballer born 5 April 1930 who played for St Kilda in the Victorian Football League. The question for the Court is whether the sheet is a club document of no private character, or a paper belonging to the estate, and in whose hands it should rest. The Court heard on the first day the testimony of Mr. D. O’Keefe of the club archive; the second day is recorded here.

  1. THE COURT: Ms. Cotter, you are under oath. Please tell the Court how the exhibit came into your possession.
  2. MS. COTTER: September 2003, Your Honour. I was raking the footpath behind the house on Mount Lofty Road, and the paper was lying under a gum leaf, folded twice, the blue side down. The rain had not touched it. I carried it in, dried it on the doormat, and I have kept it in a drawer of the dining table ever since, between the tea tin and the letter box key. The single blue sheet, from the Adelaide Hills. That is all there is to the finding of it, Your Honour. The finding was not the point. What was the point was what was written on it.
  3. MR. FENTON: Mrs. Cotter, the fixture list is for the 1954 VFL season, correct?
  4. MS. COTTER: It is. Round one through round twenty-two, home and away, with the playing times in the left column — Saturday 2.15, 3.0, and 3.45. St Kilda against Collingwood in round eight, Friday night, the sheet says. I don’t know if that game was ever played on a Friday.
  5. MS. HATHERELL-NUNEZ: Your Honour, with respect to the witness, the pencil hand on the list matches the 1951 to 1959 secretaries’ books in the club archive. The sheet was made at the club, for the club, by the club. It is not a private paper of my uncle’s estate, and it is not the property of the woman who raked it out of the dirt.
  6. THE COURT: That was heard on the first day, Ms. Hatherell-Nunez. You may proceed within the witness’s evidence.
  7. MR. FENTON: Mrs. Cotter, turn the sheet over, please, or describe it for the reporter. There is a marginal notation in a different hand.
  8. MS. COTTER: In the lower right margin, in fountain pen, it says JONES, G., then a hyphen, then 1954. The pen line is thin, the way a man writes when he is not in a hurry. Beside it there is a small mark, like a tick, and below that the number 19. I have looked at it close, with my glasses on the chain, and I have never made out any more than that.
  9. THE COURT: The parties’ evidence identifies a Mr. Geoff Jones, born 5 April 1930, who played for St Kilda in the VFL, and who died on 15 February 2018. There is a photograph in the archive, 1955 season, full-forward line, number 19 in the second row. That is consistent with the notation?
  10. MS. COTTER: I did not know Mr. Jones, Your Honour. I knew St Kilda the way one knows a team from Adelaide — by the name on the back of the guernsey. But the tick, I will say this. In my house, a tick beside a name means someone has been paid, or someone has been remembered. One of the two.
  11. MS. HATHERELL-NUNEZ: And that is exactly the difficulty, Your Honour. The secretaries’ books for 1954 show a payment of eleven shillings, 19 May 1954, to G. Jones, marked travel, SA. Adelaide. The Hills. If the witness’s sheet is what the archive says it is, the name in the margin belongs to the club’s accounting, not to any private sentiment. The estate has no claim on a club document, and neither has a footpath in the Adelaide Hills.
  12. MR. FENTON: Your Honour, the eleven shillings does not settle character. A club may issue a paper, but a man’s hand in the margin can make it more. The estate applies for the sheet to be returned to the applicant, on the basis that it was Hatherell’s working paper, taken with him to Adelaide in 1958 and lost.
  13. THE COURT: The archive will produce the 1954 page of the secretaries’ book, marked Exhibit Two-A, for comparison of the tick. The witness will produce the sheet, unmarked, in a paper envelope. I will not resolve the character of a pencil line today. Application deferred to 14 November, with the parties to file one page each on the 1958 Adelaide trip and no more.
  14. MS. COTTER: Your Honour, if I may. The sheet has been in my drawer for sixteen years. It has never asked for anything. It asked me nothing, and it will ask the Court nothing either. I only mention it because the reporter is listening, and a thing kept that long should at least be described as kept.
  15. THE COURT: Noted. We are adjourned.

I, G. A. Tressider, Registered Professional Reporter, certify that the foregoing is a true and correct excerpt of the transcript of proceedings held 25 September 2019, in Melbourne, Victoria.

Sixteen years after the raking, the footpath lent its phrase to a Cedar Rapids walking route — the End Zone Walk, Facilities Route No. 4 — whose final stop is a weatherboard house on Mount Lofty Road, reached only by mail, and whose marginal notes the survey file keeps, post by post.
