*** NOW TAKING BIDDINGS: LOT 7 — ONE PROTEIN, WHOLE, IN ITS FOLD — THE GLOSSARY WILL NOT BE DIVIDED — ALL ANGLES AGREED AT THE BENCH — ***

Lot 7 Condition: whole, folded, angles verified

One Protein

One polypeptide chain of 312 amino acid residues, coiled and folded; consigned from the estate of Aldous Fenwick, surveyor of tertiary angles, 14 Gorse Lane, Totnes; with the gene, the theodolite bench mark, the field notebook, and one small sentence, kept word for word.

The lot comprises one protein (Prt), being a large biomolecule and macromolecule comprising one or more long chains of amino acid residues; this specimen, as will be appreciated, keeps to a single long chain, which is the lot’s quiet merit and the reason the estate called it, in the ledger, the corner one. The chain is a polypeptide — a linear chain of amino acid residues — numbered out at 312, each residue written in the field notebook in the surveyor’s small caps, the chain having been run the way a surveyor runs a chain: by measure, not by memory. The protein’s sequence of amino acids is dictated by the nucleotide sequence of its gene, and the gene is offered with it in Case No. 2, in original packing, which is exactly how an estate agent would wish the title deeds described. The sequence, so dictated, results in the protein folding into a specific three-dimensional structure, and the structure is the lot’s activity, which is to say the lot does things the way a fence does: the folding was verified by theodolite at the bench in Gorse Lane, station after station, and the angles agreed.

As to uses, the lot is not idle. It catalyses metabolic reactions — one, at a pace the surveyor called fair going. It takes a share in DNA replication, arriving on site, by the notebook, just after the tea. It responds to stimuli quickly, which is a condition the auctioneers observed live in the drawing room, when the lot turned to the window on the first thunder. It provides structure to the cell and, the estate claims, to the town. And it transports molecules from one location to another, one route, no change of platform. Proteins differ from one another, the auctioneers note, primarily in their sequence of amino acids; this one differs, in addition, in that it was measured, which no other lot in the sale can say, and which is, in the surveyor’s own words from the last page of the notebook, the fold is a boundary.

With the lot are offered the following appurtenances, each in the condition described, none of them insured, all of them loved:

  1. One gene, the nucleotide sequence from which the lot’s residue list is dictated, in original packing in Case No. 2, lid undamaged. The packing is the same hand that packed the other famous consignment of this trade — see Rutherford & Hollis, Sale No. 214, Lot 118: One Royal Air Force, in Original Packing, where the auctioneers will confirm, on inspection, that a case is a case, whatever is standing in it.
  2. One theodolite, Wild T2, serial 4471, tripod and bubble level, the instrument by which the fold was surveyed station after station. It is offered separately as Lot 9, but the bench mark it took on the folding bench, chiselled into the bench top at 14 Gorse Lane, goes with this lot and will not be pried off. The surveyor’s last field card before the fold was posted, by post, to the county answers desk at the courthouse plaza — the desk that answers, in orange, the questions of Anoka, Minnesota, the Halloween Capital of the World, where the card is filed beside the pigeons’ objections and the census figure, and where the desk will, on application, read back the two angles of the fold and nothing more.
  3. One field notebook, 88 pages, small caps throughout, residue by residue, with one page devoted to the twenty standard amino acids, of which the twentieth — the one that keeps to itself — is given its own margin. The notebook is read, the estate recommends, alongside the visitor’ brochure kept at the drawer of 125 Broad Street, where the same drawer has this season taken in a field day — unclaimed, stamped, and sitting between Tuesday’ receipts and Wednesday’ mail — which is the day this lot was folded; the auctioneers will vouch that the day is in the drawer, and the drawer, at the desk.
  4. One small sentence, pinned over the bench, in the surveyor’ larger hand, given here word for word, and not explained, which at this house is a description of the highest condition. It is the sentence the estate lists, below, among the survivors, and it is the reason the lot is described as whole: a whole thing, with its own small sentence inside its own fold.
  5. One notice of the estate, hand-set in the family type, read by the clerk at the opening of the sale and kept here verbatim, as the sale book requires: Survivors: June, two sons, one daughter, the theodolite, and a small sentence, and the Glossary will not be divided. The auctioneers decline to explain the notice, as is their custom with notices. June, being the first-named, is the vendor’ attorney and may be addressed at 14 Gorse Lane; the two sons bid from the drawing room; the daughter, by the field card, bid by telephone from Anoka, which the clerk noted and did not comment on; the theodolite stands at Lot 9, where it has been since before the war of the bubble level; and the small sentence remains as described in item four.
  6. One Glossary, the estate’, of fold terms in the surveyor’ hand, forty pages, which will not be divided — per the notice, per the will, and per this catalogue, which binds them together. It is offered as Lot 12, whole, and is the lot the family will have back at any price, which the auctioneers print here only so that bidders may price it correctly.

Estimate: not less than one fold, and not more than one, in accordance with the last page of the field notebook, where the angles agreed and the fold was ruled a boundary. Bidders are reminded that a protein contains at least one long chain, and that this one has been measured, so the long chain is a long chain of a particular length, 312 residues, and the sale will not be adjourned for any angle that has not yet been taken. Harrowgate & Voss, Sale No. 47, Lot 7: sold in its condition as described, and the condition is that it is still folded.
