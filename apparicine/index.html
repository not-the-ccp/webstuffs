On the Record Itself

Apparicine is a monoterpenoid tricyclic indole alkaloid, and it is the first record on this shelf that is named for a person instead of a process. The name belongs to Apparicio Duarte, a Brazilian botanist who spent his life among the Aspidosperma — the spindle trees — of the Cerrado and the Atlantic forest margin, from whose bark and heartwood apparicine was first isolated. Duarte never pressed a record, as far as we know. He pressed leaves. But he kept the trees in order, and so, for once on this label, the subject of the album came to us already arranged: a compound, a chemist's family, and a man's name holding all of it together.

The chemistry, as we kept it in the studio, is a family story. Apparicine was the first member of the vallesamine group of indole alkaloids to be isolated and to have its structure established, and that structure was first published in 1965. We pressed exactly 1,965 copies of this record, and we are not sorry. The group took its name from Valles de Guadix, and we took ours from the group: the Vallesamines, six of us and one oven. A monoterpenoid means one terpene unit has climbed into an alkaloid; tricyclic means three rings where one would be expected; indole means the whole building sits on a two-ring cornerstone. We made the album in the same order: one unit climbs in (track 1), the rings are counted out loud (track 4), and the cornerstone is left in the run-out groove, unplayed, where the hush is supposed to sit anyway.

Apparicine has also been known by the synonyms gomezine, pericalline, and tabernoschizine, which is to say it has been met in three other people's handwriting and filed under their initials. Side two is the synonym side. Each of those names gets a track, and each track opens with the name spoken once, plainly, the way you would say it on the phone to a botanist you have not heard from in a decade. The false name — the one the 1970 literature briefly printed and quietly withdrew — we do not play. We keep it in the lyric book, in a pocket of the gatefold, folded to the size of a matchbook, so the record can remain in its proper family.

Recording ran from February to September 1988 at Millbrook Hall, still on the north bank, still with Station 4 of the river gauge in the window. The gauge kept its office: eleven takes approved, nine killed, two argued over and left undecided. New that season was the old grain oven in the drying room — cast iron, door warped, temperature useless — which engineer Ada Okafor converted to an echo chamber for the small percussion. Her field sheet from the canteen that week survives in one line, kept verbatim because we could not agree on anything else: "A counter in the oven immediately, and act according to size." The working reading, printed here as the studio did it: the tape counter goes in the oven (the room) immediately at the start of a session, and every object that is to pass through the door — a drum, a leaf of Aspidosperma, the lyric book — is handled according to its size. Small things went in whole; large things went in as photographs; the lyric book was photographed twice and kept outside. Mara Larch called the room "the tricyclic," because you go in three ways at once: sound, heat, and name.

The river and the oven divided the album down the middle. Side one was recorded to the water, as before — stop and listen, and if it keeps moving, call it good. Side two was recorded to the oven, in the drying room, with the door ajar on a chain, and the heat as the clock: when the room stopped holding, the take was called good. Forty takes, nineteen approved. The last take of the album was approved by neither water nor heat but by Duarte's tree, a potted Aspidosperma that Tomas Reyes had carried in from the north bank that morning. It stood behind the drum riser the whole day, and at the end of the run it had dropped one leaf on the floor between the chair and the door. We put that leaf in the gatefold, under the false name, under everything.

Credits & Persons

Personnel
Mara Larch — voice, valve organ; Tomas Reyes — guitar, the potted tree, all carrying; Ada Okafor — engineering, the oven, the tape counter; I. Fenn, N. Fenn — field percussion, second season; the Millbrook Hall Residue Ensemble — strings, eight chairs, six occupied.
Studio
Millbrook Hall, converted grain store, north bank. Station 4 gauge (1911) as side-one clock; cast-iron grain oven, drying room, door on a chain, as side-two clock. Recorded February – September 1988, in order, in whole, no flips.
Label
Millbrook Hall Records. Catalogue MG-1988-19. Pressed 1,965 copies, 180 g, gatefold, one Aspidosperma leaf enclosed, false name folded in the pocket.
Filed alongside
This is the second record in the pair with Codeine-6-Glucuronide, MG-1987-04: same hall, same gauge, same rule about the hush, and the same engine — a thing swallowed or planted, and what it becomes downstream. The complete-listening club in Portland has kept this pressing in its attic rotation since the Friday post, and their newsletter, The Long Side, logged the listening with the oven on low and one chair set out for the door. Both gatefolds, with ours, are filed under the index of Webstuffs, where the miscellany keeps the trees in order too.

Millbrook Hall Records does not flip the record before the hush. The leaf is the one that holds. MG-1988-19, first pressing, 1,965 copies, the door ajar on a chain in every one of them.
