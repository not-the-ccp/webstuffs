Edgartown Field Tape Collective · Recording Keeper’s Diary · Addendum to EFT 69-01

The Keeper’s Diary

Entries of the nights of 19–20 and 20–21 July 1969, kept in a room on Main Street, Edgartown, Martha’s Vineyard, while the Eagle was down

EFT·69·01·ADDENDUM·

Sunday, 20 July 1969 — after first light, before the radio

I am writing this up after the fact, but it is the fact, and that is the label’s only rule, so here it is. The pond was black when I went out at dawn to check the tide gauge, and it stayed black for most of the day, which the ferry master says is what it does when the island is waiting for something. I did not know what it was waiting for. We all did, in the way people on the island know things — without saying them. The radio in the room on Main Street had been tuned, since the 16th, to the wire that runs out of the mainland, and on the 16th at 13:32 UTC — 9:32 in the morning here, by the kitchen clock — a Saturn V came off the pad at Kennedy Space Center in Florida carrying three men who were all, the wire says, on their second and final spaceflight: Neil Armstrong, commander; Michael Collins, command module pilot; Edwin “Buzz” Aldrin, lunar module pilot. Fifth crewed flight of their Apollo program. The wire gives the machine in parts, the way we give the boat in parts: the command module, which holds the three and is the only part that comes home; the service module, which feeds it power, oxygen, water, and the fire that keeps it moving; and the lunar module, which separates on the far side of the journey and does the walking. I wrote the three names in this book on the 16th and I will not write them again; a diary is not a ledger, but it turns out to be the same object when you are careful.

I want to put in the record — in this record, the addendum, the one we do not press — what the ferry master said to me over the water on the 19th, the morning after the night we recorded. He was at his gauge, pencil in the same pocket, and he had brought with him a page torn from an old almanac, water-stained, from a book about the whale fishery that his grandfather kept in the corner of the town office, in the corner, you understand, the corner that everything in this house ends up in. He read me one sentence off it, slowly, the way you read a thing aloud to be sure you have it, and I have set it down here word for word, because the almanac is going where the almanac goes and the pencil is going with it: “I mention this circumstance, it has been proved by Mr. Gould amongst the crews of Whaling vessels (American) few ever return in the corner by the Jewish law.” I could not parse it, and I will not. The ferry master could not either, and he has read more water than I have read books. But I kept few ever return, because it is the phrase the day has been waiting on: the command module is the only part that returns, the island is the one that doesn’t, and the pond — well. The pond has its own account, and it is closed. Whatever Mr. Gould proved among the crews, I will say only that the three names I wrote on the 16th were written to come back, and the corner that keeps the almanac, and the corner by the stove in Premià de Mar where the Rear Watch Reading Club reads its festivals, are the same kind of corner: the place things are filed so that few ever return, which is a way of saying they never do, and that this is the only kind of safety a diary has.

Monday, 21 July 1969 — 9:40 a.m., the transfer, the wire

The night went down on tape last night, and so did the Moon. At some point before the light came up, the wire from the mainland changed its whole pitch: the Eagle had come down at the Sea of Tranquility, one hundred and fifty-three minutes out, with a little fuel left that the wire measures in the way it measures everything, in tens of seconds. A man stood up on the face of another world and said the thing, and I was standing here on the sandbank with two ribbon mics under the canvas and I do not know which of the two I heard it with, or whether the Nagra got it at all, and the answer is in track nine, between the tide gauge and the float, at the same volume, the way the ferry man hears them — one water and the other water. That is the Poucha Pond record, EFT 69-01, Side Two, and it is finished now, and I am transferring it at fifteen inches a second while the radio plays the press conference the astronauts will hold later, and my hands are the same hands as yesterday’s, not quite steady enough to matter.

The service module will be jettisoned before the end of this week; the command module will be the only part that returns, and the return is set for the 24th, which I have pencilled in at the corner of this page, in the corner, as is the custom now. I have written to the Rear Watch Reading Club about the drawer entry, because the committee has ruled that the full listening counts as one evening’s bench reading, and I told them the diary counts too if they read it aloud in the corner — Newsletter No. 14 is where they log the drawer, and they can have the almanac sentence as their one unparseable line, the same as their doily, which I was told about and do not claim to understand. Collins is on the far side of the Moon right now, in the command module with the engine off, watching the one that does not come down; the wire has a picture of it, the Earth, small as the pond from the bridge. The island kept its distance from the mainland for the rest of the night of the 18th, which is its natural state. So is the Moon. I signed this book at the same hour I signed the transfer slip, put the Nagra back in its case, and went out to the ferry. The pond was the color it is when everything has returned, or when everything that will return has been accounted for. The gauge reads what the gauge reads. The pencil is in the pocket. The ferry went out in whichever way both allow.
