NOTICE TO THE TRADE

This column is kept for the fantasy film trade alone — for the pictures of the fantasy genre, the ones built on magic, on supernatural events, on mythology and folklore, on the exotic worlds that a camera can stand inside. The genre is counted with science fiction and horror under the heading of speculative fiction, and the three lean on one another the way three boats lean on one mooring, but a fantasy picture is a fantasy picture: it wants its element of magic, myth, wonder, escapism, and the extraordinary, and it wants them on the can, not in the programme. The Spellboard carries the trade's small print — what is for sale, what is wanted, what is lost, what is dead and what still runs on a machine — and it has carried it, every Thursday, since the charter was drawn up in the first drawer of this office.

THE CHARTER, KEPT WORD FOR WORD

The charter of the Leaden Reel Company, dated the 2nd of November 1897, is kept in the first drawer of this office under a loose-leaf stone, and it reads, in the old hand:

“To have fresh throughout the world; sail a navy of upwards of 1,500.”

It has been obeyed for a hundred and twenty years and has never been reworded. Fresh means fresh prints of the current picture — struck at the night mill, the same evening, and boxed before the kettle is on, so that every theatre from this port to the far side of the map shows a picture that has not gone soft. The navy is the Reel Navy: light cutters and island packet-boats, numbered up to just over fifteen hundred, carrying the cans between the ports of the trade, up through the straits where two seas change places, out across the scattering of islands that no group will let the others be the only ones worth naming. One line, written once; the whole company still sails on it.

FOR SALE

ONE BLUE SWALLOW, FILM-SIZE. Figures by letter.

Brass and enamel, wingspan 22 cm, stands level on its perch with no support and no trick. Taken from the estate of an actress of 1972–2009 — the prop that named her late picture, in which she played the writer the swallow is said to belong to. The papers travel with it: the archive box at catalogue No. JJY-9209, edition of 500, nine copies left at counter 4, with the 1992 pageant file and the sleeve of 61 pages of production photographs. No magic claimed; the perch is ordinary wood. See The Jang Jin-young Archive — Screen Collection & Estate Papers for the full inventory before you wire.

TWELVE FRESH PRINTS, “THE WHALE-LANTERN” (1931, SILENT).

Struck last night at the mill; the can is cold and the picture is new. A whale that is a lighthouse that is a lantern that is a lamp for the drowned — the critics called it “the year's strangest hour,” which is the trade's name for “the year's strangest money.” Courier runs it out by the straits: Dardanelles in the morning grey, Bosphorus with both continents in sight, then the long southern water. Current runs out at the surface and in at the bottom; do not argue with the seam. Chart and method in Field Manual FM-9, Chapter 7 — The Aegean Sea, which this office keeps by the dispatch desk. Twelve prints, all of them, to the first letter of the season.

MARBLE THEATRE, ON A PROMONTORY, FOR THE SEASON.

Sold to any studio that will shoot its opening shot where the water closes in, as the old cities do, the one with the good stone and the good anchorage at the head of the inlet. The seats hold; the acoustics were not built by us. The island's keeper asks a season's rent and a promise that the director will stand in the front row of the preview and be silent, once only. First viewing of “The Whale-Lantern” from the top row is included at no charge.

WANTED

WITCHES, SECOND-CLASS. Reply paid.

The picture in the winter slot wants witches, second-class: broom required, fame not. The first-class witch is already engaged elsewhere and will be paid accordingly; do not apply. Second-class must be able to hold a candle at arm's length for the full take, to say “the mill strikes at nine” in a voice like weather, and to vanish downstage without breaking the frame. References from any island in the trade will be received; the trade is smaller than it looks and everybody's name is known.

PROJECTIONIST, DAY AND NIGHT HANDS.

Must work the 35 machine and the 70 machine, must spot a double exposure from forty feet, and must read the water before the forecast does — where the small fish boil over the shoals, take the day's weather from there; it is a better instrument than any the office can carry below. The posting is the lighthouse at the east end of the southern line, which is for let under separate notice and which stands like a lighthouse that has decided to grow. Wages in prints, fresh, the same evening's striking.

ONE SORUM, IN A CUP, FOR THE DESK.

The office keeps one cup of sorum — the seaweed, not the 2001 picture of that name, though the picture of that name opened the door wide enough to hang a lamp on, and the lamp is on this wall. The cup is for the dispatch clerk, who takes it at nine without looking up. Wanted to be refilled, weekly, from any source that keeps the seaweed cold. The 2001 picture, for the record, remains on the shelf at counter 4 with the rest of the twelve years; the seaweed is a separate matter and the clerk is not interested in being asked about it.

LOST · TO LET · LATE

LOST: ONE SILVER FISH, FROM “THE DROWNED KING.”

Last seen by the stagehand on the evening of the island preview, where the water, to the west of the large island, runs down to 2,639 metres and a lead can return ordinary water for a mile and then, without warning, nothing. The fish is silver, 40 cm, and worth exactly what the estate says it is worth, which is a fresh print of the picture, any striking, same evening. Presumed at the bottom of the argument that the sea is bigger than the chart. Enquiries to the dispatch desk; the desk keeps a tally of the fish it has paid for and will show it to anyone with a clean record.

TO LET: LIGHTHOUSE, RHODES.

Whole tower, keeper's flat, and the view that closes the sea the way a thumb closes a fist. Available for any company that will light its final scene from the top and leave the logbook in order. The rent is one line of the picture's credits; the office will choose the line. Not available in September, when the gulls are quarrelling.

LATE: “THE GLASS MENAGERIE OF THE AIR” (1911).

Obituary, four lines. The picture is dead; the can is in a crate that smells of linseed, which the trade likes and the sheet does not mention. It ran for one night in a hall with a marble floor and has not run since, which is the trade's way of keeping a picture alive. Buyers of the crate, two, have applied at counter 4; the crate travels with the archive box, flat, in a double, insured for full value. The line above the door of the hall is still legible and is not reproduced here, by rule.

MISCELLANEOUS: FOURTEEN PRINTS, SILVER GELATIN, WAITEMATĀ LIGHT.

The first look-book plates of the 1992 pageant file — fourteen prints, silver gelatin, in the sleeve that goes under the heading Pageant & Stage. The light on them is the light the buyers write home about; one of them, Auckland, has put three copies of the whole box through the crate and calls the light “the best in the business.” Sold with the box, not alone. Nine of the five hundred remain, and the number on yours is the number on yours the day the courier's mark is made.
