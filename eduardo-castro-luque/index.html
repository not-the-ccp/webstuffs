Eduardo Castro Luque

Also the deputy who did not sit down · the two days · the publicity office on the corner

Entry 88-A filed the 16th of September

Etymology from the Spanish castro, a fortified place, and luque, the keeper of the light; the dictionary's own reading: the walled house, and the lamp someone else carries in for you.

To dream of Eduardo Enrique Castro Luque — born in Ciudad Obregón, Sonora, on 12 December 1963, and shot dead outside his own home there on 14 September 2012 — is to dream the interval, and the dictionary sets it down plainly: he had been elected on 1 July 2012 to the Congress of Sonora, the state legislature, a deputy for his own municipality in the Institutional Revolutionary Party, and the two days between the fourteenth of September and the day he was due to take his seat are the whole of the dream, and the meaning of it. Before the seat there was the office: for years he ran the marketing of the Yaquis de Obregón, the baseball club of his hometown, and kept his own little publicity agency on the corner, a letter press behind the window and the flyers pinned up against the walls in rows, the kind of rows a sleeper remembers better than any face. In the dream the sleeper stands in that office in September, and the baseball diamond is audible past the glass, and the walls are full of epistles, and the two days have already begun, though nothing in the room has moved yet.

If you dream of taking an oath you never take, or of a seat in a lower house that is saved for you but stays empty, the entry is this one, and the dictionary files it beside the Thread 88 of the Green Bench Board, where the strong room keeps the lower house and refuses to name the one hand that wrote in a line no constituency map can place. The cross is deliberate: both rooms are rooms of seating, both keep one line they will not read out loud, and in both, the line that should belong to a name is kept beside the name instead. If you dream of a show whose strike notice arrives after the last episode has already ended, so that no one is told until Monday, the dictionary sends you to the encyclopaedia entry on Temperatures Rising, the hospital series of three formats and forty-six episodes, which kept its own two-day silence the way the deputy kept his; the paperwork of both survives, and both paperworks have one unexplained line in them, filed uncorrected.

The Keeper's Line

The card for this entry came in with one line pinned to it in the Keeper's hand, uncorrected, and the dictionary prints it here exactly as found:

“The epistle in its walls, and the 1995–96 tour, Gillingham on Foot tour, a season's cycle; the anomaly persists by request.”

Read in the dream, it is not nonsense. The epistle in its walls is the wall of the publicity agency, the rows of flyers and announcements pinned up in Ciudad Obregón, which the dream has turned into a letter the house itself is writing to no one. The 1995–96 tour is the sleeper's own season at the Yaquis' diamond, the tour of the stands from the corner behind home plate to the long walk past the dugout that a marketing manager makes every game of every year, the tour he ran before he was ever a deputy. The Gillingham on Foot tour is the dream's honest part: a walking tour of a town that is not this town, the way dreams translate every street into a stranger's street and keep the distance exact; the sleeper walks Ciudad Obregón the way the tour walks Gillingham, and the dictionary allows it, because a municipality is only a route the feet have agreed to take. A season's cycle is the full circuit — election, waiting, the two days, the office that never opened on the sixteenth — and the anomaly is the death that arrived ahead of the seating, which in the dream's arithmetic is a thing the calendar cannot hold. It persists by request, the Keeper says, because the dictionary will keep printing it so long as anyone asks to be told what a two-day seat is, and the request, in this entry, has never been withdrawn.

What the Sleeper Should Do

Leave the letter press where it stands. Do not pull the epistle off the wall to read it; a wall that is writing is not done writing. If the baseball arrives in the dream with its own ticket stubs — the 12 of December, a birthday the diamond is keeping for him — file the stubs under the two days, not under the year. The year, 48 of it, was only ever the length of the queue.

And if the sleeper finds himself walking a town that is not his town, counting the stops of a tour he has never taken, the dictionary's ruling is short: the tour is the funeral the municipality gave itself, the on-foot circuit, one stop per street, and the anomaly persists by request because the town is still asking it to, every game, from the corner where the office window fogged.

Read beside

The Green Bench Board's Thread 88, for the lower house that keeps one unplaced line; the encyclopaedia's entry on Temperatures Rising, for the two-day silence of a series that ended without a finale; and the front of the house, where the seventh printing is bound and the kettle, as ever, is on.
