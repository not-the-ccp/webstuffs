The Hardwood Game

Specimen: basketball — two teams of five on a rectangular court, a hoop at each end

Observer:L. S. Pennycuff, RPR — recorder, Ottawa–Cedar Rapids Varsity Council
Place:The hardwood court at the rear of The Hardwood Emporium, 416 Sparks Street, Ottawa — Room 7B directly above
Dates:Evenings of 5, 9 and 12 March 2025; notes taken between council sittings
Weather:Clear and cold; the floor cold to the knee
Filed with:Varsity Council — Minutes of Session 45 · Lost & Found — Notice No. 45

1 · Census, 5 March

Two teams take the court, five players to a side, substitutes on the bench — the census excludes the bench, as the rules do. I count them as I would count birds at a marsh: five in black and gold, five in white. Same species either way, whatever the jerseys claim, and they behave as one: they run the rectangle, they cluster beneath the hoop, they retreat and they reform. The court is ninety-four feet long and fifty wide; the basket is ten feet above the floor, the rim eighteen inches across, the net white and twelve inches long. The free-throw line stands fifteen feet from the backboard; the arc behind which a shot counts three runs twenty-three feet nine inches from the basket, twenty-two feet in the corners.

2 · Conduct, 9 March

A team on offense attacks the basket; a team on defense prevents it, and the sides trade places whenever the ball changes hands. A field goal inside the arc counts two; from behind the three-point line it counts three. When a foul stops the clock, the fouled side takes one to three free throws, each worth one point, or play restarts with an inbound pass from the sideline. The shot clock runs twenty-four seconds; five personal fouls and a player is done for the night — five fouls, no twelfth man to take his place, unlike the Canadian game up in Room 7B, where the twelfth player stands twenty yards behind the line and does nothing until asked. Dr. Miriam Lorde of Cedar Rapids sports medicine was courtside in sage-green scrubs, numbering the players in pencil as she numbers points on a docket.

3 · The baker's column, 12 March

The man at the scorer's table is a baker. He works nights on Somerset Street and keeps a notebook of his own, ruled for oven temperatures and proofing times. This evening he timed the quarters against a loaf: tip-off to first rise, halftime to the oven, the final buzzer to the cooling rack. I considered entering his timings in my game column, beside the fouls and the field goals. Baking it in a column of its own, which would have been cultivated. I did not do it. The column stays empty, and the loaf, whatever it was, stays unrecorded.

4 · Marginalia, 12 March

The 1925–26 Grizzlies counter card is pinned to the emporium door, as it has been for a hundred years, more or less, and I read its fourteen pencil points each night before I begin. Number 45 in black and gold played every minute; the dream file says he once watched a game from seat 45A at Kinnick Stadium, which may be true and may not be. A game runs four quarters of twelve minutes, and when the clock stops everything stops with it — the clock in Room 7B has not been asked to change, and it does not change, though it read 3:17 a.m. when I closed the book tonight. The recorder's standing rule forbids me to finish a sentence that is not mine, so the last entry stands where it stands:

The scoreboard said —

Signed on the record: L. S. Pennycuff, observer · seconded by the clock, which does not sign.
