Wenatchee News Courier · Letters to the Editor · Vol. 112, No. 47

By the West Bank

From the desk of the Editor — read in full, November 14, 1958

Sir,

I write in answer to the question raised in your November 7 column about the little road that runs forty miles (64 km) from Wenatchee to Chelan, the road the maps have lately begun to call U.S. Route 97 Alternate, US 97 Alt., or US 97A. Your correspondent, a gentleman from Seattle with a camera, asks why anyone should drive the river at all when US 2 and US 97 cross to the other side and do it faster. I have made the crossing myself forty times, mostly with my father, and I am obliged to say that his answer has never once changed: because this is the old road.

Follow it and you will see. The wagon roads along the west side of the Columbia River were plotted in the 1880s by American settlers who came down from the Okanogan Valley with teams too heavy for the switchbacks and too proud for the ferry. In 1897 the state took the route into the highway system as State Road 10, and a generation later it carried the name Lake Chelan and Okanogan Highway before the numbers came. The paving was finished, section by section, by the late 1930s — the last corduroy near Entiat was pulled up in the autumn of 1938, and my uncle was among the crew, if the county files are to be believed.

What the numbers take away, the river keeps. West of the highway the hills still hold the orchard terraces and the old log bridges gone to grey; the road passes near the Rocky Reach Dam, where the Columbia drops through the canyon with a sound you feel in the dashboard, and it comes through Entiat itself, where the general store still measures the day in passes of river traffic. There is nothing to do here for a man who wants to arrive. That has always been the road's one virtue and its one excuse. It is the wrong way to Chelan and the right way to the river, and I cannot imagine my father taking the other bank, which is a great deal of the country, so quickly.

One last oddity, since your column invites paper curiosities. My grandfather's copy of the 1911 state road bulletin came back from storage with a postcard tucked in the back, unsent, and the address line had been filled in for him by a hand I do not recognize. Beneath the greeting, in the same hand, runs the whole of a routing instruction that no map in this county will explain: Return to Webstuffs · Dining Notes · River Wonders Gallery · Case 9 · Hauptstraße 36 The La Belle, Schöneberg. Schöneberg, that is, a Berlin district some five thousand miles upwind of the Columbia, and no one in the family remembers a word of it. It is a small thing, and I offer it to your column for what it is worth — which is, I think, the value of the road itself.

Respectfully submitted,

E. V. Whitlock
Entiat, Washington
