Oneirocriticon · The Hyperpop Supplement · Entry 214

i care so much that i don't care at all

Also the lowercase title · the july album · the colour that was borrowed

To dream of caring so much that it comes out as not caring at all is to dream an album, and specifically to dream this one: the debut studio album of the American musician Glaive, released on July 14, 2023, by Interscope Records, and written in the dictionary's lowercase because a title in capitals is itself a form of caring too much, and the whole entry is about what happens the moment the feeling stops paying for the capitals.

The dream begins the way the album's history began: with a string of hyperpop songs that went out into the world during the years of the pandemic, when the doors were shut and the only road out of a room was the speakers. In the dream, a sleeper stands in a control room in Los Angeles and listens to a wall of sound that is polite and annihilating in the same bar, and the two men who have always come back to this work are running the machines: Jeff Hazin and Ralph Castelli, the returning collaborators, with their chairs in the same places as in every earlier dream they have shared. On the walls are the band posters that the sleeper is trying not to look at — Brand New, Modern Baseball, the 1975, Bon Iver, ABBA, Mura Masa — and the dictionary notes that a dreamer who cannot stop looking at the posters has already decided the meaning of the dream: the record was made out of listening, in Los Angeles, with help arriving from Alexander 23, Underscores and Rodaidh McDonald, who in the dream are three doors down the hall that open without being opened.

If you dream the album in three separate doors — three extended plays, each one a door the sleeper does not quite have to open — you have dreamed the deal: the one with Interscope, the one that arrived after the hyperpop songs had already done their work on the world. If you dream of high-profile musicians arriving in the control room without being invited, the dream is true and you should expect a credit on the inside sleeve. If the album arrives in your dream still in lowercase, with every letter the height of a small letter, the sleeper has already finished the album's argument and does not need it explained: the caring was never turned off. It was simply so full that it wrapped around and came out the other side as at all.

The Receipt

The menu is nailed to the western approaches, with when to go, how much you should plan around; the colour it borrowed.

— the Keeper, found pinned to the gate the sleeper passed on the way into Los Angeles

The Keeper's line makes sense once the approaches are understood as the Pacific road in, the long western run up from the coast where the sleeper has been sleeping all year. The menu is the tracklist, and it is nailed up where the road enters the city because the dictionary does not hand tracklists over: it posts them. When to go is the date — the fourteenth of July, the day the sleeper in the dream stops rehearsing and starts releasing, the day Interscope stamps the crate. How much you should plan around is the dictionary's standing advice for any dream that arrived during the pandemic years: plan around it for two years, because what was made then is not ready when you think it is, and the three doors of the extended plays are the plan. And the colour it borrowed is the last thing the gatekeeper allows: every album keeps its own colour, but this one borrowed its shade from the posters on the wall — the yellow-and-pink of the ABBA years, worn until it was a stranger's colour, which is the only kind the dictionary admits. A colour borrowed in the dream is a colour you may keep; a colour invented is returned at the gate.

Of the Western Approaches

The dictionary is asked, weekly, to rule on dreams in which the sleeper is warned. The entry for the night of the lahar keeps two months of warnings that were dealt, card by card, one day at a time, and the board it is played on is a valley, and the lesson it files is that a warning is not a rescue — see Plate VII, the Night of the Four Flows, rule 13.1, where the association is ready in October and the rule does not require anyone to listen. This entry keeps the same night read the other way round: as a july fourteenth, two years after the first door opened, in which the thing that was warned about arrives not as mud but as a catalogue number, and the sleeper is not in the valley. The caring, in this reading, was the warning all along, and it was given in time.

The dictionary is also asked what to do with a line that arrives in the wrong register — a line the printer coughs up uninvited, in the wrong alphabet, for a day book no one asked it to write. The recipe shelf has one: Golden Horn Bread, with its long preamble, where the kitchen printer gives up the line about the boar and the proprietor files it under answers. The Keeper of Entry 214 files the pinned line the same way: uncorrected, word for word, under the heading the colour it borrowed, because a line found in the wrong alphabet is still a line, and a line still has to be kept somewhere. The drawer below the recipe shelf holds a naturalist's field notes on Diocletian, kept under the bread's long preamble, where the printer coughs up a second line in the wrong alphabet for the day book — a line about a majority and a mechanised creativeness — and the keeper files it the same way: uncorrected, word for word, under the only voluntary abdication of the Roman throne.

Crossings

To dream of a recorded song with a date on it and a label on it is to dream the shelf that keeps every recorded song filed by its entry number: the encyclopaedia that gave Stranger in Moscow, Entry SM-1995/05, its six isolated figures and its twenty-country chart. To dream of a studio album's field notes, of a habitat and its fledglings, is to dream Notebook No. 47 on The Cookbook, which the dictionary keeps in mourning ink and reads beside this entry whenever a sleeper reports a debut that arrived wearing someone else's colour on purpose. And to dream of the whole shelf standing, with its front page and its file drawers, is to dream the front of the house, which the dictionary admits even this dream is worth one entry.

Leave the control room door open. Keep the two chairs where they are. If the posters on the wall start to answer, answer in lowercase.
