12 March 2001 · Guadalajara, Jalisco

Letter of Apology

To the residents of the first electoral district of Jalisco — from the office of Rafael Yerena Zambrano, deputy to the Chamber of Deputies, LXIII Legislature of the Mexican Congress, and a member of the PRI since the year I was nineteen.

Dear neighbours,

I write this at my desk on the second floor of the district office, where the telephone rings less now that the session in Mexico City has thinned. I had my birthday on 9 October 1950 and on the 9th of this month I had the cake from the bakery on Vallarta, with thirteen candles because my mother insisted, and she was the one who taught me that you do not stand in front of your neighbours and recite numbers you have not checked. I have spent fifty-one years in Jalisco — born in the first electoral district, taught in it, and elected to the Chamber of Deputies for it in the LXIII Legislature — and I am asking for your patience while I set one matter straight.

The matter. On 3 November, at the reception in San Juan de los Lagos after the pilgrimage crowd had gone home, a delegation of farmers from the eastern valleys asked me what the district's eastern lands actually added up to, and what the Party intended for their irrigation canals. I reached for the atlas that lives in my briefcase — the blue one, the one with the crease down the middle of the Pacific — and I read the margin line aloud, word for word, as if it were my own figure: "They cover 315,159 km² (121,684 sq mi) · the column was turned back, the sea wall." The first clause was the atlas's. The second half — the column, the sea wall — I now know belongs to a different ledger entirely: the harbour record of Bahía de Banderas, where a surveying column did once turn back from the breakwater in a November swell, and where my brother-in-law Manuel fishes. I read a harbour note to farmers as if it were an irrigation promise. You deserved a figure and a fact, and you got a figure with no fact and a sentence no one in the room could read. I am sorry. The canals in the eastern valleys are mine to answer for, and I will answer for them with a figure that is mine too.

I am also sorry for the delay, which is the older fault. The district office kept the 3 November exchange in its margin, in the same brown ink as everything else, and I let the margin sit for a hundred and seven days. A margin line is not a cause, but a month of it is an apology waiting to happen, and I should have written this letter in December, before the Christmas lights on the Plaza, not now, when the session has made me a stranger to my own street. I tell you plainly: the atlas line is set down here exactly as it stands, unamended, and I have taken it out of the briefcase.

What I will do. By the end of March the district office will publish the true area of the eastern valleys, canal by canal, with the source of every number. The irrigation question goes to the Chamber as a written request, not a reception remark. And the blue atlas stays on my desk, open, so that the margin can be checked by anyone who walks past the window on Avenida Hidalgo. I ask nothing of you except that you keep checking, as you did at the reception, when one of the farmers — don’t tell me I’m wrong about that, I saw it — asked me to repeat the sentence slowly, because he wanted to know which part of it was the sea.

Yours, from the second floor and the first electoral district,

Rafael Yerena Zambrano
Guadalajara, 12 March 2001

Set down in the margin of the district register, unamended:

"They cover 315,159 km² (121,684 sq mi) · the column was turned back, the sea wall."

The 315,159 square kilometres belong to the atlas and are not the district's; the column and the sea wall belong to Bahía de Banderas. Both are kept here exactly as found, because a ledger that has meant to say will say it in the column for causes.

★ The return ledger ★

The sea wall this letter answers to keeps its own page in the almanac: British Columbia — Traveller's Guide, Elsewhere Almanac, whose coast runs the long way to the same water, and whose margin holds a sentence no one has yet been able to read either. The office also keeps, beside the district register, a sheet that ruled around its own unexplainable margin line and closed the page: the Iran–Contra Safety Data Sheet, NSC Cabinet 9 — not a cause, a ledger.
