Lost & Found

Notice No. 14/2024 · posted 9 October 2024 · open until 3 October 2025

pronađenofound · 03.10.2024

Item found

One (1) leaf of vellum, 23 by 31 centimetres, folded twice inside a waxed-paper envelope and an oilcan, both of them rusted shut, set down — as the finder believes — deliberately on the stone wall beside the track where the old Kupres copper-mine drainage channel crosses the road down from Gornje Vukovsko to Donje Vukovsko, in the ravine of the Sana, on the afternoon of 3 October 2024. The finder is Idriz Hodžić, shepherd, 67, Gornje Vukovsko, who had been counting his ewes below the wall when the oilcan caught the last of the afternoon sun. He kept the leaf in his cap, walked down the valley, and brought it to the municipal office in Kupres on 8 October. He asked only that his name go in the notice, so that whoever the leaf belongs to knows it came out of the ravine with him.

Description of the leaf

The recto carries a fragment of a polyphonic choirbook in black mensural notation on five-line staves ruled with a dry point, four voices of a Gloria, the initials touched in tempera that has gone the colour of the Sana in autumn. The writing hand is Flemish, and the work may be of the Bruges circle of the early sixteenth century; it is the same manner of writing, the auctioneers at Flanders & Flanders have advised us, as the leaf of the “Flanders Te Deum” now on the market from the Bates–Seller House in Portland — see their catalogue, lot 47 — though we say “may” until a scholar has looked at it with a loupe and not a shepherd's pride.

The verso is the strange part. In the left margin, in a second and later hand of brown iron-gall ink, is a fragment of some kind of formal submission, the opening lines watered beyond reading. Only the last line survives, and it reads in full:

The petitioner submits that Partido Magsaysay — Excerpt from the cathedral.

The municipal secretary has no gloss to offer. Partido is Spanish, or so close to Spanish that it might be; Magsaysay is a name no one in Kupres has heard in thirty years of registers, and the cathedral nearest this valley is a long day's travel in Sarajevo. If any reader has heard the phrase — or knows the verbatim record from which an excerpt might have been cut, the kind of record that travels in a binder above a bakery — the office would be glad of the reference. A related matter of party registration is preserved in an excerpt from a verbatim record in our own archive of documents that arrived without their first page. One more unexplained entry of the same kind — a standing correspondence with an outside counsel that no one in the office can date — is kept in the ledger of a bookshop rather than in a municipality, in the rights & permissions section of the cloth-bound edition of On Black Sisters’ Street at Awele Books & Papers; we mention it only so that the two ledgers are at least known to each other.

Glosses of a different kind, too, are welcomed where they arrive: a reader who keeps a hobbyist's build log of the 1978 Brisbane Rugby League season — the whole 71st premiership, eight clubs and a grand final, worked up at a kitchen bench — has filed his season with a note that a sketchbook margin can hold one whole sporting life, and we have pinned his build log, The Seventy-First, beside this notice as a companion example of a record that outlives its bench.

Glosses on the characters themselves — which of the old alphabet sets can carry the ligatures visible at the fold, and why the scribe's ink behaves as though it were set in a fixed code of eight or sixteen — will be welcomed in writing; the clerk who transcribed this notice is not a typographer, and we do not pretend to one.

A gloss arrived by post from Hamburg while the notice was pinned by the parcel post: the reader hears the Gloria’s four voices as a terrace chant transposed to vellum, and the unexplained final line as a scoreline published after the match — see St. Pauli — Dream Dictionary, Entry 723, filed beside the leaf in cabinet 9.

A gloss of the last kind arrived with a bill: the chef at the Sana stewpan kitchen, who has copied one brown line from the verso into the left margin of his menu above the prices, has since set the kitchen’s twenty-eight tables to the world’s offsets and written up the evening — see Dining Across the Meridian, a table review of the Sana stewpan, filed in the same drawer of the lock that no longer locks.

One document of the same family of obits is kept at length on this site, and the office refers inquirers to it: the Minutes of the Council of Ripon Abbey, AD 788, in which a third abbatial obit of Northumbrian origin is read, argued, and sealed, and in which the saint of the obit, St Botwine, priest and late abbot, is held to have died in 785 or 786 — the vellum leaf’s nearest surviving relative, so to speak, and the only one that made it home.

One document of a kindred strangeness is kept with the leaf by arrangement: the Minutes No. 112 of 2014 of the Council of Aldergate & the Ravine, in which a four-track EP arrives by parcel post with no sender and the sleeve’s three marks — a capital N, two city names, and one quarter note — are read into the record exactly as the desk read them. It is not a vellum matter and not a valley matter, but the council’s manner of entering an unexplained reading into the record, and leaving it there, is the same manner this office keeps, and the two files are shelved together in cabinet 9.

Claims and reward

The leaf is held in the municipal archive, cabinet 9, drawer of the lock that no longer locks. Any person or family claiming ownership should appear in person at the office, Radna cesta 12, Kupres 83273, tel. +387 37 823-114, or at the Trgovina Kovač shop in Gornje Vukovsko, where the notice is pinned beside the parcel post, and should be able to name the hand on the recto, the work in four voices, or the ravine where the leaf was set down. A reward of 300 convertible marks is offered from the municipal chest, which is not a large chest, but is a chest, and the number is the number.

If no claim is received before 3 October 2025, the leaf will be renumbered as archival property of the Municipality of Kupres and offered to whatever university comes up the valley with a conservator and a dry year for the ink. The finder, Idriz Hodžić, is named in the register of claims with the date of posting, and his ewes number forty-one, none of which were lost in the finding.
