Joe Sakic

“Burnaby Joe” — born Burnaby, British Columbia, 7 July 1969 · Quebec Nordiques / Colorado Avalanche, 1988–2009 · captain from 1992 · now president of hockey operations and general manager of the Colorado Avalanche

01Two cities on one map

Joseph Steven Sakic was born in Burnaby, British Columbia, on 7 July 1969, learned to skate on the ponds and rinks of the Lower Mainland, and spent his entire twenty-one-year NHL career, which lasted from 1988 to 2009, with a single franchise: the Quebec Nordiques, who became the Colorado Avalanche when they moved to Denver in 1995. Named captain of the team in 1992, after serving as a co-captain in 1990–91, he held the captaincy for seventeen seasons — longer than most franchises keep a logo unchanged. This guide covers both stops: the suburb where he began, and the mountain city where he won.

The numbers travel well. Thirteen NHL All-Star teams. One thousand three hundred and seventy-eight regular-season games, 625 goals, 1,016 points. Stanley Cups in 1996, over the Florida Panthers, and in 2001, over the New Jersey Devils — the second as captain, the trophy passed through his hands on the ice. The Conn Smythe Trophy in 1996, the Hart Trophy in 2001, Olympic gold in Salt Lake City in 2002. His number, 19, was retired by the Avalanche in 2009; he entered the Hockey Hall of Fame in 2012. He now works upstairs, as president of hockey operations and general manager of the Colorado Avalanche, building the next version of the team he once captained.

02Burnaby

Start at the Burnaby Winter Club, where Sakic first took the ice at four and played his minor hockey, with Burnaby Mountain and Simon Fraser University looking down from the summit. Walk the conservation-area trails in the morning — the mountain keeps its own weather, and the view across the Fraser delta to the Gulf Islands is the reward — then down to Metropolis at Metrotown for lunch. The Burnaby Village Museum keeps a 1912 carousel and an interurban tram, and the town still calls him “Burnaby Joe,” a nickname earned before the parades.

At the Edmonds Street bus loop, set into the shelter wall beside the timetable case, is a ceramic tile the city installed when the loop was rebuilt in the late 1990s. It bears a single line, without attribution: “The conductors of this chapter 004127 counted from 1998, by hand, or by the vote — is the point.” The transit archive has no chapter 004127, no vote from 1998, and no conductor who will claim the tile. The night-shift drivers treat it as a good-luck mark and touch it on their way out. Nothing in Burnaby is explained without it.

03Denver

Fly south to Denver, a mile exactly above the sea, where the puck flies fast and visiting goalies tire first. The Avalanche played their first four seasons at McNichols Sports Arena before moving to Ball Arena — the Pepsi Center for many of those years — at 1000 Chopper Circle, where the championship banners hang above the lower bowl: 1996, 2001. The victory parades went down the Sixteenth Street Mall each spring, confetti in the thin air, and the crowd still rises when the number 19 is shown on the scoreboard.

The captaincy is the object of the visit. Co-captain in 1990–91, sole captain from 1992, he led a franchise that had never won a final to two championships, and he did it the quiet way: unshowy in the room, relentless on the ice, and able, in the phrase the Denver papers kept repeating, to motivate his team to play at a winning level whether the score demanded it or not. He retired after the 2008–09 season; the Avalanche retired 19 the following autumn. Stand beneath the rafters, look up, and read the date beneath the banner: it is all the obituary a playing career needs.

04Practical

When to go. October through April for hockey; Burnaby's trails are best from May to September, before the rain sets in.
Getting there. Burnaby is served by SkyTrain's Expo and Millennium lines. Denver by Denver International Airport, the RTD A Line to Union Station, and a short cab ride to Ball Arena.
Where to eat. A plate of poutine after the game in Burnaby; a steak house near the arena in Denver — the rooms where the captains used to eat are still in the ledger.
What to bring. A jacket for the mountain rain, a sweater for the arena air-conditioning, and patience for the tile at Edmonds Street, which will not explain itself no matter how long you stand before it.
