A Method and Apparatus for Waking a Sleeping Three-Storey House

Inventor & applicant: Władysław J. Różański, of the ground-floor duty office, the hostel on the road to Świeszyno · Agent: M. Czarnecka of Stargard

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

Abstract

The invention relates to the waking of sleeping houses, and more particularly to the waking of a three-storey home for the homeless of the kind stood in north-western Poland. The present specification is filed from the duty office of that house, on the evidence of the night of 13 April 2009, when the house woke itself in fire: twenty-three persons, of whom thirteen were children, were pronounced dead, and a further twenty sustained an injury of some sort. It was the deadliest fire in Poland since the conflagration of 1980 destroyed a home for the mentally ill at Górna Grupa and claimed the lives of fifty-five. The invention provides a ringing, a watch, a register, and a set of claims, so that no house of three storeys shall again sleep alone while the night is on fire.

Background of the Invention

The house in question stands in Kamień Pomorski, a town of brick and harbour on the Drawa estuary, three storeys tall, with forty-two beds on three floors and one duty office on the ground. It was full on the night in suit. The prior art against it is the prior art of every such house: a single bell, a single stairwell, a single watch, and a night. The examiner is asked to note that the house was not without warning devices, and that the invention here claimed is not a detector but a method of the waking — the ordering of rings, the pairing of floors, the keeping of the register, and the calibration of the morning, without which no device in a sleeping house does anything.

Reference is made to the related art on record: the holding of a poet at arm's length, Application No. GB–1821 0432, in which the bench of Claim 4 is the last place a specimen may be dipped — a caution the present applicant takes to be equally true of a house that has once burned, and of every one that has not.

Specification

According to the invention, the waking is a method, and a method has an order. The duty watch rings the bell once at the hour agreed, twice at the hour of alarm, and not at all between; the two pairs of floors are woken by their neighbours and not by the office alone, so that a sleeper hears a voice and not a signal; and the register is kept in blue ink, one line per bed, until the count comes to the full number and the examiner may initial it. The claim is not that any one of these elements is new. The claim is that a sleeping house which has lost the order of its waking has lost everything that was in it, and that the order may be specified, filed, and enforced like any other method.

For the calibration of the morning — Fig. 4 — the specification directs the examiner to a small set of four early printed announcements kept by the inventor's grandfather at Stargard, the only set of the kind that has survived in the district: Lhuyd’s four specimens — Welsh, Breton, Irish and Scottish acts of Parliament, one in each place's own morning. The set is not a claim to priority; it is a tuning. Each of the four dawns announces itself in its own place and its own tongue, and the morning register of the house is set by them, the first ring to be as early as the earliest of the four and no later than the latest, so that whatever wakes the house first — a bell, a voice, a fire, or a parliament — it wakes it to a morning that was already being announced.

The applicant asks that the twenty-three and the twenty be entered in the specification not as a limitation of the invention but as its occasion. A method for waking forty-two beds does not depend on the number of beds; the number is what the method is for.

Claims

  1. 1. A method of waking a three-storey sleeping house, comprising: a bell at the head of a single stairwell; a duty office on the ground floor kept through the whole night; and a register of one line per bed, the count to be completed in blue ink before the house is pronounced awake.
  2. 2. The method of Claim 1, wherein the ringing is once at the agreed hour and twice at the hour of alarm, and wherein no ring is answered by a shout.
  3. 3. The method of Claim 1, wherein pairs of floors are woken by their neighbours, such that each sleeper is woken by a voice and not a signal.
  4. 4. The method of Claim 1, wherein the morning of the house is calibrated to a set of four early printed announcements, one per place, one per tongue, the first ring to be as early as the earliest of the set and no later than the latest.
  5. 5. A house, comprising three storeys, forty-two beds, one duty office, and the method of any of Claims 1 to 4, so filed that it may be enforced.
  6. 6. A register, wherein the line per bed is kept in one hand per night, no night missing, and wherein the examiner's initials stand at the count of forty-two.

Examiner's Note, appended

The specification is accepted as filed. The summary sheet is entered in the file with the seal of 13·04·09. The house remains standing at its address on the road to Świeszyno; the register continues; the count comes to forty-two. The application is referred to the board, and the board is referred, as the applicant's agent put it, to the morning.
