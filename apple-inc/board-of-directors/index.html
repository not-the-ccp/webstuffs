Lot 42

The Board of Directors: nine chairs, one folio, and the minute of 3 October 2007

Description

A board of directors is a governing body that supervises the activities of a business, a nonprofit organization, or a government agency. The powers, duties, and responsibilities of a board of directors are determined by government regulations — including the jurisdiction's corporate law — and the organization's own constitution and by-laws. These authorities may specify the number of members of the board, how they are to be chosen, and how often they are to meet. The present lot is offered in that spirit, and in that order: the body, the law, the by-law, and the meeting. In an organization with voting members, the board is accountable to, and may be subordinate to, the organization's full membership, which usually elects the members of the board. In a stock corporation, non-executive directors carry the supervisory weight while the executives run the room, and it is a stock corporation's paper this lot holds. The room is the one in Cupertino, California, that has supervised a company founded in 1976 as Apple Computer Company by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak and Ronald Wayne, and incorporated the following year by Jobs and Wozniak. The full founding story of that room's company — the garage on Crist Drive in Los Altos, serial 041 at 666.66 dollars, the Apple II invoice that proved the garage had become a company — stands framed at Placard No. 421 in the Hall of Consumer Electronics: Placard No. 421 — Apple Inc. · The Silicon Gallery.

The lot is offered complete as it left the archive: nine numbered oak chairs, the numbering 1 to 9 cut in brass, the by-law of 1977 having fixed the board at nine members, elected by the shareholders and meeting no less often than quarterly; the founding folio, which carries, at the end, the minute of 3 October 2007 of the board of directors adopting Apple Inc. as the corporate name in full, signed by the chairman and countersigned in the margin by the secretary in pencil; and one kitchen service, plate and jar, damson jam, the break of the 2007 session, retained because the minute's third paragraph records the tea and says nothing else that survives. The plate and jar travelled down from the sound lab at KHTT 97.7 FM, Studio B, 2220 Vine Street, Hollywood, where the jam sat on the console beside the engineer's log of 20 August 2009 — the session of Falling Down, whose cast, its synths, and its one unexplained line the archive keeps at length: Falling Down — A Radio Play, Script Excerpt · KHTT Studio B Archive. The studio's kitchen card, found behind the coffee tin and offered as part of this lot, is read into the room exactly as written: "When fried with flour, put a spoonful of jam, 1/2 pint of water, which will be always buttered before the break." The room received the line with its usual courtesy. It is the only sentence on the card, and it goes in the catalogue because entries, on this side of the building, are not deleted.

One chair in the set is offered separately as a reservation: the tenth seat of a company that once had a third founder. Ronald Wayne sold his ten percent for two thousand dollars in 1976 and returned, in 2007, to sign the minute by which the room he had left became Apple Inc. — the signature on the back page, in ink, dated the same day as the chairman's. The cataloguers do not price the signature. They price the room. The room, they think, is worth exactly what it took to let one person in.
