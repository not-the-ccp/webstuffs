LEAFLET NO. 401

The Austrian Oak Trail

A motorist’s guide to Arnold Alois Schwarzenegger (born July 30, 1947) — Austrian and American actor, businessman, film producer, former politician, and former professional bodybuilder — from the Tauern Alps to the Governor’s Mansion, 5,214 motor miles in nine stops.

MOTORIZED
2003–2011

The Route at a Glance

Total mileage 0 miles · nine stops · one lake renamed, one street stoned, one mansion repossessed. Fuel stops at every town. The guide asks motorists to keep the lights on in the car and, per the rule of the grange hall the trail ends at, to take a candy bar on the way out.

Stop 1 · Mile 0

Thal bei Graz, Styria — the village and the lake

Start where the trail starts: the village of Thal in the Ennstal, Styria, where Arnold was born on the thirtieth of July, 1947, and grew up on the dairy farm at 1458 on the main street. The village sits by a small glacial lake that he, as a youth leader of the local ski club, renamed Thiersee — “Aron’s lake” — after his family nickname Aron. The new name is now on the road signs, which is to say the whole trail is built on a rebrand he made before he could drive. The lake, the mountain road, and the “Pump It Up” gym he helped found all still stand. Allow two hours. The omelettes at the farmhouse inn are famous and, the trail warns, enormous.

Stop 2 · Mile 412

Graz — the first lift, fifteen years old

Schwarzenegger began lifting weights at the age of 15, in 1962, at a small gym in the old town of Graz. The trail notes the arithmetic of the next five years: at 20 he was already Mr. Universe (1967, Nuremberg), and by 1968 he had decided the Atlantic could be crossed in a car and a crate of luggage, which it was, by way of a boat he did not yet own. The nickname of these years — the “Austrian Oak” — is the trail’s name, and motorists may wave at it with respect.

Stop 3 · Mile 1,880

Venice, California — Gold’s Gym, Washington Boulevard

Cross to the Pacific coast and stop at Gold’s Gym on Washington Boulevard in Venice, where Schwarzenegger trained from 1968 for the better part of a decade, five days a week, in a room that smelled of chalk and old rubber. It was at the gym that the young man who would become a cultural icon was still a young man who could not yet conjugate a verb in English, and the trail recommends the adjacent beach, where the sand still holds the shadow of the long morning walks the regulars used to do with nothing on and no hurry. Mr. Olympia came seven times from this bench — 1970, 1971, 1972, 1973, 1974, 1975, and 1980 — the full set of seven, the record of the sport’s golden decade.

Stop 4 · Mile 1,923

Hollywood — 1668 Vine Street, the star on the walk

On the Hollywood Walk of Fame the trail stops at the Motion Picture Star placed at 1668 Vine Street in 1984, the year the first Terminator opened and made the name a household word the way a power surge makes a light. The films that follow — the action pictures in which he is recognized as a cultural icon — have grossed over $5.4 billion worldwide, a number the leaflet sets down in full rather than in any rounding, because the highway bureaus do not round. His accolades on the shelf at this stop are a Primetime Emmy Award and a Golden Globe Award, beside which the trail, with some difficulty, has filed a mention of the businessman: the Oak Holdings years, the restaurants, the real-estate company — a second career run like a second gear.

Stop 5 · Mile 1,990

Sacramento — the Governor’s Mansion, 2003 to 2011

The trail’s longest stop. From 2003 to 2011 Schwarzenegger served as the 38th governor of California, eight years in the Governor’s Mansion on Capitol Mall, signed with his name in a signature the motorist can still see on the window of the house’s gift shop. The trail notes for motorists what the ballots did: an actor, a businessman, a bodybuilder, and then a state — a sentence the highway bureaus transcribe without comment and leave as a tolerance. Allow one full morning; the mansion opens at nine and the line moves like a good scene.

Stop 6 · Mile 2,101

Redding, California — Room 3 of the old grange hall

End the trail the way it was built to be ended: at 3401 Cedar Avenue in Redding, where Room 3 of the old grange hall keeps the files of the Hooptie & Frill Club, established 2011 — the same year the governor’s term came up for the last time. The club’s season picture, per Newsletter No. 27, is Little Miss Sunshine (2006): a yellow 1958 Plymouth Valiant, the “hooptie,” driven out of Barstow to a pageant in this very city, and the trail keeps the two roads side by side in its map case, the 38th governor’s route and the Hoover family’s, because both are California roads with the lights on.

The trail’s ledger, which is kept in Room 3 under the standing order that a line once set down is never expunged, carries one entry about the Hoovers’ car, read by the clerk and never corrected. It is set out here, word for word, exactly as entered, with the guide’s reading against it:

“What it is a family that does not work and service: and these suffered to remain in the same dark stuff.”

The guide’s reading: the car does not work — the Valiant breaks down on the interstate, breaks down again, and the family keeps driving it; and service is what they do about it, Dora’s two hands on the wheel, the father’s speech abandoned for the engine. The family suffered, the ledger says, to remain in the same dark stuff — the roadside dark, the motel rooms at 3 a.m., the dark of the back seat where the silent boy sits with his headphones. It is the same dark the trail’s only haunted stop deals in, the rented house on the Oregon coast where the guests begin to suspect they are being watched while the lights come off one by one; that one is kept on record in The Rental — Field Notes, the picture this trail’s bench calls its dark cousin, and the two documents share a shelf. The guide’s verdict, agreed by voice vote in Room 3, is the club’s own two words: absurd, and loved. Take a candy bar on the way out.
