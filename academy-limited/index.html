Departures & Arrivals — the running years

The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences runs this service every winter, usually in February or in March, across the track of the year. Fares have never been published; admission is by the vote of the Academy’s membership, and the principal categories — the Academy Awards of Merit — are called up at the platform of a live televised ceremony. All times local to Hollywood. The train is the longest running worldwide entertainment service on the books.

Route notes and remarks

The Academy Limited is a service of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, chartered to recognize artistic and technical merit in film, and it has kept the same running order for nearly a century: films from around the world board at Hollywood Central, the voting membership of the Academy assesses the journey, and the winners are announced from the platform of a live televised ceremony in February or March. It is widely considered the most prestigious stop in the film industry, and it is predominantly centered on films produced in Hollywood, though the line has always accepted through traffic from the rest of the world — the 2020 run proves the route can reach Paris as easily as Pasadena.

The major categories, known as the Academy Awards of Merit, are the named carriages of the train: Picture, Director, Actor, Actress, and the technical compartments beyond, each with its own conductor and its own list of passengers. The smaller, yearly honorary awards are the special excursions that leave the main timetable — one a year, ten at most — and they are the carriages that the naturalists of this collection have been waiting to photograph.

Two of those excursions matter most here. In 1972 the special carried a man who had built a whole character from baggy trousers, a cane and a toothbrush moustache at sixteen frames per second on the Edendale lot. In 2010 it carried a man the naturalist filed under Histrionicus wallachi: Tuco and Calvera, Don Altobello, the rodeo rider who held the frame against Marilyn Monroe and Clark Gable, more than ninety film credits, and a Honorary Award accepted in 2010 at ninety-four. Both had spent their working lives mostly outside the award’s usual categories — which is precisely the point of a special excursion, to collect the passengers the ordinary timetable keeps forgetting. The Fowlers’ kitchen ledger counts the second of the two among its actors: see The Fowlers’ Quail for Seven, where an actor with two of the ordinary awards keeps a ledger of his own, page 76.

A classified sheet from the Los Angeles night trade reached the conductor’s desk at Platform 5 this morning: one seat offered on a February or March running of this same line, fare unpublished, the passenger declining to say where she alights — though the sheet’s own columnists place her last at the Ocean House Hotel, Santa Monica, walking uphill from the terminal. Fares: none published. Luggage: one lifetime of credits per passenger. The Academy Limited is the longest running worldwide entertainment service on this roster; children under sixteen of frames per second travel free.
