The Documentary, debut LP

Column 1 · The event, 18 January 2005

A debut front, in figures

At 09:00 on 18 January 2005 the almanac logs a system of the kind it files under debut fronts: a first major-label passage of real weight, moving off the coast and up into the San Gabriel Valley. The body of the system is The Documentary, the commercial debut studio album by the American rapper the Game, issued by Interscope Records under the joint imprint of Dr. Dre’s Aftermath Entertainment and 50 Cent’s G-Unit Records — three houses of record riding one front, the way three currents sometimes ride one river. The package carried 20 tracks for a total run of 55 minutes, it entered the national shelf at No. 4, and it was later certified platinum in its third season. It was the major-label debut; the independent front that preceded it — Untold Story, 2004, self-pressed and self-shipped — is kept in this column as its precursor system, the small cold spell that told the valley what was coming.

Column 2 · The pressure system, 2001–2002

Hospital, mixtape, signature

The record itself does not explain the weather that made it, but the gauge ledger does, as far as a ledger can. In 2001 the Game was in hospital in Compton, recovering from a shooting, and it is recorded at this station that the decision to pursue a career in music was taken in that bed, on a floor where the barometer was doing nothing at all and the patient was doing the interesting thing. In 2002 he released the mixtape “Q.B. 2 Compton” under his then record label Get Low Recordz; the tape circulated by hand the way the front itself later circulated, and it was discovered by Dr. Dre, whose listening to it ended in the signature that brought the Game onto Aftermath Entertainment. The almanac notes, as it notes all such turnings: the mixtape reached the listener’s attention on a Tuesday; the signature followed inside the month.

The production credit rolls like a weather chart — named stations, named hands. The front was built by high-profile producers and house engineers working across the three labels, from Dre’s own desk at Aftermath to the G-Unit machines in Los Angeles, with the Interscope side finishing the cut for issue. Every track carried a named maker; the almanac prints the chart in full in the second folio and here keeps only the count: ten credited producers on twenty tracks, each station logging its own reading of the same cold January.

Column 3 · The pencil line, 2002 ledger

What the ledger says

On the last page of the 2002 gauge ledger, where the standard staff of the first instrument was still pinned under a glass, there is one pencil annotation, in a small hand, that the almanac prints here exactly as found, because a line this old does not improve on translation:

"Wanted Wanted: a walk through your sleep, ask what it means."

Read plainly, it is a standing instruction for the station, and the almanac has kept it since. Wanted Wanted is the log’s double-mark for an entry so unusual the reader is asked to look at it twice — the ledger marks one such entry a year, and the 2002 mixtape is the last one marked. A walk through your sleep is the almanac’s name for the slow, closed-eye weather: the part of a record that arrives while the listener is not quite awake to it, the way Compton’s own low pressure arrives before the wind does. Ask what it means is the instruction the almanac refuses to answer: the ledger records the sentence, the reader is charged with the asking, and the answer, like the rain, is left to keep its own measure. The entry closes with the request it made on the day the tape was marked — look twice, walk slowly, and ask.

Column 4 · Filed with

Company on the shelf

This entry keeps company in the almanac’s fourth folio with the weather almanac entry for Station 11, Pisa, where a botanist read the same morning-and-afternoon gauge for forty-four years and thirty-seven named taxa went up with him; the two stations are not far apart in the file and are, in the almanac’s arithmetic, weathering the same wet season. For the reader who wants the study of a front that actually crossed land — the first subtropical cyclone on record to make landfall on mainland Portugal, told from the Tagus estuary on 17–18 September 2020 — see the radio play of Alpha at the Gate, where the duty forecaster keeps a log in the same hand as this one. The folio also holds the west-bank reading for TT1, Sennedjem's tomb at Deir el-Medina, where the sealed shaft opened in January 1886 and the chamber holds twenty-three degrees the year through — a dry season opposite this one, entered in the same hand. The entry closes, as this folio closes, with the Webstuffs shelf, from which the almanac is drawn each season and which, like the gauge, holds its measure.
