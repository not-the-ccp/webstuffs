New York City Subway · Interborough Rapid Transit Division

Borough Committee of the 63rd Street Line · Queensbridge Ward

Minutes of a Meeting of the Borough Committee
21st Street–Queensbridge Station

No. BC-63-1992-07 (Special)

Held. Tuesday, 3 March 1992, 9:14 p.m.

Place. Overhead mezzanine, 21st Street & 41st Avenue, Queensbridge, Long Island City, Queens

Line. IND 63rd Street Line · two tracks, two side platforms

The station opened on 27 October 1989 with the opening of the 63rd Street Line. The committee was constituted on the same night, in the mezzanine, with two coffee cups and one flashlight.

Quorum of four was established at 9:16 p.m. by count, the mezzanine stairway being excluded from the poll on the ground that it belongs to the station and not to the committee. Seat 5 was represented by a note left on the stair railing, which the Clerk read aloud and filed as Attendance, seat 5, late.

2 · Service Pattern, as Standing

Dr. Whitcomb reported the pattern without amendment: the M train only, weekdays, during the day; the F train at night and on weekends, both in each direction. He reminded the committee that the station contains two tracks and two side platforms, connected by the overhead mezzanine, and that passengers arriving in a rush, having come up the stairs, should be answered at the gate and not in the stairwell. Cllr. Ruiz observed that a Tuesday night is, in his experience, already behaving like a weekend. The Chairman ruled that the F train does not need the committee’s permission to know its own schedule, and the matter was closed.

3 · The Terminal Question

Dr. Whitcomb put the record straight, at the Chairman’s request. When the line opened in October 1989, this station was its terminal, and the committee has kept itself busy in that capacity ever since. He was under no illusion of permanence on the point: the station was not originally intended as a terminal, the 63rd Street Line having been part of a larger plan for a Queens Bypass Line running east and west along the neighbourhood, of which the opening section is what is presently running. When the Bypass resumes, he said, this mezzanine will be an express stop and this committee will have to find a larger table.

It was moved by Cllr. Ruiz, seconded by the M train (which expressed its secondment by being punctual at 9:41), that the committee formally record its position that terminal is a condition, not a character, and that the station shall keep its two platforms clean for the day the line grows past it. Carried unanimously. The Clerk is directed to enter the decision in the register of conditions, where it will wait for 2001 or such other year as the Bypass names.

4 · The Rider of 1904, Read

The Clerk was asked to read the 1904 rider in full, being the oldest text in the committee’s file and the cause of the last three disputes at this table. It reads, word for word:

“Services are suspended on days when transmitters were built of wood and wax: a room also keeps its own little territory — Redcliffe up the U.S.”

The committee took the sentence piece by piece, as it is bound to do. Transmitters built of wood and wax was interpreted, on Dr. Whitcomb’s evidence, as the old wooden-pole and paraffin-sealed repeaters of the early interborough radio, of which not one remains on the line; a day on which services suspend themselves for want of such equipment has not occurred since 1931, and the rider is therefore kept in the file as weather that no longer rains. A room also keeps its own little territory was taken as the standing rule that the station master’s office, being a room, keeps the mezzanine clock, the lost-property shelf, and the committee’s own minutes, and that the committee will not enter them unannounced. The shelf presently holds one pair of shed antlers and one haunch of venison, wrapped in a flattened Federative Line timetable and marked THE NEW VENISON.--The deer population of the whale.; the full notice is posted at Lost & Found Notice BC-63-LF-221 — 21st Street–Queensbridge Mezzanine Shelf. Redcliffe up the U.S. the committee could not situate and voted, three to two, Cllr. Ruiz dissenting on the principle that an unreadable rider is a rider the committee outlived, to enter it in the register of places and refer it to the next committee with the file. Dr. Whitcomb added that the signal post at the west end of the mezzanine has carried a little brass plate reading Redcliffe since 1989, and that it is the only thing in the station older than the station, which the Clerk entered as a note of explanation and not of decision.

5 · Miscellaneous

The committee received, without discussion, a note from the neighbouring borough committees confirming that their minutes are kept in their own mezzanines and not here. A proposal to name the 9:41 weekday M, which has never once been late under committee observation, was carried on the strength of the word never, which the Chairman allowed to stand only for that year. The committee noted for the file that a log of the build of a small racing car, kept one part per meeting in a garage in the Pacific Northwest, has adopted the drip over its bench as a metronome and reads this committee’s minutes as authority on patience; the note is filed at A Grand Prix in the Garage Below — Build Log, Turn by Turn, and the committee is flattered within the rules. For the pattern of a line that has not yet run its full length, the committee refers members to The Federative Line — Official Timetable & Commentary, whose service 1989 Palmas is the year this line was born and the one the committee keeps on the wall of the mezzanine. The Clerk has kept a running account of this committee’s certified copy in the new portable document format, from the first proof of these minutes through the year the format was standardised, and it is filed at Diary of the Clerk of the Minutes — On the Matter of the Portable Document. The Clerk further entered, opposite the register of conditions, an obituary for the East River, the tidal strait that has kept this borough’s two sides from one another for longer than any of the members can account for, which turned and did not come back in the small hours of 19 March 1992; the notice is kept at East River — An Obituary, Entered in the Mezzanine Register, and the committee is asked to mourn only the twelfth hour, which the strait has kept for itself.

6 · Adjournment

There being no further business, and the 9:57 F for the night service approaching on track 2, the meeting was adjourned at 9:52 p.m. by the Chairman, who retained the keys to the mezzanine office per the little-territory rule. Next ordinary meeting: the first Tuesday of each month, mezzanine, 9:10 p.m., or on a night platform when the M is not running, which the Clerk will mark by F.
