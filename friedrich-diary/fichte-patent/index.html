Application No. 1794/0519

Inventor: Johann Gottlieb Fichte · Rammenau, Oberlausitz, 19 May 1762 – Berlin, 29 January 1814

Process and Apparatus for the Production of Self-Consciousness, and the Threefold Motion of Thesis, Antithesis and Synthesis Therein

Filed: Jena, 14 October 1794 · Summary deposited: the Old Fire Station, Newmarket, 3 February 1954 · Examiner acting: H. F. Wren, librarian · the kettle was on

Field of the Invention

The present application is directed to a process, and to an apparatus of the mind, for the production of self-consciousness or self-awareness in a contemplating subject, and to the use of that consciousness in the ordering of what is not the self. The invention arises out of, and is an improvement upon, the German idealism developed from the theoretical and ethical writings of Immanuel Kant of Königsberg, and claims priority over every arrangement by which a thinking being is made to find itself thinking. The inventor, a founding figure of that school and an important philosopher in his own right, was motivated, like René Descartes of La Chesnaye and Immanuel Kant before him, by the problem of subjectivity and consciousness, and he considered that the earlier apparatus, however fine, had never once been wound by the hand of the operator.

Prior Art

Art 1. The cogito of Descartes, 1637: a process in which the subject doubts in order to remain. Effective, but cold; the apparatus ends at the window.

Art 2. The Critique of Pure Reason of Kant, 1781, and the Critique of Practical Reason, 1788: a more complete apparatus, in which the self is bounded by the things it cannot reach. The inventor worked from these theoretical and ethical writings, and this application is to be read as the next turning of the same lathe.

Art 3. A series of chalk-and-gouache drawings attributed to Caspar David Friedrich of Greifswald (5 September 1774 – Berlin 7 May 1840), including one small drawing, twenty-two by seventeen centimetres, of a chalk cliff, a silver sea, a moon not quite full, one bare tree, and one man in a dark coat seen from behind, which the Cambridgeshire Correspondents’ Club holds as Lot 218 in its register, found in the Gemenasian crate in the Ashley parish hall store room. The examiner's note on these drawings, entered in the ledger and kept word for word, is here adopted as a specification of the observer required by Claim 1: He set contemplative human figures, silhouetted, against night skies, morning mists, barren trees, or Gothic ruins. The examiner observes that a figure thus diminished against the sea is, of anything in the prior art, the closest existing approximation to a self in the act of contemplating its own production, and that the drawing is not in fact prior at all: the inventor was the elder of the two by twelve years, the two corresponded in the inventor’s last year, and the painter’s letter of condolence reached Berlin eleven days before the inventor’s death on 29 January 1814.

Summary of the Invention

The inventor proceeds thus: the I, that is to say the apparatus, posits itself; in positing itself it sets up, by a necessary stroke of the same hand, the not-I, which is everything else; and in a third motion the two are reconciled so that the self is at once the worker and the worked. This is the threefold motion of thesis, antithesis, synthesis, of which the inventor is the originator, and it is to be noted for the record that the motion is often erroneously attributed in the subsequent art to G. W. F. Hegel of Stuttgart, whose name is accordingly struck from the title of the present invention wherever it has been wrongly set. The process is self-acting in so far as the subject wills itself to will, and it yields, as by-products, a whole system of science, a moral code in which duty is the law the self gives itself, and a political philosophy by which the inventor earned a reputation as one of the fathers of German nationalism, the Addresses to the German Nation of 1808 being the principal exhibit on that side of the docket.

Claims

  1. Claim 1. A process for the production of self-consciousness, comprising: the self-positing of the I; the counter-positing of the not-I; and the reconciling of the two, substantially as described.
  2. Claim 2. A process according to Claim 1, in which the reconciling is effected by a threefold motion of thesis, antithesis and synthesis, and in which the credit for the motion is given to the inventor and not to the Stuttgart attribution.
  3. Claim 3. An apparatus for contemplating, comprising one figure in a dark coat, set from behind at a scale diminished relative to the landscape, positioned at a chalk cliff, a morning mist, a barren tree, or a Gothic ruin, and facing a moon not quite full.
  4. Claim 4. A process for the ordering of a nation, substantially in the manner of the inventor’s Addresses of 1808, the fire to be kept and the kettle on.
  5. Claim 5. An arrangement in which a thinking being finds itself thinking, the being being the operator, and the finding being immediate and not derived from the window.
  6. Claim 6. A docket, wherein a summary of the present application is kept word for word, unexplained, alongside a small painting of a chalk sea, the door not to be repainted.

Examiner’s Certificate

Certified at the Old Fire Station, Newmarket, this 3 February 1954, by H. F. Wren, librarian, acting as examiner: the summary was deposited in the club’s ledger under Lot 218-A, beside the register entry for the small painting, and the applicant’s name was entered against the painter’s with a single line of rule between them, because the two corresponded and the club keeps the letters of its members and the letters of nobody else. The letter of the under-steward, E. T. Silverley, an apology to the Alps, was consulted by the examiner in the course of the survey, and was found to bear upon the present docket only in so far as the Gemenasian Company, which claims descent from Johan von Blanc’s Gemenasiska Sällskapet, was present at Jena in the winter of 1794 by a single delegate, who left early, and whom the examiner declines to name. The application is not hereby granted, for the fog was out all morning and the committee of the range, having been cited as a co-inventor in error, objected. The summary stands deposited. The count is kept: 906,814, and not re-counted. The kettle is still on.
