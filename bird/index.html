IN MEMORIAM

Bird (Aves)

1998 — 2024 · of the household above the old bakery, Magsaysay Avenue, Naga City, Camarines Sur

In lieu of flowers, leave a kernel of millet on the rigging, or a line in the guestbook below. The household reads everything twice. The rule on the first page of the ledger is whatever is voided, bake, and Card 314 has been filed accordingly. · The club meets at seven; the fans turn slow; bring nothing at all, if that is what you have. · In lieu of flowers, leave a kernel of millet on the rigging, or a line in the guestbook below. ·

Bird, of the class Aves, died on the second Sunday of November, 2024, at the kitchen above the old bakery on Magsaysay Avenue, in the yard of the window where she had been perched since March of 1998. The class Aves is the class of birds, and birds are vertebrate animals characterised by feathers, toothless beaked jaws, the laying of hard-shelled eggs, a high metabolic rate, a four-chambered heart, and a strong yet lightweight skeleton. Bird had every one of these on file. She was warm-blooded; the household’s thermometer has never once disagreed, and the entry for her line in the ledger is simply warm, always warm. She lived here, in this world, which her class inhabits worldwide, and the family wishes the record to show that she was counted as one of its own before she was counted as anything else.

Her order was the passerines, the “perching” birds, and the record is kept at the shelf like this: there are over 11,000 living species of birds, classified in 44 orders, and more than half of them are of her order. The family is proud that she perched, and proud of the arithmetic, and has asked that both be printed. Her world ranged, by the yardstick of the window, from the 5.5 cm (2.2 in) bee hummingbird to the 2.8 m (9 ft 2 in) common ostrich, and Bird sat somewhere in the middle of that span, which is to say she sat at the yardstick. Her wings, like all wings, are modified forelimbs; they are what gave her kind the ability to fly, although further development varied, as everything does, according to the life style of the species. Hers were used exactly twice on the record: once to the window in the January of 2003, and once to the water tank on the back of the bakery, and on both occasions she returned, which the household files under flights and not under departures.

She came in through the rigging window at the clubhouse above the old bakery in the first warm week of March, 1998, where the rigging is kept for its own beauty and the register is kept by a broadcaster the family speaks of in the third person, Salvio Fortuno — The Carrier Wave. She fed on millet from the fishmonger’s scale at the harbour, and on the Augusts, on crumb of the beet-and-blackstrap Carboniferous Coal Loaf baked at 325°F, the loaf the ledger’s coal section keeps warm, filed against the report in which her kind’s quieter loss — the great auk, one colony at a time, over four centuries — is read to the household in turn, The Extinction Event — Report, Class 8C, Coopers’ Lane School. Mabel E. Collins of Class 8C, whose school stands two doors down the avenue from the bakery, wrote her into the report’s field notes the way the class counts everything twice: once by the window, once by the radio, and Bird was the starling in both, the one the auks’ page was read over.

The ledger also keeps, pasted on the verso of the fishmonger’s almanac leaf, beside the great auk’s “June to August,” one more line in the same hand that is not Mabel’s and not her grandmother Etta’s. The line reads, word for word, and we set it here exactly as it stands:

The geologists, when they come out of the school two doors down the avenue: The Coalition Walk — A Family Recipe .

Ledger, kitchen shelf, verso of the almanac leaf. Pasted in by an unknown hand.

No one at the club has yet decoded it. Mr. Fortuno has said only that a recipe is a walk with an appetite, and that the line names both, and the file stays open at the clubhouse, which is where he has said it belongs, near the back room where the 2019 negotiations were held and walked, chair by chair, stop by stop down the avenue, in The Coalition Walk — A Magsaysay Avenue Itinerary. Bird sang loudest that spring, and the two facts are entered on the same page on purpose.

She is survived by the rigging and its register, by the spoon in grandmother Etta’s drawer, by the ledger and its first-page rule, and by Card 314 — the millet-and-oat cake, baked that December at 325°F, whatever is voided, bake. The Naga Listeners’ Club will read this obituary aloud at its next meeting, at seven, with the ceiling fans turning slow, and members are asked to bring a feather, a note, or nothing at all. The bird, too, was on the air, and the air keeps what it carries.
