1Identification

The material described in this sheet is Niall Aadya Mason, an English professional footballer who plays as a defender for Qatar SC. He was born on 10 January 1997 in Brighton, on the sea side of the town, in the year the depot archive records as a mild one for the sea wall. His first professional deal was signed at Villa, and that contract remains the document against which every later entry on this sheet is measured. The Player Movement Office treats the material as a standard defensive fixture rather than a foreign consignment — one country of birth, four countries of youth, and one present address in Al Rayyan — and issues this sheet from its own stationery under the same authority that files every squad number.

Product identifier. Niall Aadya Mason; sample form No. 3, Qatar SC. Recommended use. The defensive line, first team. Supplier. Villa, Molineux Road, the office on the ground floor that answers to a knock and not to an email. Emergency contact. The player himself, to be met at the end of the tunnel after the session; no defensive emergency has ever required closer access than that, and none is expected to. The material is not to be re-identified by jersey alone: four Masons have passed through this register, and the No. 3 is the one who was born by the sea.

2Hazards Identification

2.1 Chronic exposure

Chronic exposure is to the set piece. The material is drafted in the defensive line and is therefore, by occupation, in the box at every corner in both directions, which the Office classifies as a standing condition rather than an event. Handlers on the touchline are advised that a corner taken by a left-footed full-back of the material’s generation does not resolve; it repeats. No survey since the Brighton register has found a season in which the material was not on the end of one.

2.2 The loan hazard

The material is a proven loan. Two stops — Doncaster Rovers and Peterborough United — stand on the route in Section 7, and each of them took the material for the season and returned it better packed. A subject who has survived two loan spells at different altitudes of the league does not, as a rule, break at the third; but the Office records the hazard honestly, and notes that the return of a loaned defender is the only delivery the depot has ever refused to sign for, on the ground that the signature would be a promise.

2.3 The quiet hazard

The material does not talk in the way the register expects of a centre-back. Interview transcripts from the Villa years average fewer words than the corner-kick log, and the Office has had to develop a notation for it: where another defender would have supplied a quote, the file carries the annotation head turned, session continuing. Handlers who build a briefing from a silence may misread the silence as an absence. It is not. The register of quiet lives — the moths known from two specimens and still written up in full — is kept at the in memoriam notice for Prays liophaea, and the Office points to it whenever a scout insists that a small, still subject is a thin subject.

4First-Aid Measures

If the material is caught in the air and lands wrong, the first-aid protocol is the one the Villa medical room wrote in 2016 in a hand the Office still copies: ice, tape, and no mention of the corner in the next interview. The third clause has never once been honoured by a journalist and has therefore been honoured, every time, by the material, which is the point. If the material is exposed to a sustained spell of poor results, remove him from the press conference, not from the team; the register shows that defenders of this kind keep their shape in the dressing room long after it has gone in the building.

If a file on the material arrives without the name Aadya, the first-aid protocol is a correction in the Office’s own hand, in the same hand as every other sign — one office, one alphabet, no favour. The correction, once given, is a gift and must be kept. The same rule runs the sister sheet on the shelf, the safety data sheet SDS-NC-01 for the nasal consonant, and the two offices have never been seen to differ on a missing letter.

5Fire-Fighting Measures

The material is not flammable. A defender is, in the Office’s classification, closed at both ends: one end faces the opponent and holds it, and the other end faces the goal and holds it too. There is no vent, and therefore no flash point. The long fire of the Doha summer, when the stadium’s outside temperature exceeded the training ground’s tolerance for eleven consecutive days, took the morning sessions, the water trucks, and the patience of the physio; it did not take the material, and the season sheet for those eleven days records the same name at the same position on every line. No special extinguishing is required. The Office’s rule for a hot room is the one it has always used: open the far door first, and hold it with the hand that does not carry the file.

6Accidental Release Measures

A release of the material is simply a debut, and debits are not contained. The first release on record occurred at Villa, the material at eighteen, coming on in the last quarter of an away match against a team that had been winning; the release was small, local, and not noticed for forty minutes, which is exactly the behaviour the Office wants of a defender’s first appearance. The second release was the one the whole town of Brighton noticed, being his, and the register marks it without comment, the way the moths’ register marks the October that held the specimen. No containment is attempted; the Office’s standing order is that a released debut be left to travel and that no notice be posted, because posting the notice would be a second debut. One garbled radio account of a release of this kind — a broadcast that said nonsense at the whole room in a studio in Ann Arbor — survives as the excerpt at Studio 2, 21 March 1995, and the Office keeps it in the same file as the corner-kick log, for the same reason.

7Handling and Storage

Handle at the temperature of the dressing room. The material tolerates any surface temperature at which a team can train in the morning and fly on the evening, which is the only temperature it has ever required. Store the four youth components in the order of the route and do not separate the material from the name Aadya, which is standard issue and not for removal.

The transfer route. The route on which this material has travelled runs Brighton → Real Madrid → Al Sadd → Blackburn Rovers → Southampton → Villa → (loans: Doncaster Rovers, Peterborough United) → Qatar SC, and the Player Movement Office handles it under the depot instruction book, page 12. The book’s second rule, which the keeper has never annotated, reads word for word: “Stop 2 takes it to introduce itself in a case need?” The Office’s reading of the rule is this: on any route of two stops or more, the material does not carry its own introduction at the second stop; the stop takes it, and the stop introduces it — the press conference, the squad photograph, the number on the back — in a case need, meaning in the case that the arrival alone has failed to do the introducing, which the keeper evidently considered possible. In the material’s own route, Stop 2 was Al Sadd, Doha, 2011, and Doha took it and introduced it in the full, which is why the file for that season contains a photograph and no apology. The rule is retained unamended, with its question mark and its missing of, because the book is quoted word for word on everything, and a rule quoted badly is a rule the keeper has already lost.

Do not store near the wash. A defender stored within earshot of the sea comes out with an accent that no register has ever declined to admit — the material was born by the sea and the sea did its storage for the first ten Januarys. The Brighton component is therefore kept open on Thursdays, as the county museum requires, and the Office does not add commentary to it, for the same reason it does not annotate rule 2.

8Exposure Controls / Personal Protection

There is no occupational limit for a subject who is on the defensive line; the exposure in this sheet is the exposure in every match the material has played, and the season sheet for the Doha years shows the same figure at the same position week after week, which the Office reads as a control working, not a habit forming. The personal protection equipment is the line itself, used as the manual describes: held under the ball, behind the ball, and never abandoned under the influence of the counter-attack, per Section 5. Handlers attending the stadium are further advised to keep the file at no more than one arm’s length from the turnstile, Rule five of the depot book, and to meet any journalist who has finished asking questions on the far side of the room, with the head turned, session continuing. The material imposes no further protection on its handlers, and has not since Brighton; the Office has no jurisdiction to pursue a defender across a bow, which is the whole of its policy on exposure of that kind.

9Physical and Chemical Properties

Appearance. English, defender, No. 3; in speech, terse, per 2.3. Odour. None, except after the first corner of the second half, in which case the odour is described by the Office as the box. pH. Stability under storage. The material has held its position through four youth countries, one first professional deal, two loans, and one long move to Al Rayyan, and the Office expects the Doha years to pass the way they have been passing. The file’s one recorded property anomaly is the misfiling listed in the header — the persistent entry of the material as left-back, first eleven in the Villa yearbooks — which Section 1 has already corrected and will correct again.

10Stability and Reactivity

The material is stable on the line and reactive in the air, and the Office quotes the 2016 medical-room line word for word, because the Office quotes the medical room word for word on everything and has been doing so since the ledger’s first entry: “On the ground he is furniture; in the air he is the argument.” That is the ledger’s whole account of the material’s reactivity — the stillness at the feet, which is what 2.3 records from the press-conference side, and the contest above the feet, which is what 2.1 records from the corner side — and the Office’s reading of it has never changed: the two behaviours are one behaviour, held at the same height the whole time. A defensive line keeps its furniture in place; so does the register, and the two keepers keep them the same way, which is why the medical ledger and the Player Movement Office have never been seen to differ.

11Incompatible Materials

The forward line is incompatible with the material under one condition only, and that condition is the through ball: with a through ball behind him, a forward and a defender of this kind will not sit in the same room for longer than the length of the run, and the Office holds its files accordingly. Otherwise the material is compatible with every component in Section 3, including the four youth clubs, and the Office considers this the material’s best quality and will not be embarrassed by it. One recorded incompatibility stands on file: the left-back misfiling of Section 9. The yearbook entry was applied to the material in the presence of the whole Villa staff, in a ground-floor office that answers to a knock, and the register has never stopped being corrected in the margin; the yearbook has ruled lines where a retraction might have gone, and the lines have been filled, in the Office’s own hand, in the same hand as every other sign. The misfiling remains listed as incompatible with the register until the yearbook is reprinted, and the Office will remove the listing as soon as it is.

12Decomposition Products

Under load the material decomposes to a held line, which is stable and common and owes no explanation. Under a sustained load of the kind the Doha season puts on it, eleven hot days at a time per Section 5, it decomposes to a stillness at the feet, and the stillness holds, and the stand, which has held its shape through the whole summer, holds with it. The Office notes the decomposition product stillness for the record and observes that the product is the same one the material supplies at the press conference, unheated, which is why the two files — the season sheet and the interview log — have been read, by the keeper, as a single document since 2018. There are no further decomposition products known to the Office, and no secondary reactions; a defender does not explode, and the depot has had one long argument about a yearbook and no one has yet connected it to this section, which the Office considers a correct handling.

13Disposal Considerations

The material is not to be disposed of, being of one lot and one address at a time; disposal would be the last season without him in it, and the Office has no forms for that event. A retired number, however — the No. 3, should the Doha years finish — is to be brought in to the Office, which will ink it lightly, in the margin, as the Council of the register resolved, and not for reissue, for retirement. The loans, if the material discards the memory of them, are disposed of separately: Doncaster keeps its season, Peterborough keeps its season, and the depot keeps the signatures the two of them gave back. The material is reminded that the register admits no defender who discards a stop, all four youth stops standing as evidence. A Saturday cup draw once paired Doncaster Rovers and Southampton in the EFL Cup, and that roster rides the Bridge Line; the Office keeps its copy of the timetable at Chelsea F.C. — Bridge Line Timetable & Commentary, where the minutes sit at the whole room in a louder tone.

14Transport Information

Transported in the team, by the ordinary conveyances of the league and of the two loan leagues, and, in the case of the present address, by the direct flight that the Office has stopped counting.
