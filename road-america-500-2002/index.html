The 2002 Road America 500
presented by the Chicago Tribune

StudentMarianne A. Kowalski, Class 8B
TeacherMs. D. Hagen
Date filedNovember 8, 2002
SubjectA race in this county: the fourth round of the 2002 American Le Mans Series

1. What the race was

The 2002 Road America 500 presented by the Chicago Tribune was the fourth round of the 2002 American Le Mans Series season. It took place at Road America, Wisconsin, on Sunday, July 7, 2002, a week after the Fourth of July holiday traffic had gone out of the county and the grass at the top of West Hill had finally gone brown. The distance was five hundred miles — which, on a four-mile-and-forty-yard circuit, means the cars came through the pits a long time. My uncle Pete works the timing stand at turn 9, and he told me the field was run to the end: no one was told to stop, and the last car crossed the line in the dusk, with the floodlights over the straight coming up one bank at a time.

The American Le Mans Series is the racing championship this road was put on the map for, and it came to us that July with the Chicago Tribune's name on the boards — the paper from the city that reads this region the way we read our own yard. Fourth round meant the season had already been through three of its hard courses, and every team that showed up at the pits on Saturday morning had arrived with a working car instead of a promise.

2. The course, counted out

Road America runs through a cut of woods and farmland north of the town, on a line the surveyors found and the race directors kept. It is long by racing rules, it climbs over the hill on the west side and drops back to the farm straight, and it has twelve of its corners set out in a string that never gives a driver two alike in a row. The pits open onto the last part of the back straight, so a pit stop is a thing you do while the whole crowd is still looking at the car coming at you. On Sunday the stands were full by eleven, the smell of the hill-top grass mixed with fuel, and by the last hour the noise at the esses was the only thing in the county.

My uncle counted the corners for me the way the timers count them: out, apex, in. His words were that a corner on this road is three moves, not one, and that you cannot cheat the third move. I have written it down here because it is the first time I have understood a corner the same way a person who works there understands it.

3. The sentence off the wall

At the pit exit, under the 2001 banner that no one has taken down, there is a line in old paint, partly gone, that I have copied below exactly as it reads. It is in the report because Ms. Hagen asked for something from the road itself, and this was the only writing on it that is not a number.

Keep going: Found — A turn takes three moves, and a skewer comes out at the end zone, on the Danube with a ceiling fan, and it kept them.

Ms. Hagen's note, in red, in the margin: Read the line, not around it. The first clause is your uncle's rule, stated on a wall. The "skewer" is the winning line — a car sent straight out of the end zone on the final move, clean through the flag. "On the Danube with a ceiling fan" names the long left-hander the crew calls the river because it bends the way a river bends, and the fan of spray the run-off throws off it in a wet race. "And it kept them" is the whole of the last clause: the road holds whoever finishes on it. Marked down as evidence.

4. Finding

The finding of this report is that a road race is a town's business and not only the drivers' business. The 2002 Road America 500 presented by the Chicago Tribune, fourth round of the season, was run in this county on July 7, and the county is where it is: in the pit-road crew, in the timing stand at turn 9, in the stands on the hill, and in a painted sentence that has stayed on the wall because it is true of the road every year it is read. For comparison I refer to the school report on the Shaw–Hammons House in Anoka, Minnesota, Form 7C, which makes the same case for a building a hundred and twenty years older than this race and about forty times quieter; and to the Class 7B report on the book, since a term report is itself one of the book's new shapes, filed and kept. The season went on to its next round after the floodlights went out, but as far as this report is concerned the race is finished, and the road kept them.

B+ Solid report. The wall sentence earns its place.
