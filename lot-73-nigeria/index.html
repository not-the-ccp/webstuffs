The Lot

Nigeria, officially the Federal Republic of Nigeria, is a country in West Africa between the Sahel to the north and the Gulf of Guinea in the Atlantic Ocean to the south. It covers an area of 923,769 square kilometres (356,669 mi²), which the map in this lot accomplishes on a single sheet of 1987 Lagos-printed cartouches, folded to fit the flat envelope in which it travelled to us. With a population of more than 242 million, Nigeria is the most populous country in Africa, and the world’s sixth-most populous country, and the companion’s margin is wide enough, our cataloguer notes, to argue in pencil — the same margin the shop allows its cloth-bound editions. Nigeria borders Niger in the north, Chad in the northeast, Cameroon in the east, and Benin in the west; the four border lines are drawn in the companion’s second ink, red, over the map’s blue. Nigeria is a federal republic comprising 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory, where its capital, Abuja, is located, and the largest city in Nigeria by population is Lagos, one of the largest metropolitan areas in the world, and the city that keeps this shop, at 14 Awo Road, Oshodi, where the lot was prepared in the window seat between two parcels from Ghent.

Condition Report

The sheet is sound, the fold crisp at the equator and a little soft along the coast, as coastal things are. The companion has been read, argued in, and returned to the window seat, which is the best condition a book can be. One matter we are required to report: in the companion’s back pocket we found a loose leaf of a cook’s ledger, the first page of a recipe for a glaze, whose second line is written in another hand than the rest and which our cataloguer, being bound by the house rule that a reading once set down keeps its line, records here in full, without correction: “And I write with a little glaze, 1 pint of new milk.” The milk has not been identified. The glaze, applied to the shelf it was found on, has dried. Both are part of the lot, and both go to the highest bidder, exactly as found, in the flat envelope, no box — the lot is made to be carried, and the ledger is a ledger.

A Note on Deletions

We are asked, in writing, by readers of the letter to the editor on machine unlearning from the data stewardship office at 3400 N. Willamette, to say whether anything has been deleted from this lot. Nothing has. The map still names every state, the companion still keeps its empty margins, and the one line we could not correct has been kept, not struck, because the shop’s ledger runs in two inks and the red one is for borders, not for corrections. Where the Willamette office logs a deletion with a 412-line receipt, the Oshodi counter logs the deletion that was never made: it keeps this leaf, in its pocket, with the milks and the borders, and will sell the lot as found. Enquiries to the front of house, at the front page of Webstuffs, or to the counter, where someone, one day, will have the second half of the glaze story.
