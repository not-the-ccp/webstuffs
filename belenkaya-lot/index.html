Offered as a single lot, and to be sold as such, the board, the title, and the papers of Dina Vadimovna Belenkaya (Дина Вадимовна Беленькаа; born 22 December 1993, St. Petersburg), Russian chess player, commentator, Twitch streamer and YouTuber, holder of the title Woman Grandmaster, and — since 2026 — representative of France in the international calendar. The lot was consigned from a flat above a pawnbroker on the Fontanka, where it had been kept for four winters in a cedar box that opened with a small brass key, and it is offered with its four St. Petersburg titles, its two team seasons, its rating card at the peak of 2364, and the mother's coaching board from which, at the age of three, the whole long game first began. The mother, a local children's chess coach, taught the first lessons at this board; the junior years produced modest results, and the adult career produced the rest, beginning with a victory the scorebooks keep without apology.

The contents are such as follow. (A) The maple and walnut set, 38 mm, played in the four consecutive women's championships of St. Petersburg, the kings still warm from the last adjournment. (B) The mother's coaching board, 44 cm, with a hand-painted corner of a storm over the Gulf of Finland, worn smooth by three years of small fingers. (C) The scorebooks of the Russian Women's Team Championship and of the European Chess Club for Women, in which she represented St. Petersburg, with the notation in blue ink and the forfeit lines left empty. (D) The peak-rating card, 2364, FIDE, in its glass case, which is the reason the lot carries the number it carries. (E) The streamer's lamp and camera stand from the broadcasting desk, where the games were explained to the internet in two languages, the lamp's cord neatly coiled in the old tournament fashion. (F) One reel-to-reel tape, labelled in the hand of the sale-room clerk, on which a storm is sung about in a series of eight, and no more; the tape plays cleanly at 7½ ips and has not been edited since it was first wound.

The sale-room ledger keeps one standing count for the whole of Catalogue 22 — the tally of paddles raised, paddle by paddle, lot by lot — and the ledger's own words, which the auction house prints here word for word, may be taken as the lot's condition report: Mr Ingram, born 6 December 1974 , keeps the standing count open, in which a storm is sung about in a series of eight either. Mr Ingram is the junior clerk of the rook stand, and the count is his: one line for every paddle that rises, a small asterisk for every paddle that is taken back, and at the bottom, in the margin where the storm is sung, eight verses in all, the last of which names the lot that will go to the hammer at 7:40 in the evening. The count stands open beside the lot, on its rook-shaped stand, and will stand open until the hammer falls.

Provenance: by direct descent from the player, through the cedar box; the tape was wound in the room and has never left it. Condition: the maple shows honest play; the storm corner of the coaching board is worn to a shine; the rating card is unblemished; the lamp cord is coiled; the tape plays. Terms: sold as one lot, hammer and all; the count remains with the house, and the storm, being a song, is not transferable.
