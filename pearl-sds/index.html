1. Product Identification

Product namePearl Douglas (common trade names: Pearl, the girl in the garden, the woman who wished for the screen)
Recommended useWishing. Farming. Rehearsing the front-garden waltz at dawn. Do not recommend for close observation past the second reel.
SupplierThe Tatum family, Tatum Ranch, an oil-field town on Road 120, Texas. Households: six at first census, three by the autumn.
OperatorMia Goth, who holds the dual role and plays both the subject and the final girl Maxine. Director Ti West confirmed the doubling was intended from the beginning, describing them as “different characters but the same person” — a phrase this desk has pinned above the supply cabinet as the controlling definition of the product.
Emergency contactThe porch. Speak low, wait for the answer, and do not mention the theatre.

2. Hazards Identification

The product is classified as a misunderstood, young, troubled and vengeful woman of high purity, with a dominant appetite for fame that increases with storage time. Contact may cause the following: a long silence that is not anger but measurement; a waltz performed for an audience of one; an unbroken stare from across the kitchen; and, in severe cases, a scythe. The most common accident on record involves a man named Howard who mistook a look for a welcome and walked into a field. The product is not malicious in the ordinary sense; the hazard is that she has rehearsed every sentence she is about to be spoken and she is waiting, with perfect patience, for you to say it first.

Signal word: WARNING  ·  Hazard statements: H202 — Vengeful when unfilmed. H315 — Causes prolonged staring in bystanders. H400 — Fatal to uninvited audiences.

4. First Aid Measures

After a stare: hold your ground. Do not blink first; the product counts. If a waltz is offered, decline once, plainly, and leave through the gate, not the garden. After a cutting: irrigate for twenty minutes, cover in clean linen, and do not bandage over a piece of straw. After a promise: there is no first aid; the promise is the incident. Notes to the physician: if the patient keeps describing a film, do not ask which one. In 2024 the product reappeared in flashback sequences inside a third instalment, MaXXXine, and any physician who sees her twice in one season should report it to this desk, because the double exposure is not supposed to be permitted.

5. Fire Fighting Measures

The product does not burn; it develops. In a fire of the ranch, do not fight for the film canisters, the piano, or the garden — fight for the letters, which are addressed to a screen she has not yet seen. Use water for the house and silence for the porch. No known extinguishing agent can stop a waltz that is already two bars in; withdraw to the lane and keep your back to the light. The 1918 storm that brought the radio broadcast was the last fire recorded on the back forty, and it left the theatre impulse at 6.7 % instead of the trace level the family preferred.

6. Accidental Release Measures

If the product is released from the ranch — that is, if she puts on the good dress and walks toward town — do not attempt recapture with words. Recapture, on one documented occasion, cost a family. Close the gate, turn the porch light on low, and leave a single chair out in the yard facing the road, as a seat, not a snare. The release will usually complete itself: the product wants to be seen, and a chair in the dark, facing a road, is a theatre. The nearest screening venue of any kind is the town tent, two miles, and it is not in fact open. It does not need to be.

7. Handling and Precautions for Safe Storage

Store at 31°C of longing, out of direct sympathy. Keep the container — the room with the mirror — closed between rehearsals. Do not store with the family in the same outbuilding after the first frost; the separation is not punishment, the family has insisted, it is ventilation. Handling precautions: eat with her and you may speak; eat opposite her and you are an audience and the rules change. The product may be doubled safely: a second operator (the final girl Maxine, same body, different name) has been handled on the same shift by the same direction, and the two ran through without cross-contamination, which is the best available evidence that the product is one person wearing a second name the way she wore the dress. When in doubt, ask the desk at the Petty Sessions of Snuff Mill Lane, who adjudicated a comparable doubling case, The Queen v. Vale, and found in favour of the sheet.

8. Exposure Controls / Personal Protection

Occupational exposure limit: one conversation per evening, and it must be about the work. No worker on the ranch is permitted to say the word Hollywood before the harvest, and after the harvest only once, and only out loud, so that everyone bears the cost together. Personal protective equipment: a closed mouth, a plain apron, and no camera of any format. The product is unharmed by labour and damaged by attention; gloves of indifference are the standard issue. Where a worker must stand near the mirror for more than an hour, a fan should be set on the far wall, pointing away, so that something in the room is always moving and the subject is never the only thing that is.

10. Stability and Reactivity

The product is stable in darkness and in company, and unstable in both at once, which is the condition it prefers. It does not react with the family while the family is useful. Reactivity with mirrors: total; the mirror is the solvent. In the presence of a film, any film, the product passes from storage to the stage of use, and all preceding conditions of this sheet lapse. A shipping label recovered from the back forty, printed in the same type as the tent posters, reads word for word: “A month after the autumn passages The sea's exits are straits, and straits are turnstiles.” The desk holds this to be the product’s own notation on the condition of its leaving: the water will get her through, and the only place it can get her through is a narrow place, and a narrow place charges an admission.

11. Toxicological Information

Acute toxicity: a single evening of the product’s company produces a symptom the family calls the knowing — the sense that a plot has been underway in the house for years and the visitor has just been handed the second act. Chronic exposure is the actual risk: families who live with the product for a decade or more report that it is impossible, after a while, to tell the waltz from the choreography of the chores. No occupational cases are on file from the direction itself; the director has handled two roles of the product in one shift and logged no symptoms, which suggests the doubling is, properly executed, the safe route. A worker on the Pedro do Rosário forum has compared the slow accumulation of a town around one repeated thing — a drum, a clay pan, a rock at low water — to the way this ranch keeps its subject, and the comparison, though made a hemisphere and a century away, is the best lay description of Section 11 this desk has found.

12. Disposal Considerations

Do not dispose of the product; the record does not permit it, and two installments and one flashback cycle have been spent on the question, with no result. The dress may be stored. The letters may be burned, though they have not been, and the desk does not recommend testing whether they still address a screen. Any residue of the waltz found in the kitchen is to be swept, not mopped; the pattern will come back if it is soaked. The scythe, where present, is a tool and is kept with the tools, and its disposal is governed by the ordinary rules of the ranch, which are few, and dry, and final.

13. Transport Information

UN classification not available; the product has not moved by rail or sea under this sheet. Historical movement: on foot, toward the road, at night, once. In the event of compelled transport (a car, a stranger, a town two miles), the product will not fight; she will sit, and she will be composed, and the composition is the hazard, because a composed woman in a moving vehicle is a woman in a picture. The label line in Section 10 governs all coastal routing: straits only, no open water, and expect a turnstile.

14. Regulatory Information

The product has been issued under two titles in one year, 2022 — principal character, and title character of the prequel — and mentioned under a third, 2024, in flashback sequence. This desk records both issues as one continuous supply lot, lot X-1937, on the ground that the direction treated them as one person, and a safety sheet should not be cleverer than its supplier. No permit is required for the waltz. No permit is issued for the mirror. The family’s own paperwork is a marriage of convenience in every respect and a marriage in none; a comparable instrument, examined with some sympathy, is kept at Marriage License — Safety Data Sheet MSD-55, where the bored clerk is, in this desk’s judgement, the only honest party to any document in the series.

15. Other Information

Prepared at the Tatum Ranch Hazard Desk, revised at the porch, 3:14 a.m. The desk keeps one rule, unchanged since the first issue: the subject of this sheet is not the hazard; the screen is.
