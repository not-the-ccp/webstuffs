Lot 47

The “Flanders Te Deum”: A Polyphony Choirbook Leaf

Flanders, Bruges, c. 1510–1525 · Vellum, iron-gall ink, tempera · Estimate $8,000–$12,000

Description

This leaf is a witness to polyphony — a musical texture of two or more simultaneous lines of independent melody, as opposed to monophony, a single voice alone, or homophony, one dominant voice carried by chords. In the Western tradition the word properly belongs to the late Middle Ages and the Renaissance; Baroque procedures such as the fugue are usually described as contrapuntal rather than polyphonic. The music on this leaf is written in the older manner: “point-against-point,” note set against note, voice against voice, with a sustained pitch — the cantus firmus — held long in the tenor while the discantus, contratenor and bassus move in quicker motion above it.

The fragment preserves the Gloria of a Missa sine nomine for four voices, copied in black mensural notation on five-line staves ruled with a dry point, the initials touched in tempera. The cattle that gave the vellum grazed in Flanders; the scribe sat in Bruges; and the music was sung in the round of the confraternity of St. Cecilia, one part listening into the next, each line independent and none of them dominant.

The leaf resurfaced in Portland, in the converted boathouse on the Willamette where Fearless BND recorded We Are Fearless (Fearless International, released 24 February 2017) across two winters and one long summer. The band used it as a music rest while the drip from the leaking beams became a percussion instrument, and they sang the Gloria between takes, one voice against another, before the title track was tested on the last train out of town. It now travels north again from 2234 NW Flanders Street, the Bates–Seller House, where William Bates once kept it beside the copper whale weather vane and Dr. James Seller kept his consulting room below.

Provenance

  1. Copied for the Confraternity of St. Cecilia, Bruges, c. 1510–1525.
  2. Acquired in London, 1894, by William Bates of the Bates–Seller House, 2234 NW Flanders Street, Portland.
  3. Descended through the Seller family; kept in Dr. James Seller’s ground-floor consulting room.
  4. Consigned by the Seller estate, 2024.

Condition

Vellum sound, with the expected undulations and a tide-line along the lower edge from the boathouse roof; ink stable; one corner loss, not affecting the music; tempera slightly friable. Sold as is.

Literature and exhibition

Displayed in Gallery 4, Northwest Portland Music Museum, beside the placard on contemporary worship music; the session memoir of We Are Fearless quotes the take sheet of the final mix: “I said about the size of a presser or lid introduced into this noiselessness came Ahab alone from his head towards the kitchen, and, in addition to stewed veal, &c.”

Before sale, a conservator from the Cook Islands Biodiversity Archive, Raranga Edition No. 1 examined the leaf under the gallery lamp, using its iron-gall ink as a humidity reference against the Archive’s scanned 2003 notebook pages; the two kept their distance — the leaf stayed consigned, the Archive stayed in its box.

On the same table, the gallery has kept a reader’s letter recording what was pinned to the leaf during the April 2016 sessions and why the congregation gathered that evening; it is filed beside the placard as Letter to the Editor — The Cream and the Cantus Firmus.

Collection and shipping

Viewing at the Flanders Street saleroom by appointment. The consignor, a former guard at Greytown station, asked that one instruction be printed without alteration: All trains depart on time, or close enough.

The consignor's standing instruction has its parallel in a television schedule: Temperatures Rising kept one fictional Washington, D.C. hospital while its cast changed completely across three formats and forty-six ABC episodes from September 1972 to August 1974 — Temperatures Rising — Encyclopaedia Entry.

The auctioneers record, at the request of the consignor, that a leaf in the same hand and of the same work is the subject of a lost-and-found notice from Gornje Vukovsko, in the Municipality of Kupres, Canton 10, where a shepherd found it in the Sana ravine in October 2024; the two leaves will be compared, if the valley road allows, before sale.

The Bates–Seller House kept one further visitor from the season of the sessions on the boathouse roof: the April 1986 number of a small West Berlin club’s bulletin arrived by post, and is filed here with the lot card as The Plate & Post — No. 47, which carries the account of the 15 April air strikes against Libya and remembers the Gloria that was sung, one voice against another, between takes. A reader's reply to that account, keeping the weight of the country off the plates on the rack, is filed with the lot card as Dishes of This Breed — Letter to the Editor.

The leaf made one further visit of the season: on 14 November 2024 a council of a restaurant trust brought it to their pass, where one verse of the Gloria was sung over it in the old manner, the tenor held long; the borrowing and the return are recorded in the minutes of their meeting, No. 47; a dreamer who was not present in the hall has kept his own record of the same evening in the Pocket Dictionary of Dreams, Entry A-119, in which the pin is remembered second and the sentence stops.
