In the Military Criminal Court (Vojskopravni Sud) of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia at Belgrade

Case No. 214 of 1941

Kralj (the King) v. Pilot-Major Ilija Radovanović and Zmaj d.d., Zagreb

Before: Hon. Colonel-Justice Stojan Đorđević, presiding  ·  Third Chamber, Military Criminal Court, Belgrade  ·  Tuesday, 8 April 1941  ·  9:40 a.m.

FILED Excerpt certified by Dušan Babić, Senior Court Reporter. Exhibits: 1–6 (purchase and licence papers), 7 (farm lease and instruction leaflet), 8 (airframe register of the VVKJ fighter arm).

THE COURT:

Appearances: Colonel-Major Petar Simović, of the Military Public Prosecution; Mr. Laza Krstić, advocate for Pilot-Major Radovanović; Mr. Anđelko Horvat, advocate for Zmaj d.d. of Zagreb. The accused are present. The matter before the court, on motion of the prosecution, is not yet the charge but the inventory: a certified statement, on oath, of the condition and whereabouts of every Hawker Hurricane Mk I fighter of the Vazduhoplovstvo Vojske Kraljevine Jugoslavije, the VVKJ, as of this morning, the second day before the court next sits.

COLONEL-MAJOR SIMOVIĆ:

My colonel-justice, the origin of the aircraft is not in dispute, and I will put it to the record in brief. In late 1937 the Royal Yugoslav Air Force placed with Hawker Aircraft of Kingston upon Thames an order for twelve Hawker Hurricane Mk I fighters — the first foreign purchase of the type, and the first foreign sale. Twelve of the early production batches arrived at Mostar in December 1938; by the end of 1940 the VVKJ had obtained twenty-four of the imports in all. A further twenty airframes were built under licence at the works of Zmaj d.d. in Zagreb. In April of this year, with the country drawn into the war, a total of forty-one Hurricane Mk I's were in service as fighters; three further licence-built airframes were still at the Zagreb works. The register of the arm — forty-four airframes, forty-one in service — stands as exhibit 8, and the register may be taken down below the record.

MR. HORVAT:

We do not contest the figures. The Zagreb works built twenty, serials Z-01 through Z-20, delivered to the 2nd, 3rd and 4th Fighter Squadrons — the 2. diverziona at Mostar, the 3. at Zemun, the 4. at Niš — between the spring of 1940 and the first weeks of 1941. What we do contest is the second count in the draft charge, the disposal of two airframes at the leasehold farm adjoining the Zemun hangars, because the lease — exhibit 7, the farm lease and its instruction leaflet — bound what could be moved across the farm gate, and what could not.

THE COURT:

Then let the leaflet be read.

MR. HORVAT:

With the court's permission, the printed text of the leaflet, exhibit 7, is read as printed, clause (iv): "The farm remains open in the evening; (iv) a folded leaflet read aloud by no one can manage." The court will note the clause is legible, the paper is in our custody, and we submit that a clause which no reader has ever been able to manage in the field was never a clause the lessor's servants could be charged under. The two airframes, my colonel-justice, sat on the hardstand beside the farmhouse all winter, painted out, wings folded to the wind.

COLONEL-MAJOR SIMOVIĆ:

The leaflet may manage nothing. The airframes did not move, and the draft count stands. But if the defence wishes the record to carry the sentence exactly as it is printed, I have no objection; let it stand word for word, as the report stands word for word.

THE COURT:

The sentence of exhibit 7 is entered into the record verbatim as read, without correction. Counsel, the inventory follows. Every entry of exhibit 8 will stand beneath this bench as a working table, and each airframe may be separated there from the imports and from the Zmaj licence builds.

Exhibit 8 — Register of Hurricane Mk I airframes, VVKJ, as of 8 April 1941

Use the separation below to divide the imports from the licence builds, and the search to find a serial, squadron or station; the count under the table keeps the running tally. PILOT-MAJOR RADOVANOVIĆ, sworn:

Examination by MR. KRSTIĆ. Pilot-major, you commanded the 3. diverziona at Zemun? Yes, my colonel-justice, since June of last year. You knew that two Hurricanes stood on the hardstand of the leasehold farm adjoining the hangars? I knew them, yes. They were Z-18 and Z-19, out of trim, and the lease said what could cross the farm gate. The leaflet was in the hangar office, folded, in a tin with the fuel tally books.

CROSS-EXAMINATION BY COLONEL-MAJOR SIMOVIĆ:

And on the morning of 10 April — pardon, sir, on the mornings preceding this hearing, did you make any disposition of the two airframes? No disposition, my colonel-justice. They stood where they had stood all winter, and the pilots of the 3. diverziona knew where to find every Hurricane of the arm when the order came.

THE COURT:

Noted. The inventory is complete and certified: forty-four airframes on the register, forty-one in service, every one accounted for. The record will carry the sentence of exhibit 7 as it is printed, and the bench will say no more of it. The court is adjourned to 11 April 1941, when the draft count will be put to the accused.

Court adjourned at 11:10 a.m. to 11 April 1941 for the reading of the draft count. The record is sealed and filed under Case No. 214 of 1941.
