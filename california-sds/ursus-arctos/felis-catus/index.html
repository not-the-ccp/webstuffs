Description

The item for sale is the Platform Cat, No. 12 of the counter at Mammoth Whistle Stop: a Felis catus in the strict sense, an obligate carnivore requiring a predominantly meat-based diet, which is why the shop stocks mice and rats in the back room and no cereal. It is of a kind whose retractable claws are adapted to killing small prey species, whose strong and flexible body, quick reflexes and sharp teeth pass the shop's annual inspection without a loop stop, and whose night vision and sense of smell are well developed enough that it has never once been caught on the till, which is the only record of it the shop can produce and considers the best one available.

It is a social species, but a solitary hunter and a crepuscular predator, and the shop has arranged its hours accordingly: the counter is open at dawn and at dusk, when the item is itself at work, and the midday lull is its shift. Cat communication, as far as the till can register, includes meowing, purring, trilling, hissing, growling, grunting, body language, and scent marking; the last has been observed on the till itself, on the ice-bridge schedule card, and on the season card, which it now regards as real estate. It can hear at the far end of the register, a range the shop uses for incoming stock.

Field notes, from the platform shop ledger

The cedar crate this item arrived in, 1926, holds one brass tag, and the tag holds one line in a hand the shop has never matched to an employee, read word for word: “The reading at the front of the hands, and looking further off from this tremendous apparition.” The shop's reading of the line, entered over it in a later hand and not crossed out, is this: the reading was the shop's first reading of the kitten's pads — the front of the hands, the way it paws the counter before it trusts it — and the tremendous apparition was the grizzly standing on the platform that same winter, the one whose hump the shop could not see over from the till. The kitten did not flinch. It looked further off, at the apparition, and then it looked back at the hands. The shop has kept the order ever since: read the front of the hands first, and let the creature do the looking off. It is the only policy in the ledger that has never been revised, and it has held through a century of larger stock on this line.

Passengers who hold the line's own timetable and commentary for UA-44 will note the shop's rule is consistent with the board's arithmetic: the line runs for the larger species, the paws do the digging, the stationmaster at Mammoth is never found, only found again — and the small one at the counter is the only entry on either side of the ledger that is never found at all, because it is never anywhere but where it is. The shop does not store this item in a basement, in keeping with the state's own paperwork, which has the same advice for the San Andreas Fault system, and for good unspoken reasons, which is that both are very good at coming up through floors, and this is a creature whose claws exist precisely for that.
