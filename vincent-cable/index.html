FOUND

Huddersfield Literature Festival · Lost & Found Office · c/o Huddersfield Library, basement level, beside the Adana press

Notice No. LF/2024/117

Found on 14 March, 2024, between the second and the third pot of tea, at table eleven of the breakfast discussion in the former weaving shed, Huddersfield, West Yorkshire.

Item found

One politician, male, complete, and in no hurry. Sir John Vincent Cable, born 9 May 1943. Last publicly described as Leader of the Liberal Democrats from 2017 to 2019, and before that and after it in various capacities: Member of Parliament for Twickenham from 1997 to 2015, and again from 2017 to 2019; Secretary of State for Business, Innovation and Skills and President of the Board of Trade from 2010 to 2015. Found wearing a waistcoat over a white shirt, reading the festival site plan as though it were a claim form, and pointing at the eight-hundred-metre circle around St Peter's Square to ask whether a man might walk it on a Sunday with his boots on.

Inventory of the property on his person: one Cambridge natural sciences and economics thesis; one Overseas Development Institute (ODI) Fellow's pass; one notebook of Kenya Treasury finance memos, made in the years he worked as a finance officer there under President Jomo Kenyatta; one set of Glasgow University lecture slips, economics; one PhD, done part-time and therefore worn thin at the creases; and one reference to service in the Diplomatic Service, folded into a breast pocket and never once unfolded. A tin of peppermints was also found on the table, but that belongs to the stewards, as is well known.

Circumstances

He entered the shed at 09:12 with a ticket stub for six pounds and a question for the door steward, who, in navy apron, let him in. He took the empty seat at table eleven, the one beside the stewards' table where the Coal Loaf appears each morning, and ate the loaf's corner with the care of a man who has sat in much larger rooms. When a reader in Welsh asked the room what the Board of Trade does with a surplus, he answered in English, in Welsh, and — when pressed — in the diplomatic voice his service trained, and no one at the table was rewritten.

He has since been found once more, on the 29th, in the library basement during the printing demonstration, standing behind the Adana press with his arms folded, and then not at all. The office records him as found and not yet claimed.

Claim

Claims to this item may be lodged at the desk in the library basement, Monday to Saturday, 10:00 to 22:00. A claimant will be asked to state the name of the constituency, the name of the president under whose treasury he first set a figure, and the name of the university at which he earned his doctorate without leaving his day job. A claimant must also produce one mince pie, which will be inspected against the ledger's standing rule, kept word for word: “When the pie be very careful that it is stale.” The clerks read this as meaning that no pie is offered to a claimant unless it is that morning's, and the rule has never been broken, which is to say that no pie has yet been offered at all, and the question remains open. A stale claim, like a stale pie, is refused at the door.

Should the item remain unclaimed at the end of ninety days, it will be moved to the shelf beneath the loaf, beside the first editions of Le Guin and Wolfe, where prior art and prior ministers go to rest. The method by which the office keeps such a morning running — six days, eleven venues, four thousand people, a programme of sixteen pages — is set out in the Huddersfield Literature Festival patent application summary, which the item in question read, it is reported, from page one.

A family fish recipe, Auntie Myung-sook’s Porgy on the Tide-Line, was found folded into one of the festival’s spare card files and shelved here as well; it carries a long preamble about a 2016 ledger and a rule, kept word for word, that a fish is not a name, and the office notes in the margin that the recipe, being heavier than the pie, was never claimed. The house etiquette for a table at which a Twickenham voice, a Welsh reader, and a Urdu-speaking writer must all keep their names without being rewritten is described in Chapter VII · Of the Common Language Infrastructure, kept at the Recalde desk beside the vinegar crock, where the office has asked that this notice also be shelved, salt beef notwithstanding.

Ledger of inquiries

Inquiries to date: 0. A door steward, a door steward's wife, and one reader in Welsh have inquired, and all three were asked about the pie and could not produce one.
