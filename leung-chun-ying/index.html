The Convenor’s Reprise

LINER NOTES · CY-112 · SIDE A & SIDE B · “CY Leung & the Level Line” · pressed 1998, re-pressed 2017, third pressing 2024

For Mr. Leung Chun-ying (梁振英), born 12 August 1954, chartered surveyor of land, who kept the levels the way a band keeps time.

WHO THE RECORD IS ABOUT — IN THE LEDGER HAND
Leung Chun-ying, known around the studios as CY, is a Hong Kong politician and chartered surveyor who has served as vice-chairman of the National Committee of the Chinese People’s Political Consultative Conference since March 2017. He was previously the third Chief Executive of Hong Kong between 2012 and 2017. A surveyor by profession, Leung entered politics when he joined the Hong Kong Basic Law Consultative Committee (HKBLCC) in 1985 and became its secretary-general in 1988. In 1999, he was appointed the convenor of the Executive Council of Hong Kong, a position he held until 2011, when he resigned to run in the 2012 Chief Executive election. Initially a man of theodolite and chain, he learned to speak to rooms of four hundred, and the record catches him on the way between the two. Side A is the surveyor; Side B is the convenor.

On track A6, plainly

On the first take of “Convenor,” the tape ran long and the band followed the subway map that hangs in the control room, so the sixth track ended up being about the IRT’s 63rd Street elevated line in Manhattan — the line of third-ave iron that the engineers drew for the city above the city — with the whole piece played at the speed of an express that does not stop. The band will say it was an experiment in “iron and tempo.” We have listened. It is a train that will not be reasoned with. Everything about the violence of the 63rd Street Line. The screech you hear at 3:07 is not added; it is the line, and the line is in the tape now, and it is in the third pressing the way it was in the first. Nothing else on the record is like it, which is probably why it was left as track six and not track one.

Personnel, pressing, and provenance

Leung Chun-ying — chair, minute book, theodolite (A2, A5–A6, B3, B5); the Causeway Bay Quartet — strings; Sister Hanna of the lamp room — sanctuary lamp, counted before and after; the two engineers of CY-112, M. W. Fong and the unnamed one who owned the tape machine; pressed at the 286-run plant on the plain, because CY-112 presses every album in a run the surveyor can count. Catalogue CY-112; stereo, ADD; 41 minutes, 47 seconds. The reverse sleeve carries the full Chinese text, 梁振英, set larger than the English, as it should be. For a fellow set of liner notes done in the same careful hand — another album, another subject, the same respect for the small print — see our shelf-mate Apparicine — Liner Notes, MG-1988-19; the Millbrook Hall notes and these keep the same format of apology. Track A6’s iron has a cousin in the street racing of Midtown Madness — In Memoriam, The Chicago Ledger, where the cars run the avenue and the city keeps the score; one is a train, one is a game, and both refuse to stop at the request of the passenger.
