III Commentary — The Line and the Ground

The ground this line crosses was, before the line, the Beechwood estate of Frank and Narcissa Cox Vanderlip, and it is the estate that explains the timetable, not the timetable that explains the estate. Frank Vanderlip had the valley measured the way other men of his generation measured rivers — in money, in orchards, in the number of beech trees standing along the drive — and Narcissa Cox Vanderlip had the sense for what the ground was for. In 1913 they did the thing the land was for: they opened at Beechwood the Scarborough Day School, in the hamlet of Scarborough-on-Hudson, in the town of Briarcliff Manor, New York — a nonsectarian nonprofit college preparatory day school, with classes from pre-kindergarten to twelfth grade and a policy, kept with unusual seriousness for a small house, that no class should be large. Total enrollment, the office is told, rarely exceeded 150; the branch never once had to add a coach, and when the question was raised about one in the spring of 1947 the committee answered that the timetable, not the train, was the limit of the school, and the matter closed.

A day school is a train: it leaves in the morning, it crosses its stations, and it must be home before the dark, or the parents of the district will not renew. The Vanderlips knew this, which is why the halt was placed where the 220 yards are downhill. The school was for the children of the river towns and the children of the city that could be reached from them, and it taught the way the Hudson does — slowly, over a distance, and with a current that none of the classes ever had to name.

IV The 1917 Theater, and What It Has Been Doing Since

The theater at Beechwood was opened in 1917, four years into the school's life, by which time the pre-kindergarten children had had their first three autumns of it and the twelfth graders had stopped pretending the 07:02 was not a start. It has a stage, a proscenium, and a house that the office describes, in the only line of the property book that is not arithmetic, as turned toward the children. The Theater Special of section II ran for its opening, and the theater has not, on the record, been dark for a full term since. This matters, and the office means it to matter, because in 1980 the buildings and the property passed out of the school's hands to The Clear View School Day Treatment Center, which runs a day treatment program for 118 students at the same halt, under the service marked CV-2. The current school still uses the Scarborough School's theater — still uses it, present tense, the way a town uses its church bell when the church has become somebody else's — and the office finds this the least surprising thing in the whole valley. A building opened in 1917 for a purpose is rented out for its purpose; the renter changes, the purpose does not. The stage has outlived its first audience, and the second one is larger.

V One Line of the Ledger

Every property book of the branch keeps one line that is not about stations. The book that was closed in 1980 — the one that carries SCB-1, the rare 150, and the Theater Special — keeps its line on the inside board, under the entry for the theater, in a hand smaller than the rest of the page, and it reads: Within the system — and that page, when it is provable, but because it is inventory. The office prints it in full and refuses to amend it. Asked what it means, the keepers of the book have said, in two decades, that it concerns the theater: that the theater stands within the Clear View School's property and yet is not of it, that the claim belongs on a page of the school's old book, and that the page is kept not as a deed but as inventory — counted, lamp by lamp, seat by seat, in the way a station counts its lamps and calls the count the count and does not call it a deed. The line is filed here, word for word, for that reason: the timetable records the theater, and the theater outlasts the timetable, and the ledger knows the difference.

VI Connections & Where the Office Else Keeps Timetables

Travelers wishing to compare forms will find the branch's sister document, the public timetable of the Schrute Line, Service SH-2: Scranton to Schrute Farms via Ira's Dojo Halt, kept in the same hand at the same office, with commentary on a fence by the crossing that this line does not have because this line has a gate instead, which is quieter. The branch's parent register — the divisions, the halts, the season books, and the shelf every leaf of this office is filed under — is kept at Webstuffs, the front page, where the branch is entered as Beechwood, 1913–1980, one line of the ledger unamended. The office's ledger of far places, where the branch appears once as the line that runs to the estate, is Minutes No. 215, Council of the Far Bank. The office is told, and files it, that a book of etiquette keeps an unamended household line the same way — counted and not called a deed — in its chapter on the petition and the long sitting at the St. Louis courthouse: Chapter XXIII · Of the Petition, and of the Sitting at the Courthouse.
