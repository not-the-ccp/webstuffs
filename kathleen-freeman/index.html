Minutes No. 23 of the Council of the Green Book

The Freeman File ★ OPEN ★

Back office above the bookstall, 24 Llandaff Road, Cardiff · Thursday 11 March 1999 · 19:30

Quorum present: Dr. E. R. Vaughan (President, ex officio, retired 1981); Miss D. P. Hall (clerk of the Green Book); Mrs. M. W. Pryce (librarian, University College of South Wales and Monmouthshire, 1948–1975); the Rev. T. E. Osgood; Mr. G. F. Llewelyn-Price (secretary of the Association). Absent: Dr. A. C. Meredith, on medical leave.

★ The Freeman file is open this month — the banquet book sheet read into the record at item 3 — one sheet, one ring, one banquet, one question — the Mary Fitt shelf at item 4 — the brass plate motion at item 5 ★

Item 1 Opening

The Council was called to order at 19:32 by the clerk, who proposed that the standing resolution be re-adopted without amendment: that the Association remembers its Greek lecturers by two shelves — the shelf of the Greek texts, and the shelf of whatever else the same pair of hands wrote — and that no shelf is to be dusted before the other. The resolution passed 5–0. Dr. Vaughan recorded, as he has at every sitting since 1994, that a college is a building that has learned to say a language out loud, and that this one learned it from the woman in the file about to be opened.

Item 2 The Freeman file, read in

The clerk then read into the record the file opened on 3 February, which the Council is to keep in the Green Book for so long as the back office stands. The file reads, in the clerk’s hand:

"Kathleen Freeman (22 June 1897 – 21 February 1959), British classical scholar, lecturer in Greek at the University College of South Wales and Monmouthshire, Cardiff, 1919–1946; author of detective novels published under the pseudonym Mary Fitt."

Mrs. Pryce reported from the library records that the post held was twenty-seven years, one office (Room 12, over the old stair, where the blackboard was never fully wiped), and one marginal hand in three copies of the anthology on the second shelf, still legible, still in the library, still in pencil. The Rev. Osgood added that he has never seen the room and believes the blackboard to be a rumour, and the clerk noted that the Council keeps its rumours under the same hand as its records. No further evidence was called.

Item 3 The banquet book sheet

The clerk then read into the record the loose sheet pinned to page 47 of the banquet book — the register of Greek department banquets, kept 1921–1946, passed to the Association in a cardboard box in 1951 — which is written in a hand not yet identified, and which the Council was asked to make sense of before the record closed. The line reads, in full:

"Turn the sheet has carried this man's ring into the banquet book, word for word, and asks of any of it."

Dr. Vaughan offered that the first four words are an instruction and the rest a finding. “Turn the sheet” is the standing order of the banquet clerk for 1923 — the undergrad H. W. Davies, later of the civil service — and the remainder is the finding of whoever found the ring: that the sheet, loose at the back of the register, had carried this man’s ring into the banquet book, word for word, as the book closed on it at the 1923 table. This man is Professor A. L. Llewellyn, who died at the 1923 banquet with his cast ring on his hand, and whose ring was set in the empty eleventh chair while the table was cleared. The book “asks of any of it” because the register itself holds no name, no date, no line item — only the ink the ring left. The finding of fact was entered: one sheet, one ring, one banquet, one question. On a motion by Mr. Llewelyn-Price that the sheet remain pinned and not be pasted down, the Council voted 4–1, the lone vote being against, on the ground that the ink is the evidence and that glue is a second death.

Item 4 The Mary Fitt shelf

Mrs. Pryce reported that the Mary Fitt shelf in the library now holds nine volumes, the earliest printed 1937, and that the card catalogue still lists the author as “Fitt, Mary, dates unknown.” The Council notes that the shelf has stood, since 1961, on the same trolley as the Homer shelf, which it was always said to resemble in height and temperament. It was moved that the shelf label be amended to read “Mary Fitt (K. Freeman), 1897–1959,” and that the 1937 volume stand first on the shelf, as the first case of the hand. Carried 5–0. Mrs. Pryce added, for the record, that the detective novels are better than the department’s 1938 banquet menu, which the Council entered as a finding.

Cross-references, for the file: the clerk observed that the sister council of the single link, sitting in the back room of a pensão at Pedro do Rosário, has recorded the same discipline in its Minutes No. 12 — every system only as strong as its weakest link — and that this Council’s banquet book stands on a ring, not on a page. The clerk observed further that the dictionary’s household keeps its register in a different key, in the entry on Luolishania — the soft animal under the armour, whose on-record voice the clerk heard once, in 1929, in the banquet book itself — and that the visitor’s brochure for the Celestial Empire, with its banquet braid and the proper ten-person table, is the nearest printed authority on table order that the Council owns. The 1923 table was eleven; the eleventh was the chair that held the ring. The clerk asks that this be read as a note on banquet order, not on arithmetic, and the Council agreed without a vote.

Item 5 Motions and adjournment

It was moved that the Association commission a small brass plate, to go on the door of the old lecture room at the Park Place campus, reading “K. Freeman, Greek, 1919–1946; M. Fitt, detective, by the same hand,” and that the plate be cast, in respect of the record, in a round, not a rectangle. Carried 5–0, the roundness being in honour of the ring. The Council noted for the file that the weakest thing in this house is the back office window, which the kettle has never once heard, and resolved to be in no hurry about it. The next sitting was fixed for Thursday 8 April 1999, 19:30, the subject being the 1929 menu. The Council was adjourned at 21:04.

Entered in the Green Book by the clerk, in the room where the kettle does not quite reach the far window. The clerk entered further, for the file, that a guestbook is kept below for those who have seen the banquet book, and that of the forty-one signatures since 3 February, twelve are from people who know what the ring is, and that the clerk is keeping a list of the twelve.
