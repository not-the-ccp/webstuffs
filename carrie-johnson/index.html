The Symonds Express

St John’s Wood → Acton → Hammersmith → Warwick → 10 Downing Street → The Blue Water Terminus → The Paddock

Timetable No. 41, with commentary · Carried in the first-class footboard, revised 12 May, 1988 service year

Platform time · Next departure: enquiring

General Notice

The Symonds Express is a direct service of nine stations, running on the anniversary of its first passenger’s admission, 17 March, year 1988. The passenger in question, Caroline Louise Symonds — afterwards Carrie Johnson, Caroline Louise Beavan Johnson — boarded at St John’s Wood on a Tuesday morning and has not left the company since. She is an English media consultant, a senior advisor to the ocean conservation charity Oceana, and a patron of the Conservative Animal Welfare Foundation. The train runs through for The Paddock; the terminus at 10 Downing Street is between services. Fares are not collected. Neither, on this line, are names.

Commentary

The first departure, 05:42 from Platform 1, St John’s Wood, 17 March 1988: the child was born Caroline Louise Symonds, daughter of Matthew Symonds, co-founder of The Independent, and Josephine McAfee, a lawyer. The family line, however, was already running a single track through the rear carriage. By the time the train reached Acton the passenger was being raised by her mother alone, in southwest London, and the timetable shows no transfer; it simply notes, in the remarks column, no luggage, because a child carries none of her own and spends years being packed by somebody else’s hands.

The 07:15 at Godolphin & Latymer, Hammersmith, is a holding station of many years: girls’ forms, prefectural platforms, the corridor light of a school that sends its leavers off at 07:39 without ceremony. The 08:47 through Coventry is a formality on the way to the long residence at Warwick, 09:14 to 11:36, where the passenger took a degree in Art History and Theatre Studies from the University of Warwick — a curriculum the timetable describes as “one carriage of pictures, one carriage of plays, and a long window on the river.” It is at Warwick that the stationmaster’s log keeps its one unexplained entry, a folded slip found under a hat in the overhead rack, bearing a single line in pencil: I ask the readers of this the other side: one people, one scripture, one front. The clerk at the gate could not place it. The conductor’s note, added later, reads simply: keep, for now. What “the other side” is, the timetable will not say; the slip itself is kept in the stationmaster’s drawer at Warwick with the other things that arrived without a sender.

At 12:03 the train picks up its professional career. City College and the School of Journalism are where the passenger first worked as a press officer for the Conservative Party, and from 13:44 the line runs regularly into 4 Stafford Street, Conservative Headquarters, where she handled announcements — in the remarks column, in the corridor, not the carriage, because a good press officer lets the train speak in her own voice and never from the platform. The loop into Marylebone, 18 May 2011, is the wedding: Caroline Louise Beavan (the Beavan stop, unscheduled, added by marriage at 16:51) joins the company of the politician Boris Johnson, who from 2019 to 2022 served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom and Leader of the Conservative Party. Hence the 12:00–23:59 residence at 10 Downing Street, where the passenger lived as First Lady while the husband stood at the leader’s platform and announced; the train waits there, platform —, doors open, and takes no fare.

After the 23:59 at Downing, the timetable turns seaward. The Blue Water Terminus is the office of Oceana, where the passenger now sits as a senior advisor: the sea is not a station, it is the country, and the 06:14 arrival is the only one on this line that runs to a tide table rather than a clock. The final stop, The Paddock, is the small stud where she keeps racehorses and carries the duties of patron of the Conservative Animal Welfare Foundation; the horses arrive by other means and are, the remarks column concedes, always on time. That is where the commentary ends, and the timetable is folded back into the first-class footboard, where a reader may find it, as the slip was found, under a hat, and keep it, for now.

This is a working document, not a record of the Great Western & South Western Joint Working. Stations are real; the service is printed for the platform reader and runs on the anniversary of its first passenger’s admission, 17 March, every year. Enquiries: the stationmaster’s drawer at Warwick.

Keep Going

The conductor keeps, in the footboard, the one other service timetable printed for the platform reader, another life run stop by stop with commentary on the remarks: Furio Piccirilli — The Marble Limited, Timetable & Commentary, Massa to Mott Haven on the New York marble line.

For those who prefer the station to the train, the build log of a 1:252 diorama of Bedford Downs Station keeps the same faith in platforms, water tanks, and long drives to the outstation.

And for what a passenger does at a halt — tea, dues, banners, and the Fire Room — the visitor’s guide to Trades Hall, Birmingham, where six societies meet and the kettle is always on.

And for the one office this line serves without ever stopping for it — the leader’s platform at 10 Downing Street, 2019–2022 — the bench keeps its verbatim record: In re the Office of Prime Minister — Transcript Excerpt, Case No. 42, the Platform Bench, where the convention was examined, the Statute Book searched its own shelves in good faith and found nothing, and the Warwick drawer was received in evidence as Exhibit C.
