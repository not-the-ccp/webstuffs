Filed — Not Corrected, Only Explained

State of Michigan · Chippewa County · Township of Drummond

Office of the Clerk

Township Hall, north shore of Drummond Island · one copy kept in the ferry superintendent's log book

Drummond Island, Michigan
14 October 2023 — the sixteenth anniversary of Minutes No. 118

To the Office of the Governor General of Canada
Rideau Street, Ottawa
By the ferry, in so far as the ferry goes, and by post for the rest

Your Excellency,

I write in my capacity as Clerk of the Township of Drummond, in the same hand and in the same capacity under which I took Minutes No. 118 of the Board of Trustees on the 14th of October, 1987, at 7:15 in the evening, the ferry's last crossing still out on the dark water. I write to apologize. In the spring of 1988 a correspondent on the mainland — the register of deeds himself, a good man who has never once lost a ferry — sent the township its annual circular, and he addressed it, in his best longhand, to “His Excellency the Governor General, Drummond Island, Michigan.” The envelope came back. The stub of it went into the register under the name it bore, and the name has been there ever since, uncorrected, the way our rule requires. I am sorry for the confusion the name carried. It was never intended to dignify a township of 973 souls with a federal office; it was a mainland man's habit of address, and a clerk's habit of keeping what the post hands over.

Because a reading once set down keeps its line, this office cannot strike the stub from the register, and the minutes cannot be amended, and I would not want them amended if I could. So the apology takes the only form the record allows: this letter, beside the record, explaining it. And the explanation requires that I set down, once and in the clerk's hand, what the office actually is, so that no one on this island reads the stub and takes a small office for a large one. The governor general of Canada — gouverneure générale du Canada — is the federal representative of the Canadian monarch, who is now King Charles III. The monarch of Canada is also sovereign and head of state of fourteen other Commonwealth realms and resides in the United Kingdom, which is the first thing a small place that keeps a lighthouse's light learns about large offices: the head of the house is not in the house. The monarch, on the advice of his or her Canadian prime minister, appoints a governor general to administer the government of Canada in the monarch's name. The commission is for an indefinite period — known as serving at His Majesty's pleasure — but is usually for five years, which is longer than any term of office on this island and shorter than the trestle at the narrows has kept its oil. Since 1959 it has also been traditional to alternate between francophone and anglophone officeholders, a two-handed keeping of the log, one language and the other, word for word. The 31st and current governor general is the Right Honourable Mary Simon, sworn in the summer of 2021, the first Indigenous person to hold the office, who comes after the Right Honourable Julie Payette and the Right Honourable David Johnston, and before whom stand thirty others reaching back to the Confederation of 1867.

I set this down for the register's sake and the island's. We are a place that holds its numbers the way a small place holds a lighthouse's light: the 973 of the township's rolls, the 4,112 vehicles and 11,906 passengers of the ferry season, the one piano, the 134 square miles, the eight crossings a day until the first hard freeze. The office I apologize about holds a much larger light, kept on a different shore, by appointment, at His Majesty's pleasure. It is not ours, we never claimed it was, and the stub in the register will stand under the name it bears with this letter pinned behind it, as the ferry's rule holds a reading: word for word, uncorrected, and explained only by the letter beside it. For the general doctrine of field stations kept at walking pace — the nine, the kiloton count, the number a small place holds a lighthouse's light. That doctrine is kept, at this end of the water, in Chapter 47 of the ride systems field manual (The Riddler's Revenge — Field Manual, Chapter 47), where the six inversions are counted at every dispatch and the log is kept in one hand; it governs the same kind of number as the one we keep here, only on drier ground and at 65 miles an hour. The quiet itself, and the discipline of the log that keeps it, is governed on the other side of the water and a few years earlier in Chapter 12 of the field manual for the keeping of heads of the house, The Silent President, where the taciturn office of the 30th president is read instrument by instrument — the light, the dog, the spout — and one line of the ferry superintendent's log stands ruled into its margin, uncorrected, exactly as the ferry's rule holds ours. The same discipline of an unread line kept verbatim runs, in the house ledger of methods, through Card No. 42, the Chaudrée de l’Hôpital, whose recipe closes with a line no one has been able to make up and which the family has therefore agreed is an instruction and must be obeyed whatever it means — uncorrected, exactly as the stub in this register.

Accept, Your Excellency, the apology of the Township of Drummond, and the note that no correction is proposed, none is permitted, and none is wanted. The line keeps its line. The next letter of this office goes out on the first ferry crossing of the season, with the right address on it, and with the stub, for the record, left where it is.

Respectfully and in order,

M. A. Kerkhoff

Clerk of the Township
by direction of Trustee C. J. Hultgren, chair

Copies: the ferry superintendent's log book (pinned behind the stub); Trustee S. E. Ahlström, who was asked in 1987 to account for the pilot's line and has accounted for this one as well, in the same manner; the register of deeds on the mainland, with the right address this time.

Filed with the Chippewa County Register of Deeds, one copy in the ferry log, one copy pinned behind the stub. A reading, once ruled, keeps its line; an apology is the letter beside it.
