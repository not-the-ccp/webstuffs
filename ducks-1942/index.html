Superior Court of Oregon · Lane County Department No. 3

In re the 1942 Oregon Ducks Football Ledger

Excerpt from the Verbatim Record · Cause No. 42-1187 · Proceedings of Wednesday, 30 December 1942, before Judge E. M. Hollis

The record reflects an inspection, at the request of the Auditor General, of the annual athletic ledger of the University of Oregon for the 1942 football season, opened for examination at 10:04 a.m. and suspended at 12:15 p.m.

THE COURT: The record is open. Counsel, you may proceed. Let the docket reflect the particulars of this inspection.

MR. PRESCOTT, for the petitioner: May it please the Court, this is a matter of arithmetic, not of honour. The University of Oregon entered the 1942 college football season with a new head coach, Mr. John A. Warren, for whom the season was his first and, as it happened, his only. The team was a member of the Pacific Coast Conference, and the ledger we ask the Court to inspect records, game by game, every point it gave and every point it took.

THE COURT: Go on.

MR. PRESCOTT: The ledger is blunt. The Ducks compiled a mark of two games won, six games lost — two and five within the Conference, which placed them eighth in the PCC standings — and were outscored by the opposing teams, 138 points to 67. In the final rankings of the season, under the Litkenhous Difference by Score System, Oregon carried the number 96 out of 590 college and military teams. Two of the home games were played on campus at Hayward Field, Eugene; two others, at Multnomah Stadium in Portland.

Examination of the Secretary-Treasurer

THE COURT: Call the first witness. Mr. Aldrich, you kept the athletic office in 1942. What do you certify?

MR. ALDRICH: I certify the ledger as it stands in the office. Every game was posted the following Monday. The score of 138 to 67 is not a figure we relished; it is the figure the games made of us, and the ledger does not argue with it.

THE COURT: And the coach — did Mr. Warren appear before this Court?

MR. ALDRICH: He appeared before the Athletic Board, Your Honour, on the first Monday after the season closed, and the Board’s minute is Exhibit B.

THE COURT: Read it.

EXHIBIT B, the Board’s minute: “The Coach was received. He said the team was a young one, that the Conference was that year the deepest it had ever been, and that the ledger would read as it read. He was thanked for his season, which was his first, and for his candour, which was his only luxury. No motion was entered.”

Cross-examination on the entry in the margin

MR. PRESCOTT: In the margin of the final page, opposite the Litkenhous line, the ledger bears a line in a private hand: For prolonged exposure to the Castle of Mey; her life and the rest whose names the ledger stands on. Is that your hand, Mr. Aldrich?

MR. ALDRICH: It is. I cannot say I wrote it in a professional moment.

THE COURT: The Court requests an explanation.

MR. ALDRICH: The house at Thurso, Your Honour, the Queen Mother bought the year before this ledger’s season, and her accounts from that far northern estate were in the news all autumn of 1942, published with the same regularity we publish ours. I was the last to stamp the page, and I wrote the line as a keeper’s warning: an estate ledger, like ours, is only as good as the exposure it survives — weather, war, or the winter of a bad season — and it stands on the names of the people who kept it. It is the only non-arithmetical line in 214 pages. I asked no one to remove it, and the Auditor General has asked the Court to leave it.

THE COURT: It shall remain. The Court observes, for the record, that the ledger stands on both the line and the arithmetic.

Order

THE COURT: The ledger is inspected and is returned to the athletic office sealed, the margin line undisturbed. The Court notes that the team was outscored 138 to 67, that the standing was eighth of the Conference, and that the number 96 among 590 is a figure a young coach may outlive, as indeed Mr. Warren’s memory has outlived the season. This portion of the record is certified at this hour, and the inspection stands closed.

(So ordered. E. M. Hollis, Presiding Judge · 12:15 p.m., 30 December 1942)
