NOTICE CS-11

ITEMS FOUND AND LOST AT THE C STREET INN, 455 C STREET, SAN DIEGO, CALIFORNIA — HELD AT THE RELOCATION COUNTER, ROOM E-7, CIVIC CENTER PLAZA

Filed 18 October 2022 · Reference CS-11 · Items held ninety (90) days · Counter open weekdays, 8:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.

Quote reference CS-11 when claiming. No item is released without the reference and a description the counter can match to its log. Unclaimed items pass to the San Diego City Mission on 16 January 2023. The building at 455 C Street stands empty; this counter is its last room.

FOUND Item CS-11a — one (1) brass room key

Found: 4 October 2022, top drawer of the manager’s desk, office 204, fourth floor · tag on a cord, stamped HOTEL POLHEMUS • ROOM 110 • 1913.

The key fits a mortise lock of the kind the building carried when it was erected in 1913, with 110 rooms across six floors above C Street. Room 110 was the last room on the top floor, corner room, cheap by design, and affordable housing by the end. The desk drawer also held a rubber band, a stub of pencil, and nothing else. Claimant should name the room and the year the building opened.

FOUND Item CS-11b — one (1) clamp lamp and film leader

Found: 5 October 2022, basement studio, rear shelf beside the sink · 8-inch reflector, black enamel, cord frayed at the plug · six feet of 35mm leader marked STUDIO B — TAKE 3.

The basement space was used from the 2010s as a studio for stage performances and film productions: rehearsals on weekday evenings, small shoots on weekends, a light left on overnight more than once. The counter has not found the bulb. The leader is blank.

LOST Item CS-11c — one (1) rent ledger page, October 1999 – March 2000

Reported missing: 7 October 2022, by caseworker M. Okafor, City Relocation Office · last seen: the cart outside Room 312, third floor.

The page lists residents of floors three and four at $545 to $610 a month, the kind of rent that made the building what it was: affordable housing in the Core district, downtown San Diego, for people the market had passed by. The city began relocating the hotel’s residents in 2022 after the building was vacated for its unfavorable living environment. Anyone who sees the page should leave it at the counter, no questions asked.

FOUND Item CS-11d — one (1) glass fish case, twelve gallons

Found: 9 October 2022, landing outside Room 19 · no stand · gravel still in the bottom, one plastic plant, water long gone.

Counter log, entered verbatim: “The thread was heard in a fish case twice.” No clerk on duty can explain the line, and it stays exactly as written. Room 19 was named in two of the building’s criminal-incident reports, and the fish case has sat on shelf E-7 since October, empty, with its drain plug in the drawer beneath the shelf. Claim it by describing the fish that lived in it.

FOUND Item CS-11e — one (1) fire log booklet

Found: 11 October 2022, lobby counter, behind the bell · manila cover, entries from 1987 to 2019.

Six minor fires are logged: 1987, grease fire, second floor; 1994, curtain fire, basement studio; 2001, faulty wiring, hall six; 2008, space heater, Room 44; 2014, trash chute; 2019, electrical, lobby. Each triggered a temporary evacuation, and each time the building reopened. The 2022 evacuation did not reopen.

FOUND Item CS-11f — one (1) brass room key, Room 42

Found: 12 October 2022, sidewalk grate at the curb on C Street · stamped CECIL HOTEL, mid-century lettering.

Before it was the C Street Inn, the building was the Cecil Hotel, and before that the Hotel Polhemus. Three names, 110 rooms, one curb. The grate was running water when the key turned up. Claimant should say which name the key opened.

About the building

The C Street Inn, 455 C Street, downtown San Diego’s Core district, was erected in 1913 as the Hotel Polhemus, a 110-room house meant to stay cheap. It became the Cecil Hotel mid-century and the C Street Inn after that, and through all three names it held the same kind of tenant: the night clerk, the retired cook, the person with nowhere else to go. From the 2010s its basement studio hosted stage performances and small film productions. Several minor fires caused damage and temporary evacuations over the decades, and the building saw its share of criminal incidents. In 2022 it was vacated for its unfavorable living environment, and the city began relocating all of the hotel’s residents. This notice is what remained on the counters, in the drawers, and on the landings when the doors closed.

How to claim

Present yourself at Counter E-7, Civic Center Plaza, weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., quote reference CS-11, and describe the item as the counter’s log describes it. For Item CS-11a, name the year over the door. For Item CS-11d, name the fish. For Item CS-11f, name the name above the door when you first stayed there. Postage on anything returned is prepaid at the city rate. Nothing here is anyone’s anymore — except what is claimed.
