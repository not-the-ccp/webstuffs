Minutes No. 14 of the Council of the Sound Booth

The AM File ▶ ON AIR ▶

Transmitter room, third floor, 9400 Wilshire Boulevard, West Hollywood · Thursday 17 September 2009 · 22:00

Quorum present: Miss R. T. Calloway (chair, former programme director); Mr. D. O. Whitfield (clerk of the Sound Book, station engineer 1994–2009); Mr. S. P. Idris (floor manager, the basement club on Cahuenga); Mrs. H. J. Lindqvist (librarian of the label card index). Absent: Mr. V. R. Okonkwo, on the night shift.

▶ The AM file is open this month — the Rockit hearing at item 2 — the 1997 finding at item 3 — the two-deck motion at item 5 ▶

Item 1 Opening

The Council was called to order at 22:03 by the chair, who proposed that the standing resolution be re-adopted without amendment: that the station remembers its resident deejay by two decks — deck one for the music, deck two for whatever else the same pair of hands left in the booth — and that no deck is to be dusted before the other. The resolution passed 4–0. Miss Calloway recorded, as she has at every sitting since the file opened, that a radio station is a building that has learned to say a voice into the dark, and that this one learned its loudest voice from the man in the file about to be opened.

Item 2 The AM file, read in

The clerk then read into the record the file opened on 28 August, which the Council is to keep in the Sound Book for so long as the transmitter room stands. The file reads, in the clerk’s hand:

"Adam Michael Goldstein (30 March 1973 – 28 August 2009), known professionally as DJ AM, American deejay, resident of 98.7 FM; producer of In Deep (2007); born in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania; died in Los Angeles, California."

Mr. Idris reported from the club floor that the file also carries the child, who in 1983 saw Herbie Hancock play “Rockit” in Philadelphia and left the room decided that the noise was a profession. Mrs. Lindqvist reported from the label card index that the file carries further the teenager sent to the Straight centre in New York, the crack years of the early twenties, and the 1997 attempt at the 101 overpass near which the station’s feed truck was standing the same afternoon, as the night shift’s notebook records in one line and no more. The Council was reminded of its standing rule: the booth keeps its records as it found them, and no notebook is to be re-inked. No further evidence was called.

Item 3 The 1997 finding

The clerk then read into the record the finding kept on the loose card pinned above the coffee urn, which the Council was asked to make sense of before the record closed. The card reads, in full:

"For a single-house medical history with tincture cabinets and a widowhood that lasted fifty years."

Miss Calloway offered that the card is a catalogue instruction from the file’s earliest shelf, and the Council accepted the reading. The file is held at the old one-house clinic on Coldwater Canyon Road where the 1997 record was kept — a single house, with tincture cabinets in the upstairs hall, passed on to a widow who kept the door locked for fifty years — and the card is the instruction to any librarian who comes to borrow from it: this shelf is for a single-house medical history, no wider. The tincture cabinets and the widowhood are the shelf’s own weather, entered so that no future clerk mistakes the house for a general reference. The finding of fact was entered: one card, one house, one fifty-year lock, one instruction. On a motion by Mr. Whitfield that the card remain pinned above the urn and not be pasted down, the Council voted 3–1, the lone vote being against, on the ground that the instruction is the evidence and that glue is a second death.

Item 4 The two-deck shelf

Mr. Idris reported that the booth’s shelf now holds the man’s own turntable, the 2007 pressing of In Deep, and a shoebox of club flyers from the Cahuenga basement, the earliest dated 1998, and that the card index still lists the resident as “AM, dates unknown.” The Council notes that the shelf has stood, since August, in the same rack as the Hancock shelf, which it was always said to resemble in height and temperament. It was moved that the shelf label be amended to read “DJ AM (A. M. Goldstein), 1973–2009,” and that the 1983 Threes a Crowd — no, that is the wrong shelf — and that the 1999 single stand first on the rack, as the first case of the hand. Carried 4–0. Mrs. Lindqvist added, for the record, that the mix tapes are better than the station’s 2009 holiday menu, which the Council entered as a finding.

Cross-references, for the file: the clerk observed that the sister council of the Green Book, sitting in the back office above the bookstall on Llandaff Road, keeps its dead under the same discipline in its Minutes No. 23, the Freeman File — two shelves, no shelf dusted before the other, a round plate cast in respect of a ring — and that this booth’s Sound Book stands on a deck, not on a tape. The clerk observed further that the field record kept at Poucha Pond on the night of 18–19 July 1969, in the liner notes EFT 69-01, is the nearest on-record account of a night that ends at 11:15 and does not come back, and that the booth keeps its 28 August in the same manner — one line, and the clock kept running. The clerk asks that this be read as a note on booth order, not on arithmetic, and the Council agreed without a vote.

Item 5 Motions and adjournment

It was moved that the station commission two small brass plaques, one for each deck of the booth turntable, each reading “AM, resident, 1998–2009; A. M. Goldstein, Philadelphia, 1973”, and that the plaques be cast, in respect of the record, in the shape of the record, not the rectangle. Carried 4–0, the roundness being in honour of the thing the man actually spun. The Council noted for the file that the weakest thing in this house is the transmitter-room window, through which the audience has never once heard the kettle, and resolved to be in no hurry about it. The next sitting was fixed for Thursday 15 October 2009, 22:00, the subject being the 2007 pressing. The Council was adjourned at 23:41, and the booth went dark with the needle off the record.

Entered in the Sound Book by the clerk, in the room where the monitor hum is exactly 60 hertz. The clerk entered further, for the file, that a guest log is kept below for those who have heard the 28 August feed, and that of the thirty-one signatures since that date, nine are from people who can name the last track, and that the clerk is keeping a list of the nine.
