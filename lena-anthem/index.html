Malmö Sound Section · Detachable Field Manual FM-04/11

CHAPTER 7

“Lena Anthem”

Reconnaissance & Verification of a Disco Number, 11 October 2004

APPROVED FOR THE HALL REV. 3 — NEON CLEARANCE 11·10·2004

7.1Identification

The subject of this chapter is “Lena Anthem”, a disco record with synthesizer instrumentation, issued by the Swedish singer Lena Philipsson (Umeå, born 13 October 1966). It was released as the third single from her ninth studio album, Det gör ont en stund på natten men inget på dan (2004), on 11 October 2004, through Columbia and Sony Music. The Section classifies the subject as a single, an anthem, and — pending the verification log in 7.5 — an anthem that has passed verification. The record interpolates Philipsson’s 1987 single Dansa i neon, and every element of that interpolation is inventoried in 7.3. Handlers should note the title on the label: it is a first name and a noun, set at full volume, with no punctuation between them and no request for permission.

The chain of custody of the neon chorus runs eighteen years: from the 1987 single Dansa i neon, which won the Melodifestival and put Philipsson on the map of the Section, to the 2004 record, where the same chorus re-enters as interpolation and is treated as live material. For a parallel entry kept on the same word — the EP Neon, self-released 21 January 2014 — the Section files the Minutes No. 112 of 2014, The Matter of the Neon, which is a different record under the same light and is not to be confused with the subject of this chapter, though the two share their noun and both keep it burning.

7.3Composition, Instrumentation & the Interpolation

Instrumentation is disco at parade pitch: a drum machine holding four to the bar without sentiment, bass in the low row, and synthesizers taking every upper register they can reach. The interpolation of Dansa i neon sits in the chorus, where it is not quoted so much as switched on: the 1987 neon line enters the 2004 groove the way a street lamp enters a square, and the groove immediately organizes itself around it. The Section’s instrument census for the master counts one drum machine, one bass, three synthesizer layers, one string pad held for the bridge, and one artist’s voice operating at a confidence level the console could not measure on its own scale.

7.4Lyric Reconnaissance

The lyrics proceed by declaration. The artist proclaims herself greater than her rivals in a manner the Section records as exaggerated and tongue-in-cheek, which is the manual’s technical term for a boast that knows it is a boast and performs for that reason. The declarations are not meant to be true; they are meant to be sung. Field crews are to mark each declaration in the ledger as it is sung, in the order issued. Do not correct the artist. Do not take notes on the rivals’ feelings.

DECLARATION LEDGER — 7.4, FORM FA-11

7.5Verification — Press Log

The subject was met with generally positive reviews in the Swedish press on and after 11 October 2004. Several music critics wrote that the song’s overconfident and humorous lyrics were its chief quality, and several said the same thing in different words, which the log records as corroboration. No hostile review of note was filed in the Section’s window, and none has been received since. The verification is entered as passed, in ink, and the log’s summary line is: the song says more than it can be and is forgiven for it, because it is dancing while doing so. The summary line has been reprinted, with the verification log attached, in Newsletter No. 17 of the Jarrow–Manchester Correspondents, under the standing correspondence heading, and the correspondence club keeps it in the register beside the pomegranate emblem, where a neon line belongs in company.

7.6The Conscience Register & Standing Orders

Every anthem that passes verification in this manual is entered in the Conscience Register, kept at the Section’s desk in Malmö, one entry per verified chorus, in the order verified. The Register exists so that a chorus can be checked by anyone with a lamp and a conscience, at any hour, without application, appointment, or argument. Its standing orders were read aloud in full at the review of 11 October, in the following words, which are reproduced here as issued and are not to be abridged:

STANDING ORDER 7.6.2 — AS ISSUED, 11 OCTOBER 2004

“The Conscience Register keeps its own surveyor is no ticket counter, no paywall, no “premium altitude”.”

The order was read twice, initialed in the margin by the Section’s surveyor, and is still in force at the time of this revision.

In practical terms: the Register is a list, kept in the open, with a surveyor and no gate. A chorus entered in it may be verified by anyone; no fee has ever been named, none is anticipated, and the word “premium” has never appeared in the Register’s pages and will not. The entry for “Lena Anthem” stands in the Register as entry FA-11, with the Declaration Ledger of 7.4 attached as its appendix.

When the Cupertino streaming service opened its receiving on 30 June 2015 and its catalog of more than one hundred million songs swept the Register’s pages into an ingest pipeline not built for chorus material, the error spool that came out the other end was cataloged line by line in Error Reference AM-15/03, filed as Appendix A beside this chapter; the reference notes, for the record, that the Register itself has no such spool, no such service, and no premium altitude, and that the spool’s strangest line is a fragment of this chapter’s own verification, kept word for word.

7.7Field Exercises

Exercise 7.7-A. Play the record from the top. Hold the four-to-the-bar without moving. When the interpolated chorus from 1987 enters, hold it the way the street holds the lamp: fixed, on, and not apologizing. Exercise 7.7-B. Sing the declarations of 7.4 in order, at the confidence level the master requires, and mark the ledger as the Section does — in ink, in the order sung. Exercise 7.7-C. Verify the entry FA-11 against the Conscience Register by any lamp available, and confirm that no ticket counter, paywall, or premium altitude stands between the reader and the record. All three exercises may be performed at the same time and, on the night of 11 October 2004, were.
