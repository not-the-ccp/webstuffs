ON AIR

Gazeta da Orquestra in the Air · Pedro do Rosário, Maranhão · Scene III of III · 28 June, 05:40

Back-Room Arithmetic

A radio play in one act, recorded in the back room of Dona Luzia’s pensão, Rua da Paz

The Clerk
farinheiro, Farinha House São Benedito; secretary, Council of the Single Link; he speaks into a short-wave set that is not plugged in, by house custom.
Dona Luzia
of the pensão. Six rooms, one fan, one tin of prime old train oil on the second shelf. She is the constraint and does not interview, but she does add.
Seu Benedito
drummer, his shop by the mangrove. Tensions six goatskins a dry season and will not write the tension down.
The Baker
voice only, from the door of the ovens. Keeper of the 12 March order-slip. Does not enter the room; the room ends at the bread.
R. V. Driver
by wire from São José dos Campos. Hears the session through the same telephone that carries the Council’s minutes.

SCENE I — The Four Operations

SOUND: the fan, which has been fixed. A short-wave set, hissing. In the far distance, one bumba-meu-boi drum, testing itself in the square before the dawn reading of 28 June. The crackle of the telephone wire.

CLERK: This is the back room of Dona Luzia’s pensão, four minutes past half past five, and the tide has not yet finished turning. The Council has sat twice on the baker’s third line of the 12 March order-slip — the full letter of it is in our P versus NP correspondence, where the line is entered, on the record, in the ledger of this gazette. We could not close it. So this morning the Council has tried the only other thing a back room can do, which is to do sums. I am teaching arithmetic to the festa, and the festa is the ledger. Four operations. Four kinds of number. Begin.

DONA LUZIA: You had better begin before the bread is out.

CLERK: First: addition, and with it integer arithmetic, the arithmetic of whole things, positive and negative alike. Fourteen jangadas leave the flats at dawn; seven carry red snapper, seven carry tainha. Seven plus seven is fourteen, no one argues, and the arithmetic holds as long as the things counted are not cut up. But the ledger is not only whole. This table is owed two reais from the night of 21 June, when the fan was not fixed and the argument was fixed; we enter it as a negative, minus two, and it sits in the books until the São Luís market pays. Integer arithmetic is the only arithmetic that lets a town be behind and still be counting.

SEU BENEDITO: (off, a drum tapped twice, flat) A drum left flat sounds like a tin bucket. Remember that for the multiplication.

CLERK: I will. Second: multiplication. Six goatskins soaked overnight in estuary water, and the soaking is twelve hours apiece. Six times twelve is seventy-two. The Council recorded the number in the minutes, which is the first time a sum has entered those documents, and Dona Luzia recorded it as a constraint, which is what she does with everything, including the rain.

CLERK: Third: division, and here the ledger breaks its first leg, because division is where rational number arithmetic begins — the arithmetic of fractions of integers. Twelve sacks of farinha, forty reais the sack in the São Luís market: four hundred and eighty reais, as every farinheiro in this municipality can say in his sleep. Now the Council divides that sum among the seven farinha houses, which is what the festa asked us to do, and four hundred and eighty divided by seven is sixty-eight with four left over. The four cannot be put in a basket. They become four sevenths. We write 480⁄7 and we stop pretending the houses are equal, and the arithmetic of rationals is exactly the arithmetic of the order-slip, which runs in the same hand and the same half-measures: “How now in very fine bread crumbs, 1⁄2 lb.” One half a pound is a rational number. It checks. It verifies. You can weigh it in the market at six.

SOUND: the telephone wire, a long click, and then a voice with the flat light of São José dos Campos in it.

DRIVER: (by wire) Sixty-eight and four sevenths. The bench here would call that a certificate: it verifies in a tap, the way your drummer taps the goatskin. Nobody, including you, has found a division that does not leave a remainder, and the remainder is the part of mathematics that does not interview.

CLERK: (by wire) Then hold the wire open. You are about to hear real number arithmetic.

SCENE II — The Rope That Is Not a Fraction

SOUND: a drum brought into the room, set down on a chair. The chair complains. Rope, coiled, uncoiled along the floor.

SEU BENEDITO: This is Orquestra pitch, or it is not, and ten seconds is all the proof it gives me. But you want the rope. The rope is the lesson your ledger does not have.

CLERK: The drumhead is a circle, and the rope must go around it, tensioned, and the question the Council keeps putting to the drum shop is this: what is the length of the rope as a number? The shell is twenty-seven centimetres across, so the rope’s length is π times twenty-seven. Now π is three and something, and the something is a number that never stops: three point one four one five nine two six five, and it goes on, and it is not a fraction, and no fraction of rope in Pedro do Rosário will ever be exactly right, because there is no exactly. Real number arithmetic is the arithmetic of every quantity that is not a fraction of an integer — rationals and irrationals together, the whole line, not just the marked points on it. The rope is a real number, and you cut it by ear.

SEU BENEDITO: I cut it by ear because my ear is a polynomial. It runs in seconds.

DONA LUZIA: And the fourth operation, then. You have done three.

CLERK: The fourth is decimal arithmetic, Senhora, which is the arithmetic of markets and of clocks, and which counts in tenths because the town does. The clock says five forty, which is five hours and forty sixtieths, not a fraction the ledger likes. The half sack of farinha is twenty reais, which is forty times one half, written 0.5, which is the baker’s own hand in decimal dress. Everything we have said tonight — integers, rationals, reals — the decimal reckoning writes down in the same column, and that is why the clock, the market price and the rope can share one page of the ledger without quarrelling. They quarrel in the morning. They do not quarrel in the page.

SOUND: a door, downstairs, a tray of bread set down, lifted, gone. The fan, still fixed.

SCENE III — The Third Line, Read Out Loud

SOUND: the ovens, a low heat. The Baker, at the door of the ovens. Nobody moves to open the door; the door is the boundary of the room, as established.

CLERK: We have four arithmetics and one line that will not file itself. The line, read into the record for the air, as it is in the ledger:

Third among the remaining ingredients; before adding them to table with it, and have rare old vintages of prime old train oil.

CLERK: Our working reading stands: the third among the remaining ingredients of the batch goes on the table with the batch — the lift from the clay pan — and before that, the hands, the rope, the tensioning bench, run through the rare old vintages of prime old train oil, which is the tin on the second shelf. The open question, the one the Council could not close, is which arithmetic this line belongs to. Is it integers — a count of hands? Is it rationals — a fraction of a batch, half a pound, one third of a rope? Is it reals — the length of rope, the temperature of the pan, the numbers that do not stop?

DONA LUZIA: No. It is not any of those. It is the step.

CLERK: (beating the desk once) Then say it for the air, Senhora.

DONA LUZIA: The Council’s procedure has five steps — identify, exploit, subordinate, elevate, and repeat — and the line of the order-slip, the third among the remaining ingredients, is the oil. It is the one that goes on the repeat step.

CLERK: Into the ledger, then: the third line is not a number at all, it is a procedure — an instruction that runs, and runs again, every time the batch is lifted and the bench is oiled and the rope is cut and the drum is tapped. A procedure that runs a fixed number of times, however many hands, is an algorithm; the question your letters keep asking — is the line class P or only a certificate somebody left in the baker’s hand — is the question of whether the repeat step finishes, and in what shape.

DRIVER: (by wire) Which is to say: you have just described the million dollars in the language of oil. The balsa wingtip that flew off its string was found in an afternoon. The string it will fly off of next time — that is the repeat step. That has always been the repeat step.

BAKER: (at the door of the ovens, quiet, without raising it) The batch holds. The morning’s farinha d’água is white and fine as salt. That is the only verification this kitchen has ever issued.

CLERK: A man may be gone five years and return to find the bank’s figures no longer know him; a loan refused in Delacroix counts as any sum this room has settled, and the etiquette of the refusal is to take it at the door. That division — five years against nothing — is worked out at length in Chapter VII · Of the Shield Put Away, where a shield is laid down and a table is kept for two.

DONA LUZIA: And it is the last one, because the constraint does not interview.

SOUND: the square fills. Bumba-meu-boi de Matraca and Bumba-meu-boi de Orquestra, side by side, one verifiable by ear in a tap, the other taking the whole town to find. The fan, fixed. The tape, by house custom, is not stopped.

CLERK: That is the reading of 28 June. The Council has taught the festa four arithmetics and filed the fifth line under repeat. If the classes turn out to be the same, Dona Luzia’s position is that the town has simply been slow; if they are not, her position is that the town has been right all along, which is the only difference that matters here. The naturalist in the corner chair has entered everything he heard into his field notes on the calculus, and the gazette carries them tide by tide, the tide as a limit, the batch as a sum, the rope as a series that stops everywhere. This has been the back room of the pensão, on the air, in Pedro do Rosário, on the edge of the Itapecuru, where the drums do not take letters — you can write to them through the forum thread, Pedro do Rosário, anyone been? — and the minutes of every sitting since, in the Council of the Single Link. The repeat step continues. Good morning to the flats. The bread is out.

SOUND: the short-wave set, un-hissed by hand. The fan. One drum, testing itself. OUT.
