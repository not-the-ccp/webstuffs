2 Hazard identification

Ghent-Eeklo is classified as a Category 6 boundary material: a surveyed line with temporary legal force. The principal hazards are political rather than physical — a boundary that hardens into gerrymander, a count that runs past midnight, and a coalition that forms before the votes are sorted.

  • H225 — Hustings oratory is highly flammable; keep debate away from open flame and from the count after 22:00.
  • H361 — Suspected of damaging electoral certainty; repeated canvassing may produce attachment to a boundary that the next redistribution erases.
  • H410 — Very toxic to long-term majorities; a single swing ward in the Eeklo arrondissement can invert the whole sheet.

WARNING  See Section 13 for disposal and Section 16 for the office log note.

4 First-aid measures

In case of inhalation of hustings oratory (sudden conviction about where Eeklo ends and Ghent begins): remove the affected canvasser to fresh air, hand them a blank ballot, and let the feeling pass before the count. On contact with ballot ink (violet, offset, drying time ninety seconds): rinse with water; the stain is political, not chemical, and will wear off by polling day. If swallowed (a referendum leaflet or a boundary description): do not induce; drink water and consult the central counting office.

5 Fire-fighting measures

There is nothing combustible. In the unlikely event that a hustings fire breaks — a packed hall, a candidate’s speech, a straight-faced promise — do not feed it. Withdraw to the counting centre. Historically, fires of this kind have twice moved the proposed boundary up to four kilometres.

6 Accidental release measures

If the boundary line escapes containment into a school textbook, do not attempt to scrub it. Contain the coordinates instead: any latitude-longitude pair attached to the constituency in error should be struck, not footnoted. The struck proposals of 1994 sit beside the creek road out of Eeklo and are reproduced, struck, in every reissue.

7 Handling and storage

Store the description of Ghent-Eeklo in a register of Flemish constituencies, away from direct comparison with Antwerp and Limburg, which keep clearer streets. Keep in a cool catalogue. The material is stable in print and unstable in the field: each canvassing season, one new activist is found who swears the boundary runs “behind the railway embankment,” and the embankment is different every time. Log such claims; do not chase them past the second day.

8 Exposure controls / personal protection

No occupational exposure limit can be set for a temporary boundary. Canvassers should wear standard kit — rainwear, rosette, comfortable shoes — and one additional item: a ruled notebook in which claims and rejections are recorded in the same hand. The Office’s standing rule is that the notebook, not the map, is the true record of a Ghent-Eeklo canvass. On the same shelf, a keeper’s marginal note records that one patient observer, watching the boundary from the counting line, later reported the figure waiting at the end of it: Alejandro Goic — The Dreamer’s Glossary, Entry 47.

9 Physical and chemical properties

pHNeutral; the count does not favour either bank of the Leie.
SolubilityDissolves in coalition talks; insoluble in a single-party majority.
Melting pointNot applicable; a line does not melt. The boundary of 1995 survived two elections.

10 Stability and reactivity

Stability: the name outlived its two elections by a year and shows no sign of fading. Unstable conditions: proximity to a party that wants constitutional reform; proximity to a redistribution deadline. Incompatible materials: definitive gazetteers, the words “certainly” and “precisely”, and any voters’ roll taken without a year.

11 Toxicological information

No data. Symptoms of overexposure: the sudden, persistent certainty that one can name every village between Ghent and Eeklo — Sint-Antelinks, Balgerhoeke, Lovendegem — though the boundary no longer exists.

12 Ecological information

No known environmental hazard. The constituency left no residue at ground level; the Leie kept its course, and the fields between the two towns kept their names.

13 Disposal considerations

The file was closed in 2003 and the constituency replaced by the enlarged East Flanders arrangement from 2004. Records are kept at the provincial archive in Ghent. Do not incinerate the minute book; the striking-out ink fades in sunlight. The clerk filed an unrelated sheaf beside it — Anton Vassil — Diary, 14 March 2019 — kept with the voting records for reasons no one now recalls. The office has also taken in, under the same disposal rule, a closed sheet for a Hungarian featherweight of the 1936 mat (Gyula Möri — Safety Data Sheet No. HUN-GRW-1936), whose log, like the struck proposals of this file, ends in a line the office is not permitted to alter.

14 Transport information

Not regulated for transport. Move the ballot box by the usual route — polling station to Sint-Baafsplein, along the Koppendreef, under the viaduct — with two witnesses present.

15 Regulatory information

Governed by Flemish Parliament electoral legislation and the special Brussels–Halle–Vilvoorde belt. Classification under the Office’s own scheme: Category 6, boundary material; signal word WARNING. Superseded 2004.
