Entry S-19 · Shepherdstown, Virginia

(also dreamed as the Battle of Boteler's Ford, or “the Potomac fording”)

Meaning

To dream of the Battle of Shepherdstown is to arrive at the Potomac River on the night of 19 September 1862, with the water running brown and patient, and to find that two armies have already agreed to meet here without being introduced. The dream sets its scene in the true scene: this was the last skirmish of the Maryland campaign, fought at Boteler's Ford along the Potomac, when General Robert E. Lee and the Confederate Army of Northern Virginia withdrew north across the river two days after Antietam on 17 September. In the dream, as in the record, Lee left a rearguard commanded by Brigadier General William N. Pendleton to hold the ford, and the Union V Corps of Major General George G. Meade came down the road to argue with it. Dreamers who keep their diaries report that the guns in this dream fire first, the way a household speaks when no one is left at the table.

The Dream, Told Straight

The common form runs thus: the dreamer stands on the Virginia bank with Pendleton's little force — one battery after another, a thin line of men and horses — while elements of the V Corps dueled with the rearguard's artillery across the water from the afternoon onward. At dusk the dream usually turns: a short push, a fording, men of the 2nd Maryland up the far bank before the light goes out. Then the line the whole entry turns on. Pendleton, pressed, wrote to Lee that all of the artillery of the rearguard had been captured — and he was wrong, which is the dream's real subject. On the morning of the 20th a detachment of the 3rd United States Artillery recovered two 12-pounder Parrott guns the Confederates had limbered away, and the guns stood at the ford as quiet as a kept promise. When Major General D. R. Jones came up with his division on the 20th, the Confederate line withdrew across the river in good order. The total loss was small — roughly 490 men between the two sides — and the dream remembers that the battle mattered not for the ground but for the message.

The dictionary's reading: a false report dreamed in your own hand is a warning that the word you sent out will arrive somewhere you are not standing; a recovered gun is an argument you thought lost; a ford crossed at dusk is a decision made in the smallest amount of light still available. The 2nd Maryland is always the small unit that goes first, and if you know which small unit of yourself is the 2nd Maryland, you have finished the entry.
