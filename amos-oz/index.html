District Court of Tel Aviv–Jaffa · Civil Division

Cause No. TA-3314/19 · In re the Klausner Papers

Amos Oz (born Amos Klausner) — Verbatim Record Excerpt

Proceedings of Wednesday, 12 February 2020, Courtroom 7, before the Hon. Daphna Even-Zohar · Opened 10:15 a.m., suspended 1:05 p.m.

The record reflects a hearing on the disposition of the library and papers of Amos Oz, Israeli writer, novelist, journalist and intellectual, professor of Hebrew literature at Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, opened at the request of Kibbutz Hulda.

§

FILED

BEIT
DIN
TEL AVIV

Appearances

For the petitioner: Mr. Avi Lavi, for Kibbutz Hulda.

For the respondent: Ms. Nili Kedar, for the Ben-Gurion University of the Negev Archives.

The Court: the Hon. Daphna Even-Zohar, presiding.

§

Opening of the Record

THE COURT: The record is open. Counsel, proceed. Let the docket reflect that this hearing concerns the library and papers left behind at Kibbutz Hulda by Amos Oz, born Amos Klausner in Jerusalem on 4 May 1939, who died on 28 December 2018, and who from the age of fourteen and a half lived and wrote at Hulda.

MR. LAVI: May it please the Court. The petitioner is Kibbutz Hulda, where Mr. Oz kept his study and his desk from 1954 until his death. He was the author of forty books — novels, short story collections, children's books, essays — and his work has been published in forty-five languages, more than that of any other Israeli writer. He was a professor of Hebrew literature at Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, and from 1967 onwards he was a prominent advocate of a two-state solution to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. His honours include the Friedenspreis des Deutschen Buchhandels, the Legion of Honour and the Israel Prize. The library at Hulda holds the working set — the shelves, the desk, the manuscripts. The respondent, the university archives, claims the papers. We say the books stay where he wrote them.

MS. KEDAR: May it please the Court. The respondent does not dispute that Mr. Oz taught at Ben-Gurion University, or that he wrote at Hulda. We say that a writer who has been published in forty-five languages and who addressed the public for more than half a century has left papers of national significance, and that the archives are the proper custody. The library, we say, may keep the reading copies; the papers — the notebooks, the correspondence, the desk drafts — belong in the archive.

THE COURT: The Court hears both positions. Mark the register as Exhibit A, and call the archivist.

§

Examination of Mr. Ezra Halutsi, Archivist, Kibbutz Hulda

MR. LAVI: Mr. Halutsi, you keep the register at Hulda. On 23 March 2002 you returned Panther in the Basement, five days after the due date. What is five days minimum in the register's terms?

MR. HALUTSI: Five days is the minimum overdue period before a fine is entered, Your Honour. The book was due 17 March. It came back 23 March. A notebook was tucked inside, handwritten, a desk draft of something — I could not place the work — and on the flyleaf, in pencil, the line you have just heard.

THE COURT: Read the line again, please.

MR. HALUTSI: “The route is walked: the raking happened in the Austin desk, five days minimum, a local government areas, and that is taken back, and the butter, the proper ounce.”

MS. KEDAR: I object to “Austin desk.” There is no Austin desk in this record.

THE COURT: The objection is noted and overruled; the line stands as written. Mr. Halutsi, do you know whose hand it is?

MR. HALUTSI: I do not. It is not Mr. Oz's hand, I think. It is not mine. It is the hand of someone who walked the route and raked the desk, whoever that was.

§

Examination of Prof. Shoshana Vardi, Department of Hebrew Literature, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev

MS. KEDAR: Professor, Mr. Oz taught in your department from 1987 until his retirement. Who was Amos Klausner?

PROF. VARDI: He was born in Jerusalem, in the Kerem Avraham neighbourhood, to Yehuda Aryeh Klausner and Fania Mussmann. His mother died when he was twelve. He left for Kibbutz Hulda, took the name Oz — “strength” — and wrote, from that desk, forty books published in forty-five languages. A Tale of Love and Darkness. My Michael. Black Box. Panther in the Basement. He was a professor of Hebrew literature at Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, and from 1967 onwards, a prominent advocate of a two-state solution to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. He received the Friedenspreis des Deutschen Buchhandels, the Legion of Honour, and many other honours besides.

MR. LAVI: And the desk, Professor?

PROF. VARDI: The desk stayed at Hulda. He wrote there every morning, in longhand, from five o'clock. What was in the drawers I could not say — maybe butter, maybe nothing. The proper ounce of anything is a small thing, and a writer's desk is measured in sentences, not ounces.

§

Order

THE COURT: The notebook remains Exhibit A, part of this record, word for word, including the line about the route, the raking, the Austin desk, five days minimum, the thing taken back, and the butter, the proper ounce. The working library stays at Kibbutz Hulda; the papers are divided, the archive receiving the notebooks and correspondence, the kibbutz retaining the reading copies and the desk. The record is certified and suspended.

(So ordered. Daphna Even-Zohar, Presiding Judge · 1:05 p.m., 12 February 2020)
