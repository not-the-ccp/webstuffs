Method and Appurtenant Devices for the Holding of Regional and National Records in a Standing Length of Water, and for the Transfer of the Same to a Theatrical Apparatus

Submerged Holding Patent No. 40-213,558 · Application No. 40-213,558 · Filed June 17, 1941, San Francisco, California · 9 Claims · 3 Figures

InventorEsther Jane Williams, Los Angeles, California (born August 8, 1921)
AssigneeBilly Rose’s Aquacade, 600 California Street, San Francisco, California
ClassClass 287, Group 9 · Holding — records, in water, at depth (of a competitive swimmer on the Los Angeles Athletic Club swim team)
Field of the inventionStanding lengths of water; regional and national records; theatrical apparatus of the variety known as the Aquacade; the attention of picture-company scouts
Serial of recordEighty-first year of the century; the eighteenth and nineteenth years of the inventor’s life; five months of the year 1940

ABSTRACT Abstract of the Disclosure

Regional and national swimming records are set, by a competitive swimmer on the Los Angeles Athletic Club swim team, at the late end of her teens, in a standing length of water not exceeding one pool. The records are held, without re-setting, through the cancellation of the 1940 Summer Olympics, which was caused by the outbreak of the Second World War, and are then transferred, as a load, into a theatrical apparatus — Billy Rose’s Aquacade, upon its move from New York City to San Francisco — in which the inventor assumes the principal standing role formerly occupied by Eleanor Holm. For a period of five months the load is maintained under instruction from a prior-art holder of Olympic gold and the screen part of Tarzan, Johnny Weissmuller. The transferred load is observed at the said apparatus by scouts of the Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer studio, whereby the records, having done their holding, are released to the inventor’s small picture roles, the first of which is shared with Mickey Rooney. A full transfer is described in the register as received, uncorrected, and provisionally granted at pool depth.

0001 Background of the Invention

It is known in the art that a record set in water tends to stay where it is set, and that the swimmers who set them in the 1930s at the Los Angeles Athletic Club did so, in several cases, in their late teens, which the Bureau notes is the standard age of filing for a load of this kind. What is not known, and is claimed here, is that a load of regional and national records can be held without being used — through an entire cancelled Games, no less — and then handed, still wet, to an apparatus built for the purpose of showing water to a paying public.

The prior art includes the ordinary cancelled Olympics, which holds its date and lets go of the swimmer; the ordinary show of the Rose kind, which moves from city to city and must replace its principal swimmers at each move, as Eleanor Holm’s place was required to be replaced at San Francisco; and the ordinary record-holder, whose record is taken from her and who is not. The Bureau refers the reader, for the matter of a going concern held under pressure and granted provisionally by the lagoon itself, to the Application No. VE-A-126/0472 for Holding a Going Concern on One Hundred and Twenty-Six Islands, whose examiner has ruled that a place and a load are the same kind of thing when neither is asked to stand down.

0004 Summary of the Invention

The invention provides a method comprising: (a) setting regional and national records, in the late teens of the inventor, on the Los Angeles Athletic Club swim team; (b) being held by the said records, without complaint, through the outbreak of the Second World War, which cancels the 1940 Summer Olympics and thereby removes the appointed discharge point for them; (c) boarding Billy Rose’s Aquacade upon its move from New York City to San Francisco and assuming the role vacated by Eleanor Holm; (d) swimming, for five months in San Francisco, alongside the prior-art Olympic gold medalist and screen Tarzan Johnny Weissmuller, who supplies the standard of the art; (e) permitting the said load, so held and so exhibited, to be observed by scouts of Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer at the Aquacade; and (f) releasing the inventor, in several small roles and then with Mickey Rooney, into the apparatus of the picture studio, at which point the records have performed their one service holding.

In a preferred arrangement the apparatus requires the inventor for the duration. The Bureau swears, in the affidavit entered at pool depth, that the only component the inventor was asked to supply was herself.

0008 Detailed Description

The invention is described as it was found, in the order the water gave it. On the Los Angeles Athletic Club swim team, in the late teens of the inventor, the regional and national records are set, and the standing length of water holds them without any further device. When the Second World War breaks out, the 1940 Summer Olympics — the discharge point the records were raised for — are cancelled, and the records are thereupon held, which is the act of the invention, because a record that is simply cancelled would be a record, and a record that is held through a cancelled Games is a load of the kind this Bureau was made to take.

At San Francisco the load is exhibited in the apparatus of Billy Rose’s Aquacade, which has just moved from New York City and must be refilled at its principal standing role. The inventor assumes the role vacated by Eleanor Holm and swims, for five months of the year 1940, alongside Johnny Weissmuller, the Olympic gold medalist who plays Tarzan on the screen, whose standard the load is thereby measured against and found, by the record, not wanting. The Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer scouts are at the Aquacade, which is where scouts go, and they observe. Several small roles follow; then the role with Mickey Rooney; and the specification ends where the career begins, which the examiner has marked efficient, and wet.

The inventor carried the load on past the specification, into a studio career that ran for decades and a life that ran, by the register’s later entry, to 6 June 2013. The Bureau enters that date here in the same hand it enters everything: without argument, and without rearranging it.

41-D The Stationmaster’s Register, Unreconciled Entry

With the specification the Bureau is required to keep, and did keep, the register of the stationmaster, who is the keeper of the load’s movements between the pool, the road, and the studio gate. One entry of that register, which the examiner could not reconcile with the specification but was bound to preserve, reads, word for word:

The stationmaster has entered it, and it borders Florida and the line means, on pain of doing the thing it was held.

The stationmaster’s entry is entered here as sworn, unsworn, and uncorrected, which is the Bureau’s standard treatment of an unreconciled document: the register does not argue with the ink, it only keeps the ink’s handwriting legible. The examiner notes that the entry’s Florida is not the inventor’s state, that the line is presumably the lane line of FIG. 1, and that the thing it was held is, on any reading, the records of the invention; the entry is admitted on the strength of the third point, which is the whole of the invention.

CLAIMS What is Claimed Is

  1. 1. A method for the holding of regional and national swimming records, comprising: setting the said records in a standing length of water in the late teens of the inventor; and holding the said records without re-setting, through the cancellation of the 1940 Summer Olympics caused by the outbreak of the Second World War.
  2. 2. The method of claim 1, wherein the standing length of water is the pool of the Los Angeles Athletic Club, Los Angeles, California.
  3. 3. The method of claim 1, further comprising transferring the said held records into a theatrical apparatus upon the apparatus’ move from New York City to San Francisco, and assuming therein the principal standing role vacated by Eleanor Holm.
  4. 4. The method of claim 3, wherein the said theatrical apparatus is Billy Rose’s Aquacade, at 600 California Street, San Francisco.
  5. 5. The method of claim 3, further comprising swimming, for a period of five months, alongside the prior-art Olympic gold medalist and screen Tarzan Johnny Weissmuller, whose standard the records are measured against.
  6. 6. The method of claim 3, further comprising permitting the transferred records to be observed at the said apparatus by scouts of the Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer studio.
  7. 7. The method of claim 6, further comprising releasing the inventor, upon the observation, into several small roles, and then into a role shared with Mickey Rooney, at which point the method is finished and the career is not.
  8. 8. A standing length of water of the class 287-9 kind, wherein the water holds the records of any one of claims 1 through 7 and is provisionally granted at pool depth.
  9. 9. An apparatus for the exhibition of held records, comprising a moved show, a vacated role, an assuming inventor, and five months of the year 1940 in operable relation, whereby the load is exhibited, observed, and released in that order and no other.
