Thursday, 14 July 1999 · Durban, Natal · Fifty Cents

The Durban Evening Ledger

Classified & Personal Columns · Page 12 of 24 · No. 4,871

Last ads for to-morrow's paper: 3 p.m. sharp. Fifty cents a line, minimum three lines, prepaid. The Ledger takes no charge for the name of your curry, but it will not print two of them.

A Food & Provisions · Buns, Loaves & Curry

Bunny Chow, Whole or by the Quarter

One loaf of white bread, hollowed to a bowl, filled from a mutton curry of the old Durban cut, salad on the side, thumbed-hole facing you the way it should. Served from the van at 441 Umbilo Road, Camps Bay, from 11 a.m. to 4 p.m., Tuesdays to Saturdays. Ask for S. Ram. The quarter loaf — the kota, as the bazaar says — is R6.50 and will sit exactly in one lap. The skhambane, half a loaf, is R11. No phone orders for the thumbed-hole; the thumb must be fitted, not specified.

Shibobo — the Full Loaf

For the party, the picnic, the funeral wake, the football final at Kings Park: the shibobo, one full loaf hollowed and filled, with the salad packed in a separate paper as is proper. From M. Naidoo's, 91 Grey Street. Call 32-4172 by noon for afternoons from Friday. A shibobo is not a quarter loaf stretched over a quarter loaf's price; we will not mix the names in the ledger, and neither should you.

Sikhani Chutney, by the Kilogram

Dark, sweet-sour, the colour of the last light on the bay. The chutney that goes on the edge of the loaf where the curry does not reach. Weighed, not guessed, at the counter of M. Naidoo's, 91 Grey Street. Two hundred grams to a traveller's card, one kilogram to a family, more to a van. The jar must be yours or borrowed honestly.

Wanted: A Second Hand for the Lid

One left hand, steady, wanted to lift the curry pot lid at a Grey Street stall, 4 to 9 in the morning. The hand to be Indian, Malay, or otherwise used to a hot kitchen; the other conditions at the counter. Apply with one line about your own mother's curry, which is not a test and yet is.

B Lost

Lost — One Skhambane, Half Loaf

Lost on the 51 tram between Grey Street and the Beach front, Wednesday about 5 p.m., one skhambane, half a loaf, thumbed-hole on the left, curry of the chicken cut, salad still in its paper. Found by one who was not me. No reward but the name of the street, and a story of the crossing, which I will keep.

Lost — One Bread-Knife, Hollowing Size

Lost from the van at 441 Umbilo Road, a bread-knife, long blade, the kind that cuts the loaf's crown in one pass and leaves the bowl whole. Without it the loaf is only a loaf. Return to the van, Tuesday to Saturday. The van will wait, but the curry does not.

C Found

Found — One Note Card, Greyville South Stand

Found Tuesday, under the timing rail at the South Stand of Greyville Racecourse, a note card, little and fir-cut, found in a paper that had once been a wrapping. The card reads, in a left hand, word for word: “No haggling over the South Stand keep the line found a note a little fir-cut card with a ten-lap qualifying race, ten laps were done at once.” It is the whole of the card, and we print it the way it stands, because a found thing does not get to be corrected. Claim at the Gate 2 lost-property desk with the name of the curry the card came wrapped in, which no two of us will guess the same. No fee but that.

Found — One Serving of Salad

Found on a park bench at the Esplanade, one serving of salad in a paper, lettuce, tomato, onion, no bread, no curry, still cold. To the person who left the bunny somewhere and came back for the salad by itself, which the bench does not hold and will not hold again.

D Situations & Rewards

A Situation Wanted

A young man, well used to a hollowed loaf and a long queue, wants a position at a city where the bunny chow has already made its own name — the adapted one, the quarter loaf, the kota — and the counter does not have to explain the thumbed-hole. Wages by the hour; one free bunny a week not negotiable. References from two bazaars.

Reward

Reward for the return of one paper wrapper, the grey kind, found on a bus seat at 3 p.m., Wednesday, with a corner of white bread and the curry stain of a mutton bun still in the fold. The wrapper belonged to a man who eats standing at the counter and who will not get through the afternoon without it. Reward: a standing lunch at M. Naidoo's, the account in his name, the curry his choice.

E Personal

Missed Connection

To the woman at the Camps Bay van, red scarf, who took the thumbed-hole in the right hand and laughed at it, Tuesday: it is a left-hand hole, and I told you so, and you were right not to mind. The quarter loaf you shared was R6.50 and I paid the whole of it, which you will not find in the ledger and which is not a reproach. Meet me at 441 Umbilo Road, Thursday, 11 a.m. Bring your own salad. — A.

To the Van That Moved

To the van that was at the corner of Grey Street and Florida for eleven years and is not there now: the line in the morning was not for the bread, which any shop has, and not for the curry, which three places have. It was for the quarter loaf cut the way yours was, and for the lid lifting at seven. Wherever you stand now, the line goes with you. — The people at the corner.

A Name to a Loan

The man who lent me a paper at the shibobo counter, 28 June, when my own was wet from the bus: your name was not on it and I asked, and you said it twice, the second time slower, and I have it in a card box with the rest of my debts, which are mostly buns. When I meet you at a counter, I will buy the next one. — R.

F Notice to Vendors

Notice — On the Names of the Loaf

It is noticed in these columns that a quarter loaf is called, variously, a kota and a skhambane, and a whole loaf a shibobo, and that the name travels with the community that kept it. Vendors are reminded that the Ledger prints the name as the vendor's own counter says it, and that a dish known in one street under one name is not a different dish in the next street under another. The bread is hollowed the same. Only the word is adapted, which is what words do at a counter.

Readers may file their own buns and quarters beside the neighbouring ledgers: the evening ledger of The Little Monsters, which keeps its own classifieds for the 19 August, is kept at The Cure — Classifieds & Personals, and the halfpenny sheet of the Vanity Fair Gazette, Thursday 22 June 1848, which prints a situation wanted by a young orphan, is kept at The Vanity Fair Gazette, No. 137. Dining rooms that price a whole evening instead of a quarter loaf may be found at The Family — Gotham Ledger, Dining Column. All roads in the collection return to the Webstuffs shelf.
