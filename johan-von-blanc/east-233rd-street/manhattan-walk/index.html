5 WALK 10.2 MI

Manhattan — Battery Place to Inwood, on foot, in one day

Card No. 12 of the Street Register · 10 stops · 10.2 miles / 16.4 km · depart 8:15 a.m., Battery Place · return on the 1, Inwood–207th Street

The register sent the walk across the city line, because the 5 does not stop at the city line, and because the board wanted a walking count for the island the way it wanted one for the three miles of East 233rd Street. Manhattan is the most densely populated and the geographically smallest of the five boroughs of New York City: about 22.8 square miles of it, a little under 59 square kilometres, holding more than a million and a half people. It is coextensive with New York County, which is the smallest county by area in the state of New York and one of the smallest in the United States. The island sits near the southern tip of the state, almost entirely on Manhattan Island itself, and is centrally placed in the Northeast megalopolis; it is the urban core of the New York metropolitan area, and the city’s economic and administrative center, and it has been described as the cultural, financial, media, and entertainment capital of the world. Before European colonization, the island was Lenape country, and the oldest street on the card below, the Bowery, runs along that older path. The walk takes it all the long way down Broadway and up, ten and a second-tenths of a mile, and it is the same count the company kept on the road: plainly, with the numbers in, so the back bench can hear every one.

As with the thread: leave the card at each stop where there is a hall table, with the hour of departure written plainly in the corner, and do not cut anything with a knife.

  1. 10.0 mi

    Battery Place & Bowling Green 8:15 a.m.

    Start where the walk started uptown: Broadway begins at Battery Place and at Bowling Green, at the southern tip of the island, where the piers used to stand and the harbour is still there under the traffic. The court uptown took its count from here and wanted the intervals, not the stations; the record is kept at In re Broadway — Verbatim Record Excerpt. Turn the first corner and the island takes you: north-west on Broadway, and it does not stop.

  2. 21.1 mi

    City Hall Park & the County Line 9:05 a.m.

    The whole island is a county — New York County, coextensive with the borough, and the smallest county in the state of New York by area. The brick of the old City Hall stands in its park at the corner of Chalmers and Chambers, and this is the administrative center: the borough the city runs itself through. Count the blocks from here to the waterfront and you can believe the square miles; the register does not.

  3. 31.8 mi

    Trinity Church & Wall Street 9:40 a.m.

    The financial capital of the world, if you will have a capital, is a right angle at Trinity and Wall: the 1814 church on the south, the bronze bull to the south-east, and the market that prices the whole megalopolis in the mornings. The board’s standing joke is that the company once held a season’s receipts here, in a vault, and the vault’s ledger was in a hand not in ours. The buns keep: molasses and caraway, glazed while hot — card 1796 from the archipelago fleet, written out in full at Slack-Rope Buns — A Family Recipe.

  4. 43.4 mi

    The Bowery 10:50 a.m.

    Before European colonization the island was the Lenape path, and the Bowery is the oldest street on the island, a Dutch road from the battery to the upper town that keeps the old line even where the buildings are glass. Walk it at a pace the back bench can hear; it narrows and widens on the old survey, and at 3rd Avenue it turns the corner onto the numbered streets and gives you the grid: 137 of them, twelve avenues across, and a compass that has been arguing with the river for two hundred and fifty years.

  5. 54.2 mi

    Union Square 11:35 a.m.

    The market square, the linden avenue, the streetcar stop where the 5 used to run on the surface before the tunnel took it under. Lunch here, and leave the card at the corner deli’s table; the hour of departure in the corner, plainly. Halfway is not a stop the register likes, but the count is honest: four and a second-tenths miles walked, and the island still going north.

  6. 66.5 mi

    Times Square & 42nd Street 1:20 p.m.

    Here the 5 comes up Broadway to its own name, the Times Square–42nd Street complex, and the media and the entertainment of the capital are the two faces of one corner. This is the stop the two-and-five riders keep. At 1:20, on a Sunday the week after the thread closed, a card was found at the hall table of the platform bookshop, written plainly in the corner the company’s way, and it reads, word for word:

    WPRdReg Two-and-five rider Sun 08:19 Post 4 Good.

    Handle, rank, day, hour, the post it answered, and the whole review of the walk in one syllable. The riders of Thread No. 88 know the format; Post 4 is the one that gave the intervals for the two-and-five riders, and Good is the whole of the verdict, the way the obituary gave him. We have not argued with it.

  7. 77.2 mi

    The West Side of Central Park 2:30 p.m.

    Two and a half miles of the island’s green margin, on the Broadway side of Central Park, from 59th to 110th. The smallest borough holds the largest park the city has, which the register files under paradoxes, without a note. The lamps the company carried are not in the park; the park is in the count. Water, benches, and the smell of the pond the whole way, and the last bench of the west drive kept for the back bench.

  8. 89.1 mi

    125th Street, Harlem 4:10 p.m.

    The corner at 125th Street and the 6: Marcus Garvey Park, the bandshell, the bakery on the corner that will bake you the slack-rope bun if you quote the card — 1796, molasses and caraway, glazed while hot — and it is the same recipe that sailed aboard the archipelago fleet and that the buns card keeps. The company’s habit is that wherever it lodged, one card was left at the hall table; Harlem is where the lamps burned longest, and the cards are still being left.

  9. 99.8 mi

    157th Street 5:25 p.m.

    The 1 at 157th Street, and the last numbered street with the island’s name still in the block. The obituary gave its man the way this card gives its island: plainly, with the numbers in. The director died at Karlskrona on the twenty-first of January 1796, and here at 157th the island is only a block and a half from ending, which is the whole of the arithmetic of the smallest borough: 22.8 square miles, a million and a half souls, and the count goes on.

  10. 1010.2 mi

    Inwood & the Last Look North 6:05 p.m.

    Inwood, 207th Street, the northern tip. From the corner of the last block you can see over the water to the Bronx — Woodlawn, and the park beyond it — and the walk is the same view the register keeps, only from the other end: the borough you walked in one below the one you started in three miles below the one that answers it. Take the 1 south or walk back the way the thread went, and take the line with you: the braid gets unplaited, one strand at a time, the last to the host; the island gets walked, one mile at a time, the last to the back bench.
