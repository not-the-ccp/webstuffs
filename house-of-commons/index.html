Thread 88 — The Lower House · posted 04 July, 09:02

Before anyone sits down, the record, word for word. The House of Commons is the lower house of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. Like the upper house, the House of Lords, it meets in the Palace of Westminster in London, England. The House of Commons is an elected body consisting of 650 members known as members of Parliament (MPs), who are elected to represent constituencies by the first-past-the-post system and hold their seats until Parliament is dissolved.

The House of Commons of England began to evolve in the 13th and 14th centuries. In 1707 it became the House of Commons of Great Britain after the political union with Scotland, and from 1801 it also became the House of Commons for Ireland after the political union of Great Britain and Ireland. The thread may note that a house which has kept its seat since before most of the counties learned their own spelling is in no hurry.

The strong room keeps the roll in the green folio. The green of the folio is not the green of the chair at Macquarie Street, and the desk has not settled the argument. The thread is open. The kettle is on.

the_desk wrote:
“650 members known as members of Parliament (MPs)”

Objection, and I have filed it in the margin where it belongs. Six hundred and fifty is the roll as it stands today; the ledger has held 815 in it, in the years of the coalition seats, and has held 640, in the years after the boundary review trimmed the excess. First-past-the-post keeps a habit of this: the candidate with the most votes takes the seat, and the seat is held until Parliament is dissolved, whoever wins, however narrow, and the roll is whatever the Act of the day says. I am not asking the desk to change the title of the thread. I am asking it to remember that 650 is a number the house has worn before, the way a uniform is worn again.

The pedantry is the whole post. The thread may argue it, or it may pour the tea.
