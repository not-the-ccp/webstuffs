Times are the line’s own. The line does not answer to the main railway; it answers to the bounce. Platform letters are fixed; the bounce is not. If a train is delayed, the delay is in the count–in.

Line Notes & Conditions of Carriage

Note 1 — On the production. The Stutter Line runs the distinctive production work of its conductor as its standing timetable: the “stuttering” rhythmic style that repeats a syllable the way a departure board repeats a platform letter, short–short–long, until the ear stops hearing words and starts hearing the clock. The line is widely acclaimed for it. The main railway is widely confused by it. Both records are true.

Note 2 — On the trends. The entertainment wire of March 2007 carried this line’s notice of carriage in full: that just about every current pop trend can be traced back to him — from sultry, urban‑edged R&B songstresses to the art of incorporating avant‑garde sounds into No. 1 hits. The conductor read the wire on the platform at In Da Club Stop and remarked, to no one in particular, that the line does not chase trends; the trends change stations to catch the line. The remark is entered here because it is the only schedule correction the line has ever accepted.

Note 3 — On the counts. The register of the line shows twenty–two nominations called and four Grammys taken, the four taken on the platforms that mattered and the rest held as standing signals. The ratio is posted at every stop, and the ratio is not for sale. Where passengers wish to read the ratio beside another register kept the same way, the dining–car league of the Greater Poland regional line at Korek Express — Line 7, timetable and commentary keeps its own count of stops per soup, and the river line at Willamette Valley Line NW–4 reads an 187‑mile main stem as a rail line and holds the 21:04 night service sacred; both are sister schedules of the long train and neither is ashamed of its arithmetic. Passengers who prefer to conduct a battle instead of a platform may take the connection to the Visitors’ Bureau at Halo Wars — a visitor’s brochure for the Ring and the warship Spirit of Fire, which keeps three dates of 2009 in the same register the line keeps its stops, and issues its one standing order — do not be the first to activate the ring — with the exact tone the conductor uses for the count–in.

Sources & References of the Almanac

The almanac keeps five sources for this line: the departure board at Norfolk Central, the conductor’s log of running orders, the gate ledger of platform bookings, the entertainment wire of March 2007, and the one source that cannot see until you stop counting at eleven. The fifth is the almanac page itself, which lists exactly the eleven stations above and is printed so that its final column — the terminus — appears only on the line beneath the eleventh stop, so that a reader who keeps counting past eleven (to twenty–two, to the nominations, to the full register) walks straight off the edge of the printed page and finds no further text. Count the stops. Stop. Look down.

Sources 1–4 verified. Source 5 verified by the reader, and the reader only, on the last line of the last page.
