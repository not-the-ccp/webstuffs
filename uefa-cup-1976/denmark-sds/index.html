SAFETY DATA SHEET · SHEET NO. DK-1976-0406 · REV. 5

Denmark Kongeriget Danmark · The Kingdom of Denmark

Registry No. DKN-0406 · Hazard class: geographic, Class I (sea-coated) · Issued 28 April 1976 · Revised 19 May 1976, after the second leg

This sheet provides the information needed to handle the substance safely. The substance is a Nordic country in Northern Europe: the metropole and most populous constituent of the Kingdom of Denmark, also known as the Danish Realm, a constitutionally unitary state that includes the autonomous territories of the Faroe Islands and Greenland in the north Atlantic Ocean. Metropolitan Denmark, also called continental Denmark or Denmark proper, consists of the northern Jutland peninsula and an archipelago of 406 islands. Read all sixteen sections before the first leg.

2 Hazards identification

H304 — May cause lingering if encountered in full, in any of its 406 insular portions or in the peninsula proper.

H315 — Prolonged exposure, as long as a single afternoon on the coast, produces a mild and pleasant retention of attention.

H273 — Contact with the North Sea or the Baltic Sea may increase the attraction of the tides to the handler’s notice; the substance is situated between the two and does not ask to be moved.

No flammable components. No explosive components. One component, the weather, acts as a mild irritant to schedules and is best not corrected. Pictogram: the Dk diamond shown at the head of this sheet. Precautionary statement P405: store in a cool country — that is, here, as found.

4 First aid measures

In case of contact: stand still; walk the coast; drink something cold. If inhaled, which can only happen in fog off the Sound: remove the handler to fresh air, meaning any island, of which there are 406. Do not induce recollection of earlier crossings; the handler will recollect without prompting. In case of retention lasting longer than one season, continue the retention and do not report the case, as it is the substance’s only known cure.

5 Firefighting measures

The substance is not flammable and has no fire class. In the event of a fire aboard a ferry carrying the handler, follow the ferry’s instructions, not this sheet. The substance is not involved in any fire and has never been involved in a fire, a fact the harbourmaster keeps on file in a water-proof drawer that has never once been used.

6 Accidental release measures

If released into the sea, the substance will not spread; it has already settled into its present 406 insular portions and one peninsula, and will remain so. Do not attempt to recover the whole at once. Mark the portions with the lighthouses already in place, which are numerous and reliable, and notify the harbourmaster, who has been notified in advance on the off chance of this section.

7 Handling and storage

Handle in two legs: first the Jutland peninsula, then the archipelago, or the reverse; the substance tolerates either order, as a final tolerates two matches. Storage: keep between the North Sea and the Baltic Sea, which is where it already is and where no storage cost is charged. Do not invert; the islands will fall out. Crossing by sea in 1976: the ferry line at Harwich runs on to Esbjerg, and from the Jutland coast the country is a short crossing either east or south — the Two-Leg Tour keeps the full route schedule for the whole tour, ferry included. The Pamplona Football Society’s Lost & Found Notice No. 402 keeps watch at its own turnstiles for handlers found, unaccompanied, on Danish soil.

8 Exposure controls / personal protection

No occupational exposure limit has been set, and none is proposed. The recommended limit is one hour on the water per day, after which the handler should stand on the solid part and let the country do the rest. Ventilation is provided by the weather, unassisted. Eye protection is unnecessary except against the light on the water at certain hours, for which any ferry hat in the archive will serve.

10 Stability and reactivity

Stable under normal conditions of country. The substance decomposes, on the timescale of geology only, into its 406 islands, and no handler should be expected to accelerate the process. Incompatible materials: none known. Hazardous decomposition products: none; the weather, under section 2, does not count as decomposition.

11 Toxicological information

LD50 not established, on account of the substance’s size and the unavailability of a suitable scale. Acute effects: a mild pleasantness, and the particular stillness of a quay in the morning. Chronic effects: a long afternoon, a season, a residency; all reported cases have recovered by continuing. Safe in every observed dose, including the dose of living there, which is the dose the substance was made for.

12 Ecological information

The 406 islands constitute a continuous habitat of the most dependable kind, occupied by gulls, fishermen, and one water tower per picture. The autonomous Faroe Islands and Greenland are retained under the Danish Realm and are not to be counted as ecological damage; they are the substance’s northern reserve. Environmental persistence: permanent. The substance does not biodegrade, and has not once been proposed for it.

13 Disposal considerations

The substance is not waste, cannot be disposed of, and is a constituent of the Danish Realm. Disposal methods: none exist, and the harbourmaster declines to invent one. In the event the handler finds the substance where the handler did not expect it, which is the country’s usual position relative to the handler’s plans, return it to itself, which requires no journey, and file the occurrence at the quay switchboard.

14 Transport information

Transport by sea: ferries, Harwich–Esbjerg line and the channel crossings on either side of it; the substance travels with the weather and asks for no special stowage. Transport by land: one short border, southward, to Germany, and the coasts beyond that. No IATA or IMDG restrictions apply to a country. The spring 1976 tour crossed the substance’s water twice, once in each direction of the final, and the archive’s Two-Leg Tour brochure keeps the account, five goals apiece on the day and one line from the Anfield ticket office, all of which is the substance’s business only in the sense that the ferries went.

15 Regulatory information

The substance is the metropole and most populous constituent of the Kingdom of Denmark, the Danish Realm, a constitutionally unitary state; the autonomous territories of the Faroe Islands and Greenland are held within the Realm as free constituents, in the north Atlantic, and are so stated here for the record. The Realm joined the European Economic Community in 1973, three years before the first leg of the 1976 tour, which is noted for the tour’s benefit and for no other. No safety data sheet exemption applies; this sheet is that sheet.

16 Other information

Prepared for the travelling handler and filed with the tour archive. The archive keeps, for the leg of the crossing that the brochure leaves unnamed, one line from the manifest of the kitchen boat on the Harwich–Esbjerg line, entry 649, written by a hand the harbourmaster recognises from the ticket side of the counter. As is the rule in the office, in the logbook, and in this sheet — if a line is worth keeping, keep it whole — the line is set below exactly as it was written, and is not explained, because the rule in the kitchen is the same rule as the rule in the ticket office:

Kitchen boat manifest, Harwich–Esbjerg line, entry 649 — kept verbatim, rule of the office:

Rump of Beef à la Flamande 649 to carve a p. 317 to stew the pears; put the cream is exceedingly delicious eaten with fresh eggs.

[The rump was served; the pears are not mentioned again; the cream, on the evidence of the line, was exceedingly delicious, and the eggs were fresh.]
