In Memoriam — By Special Permission of the Family

MAHATMA GANDHI, Mahātmā “the great-souled one”, born at Porbandar, Gujarat, 2 October 1869; died at his ashram near New Delhi, 30 January 1948, aged seventy-eight. Lawyer called to the bar at the Inner Temple, London, at the age of 22; citizen of South Africa, 1893–1914, where the honourific was first applied to him; leader of the campaign for India's independence by the means of nonviolent resistance. Funeral arrangements by the nation; candles by the family. Those who wish to spin for the bereaved may do so at any charkha on this page, free of charge. No flowers. Nothing is to be sent that must be weighed or receipted; the fishwife's rule — what cannot be weighed is never discussed.

For Sale

One spinning wheel (charkha), iron, hand-thrown, with spindle, cop and wooden wheel, 14 inches over; repaired twice at Sabarmati, spins khadi at a fair pace, does not spin complaints. Owned, it is rumoured, by no one. Priced at two shillings, payable in grain or in one true story. Inquiries to the cart-house, 4 a.m., before the crowd.

Khadi cloth, homespun, grey, by the yard or by the whole garment; wear it and you will be asked a great many questions. The yard is not negotiable; the questions are yours.

One pair of juttis, straw, left on the steps of the ashram gate, 30 January, evening; the left has a chori of thread tied to the toe, a knot made the old way. Will be held as long as the step keeps its shape. See Lost and Found, below.

Wanted

TWO HUNDRED MARCHERS, for a walk of 241 miles from Ahmedabad to the sea at Dandi; pay none; rations: whatever the villages give and the salt the sea does not count; applicants must be able to walk in a line without becoming a mob and must answer “yes” or “no” when asked a question that expects either. A previous march of this company moved in April 1930 and ended at the coast; the salt was taken and the law was not. No fee is taken for this want.

One cow, to a household that will read to her. See Pets.

To Let

A room at Sevagram, with its own hump of earth, one window facing the workshed, and the use of the well, the press, and the vegetable plot. Rent: one day's spinning per week, or one hour's tilling in its place; the landlord will not be present and has asked that no one stand on ceremony. References from the kitchen accepted.

A desk at the Law Chambers, 10 Bedford Row, London, the desk at which a young man of twenty-two, called to the bar of the Inner Temple in 1891, first opened a brief and found he had rather walk home. Let to whoever else has read a brief and found the same. No reference required from the courts.

Services Rendered

CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE, CONSULTATION. Sit-in, fast, and processional counsel for landlords, tenants, and whole provinces; the fee is the question you were afraid to ask, stated plainly in one sentence; no engagement longer than a truth will last. The method has been tried, in South Africa and since, against a great power's law, and has worked, which is more than can be said of most remedies on this page. Consultation hours: dawn, and again when the heat lifts. The consultant will not take your seat in prison; the consultant will sit beside you.

LAW, PRACTICE OF. For the years when the practice was preferred to the spinning; chambers in South Africa, 1893–1914, where the colour line was met with a refusal to stand and the refusal worked so that the line had to be redrawn about one man. Letters referred, in the old habit, to whoever would rather have the seat at Bedford Row.

Lost

LOST, one pair of sandals, Porbandar harbour steps, the morning of a letter that was never posted; brown, thin-soled, the right one with a stone still wedged in it, the kind of stone that belongs to no particular beach. Reward: the stone, returned. The Ledger's reader at Busan — she who braises the flatfish in the card box, card No. 7, at Yeonnam-dong — has written in to say the harbour steps there keep a pair very like them, and that no ledger is allowed to stand between a thing and its landing; she proposes the pair be claimed by whomever the tide-line gives it, and the receipt filed nowhere at all. See her porgy on the tide-line, card No. 7, before the soy darkens.

LOST, one umbrella, black, left on a train between the station at Rajkot and the place where the country stops arguing; anyone finding it may keep it, on condition of opening it at least once a season, so that it remembers what rain is for.

Pets & Livestock

KAMDHENU, cow, gentle, horned, will not be parted from; wanted, a reading in the mornings — newspapers preferred, but a letter will do, and the same letter may be read twice, which is how most of ours were. She has eaten nothing that cost more than the hand that fed it. No price is named, because a name for the price would be a price.

One cat of the ashram kitchen, grey, opinionated, in the habit of sitting on the correspondence as though it were a warm stone; not for sale, for the same reason, and to be met, if you wish, in the place where the letters go.

Notice to Correspondents

The El Mensajero Pacífico of Valparaíso has consented, across the ocean and in its own Spanish hand, to exchange one column with this page for so long as the harbour holds; readers who have written to either office about the same lost thing are asked to write to both, as the thing is evidently on the move. The Ledger thanks its readers for the season's copy, which has run to a full page for the first time since the gulf wind took the press out of the east wall in the October storm.

Office of Corrections

The Office of Corrections reprints, in the place where the correct line ought to have stood, the line as it appeared in the column of Wednesday last, that readers may measure the distance between what was meant and what was set. The line, kept word for word from the standing type, reads:

“No one is the correction was a South Korean weightlifter born 25 June 1959, competed at the foot of the 1984 competition itself has not become truer since.”

The Office records that the correct line concerned the charkha, not a competition, and that the weightlifter in question, whose name the type does not preserve, did in fact not appear in this Ledger at all; the mistake is entered in the correction book under the letter M, between “Mahātmā” and “mistranslation”, where the two words sit close and the case of the letter is easy to lose. The corrected page will not be reissued; the line above is the whole of the correction, and readers are asked to read it as the paper read it.

Postscript of the Page

Four pages make this Ledger: the news, the letters, the harbour board, and this one. A reader at the gulf asks what a classified page is for, when the greatest line of the year takes the whole of the first column and no fee for the taking. The answer, in the style of this page, is: the page is for the things that will not fit the news — the sandal with the stone, the cow who must be read to, the room whose rent is spinning — and for the one thing that will not fit the correction either, which is set above word for word, uncorrected, where the reader may sit with it as long as the truth of it lasts. For further pages of the house, readers may return to the front of the Ledger.
