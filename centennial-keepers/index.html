Newsletter of the Keepers of the Open Card · met in the vault above the counter, 2140 Sands Lane, Las Vegas · founded 1971

No. 26 · Friday, 16 August 1996 · Price: one line in the guestbook NEW!

I. From the Secretary

The Games closed on the 4th of August at the Centennial Olympic Stadium, and the vault light went off the same night, which is how this club does a closing. From 19 July until the last of the four days, the club kept the scoreboard ledger on the vault table against the Sands Lane window: 197 nations on the list, 29 sports, 10,318 athletes, and one television with the football channel. The Games of the XXVI Olympiad — Atlanta 1996, commonly the Centennial Olympic Games — marked the 100th anniversary of Athens 1896, the first modern Games, and the club resolved, by a vote of nine to one, that the centennial toast would be water from the No. 1 Jug, because Rule 1 says wipe after the table, not before, and the toast is a table. These were the fourth Summer Games held in the United States — St. Louis 1904, Los Angeles 1932 and 1984, and now Atlanta — making the United States the first country to have three different cities host the Summer Olympics, and the first in which the Summer Games fell in a different year than the Winter Games, the Winters of this cycle having sat in 1994 in Lillehammer, where nobody in the vault will argue the club should have been, the club having been in the vault. The ledger for the season runs 63 pages. The Secretary has ruled them all.

II. The Gold Cartouche

The club met on purpose for the men's singles tennis final on 30 July. Andre Agassi took the gold against the Yugoslav Ivan Ljubičić, 4–6 6–3 6–4 6–4, in 55 minutes by the vault clock, and the manager of the hotel on Las Vegas Boulevard — keeper of the Week 47 jug, who has used it for iced tea on his desk for a decade — came in with the jug and we held the gold in it, which is what jugs are for, and which the card knows, because the base carries it: the eighth of the eight major cartouches, the gold medal at Atlanta, July 1996, chased by the same two hands since 1974. The base also carries its patina worked with old court dust and clippings, the mowers' share, a colour this club has always called the essence, and it is a colour other clubs have asked about without being told, because the essence is not for showing off. The whole card, No. 70 of the catalogue, is still open, and can be read at the silverworks under Catalogue Card No. 70, The No. 1 Jug. The week number under that base is not for showing off either. It is for the next owner, and the one after.

IV. On the Fells, and Back

The walking party of 11 June is now fully accounted for, which took four weeks and is a record. Six members went up into the fells of Northumberland and Cumberland with boots, a tin of championship balls, and the kit bag, and stayed four days on the fells and came back all six of them, one of them on the last morning being found by the sound of the bell, which is the club's word for the voice of a member who has wandered. Two things of the club's went with them in the kit bag. The first was the Compact — the statutes in their little book, the spine of which has never yet been shown any part of the fells, because the party packed it and then, in the car at the top of the hill, found that they had in fact packed the cover of the Compact and not the Compact, and so no part of the Compact was shown anywhere, and the cover is now on the mantel with a pencil mark on it. The second thing was the club's spare egg tile, blue, from the box that plays on the island east of Mauritius, taken along on the strength of a bet that nobody has remembered making. The tile did not come back. What follows is the register, kept in the box's own hand.

V. The Lost Shelf

The club registers every loss in one sentence, in the style of the box from which the lost thing came — the box of The Rodrigues Solitaire, Player's Manual No. 9, Fourth Printing, Gatehouse Games, whose spare the club keeps for its games of forgiveness, and which, like all ledgers on this shelf, closes where the record closes and is not corrected. The register as of 16 August 1996 reads, in full:

  1. The cover of the Compact, last seen in the car at the top of the hill, 11 June; on the mantel, unclaimed, with a pencil mark.
  2. One egg tile, the colour of the essence, the size of a spine never yet shown any part of the compact among the fells of Northumberland and Cumberland.
  3. One tin of championship balls, sent to the party from the friend of the club in Philadelphia who keeps the Dunlop racket, returned with the balls and one of the four orders still unaccounted for.
  4. A bell counter of pale bone, taken off the track at the May meeting to hold down a page of the scoreboard; held, not lost, in the Secretary's cardigan pocket.

Claim in person at the vault, with two witnesses. The egg tile, should it be offered back, will be examined against the essence at noon, which is the only light in which the colour is certain. The box has room for both outcomes, as the manual says of the pair.
