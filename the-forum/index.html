The Forum, Norwich

Tuesday, 14 December 2001  ·  Norfolk, England  ·  7 inches of snow and holding

NEW: the Forum page is up *** the winter ledger has its first entry *** please sign the guestbook below *** the old library, 1994, is remembered here ***

Dear diary,

The snow began at 8:40 on the platform at London Liverpool Street and it had not stopped by the time I found the new building by the river. I stood in front of the glass faæde with one glove off and my bag under one arm, and the whole front of the building looked like a shop window the size of a cathedral — the inner steel frame showing through the glazing like the ribs of a very large, very well-behaved fish, and the brick wall running around it all the way, like a belt, so that the glass is never quite free of its masonry. It is a building you can see into and a building that will not let you in without knocking. I knocked, and I was let in.

I had been here three days ago while the scaffolding was still coming down, and I had already decided I would never write about the old Central Library without the name of its replacement, even though the name changed twice. Technopolis was the first proposal, and it was rejected. The second version — the one they actually built — went by New Technopolis for a while before it settled, in the end, on simply the Forum. Opened this year, 2001, and designed by the firm of Sir Michael Hopkins and Partners, to stand over the very ground where the old library burnt down in 1994. I stood for a minute in that spot inside, on the west side, and I did not say anything, which I think was correct.

The Norfolk and Norwich Millennium Library sits at the west end of the building, and if you round the main atrium you get to offices and commercial spaces and studios — the BBC has its round of rooms around the atrium, which means that on any given morning there is a radio voice somewhere inside this glass box talking to the whole country while I wait for the kettle on the first floor. Three buildings in one, and all of them keeping their own clocks.

The first entry of the winter ledger this evening: 7 inches on the Forum’s roof, measured from the east stair with the carpenter’s stick. I have written it in the blue book on the kitchen shelf, and A seven-inch followed in the ledger keeps its habits accordingly — once it is down, every forecast over Norfolk reads the same, and I shall read it back in the same hand, and I suspect the building will keep its share: glass, brick, and steel, all three in their places, all three keeping their habits.

That is all for to-night. The kettle is singing and, as I write, the snow has stopped at exactly the number the ledger wants. — E.

— written from the room above the chandler’s, Norwich, under the blue lamp
