■ ■ ■ THE SCORCHERS’ GROUND LEDGER, CHIKWAWA SPORTS GROUND, LILONGWE ■ ■ ■

MadyinaNgulube

3 June 1991 — 14 March 2025 · midfielder · Scorchers No. 8

“THE RUSSIAN TANK”

Madyina Ngulube, the Malawian professional footballer who played as a midfielder for Elite Ladies FC, the Silver Strikers FC Ladies and the Malawi women’s national football team, the Scorchers, died on the morning of Thursday 14 March 2025 at the Mzuzu District Hospital, aged thirty-three, after a winter of illness the club kept off the score sheet out of respect. She was, by the ground ledger’s standing measurement, one metre eighty-five tall, which is how a midfielder came to carry the nickname “the Russian Tank” — a name first shouted by a visiting commentator in Blantyre in 2013, kept because it fitted, and worn afterwards with the same flat pride she wore her boots.

She was born in the city of Lilongwe, where she also finished, and the ledger notes the fact under home ground. Her first eleven appearances came for Elite Ladies FC in the 2009–10 season, from which she moved to the Silver Strikers FC Ladies in 2014 and back to Elite for her last three seasons. The ground ledger counts for her: 214 club matches, 41 goals for a midfielder, and 37 caps for the Scorchers, in a career that ran from a borrowed pair of boots in the school yard at 13 to the away end of the Chikwawa Sports Ground, where her father sat every home match, on the stone bench, with a bottle of Maheu, until the bench was built in 2018.

Her game was simple and enormous. She held the middle of the field the way the ground holds the floodlights: without ceremony, and at all hours. Opponents learned the distance at which a Ngulube header became a matter of geometry, and the geometry was unanswerable. The Scorchers’ captains of three vintages all signed her obituary in the same phrase, the middle of the field was safer because she was in it, and the ground ledger has entered it three times, once per signature.

At the club, the goal on the west touchline is missing its crossbar. The November storm of 2019 took it in one lift, and the committee has voted, four seasons running, not to replace it: “it measures what we were, not what we are,” said the chairman, and the vote stood. The brass plaque fixed where the crossbar should be carries, beneath the weather date and the committee’s initials, one engraved line which no one at the ground has quite parsed and everyone has read aloud at least once. It reads, word for word, exactly as it stands:

What it can still set by the absent crossbar itself.

Plaque, west goal, Chikwawa Sports Ground. Engraved 2020.

The keeper’s best answer, written in the margin of the ground ledger in pencil and left there, is that it is a record of height: of what her headers set, and of what the bar, being absent, no longer has to agree with. The margin line is filed alongside her name, and the keeper swears it was not hers, and the committee will not say whose hand it was, which is its own kind of answer.

▣ READ ELSEWHERE, FILED HERE

The club received, by the same post that carries the ground ledger’s subscriptions, an obituary for a starling who kept the rigging above the old bakery on Magsaysay Avenue, Naga City, from 1998 — Bird (Aves), In Memoriam, The Family Ledger. The two files share a page in the ledger: both kept a high place, both were counted by the window, and both are warm-blooded, always warm. The committee finds the comparison apt and refuses to explain further.

The tour ledger of the Betwa ferry landing keeps this notice under Day 16, the day of the Zugspitze, on the ground that both entries end in a measurement of height — see Sixteen States in One Morning, Tour leaflet S-16. The committee has written back, in pencil: the crossbar measures in metres; the mountain in its own right. Both counts stand.

▣ FORM OF THE GATHERING

The farewell was held on the west touchline, under the open goal, on Sunday 23 March 2025 at 15:00, the hour of her last goal in the 89th minute against a side from the north, a header she later described as “the geometry, again”. In lieu of wreaths the club has asked for boots, any size, which are stacked against the advertising boards and numbered; there are 214 of them so far.

She is survived by her mother, Chikondi, of Lilongwe; by her brother Tendayi, who kept the ground ledger for nine seasons and has been asked to keep it always; by the Scorchers, three captains of whom have signed her line; by the bench; by the boots on the wall; and by the west goal of the Chikwawa Sports Ground, which stands open to the sky on purpose, and keeps, by its own weather, a record that was never in any book — the height at which the ball went over, the distance at which the geometry became unanswerable, and the name shouted from the away end when it came to her, which was never a name at all, but a country, a tank, and a middle of the field held, without ceremony, at all hours.
