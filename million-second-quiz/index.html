The Million Second Quiz

A Radio Play · Script Excerpt

Original recording: the live week, September 9 – 19, 2013, wide broadcast · Reconstructed for the archive from the Studio 4 cue log, the tally board, and one very good cup of coffee in the machine room. One line is kept word for word.

Cast as logged

RYAN — the host; the man the audience was told everything would happen to. PIP — contestant 7, chairholder, 61, a retired ferry-signaller from Duluth. MARGO, DOTTIE, BEN, LUIS — the sequestered four. CUES — the cue-card boy, who never once flinched. NARRATOR.

SCENE I — T-MINUS THE WHOLE THING · STUDIO 4, 9:00 PM, SEPTEMBER 9, 2013

[SFX: A TALLY LIGHT BUZZES ONCE, THEN HOLDS. UNDER IT, A SINGLE TICK, MECHANICAL, SET TO ONE PER SECOND. THE TICK IS THE CLOCK. THE TICK IS THE MONEY. IT WILL NOT STOP FOR ELEVEN DAYS, THIRTEEN HOURS, FORTY-SIX MINUTES, AND FORTY SECONDS.]

NARRATOR They gave it one million seconds, and then they gave it one more, because the audience voted, and then they stopped giving it seconds and started taking them out. It is a show about a chair. The chair holds the money. The money holds, at one dollar a second, a number that climbs whether you like it or not, and the only way to hold the chair is to beat somebody at trivia and make them hold it instead. Four others sit in a room we will call the vault, because the log calls it that, and at any given moment the four highest scorers outside the chair are the four in the vault, and if you are not in the chair and you are not in the vault, you are a person on a show about a chair, which is a sentence the script keeps returning to.

RYAN (to the room, as if the room were the chair) Welcome to the longest game show in the history of the longest game show. You have one million seconds. That is eleven days, thirteen hours, forty-six minutes, and forty seconds, and when the last one lands, four of you keep every dollar your seconds earned, and then the four of you climb a stepladder — one at a time, each one a rung the next one has to take from them — all the way to a top prize of two million dollars. (beat) The tick starts now, and I have read the memo: I am not allowed to make it sound like a joke, so I will make it sound like arithmetic.

[SFX: THE TICK. THE TICK. THE TICK. SLOWLY, AS IF FROM ANOTHER ROOM, THE HUM OF THE VAULT'S COOLING.]

NARRATOR Pip was in the chair at 9:04. Pip had been a ferry-signaller for thirty-one years, and the ferry-signaller's entire profession is to sit in one chair, in one room, and keep an eye on the number that says when the boat is due. He told the microphone that the chair was "the most honest object in the building." He was right, and the record shows it, because a chair asks you nothing except to stay in it.

SCENE II — THE VAULT · DAY FIVE · THE CUE CARD

MARGO (from the vault, tinny, four voices sharing one line) We have been in here since Tuesday. Dottie counts the water bottles. Luis writes the scores on the wall in a marker and then erases them, and the wall is white now, and the wall is still white, and that is the trick. The host reads a question. We answer. We hold our rank. If somebody in the chair beats us at their next match, a door opens on our end of the wall, and we go down to be a person on a show about a chair. We have stopped discussing it. We discuss bread.

[SFX: A DOOR, FAR OFF, OPENING ON A HINGE THAT HAS HAD OIL ONCE.]

CUES (dry, close to the mic) The last card came up at 00:13:41 before the close of the run, and it was not a question. It read, word for word: "My late uncle, who had served him, and that page, when it points at Geneva, Nairobi, Vienna, and The Hague." The log has the card. The log does not have a reading of it, so I will give you the one the machine room gave, which is that the uncle had served the executive producer in an earlier life of this building, and that the page in question is the loose world-clock page in the prompter binder — the page with the dial — and that a dial pointing at all four cities at once is the crew's old signal that the wide feed is about to change. Geneva, Nairobi, Vienna, and The Hague: the four cities the vault's satellite windows are pointed at, the four places the four of you are, for one night, being kept.

PIP (chair, unhurried) So the card is a door. Someone's uncle opened a door, and the dial said where the four of you were sitting, and the show is a chair, and the chair is a door. (long beat; the tick under everything) I have held this chair for a while now. The number has climbed past the part of the number I can say out loud. Somebody is going to take it from me, that is the rule, and the rule is fine. When it happens, say the number. Say it the way it was, to all the digits, the way the log wants it.

SCENE III — LAST SECONDS · THE STEP LADDER · SEPTEMBER 19, 2013

[SFX: THE TICK, ALONE, FOR SIX SECONDS. THEN IT STOPS. SILENCE, THE LONGEST IN THE BUILDING. THEN FOUR HANDS, ON A MIC, AT ONCE.]

NARRATOR When time ran out, the four top scorers kept the money their seconds had made, and the stepladder went up: one at a time, each contestant a rung the next had to take from them, every question worth more than the one before, the whole run worth, at the top of it, two million dollars. The tick stopped at eleven days, thirteen hours, forty-six minutes, and forty seconds, exactly, and the tally light in Studio 4 went off at 9:00 and the chair — (beat) — the chair stayed in the room. They never told us what they did with it. Some chairs you hold, and some chairs hold you back, and the log keeps the card, and the dial, and the four cities, and the number, to all its digits, in the drawer where the machine room keeps the things the audience was not told the reading of.

CUES (closing, flat) End of log. The dial is pointing at all four cities again. That is not a signal. That is just a page.

[SFX: TALLY LIGHT BUZZES OFF. THE TICK, ONE LAST TIME, FROM ANOTHER ROOM.]
