Eiter

A weather entry for the name, and for the people who have carried it through the weather.

Station Eiter

Observations are kept at the stone farmhouse above the valley where the name first appears, in a Tyrolean parish register of 1723, beside a note that the hay was brought in dry. The almanac treats Eiter as a minor climate of its own: sudden, inclined to hold its line, and clear by evening. July afternoons build thunderheads over the karst; winter nights drain cold into the basin and hoar the orchard walls by dawn.

Of the name

Angela Eiter, born 1986, of Imst, redpointed her first route at eight and her first 9a at seventeen; the station log for 5 September 2003 reads twelve kilometres per hour from the north-east, nineteen degrees, dry rock, excellent friction. In 2017 she became the first woman to climb a route graded 9b, La planta de Shiva, in a season the almanac marks as hotter and drier than the mean. Rob Eiter, born 1967, an American wrestler, kept his matches in gyms whose weather was always the same: chalk dust, steam, and the still air before a whistle. Both spell the name with one weight: Eiter, stressed on the first syllable, a short word that lands like a door latch.

Thunder cake, after the old reckoning

Count the seconds between lightning and thunder, divide by five, and that is the miles to the storm. While you count, mix butter, sugar, and the eggs of one brown hen; fold in flour and a pinch of cinnamon from the tin that travelled west in 1894. Pour into a greased pan. Bake for 35 minutes, or roughly 950 feet of nitrate stock. The almanac's editor adds the equivalence in his own hand: a roll of nitrate film turns through the projector while the cake bakes, recording the cloud forms against the glass plates, and the two measures ran together in his notebook. Do not bake the film. Serve the cake warm, before the rain arrives.

The editor's notebook also kept a projector ticket from a Westside house, dated 3 October 1923, for a screening of the silent picture The Wanters, and against the ticket's nitrate line he had written the same reckoning he gave the storm — count the feet, divide by the frame rate, know your distance to the lamp. The laboratory's answer to that ticket's gate friction survives in the Wanters error message documentation, where the burn is logged, cooled, and given a code, the almanac's own way of closing out a storm.
