Field Notes No. 208

A Legislator of Metropolitan Lima — observed 2021–2026

Being the daily observations of Prof. E. Quiroga, Ornithologist (ret.), kept in the notebook formerly belonging to his father, from a window in Jesús María and one bench in the plaza of San Martín

SubjectAdriana Josefina Tudela Gutiérrez
Born20 August 1988, Lima
TradeLawyer; later politician
Office heldCongresswoman, Metropolitan Lima, term 2021–2026
Local nameLa del perchero — the one of the coat-rack
First observed28 July 2021, 6:40 a.m., Av. Argentina, southbound walk

28 July 2021 — Thursday · the first mark

18°C, overcast, no wind. The mist on the river bank lifted by seven.

Marked the subject for the first time at the southbound crossing on Avenida Argentina, half an hour before the galleries opened. She walks fast, alone, without the usual escort of aides, which in this habitat marks a specimen that has not yet learned to travel in a flock. Grey jacket, documents under one arm, a green bus pass she showed to the guard at the gate with the same flat patience with which lawyers anywhere show things. I counted her steps the whole block: forty-one. I write the number down because it is the only honest thing about a parliament, and numbers are what the river gives me in exchange.

Background, from the printed notices I kept in the field: born 20 August 1988, a lawyer by training, elected to the Congress of the Republic to represent Metropolitan Lima for the 2021–2026 term. The habitat is the one I know best — the same avenues I have counted birds on for thirty-one years, only she moves through it on foot, in reverse season, arriving as the pigeons leave and departing as they come back.

14 September 2022 — Friday · the roost

17°C, rain in the morning, sun by eleven. A single heron on the Malecón, all day, unmoved.

Sat at length on the bench in the plaza of San Martín, under the monument, and timed the galleries. The subject enters before nine in four of five mornings, which is not punctuality; it is an appetite. She roosts on the second gallery, seat 7C by my count, near the window, always on the side the light comes in from. The coat goes on the rail beside the seat — this habit gave her the local name in the canteen, la del perchero, and I record the name because a specimen that gives its inhabitants a name for it has already won half the observation. At 9:12 she opens the folder; at 10:03 she signs; at 10:10 she closes the folder without reading the last page, which the canteen women take for modesty and I take for what it is: a lawyer’s habit of knowing where a document ends.

Her feeding grounds, from the notice board: San Isidro on Tuesdays, La Molina on Thursdays, the municipal offices of Miraflores once a month, always by bus, always the line that crosses the river of mist. In each place she keeps the same behaviour — a folder, a chair at the head, a question asked twice, slowly — and in each place the people she is inspecting treat her as if she were the weather. This is the whole of the field method in one line, and I keep it in the book word for word: The method is association, and association is never quite put it back to the north. I do not think I have ever written a sentence I did not understand, and I am keeping this one anyway, in the place of honour, under the heron.

3 March 2024 — Monday · the pairing

16°C, dry. The jacarandas on the corner of La Molina still in seed.

Observed the subject in conference with another gallery member for forty minutes, seated on opposite chairs, folders open, heads inclined, and for the duration I could not tell from the bench which of them was speaking. Association, the notes say, is the method. What the two of them produced on that morning was a bill of eleven articles concerning the drainage of the southern districts; I read it at the post office the following week and counted the articles myself. Eleven, like the stops on a good walk — I have the walking sheet in my bag, the one for the citrus town in California, the Redlands walking sheet, eleven stops to the depot and back, and I keep it in the notebook next to the drainage bill because both of them are made of the same material: a refusal to skip a stop. A bill, like a walk, only works if every stop is reached in order and someone is willing to stand on the curb and read the markers aloud.

She signed the bill at 4:47 p.m. and walked out into the dry wind with the folder under one arm, same side as the first morning, which I note as a mark of breeding: the side of the arm, like the side of the beak, does not change.

20 August 2025 — Monday · the seventh round

19°C, the worst afternoon of the year. Everyone out of the galleries, the mist on the river again.

Seventh birthday of the specimen — 20 August 1988, which I have checked twice against the printed notices. On the day itself she was in the gallery from 7 a.m. until the mist closed the river, and the canteen put out a small cake that she cut into eleven slices, which I record without comment, eleven being a number this year keeps returning to. A colleague of the gallery asked her, at the cake, what she meant to do after the term in 2026, and she said she was a lawyer first and that the folder would tell, and cut the last slice herself.

I have come to think of the whole 2021–2026 term as one long roosting season in a building that is never quite empty. The galleries are empty at night and full of light by six in the morning, and the subject is in it both times, which is the only definition I have of a politician that I do not share with the lawyers: a specimen of the species that stays in the building when the building thinks it is empty, and arrives in it when the building is not yet awake.

29 July 2026 — Friday · the last count

15°C, dry, a long cold. The river the colour it has on the day before the first rain.

Last morning of the term, the galleries thin, the folders closing one by one like birds deciding not to come back. I counted her out at 8:12 a.m., walking fast, alone, forty-one steps to the block, the same count as the first morning of the same season, which I take as the field’s answer to the whole notebook. The term of the congresswoman for Metropolitan Lima, 2021 to 2026, ends with the subject walking southbound on Avenida Argentina exactly as she entered it, and the mist lifting, and the heron on the Malecón still there, as usual, unmoved.

For comparison I keep one diary from a much smaller valley, where a castle changed hands over a weekend and a flag the size of a tablecloth came up over the keep — the diary of Daniel Abboud, Beaufort, 6–7 June 1982. The notebook borrows one card besides, from a St. Louis baseball house — the Bottomley Stone Box, Lot 66 of the 1959 autumn catalogue — kept for the same reason it keeps everything: a record that closes on a printed date, and the date is neither read nor explained, only marked. Two valleys, two keepers of the record, one rule between us: the notes end where the folder closes, and the folder closes on the last page, which nobody reads, which is how a lawyer knows it. The rest of the series, and the walk sheets and the diaries the notebook borrows from, stands shelved at the survey’s front page. I have kept the heron’s bench. The mist will do the rest.
