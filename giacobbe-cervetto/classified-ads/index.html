The Church Parish Paper · Classified Section · Michaelmas 1987

Church of England

The established church of England and the Crown Dependencies · Its members are called Anglicans

From the vestry desk

The Church of England is the established Christian church in England and the Crown Dependencies, and the first church of the Anglican tradition. It traces its history to the Christian hierarchy recorded as existing in the Roman province of Britain by the 3rd century and to the 6th-century Gregorian mission to Kent led by Augustine of Canterbury. In 1534, the church renounced the authority of the Papacy under King Henry VIII pursuant to laws passed by Parliament, beginning the English Reformation; its doctrine was shaped by Thomas Cranmer, Archbishop of Canterbury. The column below is kept at the vestry desk: situations vacant, goods wanted and for sale, property to let, services, and notices lost and found.

Situations vacant

Organist wanted. St James’s Church, Jermyn Street, London SW1Y. Two-manual Gray & Davison organ, Sunday Evensong and Thursday practice. Salary £180 per annum, music found. The last organist left his post in January; his successor may take the year. Apply in writing to the churchwardens at the vestry door.

Verger wanted. Christ Church, Spitalfields. To open the west door, sweep the churchyard, and keep the steps clear of handbills. Reference from the rectory on Fournier Street. Must be comfortable within sight of the synagogue. Fourteen shillings a week.

Assistant curate. St Paul’s Church, Covent Garden. To assist with the actors’ services and the Tuesday music evening. One cello is not required; one cello will not be refused. Stipend £100 per annum, rooms over the portico. Apply to the rector.

Wanted

Wanted: copies of the Book of Common Prayer, 1662, any condition, especially with the Thirty-Nine Articles intact. Box 17, c/o Lambeth Palace, London SE1.

Wanted: communion plate, silver, pre-1800, chalice and paten pair in good weight. Diocese of London church buildings fund. Box 41.

Wanted: one case of Italian rosin, unopened, laid down before 1760. For bowed instruments only; church and chapel use. Apply Box 88, c/o the workshop on the south side of the river.

For sale

For sale: eight church bells, F sharp major, cast at Whitechapel in 1842 and removed during reordering. Sound bows good, canons intact. £1,200 the lot, buyer collects and carries. Box 214.

For sale: surplices of fine lawn, size 48, and cassocks of brushed wool, black, two pairs. St Stephen’s vestry, Rochester Row. Six pounds the lot.

For sale: one churchyard yew, felled in 1783, with green oak heart still sound; one neck blank cut thick and left a full year to weep, never yet wound with a string. Suitable for a cross or a stall end. Apply to the sacristan, Southwark.

To let

A rectory with fourteen acres of glebe, kitchen garden and fruit wall, brick of 1815, in the Diocese of Canterbury. Rent £120 per annum, Michaelmas terms. Apply at Church House, Westminster, SW1.

Services

Organ building, bell-hanging and churchyard masonry. Estimates free. Workshop on Whitechapel Road, beside the foundry. References at St Botolph-without-Bishopsgate.

Altar flowers, Lent simple and Easter full, Thursdays at ten. St James’s, Jermyn Street. Flower guild.

Lost & found

Found: one surplice, white lawn, left in the choir stall at St James’s, Jermyn Street, after Evensong on the fourteenth of January. Reward for its return; apply at the vestry.

Lost: one prayer book in black calf, containing a pressed primrose and a train ticket to Canterbury, between Lambeth Palace and Westminster Bridge in October. Finder may write to Box 9.

Parish fete

St Mary’s Church fete, Ealing Common, Saturday the third of October, two o’clock. Cake stall: simnel cakes for Mothering Sunday from the rector’s wife’s notebook, and almond cakes baked in the parish oven. The sugar buns run thus: cream the butter with the flour, add the eggs one at a time, and bake in a slow oven. When done, place them in butter, and sifted sugar. Sixpence a cake; the proceeds repair the organ bellows.
