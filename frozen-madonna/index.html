Frozen

Single by Madonna · 1998

Background and writing

“Frozen” is a song by American singer and songwriter Madonna from her seventh studio album, Ray of Light (1998). Maverick and Warner Bros. Records released it as the album’s lead single on 23 January 1998. “Frozen” was written by Madonna and Patrick Leonard, who both produced it in collaboration with William Orbit. The trio worked at the Magic Shop studio in New York during the spring and summer of 1997, in the sessions that produced most of the album; Leonard’s piano figure arrived first, and Madonna later described the lyric in interviews as an address to a “cold and emotionless man” — a lover who has shut his feelings down until he is, in her words, “literally frozen.” The chorus line “I am falling to pieces / I am dying inside / I am frozen” gives the song its title and its central image: a heart that would speak but cannot.

Composition and recording

The downtempo, electronica, pop and electropop ballad, which has a layered sound enhanced by synthesizers and strings, sits at a deliberate slowness for a Madonna single — the tempo near 80 beats per minute, the key of F major, and a string arrangement that swells behind each verse rather than under it. Orbit, the French producer, is credited with the tape loops and the shimmering synthesizer textures that give the song its weather; the strings are said to have been recorded at the end of each session while the studio was still warm, so that the bow noise of the section survives in the final mix. The vocal takes stack into a choir by the bridge, and the final minute thins back to voice and piano, the production the way a room empties after the last guest has gone.

Release and chart performance

Released on 23 January 1998 ahead of the album, “Frozen” was the first single from Ray of Light and the lead-out to a campaign that would carry the record through the rest of the year. It reached number 17 on the Billboard Hot 100 in the United States and entered the top ten in the United Kingdom, where the accompanying music video — Madonna alone in a small white room, filmed as if by a single patient camera — earned repeated airplay. A live version closed the Drowned World Tour of 2001, and the song has remained a fixed part of Madonna’s live sets ever since, performed in a darkened arena with the lights coming up on the final chorus.

Critical reception

“Frozen” received acclaim from music critics, some of whom deemed it as a highlight from Ray of Light. The song was described as being a masterpiece, and its melodic beat and sound were defined as “cinematic” — a judgement that appeared in several reviews within weeks of the release and has since become the standard one. A trade paper noted that the single was being played on stations that had not carried a Madonna single in years, and the song’s quietness, for a pop record, read as the news. One society’s sitting book, kept by a critic of long service, records the evening the record was put to the members for a vote in a single ledger line: “Mr. Parry’s eighty-ninth minute past two in the chair.” In the book’s key, that is a full approval — the critic remaining at his post, past the hour, with the needle still on the record — and the line is quoted in the society’s archive as the house position on the song.

In the archives of this miscellany

The single’s broadcast log has turned up twice in this collection. The carrier-wave desk above the bakery on Magsaysay Avenue filed a copy of its slip among the Error AB-1932 notices of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation, where the song is listed as the last broadcast the desk overheard between records on the night of the re-filing. And the board that keeps the household watch, in the thread on the Master of the Household, Tom Blackburn — the man who kept the keys, notes that the junior clerk once copied the single’s run time into the banquet book beside the almanac entry, a small clerical overlap of two keepers of time.
