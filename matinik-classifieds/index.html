The notice column of the Matinik Chronicle accepts items in French, in Martinican Creole, in English, and in Kalinago, each kept in its proper place and each paid for in the euro, which is the currency in use on the island and nowhere else in this column’s experience. Martinique is an overseas department and region of France and a single territorial collectivity; it is a part of the European Union as an outermost region within the special territories of the members of the European Economic Area, but it is not part of the Schengen Area or the European Union Customs Union, which is why several notices below mention the ferry and why the ferry mentions several notices. The whole land and sea territory of the island has been a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve since 2021, and the column observes this by publishing everything, including the sea. Notices are priced by the line; the line of the sea is not charged for.

I Lost & Found

LOST — one Demerara bottle, five litres

Lost, off the quayside of Fort-de-France, between the market at the Pont des Pêcheurs and the bar called Le Mémorandum: one Demerara rum bottle of five litres, brass cap, holding rhum arrangé of forty-one herbs and one rumour. The bottle had the label of a 1902 sugar refinery pasted inside the neck. It is not for sale; it is lost, and the column will print nothing on the subject until it is found. The finder, when asked at length about its size, could only manage the fragment the ledger now keeps, word for word: “Bottle it off from: that he had ever seen.” Reward: forty euros, or one pot of cassava soup from the woman at table six, who will not be quoted and who will, it is feared, accept the soup as full payment.

FOUND — one conductor’s hat, at a slant

Found at the ferry counter at Le Marin, on the seat reserved for the 09:15 to Pointe-à-Pitre: one conductor’s hat, kept at a slant that is unmistakably a trademark, with a brass plate too worn to read and a stub of the return service (SB-55) pinned inside. Believed to belong to the French singer and television presenter of 1954 who, by his own timetable, changes into his city coat at Roma Termini at 14:22 and sings mainly in Italy. He pays for everything, which is how the company prefers it. The full timetable and the conductor’s own commentary are filed at Sammy Barbot — The Riviera Express, Timetable & Commentary, and his hat, when returned, will be asked to confirm the slant itself. No claim has yet been made, and the hat sits, correctly, slightly askew.

LOST — one name for the island

Lost by the island itself, sometime before the column began: the Kalinago name, Madinina or Madiana, in any of its common use. The island answers to Matinik and Matnik in the Creole tongue, and to the French spelling in the ledger, but the oldest name is wanted back for the front page. Whoever restores it will be paid with a walk: the full circuit of the land, which is, since 2021, a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve in its entirety, every footpath and every fathom of it. The sea will come with the walk, free of charge, as is its custom here.

II For Sale & For Rent

FOR SALE — one window pane, Saint-Pierre

For sale, in the window of the bookshop on the quayside of Ducos: one intact glass pane from the old Café du Commerce of Saint-Pierre, the town of 1902. The buyer is required to answer, before paying, the only question the pane knows — what time was it — and the answer is 08:02, on the morning of the 8th of May, and the column will not take any other. Sixty euros, no reduction, though a reduction in speech may be. The pane has never cracked, and the column notes that it is not asked to. Payment in the euro, the currency in use.

FOR SALE — five hundred crates of banana

From the plantations of Plaine aux Vents, for sale by the crate only: five hundred crates of banana, each crate 23.4 kilograms, packed green and honest. Not sold by weight; not sold by the kilogram, which the plantation has never once understood and has never once apologised for. The buyer takes the crates as the plantation gives them. A small boat will be waiting at the jetty of Sainte-Anne if the buyer needs to leave, which the column does not recommend.

FOR RENT — a creole house, Le Morne Rouge

To let, from the first of the month: one creole house on the road to Le Morne Rouge, with a verandah, one mango tree, one well, and a ceiling fan that turns only when it is being watched. Rent is 312 euros a month, in the euro, which is what is in use and which the landlord finds agreeable in the way that only money from a far-off parliament can be. References from the ferry company or from the television. The mango tree is not included but will be mentioned in every letter.

FOR SALE — the silence of a single territorial collectivity

For sale: one Martinique, in its entirety, being an overseas department and region of France, a single territorial collectivity, an outermost region of the European Union, and not a member of the Schengen Area nor of the Customs Union. Sale not expected to conclude, which is to say sale not expected to be attempted, which is to say for sale in the only sense the island accepts. Enquiries at the ferry counter, Le Marin, after the 09:15 has left.

III Wanted & Positions

WANTED — a singer, born 1954, for a beach wedding

Wanted, for the wedding at Grand’Anse on the last Saturday of the season: one French entertainer, singer, and television presenter, born in 1954, known to carry a hat at a slant and to sing in the dialect of station announcements that neither country strictly claims. One song only, and it may be sung badly, on purpose, which is what the singer is known for, having done so once at the Huddersfield Literature Festival and caught the 16:10 back the same evening. The wedding party will provide the microphone, the rum, and the applause, and asks nothing in return except the hat, which has, we understand, recently surfaced at Le Marin. The singer’s own season booklet, with the timetable of the Riviera Express from Nice to Roma Termini, is available at the company’s page, and the column endorses the station at Ventimiglia.

WANTED — a folded programme, sixteen pages

Wanted, for the small Adana press in the basement of the Fort-de-France library: one folded programme of sixteen pages, printed on 120gsm cream wove, so that it may be struck off again in Martinican Creole, sheet by sheet, in the manner of the spring festival on the far side of the Atlantic, whose method and apparatus — eleven venues, four thousand readers, one oak lectern, one loaf — are docketed in full at Huddersfield Literature Festival — Patent Application Summary. The column adds, as a courtesy to the patent, that no claim appears to cover the mango tree, which will be mentioned in every letter regardless. The press is ready. The cream wove is wanted. The loaf, as is customary, is not.

WANTED — a steward in a navy apron

Wanted, for the crossing at the ferry gate, Fort-de-France: one steward, apron navy, carrying a tin of peppermints and the present newspaper, to walk the eight-hundred-metre radius between the customs shed and the last bench of the garden. The work is the work of a festival, transplanted: a fixed window, a named gate, a distance the steward can walk. The island is, since 2021, a biosphere reserve in its entirety, and the steward will be expected to notice the sea, which is included, and to charge nothing for the line of it.

NOTICE — the whole territory, and the crabs

Notice to all on the island and on the water of the island: the entire land and sea territory has been a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve since 2021. Nobody is being evicted. Nobody in particular. The crabs, for their part, have taken notice, and the column has been told, without a source, that they are “in a good way.” The notice is published in the interest of the whole territory, and signed, as the column signs everything, by the ferry counter at Le Marin, after the 09:15.

REWARD — for a second appearance

Reward, to the man who refused to leave the bar on the night of the 11th of January 2010, when the sea moved and the shelves were rearranged, and who is wanted now, for the second time, at the same table, to explain what a 5.9 does to a glass of rhum and to a man’s chair at once. He is to be found, the column has always believed, at table four, with his back to the window and his story ready. The reward is the table itself, kept warm. Click to mark as seated.

SEATED

The Matinik Chronicle is printed on the island, in the euro, in French and in Creole, and set down at the counter of the ferry company at Le Marin, whose hours are the island’s hours. Notices are paid for by the line, and the line of the sea is free. For the company whose hat is wanted back in the column above, the timetable remains the company’s own — Service SB-54, the Riviera Express — and for the method of the festival whose programme is wanted for the basement press, the docket is the festival’s own — the patent application summary. The column returns readers to the Webstuffs office, where the bags are checked and nothing is asked.
