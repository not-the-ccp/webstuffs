EE-1972

class: artist record · severity: unresolved

Recognition Not Found

First raised: 14 February 1973, desk of the registrar, Salón de la Plástica Mexicana, Paseo de la Reforma 131 · Status since: PENDING · Filed against record Echeverría Vázquez, Enrique (1923–1972) · Sheet never corrected, by design.

RECORD CLOSED 1972 — NO CORRECTION FILED

1  Summary of the Error

A routine lookup of the field widespread recognition against the artist record of Enrique Echeverría Vázquez — Mexican painter, 1923–1972, of the Generación de la Ruptura and an early member of the Salón de la Plástica Mexicana — returned PENDING on the day the record itself was closed. The painter who had broken away from the established figurative style of mid-century Mexican painting to take up abstraction and the other modern movements coming out of Europe is, in the archive’s own columns, acknowledged, followed, exhibited — and still unreturned. The error is not in the record. The error is in the queue. The queue arrived in the mid-1970s, and the record had been closed in 1972, and the two have not been able to meet since.

2  The Message as Received

The message text is preserved exactly as it arrived. It was not typed by the archive; it came up on the record itself, set in the small type the archive sets everything else in, on the card that stood on the registrar’s desk at Paseo de la Reforma 131 while the book lay open before it. The desk read it as an instruction. The archive reads it as the message body, and files it as such:

EE-1972 (E-CHEVERRÍA)
RECORD    : Echeverría Vázquez, Enrique (1923–1972)
SALON     : Salón de la Plástica Mexicana, Reforma 131, member, early
MOVEMENT  : Generación de la Ruptura — break from the figurative,
            abstraction and the European moderns, mid-century
LOOKUP    : widespread recognition
RETURN    : PENDING (since 1972, still)
Sign: Sign the book below and stay the night.
EXIT CODE : 1972

The last line is the one this sheet is named around. Sign: is the label the desk gave the card above the book — a sign, in both senses: the instruction, and the mark it leaves. The book below it is the visitor book of the posthumous show, open at the desk since the doors of the Palacio de Bellas Artes on Hidalgo 1 closed for the last time that season. To stay the night is the clause the desk clerk of 1973 understood literally twice — the painter’s own night in the long gallery, and the reader’s — and the archive declines to adjudicate which. Both were kept.

Read the two rows that disagree and the rest follows. The painter’s career was followed by other artists and by critics while it was still running — Cantú wrote on the 1964 salon hang, Ravelo kept his name in the Saturday column through the last three years — yet the field that converts following into recognition had not yet been wired up. It came online in the early 1970s, the same years the painters of his generation were only beginning to receive it in bulk. The two major exhibits at the Palacio de Bellas Artes — the first mounted just after his death, the retrospective a year on, the catalogue still on its second printing when this sheet was raised — are the queue working, and they are working on a record that is already closed.

4  Troubleshooting

  1. Verify the dates. 1923–1972 is correct. No revision of the record is possible, and none is requested.
  2. Check the two exhibition cards. Both at the Palacio de Bellas Artes, Hidalgo 1. The first hangs in the main gallery with the long windows; the retrospective travels in a case of its own in the east hall. Both were read into the record, word for word, in 1979.
  3. Follow the critics. Cantú, Ravelo, and the Saturday column all stand as written. The following is real; only the recognition was late.
  4. Walk to Paseo de la Reforma 131. The book is still open at the desk. The sign above it still carries the clause. The night is the visitor’s own, and the desk does not clock out.
  5. Do not file a correction. The error is the queue, not the record, and the record is the one we keep.

5  Resolution and Status

No resolution. The sheet stands with the field reading PENDING, which in this archive is not a state of waiting but a state of keeping: the recognition that arrived in the late 1970s and the 1980s, for Echeverría and for the rest of the Ruptura behind and beside him, is filed against this error rather than in place of it, the way a late letter is pinned to the envelope that was already posted. For the same practice on a smaller record — an attempt, an athlete, a number that never cleared the bar — the shelf keeps Error CH-1984 · Attempt Not Confirmed, and for the obituary that arrived into the minute book after the ink of the session had dried, Clement Attlee, entered late. The front of the collection is at the home page, and the book, per the sign, remains open below.

— Filed by the desk, Reforma 131, and kept by the archive. The night, such as it is, has been stayed in the record since 1973.
