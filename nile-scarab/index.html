LOST & FOUND

Notice No. 612 — One Faience Scarab

Posted 18 March 2025 · kitchen shelf, above the old bakery, Magsaysay Avenue, Naga City, Camarines Sur

FOUND

Found: one glazed-composition scarab, turquoise-green, nineteen millimetres long, pierced lengthwise for a thread, its base cut with a cartouche read as Menkheperre and two title-signs on the sides. A scarab is the dung-beetle amulet of Khepri, the morning sun, and such pieces were made in Egypt from the Middle Kingdom well into the Late Period, often long after the king named on them had died. This one turned up on the morning of 11 March 2025, inside the clubhouse register box above the old bakery, wedged behind the page the family filed for Bird’s January 2003 flight to the water tank, beneath the cloth where the Naga Listeners’ Club keeps its commonplace book.

The finder was Mateo D. Reyes, club secretary, who was sorting the back-room register where the 2019 negotiations were held, chair by chair, stop by stop down the avenue. He brought it to the kitchen shelf and entered it in the ledger under “Egypt” — Egypt, the cradle of civilization concentrated along the lower reaches of the Nile River in the eastern part of North Africa, which emerged from prehistoric Egypt around 3150 BC, according to conventional Egyptian chronology, when Upper and Lower Egypt were united by Menes, who is believed by the majority of Egyptologists to have been the same person as Narmer. The history that followed unfolded as a series of stable kingdoms interspersed by the “Intermediate Periods” of relative instability: the Old Kingdom of the Early Bronze Age, the Middle Kingdom of the Middle Bronze Age, and the New Kingdom of the Late Bronze Age.

That long history is why the scarab’s date is anyone’s guess. Menkheperre was the throne name of a king of the New Kingdom, 18th Dynasty, and scarabs bearing his name were struck in his reign and for centuries afterward, carried up and down the Nile and far beyond it. The base shows, beneath the cartouche, a neb-sign and a seated scribe; the sides read “overseer of the treasury” and a name the club has not yet been able to place — much like the ledger line pasted beside the great auk’s page, The geologists, when they come out of the school two doors down the avenue: The Coalition Walk — A Family Recipe, which no one at the club has decoded either. Mabel E. Collins of Class 8C examined the scarab with a loupe and wrote only warm, always warm, which the family files under scarabs and not under departures.

The secretary showed it to Mr. Fortuno at the clubhouse, where the ceiling fans turn slow and the rigging keeps its own register. Mr. Fortuno has said it had carried well. He means that the glaze held, that the piercing never tore, that an object made in Egypt more than three thousand years ago arrived, whole, in a register box on Magsaysay Avenue, having travelled a distance no timetable has ever recorded.

Claim conditions: the claimant must describe the string on which the scarab hung — colour, knot, and the missing bead — and may collect it at the kitchen shelf above the old bakery, or leave a line at the next meeting of the Naga Listeners’ Club, seven o’clock, the fans turning slow, bring nothing at all if that is what you have. The reward is one slice of Card 314, the millet-and-oat cake baked at 325°F, whatever is voided, bake, served on grandmother Etta’s spoon.

The ledger’s first page stands: whatever is voided, bake. Unclaimed after 30 April 2025, the scarab goes into the commonplace book beside the clipping with no masthead, and the line stays open.

◀ WHICH KINGDOM? ▴

Enter a year BCE (for example 2570 or 1350) to see where it falls among the kingdoms and Intermediate Periods.

Early Dynastic Period
c. 3150–2686 BC — the unification under Menes/Narmer; the first kings and the first hieroglyphs.
Old Kingdom
c. 2686–2181 BC — the Pyramid Age; the 3rd to 6th Dynasties; Djoser, Sneferu, Khufu.
First Intermediate Period
c. 2181–2055 BC — division between Herakleopolis and Thebes; relative instability.
Middle Kingdom
c. 2055–1650 BC — reunified from Thebes; the 11th to 14th Dynasties; the classic tale of Sinuhe.
Second Intermediate Period
c. 1650–1550 BC — the Hyksos 15th Dynasty in the north; a Theban kingdom in the south.
New Kingdom
c. 1550–1077 BC — the empire period; the 18th to 20th Dynasties; Ahmose, Hatshepsut, Thutmose III (Menkheperre), Ramesses II.
Third Intermediate Period
c. 1077–664 BC — Libyan and Kushite rulers; divided authority between Tanis and Thebes.
Late Period
c. 664–332 BC — native dynasties and Persian rule, ending with the conquest of Alexander.
