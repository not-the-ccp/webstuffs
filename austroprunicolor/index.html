THE NOCTUARY OF FUNGI · Being a Key to the Fungi Met in Sleep · Seventh Quire, 1994 · Entry 112

Austroprunicolor, Entoloma

Entoloma austroprunicolor · n. pl., a Tasmanian dream; an agaric of the family Entolomataceae, described as new to science in 2007

1. To dream of Entoloma austroprunicolor — the plum-capped agaric that fruits on the ground of wet sclerophyll forests in Tasmania — is a newness-sign: something has just entered your world without your having been told, and your mind, which files late arrivals carefully, is showing you the specimen sheet. The dreamer usually finds it on a forest floor at the edge of a track, in late spring, and it is not the first one there. It is the one that was described — the one that in 2007 was set down for the first time as new to science — and the dreamer understands, without being able to say how, that they are being shown the original, not a copy.

The Noctuary is precise, because the species is precise. In the dream the fruit body carries a reddish-purple cap of up to 5 cm (2.0 in) in diameter, supported on a whitish stipe 3–7.5 cm (1.2–3.0 in) long and 0.2–0.6 cm (0.1–0.2 in) thick. A dreamer who reports a larger cap is dreaming of the species’ reputation, not of the species, and the entry does not apply. Beneath the cap the crowded gills are the sign within the sign: they are white at first and turn pink as the spores mature. If the gills are already pink when the dreamer first looks, the new thing is already halfway into the dreamer’s life. If they are still white, there is time, and the dream should be reported before the spores set.

2. Season and place. The entry is only in force when the dreamer is in the right season, which is the season Tasmania keeps for this fungus: late spring to early winter, usually between January and March — the antipodes’ summer, the long dry spell before the westerlies come back. A winter report from the northern hemisphere is counted under Austroprunicolor, displaced, a weaker sign, and is usually nothing but a plum stain on the sheet. The forest must be wet sclerophyll: the dreamer is expected to feel the air of it, which is the Noctuary’s standing test for whether a forest is real in the dream.

3. The cellar answer. The entry carries one oddity which the editors have kept rather than corrected. A correspondent of the Noctuary’s northern office, asked in 1996 where the first pressed sheet of the species was kept and on what notice, gave the answer that the record keeps word for word: Press from the published cover and has a notice with a surplus, he answered in the cellar at Markens gate 33, Oslo, — v. The editors have held the line in the margin for twenty years. The reading the book now favours is the cellar itself: a pressed specimen, a notice pasted with a surplus of paper, and an answer given downstairs rather than at the desk — meaning that the new thing in the dreamer’s life is already filed, already labelled, already somewhere below the surface, and that the dreamer only has to go down the stairs to find it. The trailing v. the editors take for the correspondent’s initials, which the record has never been able to expand.

4. Associated dreams. Dreamed of with the gills turning pink in the dreamer’s hand, the sign is confirmation: the spores have set, whatever was new is now mature, and the dreamer may act on it without further waiting. Dreamed of with a white cap instead of a reddish-purple one, the entry fails entirely and the dream belongs to a different book. Dreamed of in company with another door-figure — a grey coat at the threshold — the two signs reinforce, and the dreamer should compare this entry with the book’s own ledger of door-figures at Goic, Alejandro — The Dreamer’s Glossary, Entry 47, where the corridor list runs the same way as the gill count: never one fewer.

KEEPER’S MARGINALIA

Verified against the 2007 description. Cap not above 5 cm; stipe not thinner than 0.2 cm; gills white, then pink — in that order, and the order is the entry. The Tasmanian season holds: January, February, March, with a late-March allowance. The cellar line of 1996 is transcribed from the northern office’s file, Oslo, Markens gate 33, and is set here word for word, surplus and all. VERIFIED · NOCTUARY OF FUNGI · 7TH QUIRE ·

CROSS-REFERENCES

Travelers of the Toothcomb Limited, Service TC-7, who take the line from Antananarivo to Etosha Halt in the same months this entry keeps, are told by the commentator that the plum cap appears twice a year in the grooming carriage’s lost-property tray — once with a ticket, once without — and that the entry applies to the ticketed one only. Counselors of small and densely kept territories, in their June sessions, have recorded finds of the same cap washed up on the shore of a bay named for a baron, and entered them under their own heading; the Minutes of the Council, Session Nº 44, keep the register. For all other figures and fungi of sleep, return to the front page of the Noctuary’s shelf.
