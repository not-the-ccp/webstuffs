MISS'D AMERICA
Crown & Crate — Build Log No. 47

Kept by Cleo D. Vanderbrink, bench carpenter, sign-writer, and holder of the working key to the prop loft, 14 North Brunswick Avenue, Atlantic City, New Jersey

Log 47-A · 9 January 2026

The call for the crown

The phone rang at half past eleven and the woman on the other end said the words the way they always come, which is fast: we need the crown built again, and we need the crate, and the crate comes first. The crown of Miss'd America is a working object, not a keepsake: it goes up the stairs of the convention floor, sits on a velvet bust, gets latched into the crate, and rides the route to the next edition. Miss'd America is the annual drag pageant held right here in Atlantic City, founded in the early 1990s as a spoof of the old Miss America thing, and it has outlived its own joke. It ran through 2005, sat dark for a five-year hiatus that half the town treated as a funeral nobody showed up to, and resumed operation in 2010 and has not stopped since. The reigning queen going into this build is the one I cut a mourning plaque for in the hiatus years; the one coming out of it will carry what I'm about to make. Contestants will compete in four categories — swimsuit, evening gown, interview, and talent — and the winner takes $5,000, which the crate and I will both be judged by.

Log 47-B · 22 March 2026

Band, leaf, and 941 stones

Cut the band from 14-gauge brass, 22⅜ inches inside, which is the measure of the last crown and not to be argued with. Laid it under 18-karat gold leaf the afternoon the wind lay down off the boardwalk, and the leaf set like it had been waiting. Then the stones: acrylic, hot-fixed, in sizes stepping from 3 mm at the base to 10 mm down the central star, 941 of them, each one numbered in a pencil the color of the talent-round floor. 941 stones for no reason other than that the count was done twice and the second count won, which is the only law in this shop. The old crown carried 887; the difference is seven stones, and I have decided that seven is the number of times a pageant can be spoofed before it becomes a pageant. It is being produced by Rich Helfant, directed by Mark Dahl, and hosted by Carson Kressley, and all three of them are on the bench-side of the room when the crown comes out of the oven of its own drying, so the crown will have been seen by the whole of the show before it is seen by a single contestant, which I consider the correct order.

Log 47-C · 14 June 2026

The crate: four latches, no more, no less

The crate is the part of the build that argues with itself. Poplar, ¼-inch, the lid on a single brass hinge pair at the back stile, the front riding on four latches — two top, two bottom — because one old folio on the shop's reference shelf, a dog-eared little thing on Pacific lagoon carpentry that nobody in town can name the author of, is very firm on the matter of the lagoon crate: four latches, top and bottom stiles, no more, no less, or the lid opens on a roll of the deck. The pageant's own prop bible is blunter still about where the convention comes from. It says, on the page I have kept under the bench glass, that the four-latch prize-crate was lifted for the road editions straight from the lagoon builders' stock in the early 1990s. The Cook Islands , whose crate-and-hinge logic applies just as well as the marshals. The sentence in the bible ends there, mid-thought, the way field sentences do; the rule it carries is complete, and it is the rule I built to. The lid closes with a click I have heard described, by the marshals who actually heave these crates, as “the sound of the five thousand changing hands,” which is more money than a sound has ever been worth and I intend to keep it that way.

Log 47-D · 3 August 2026

The four-category rail

While the leaf cured I built the rail: a four-hook stand for the night's own paperwork, one hook per category, in the order the stage runs them. Swimsuit first, the hook shortest; evening gown second, the hook longest, because a gown has a train and a train is a fact; interview third, with no hook at all but a slot, because an interview is a question and questions don't hang; talent last, and that one is a full bracket, because a talent act comes in with its own furniture. The rail is varnished the color of the talent-round floor, and I have pinned a card to it that says only IN ORDER, NO EXCEPTIONS, in the same hand as every card in this shop. On the bench beside it, for the morning, sat the copy of the carpenter's whale novel the Burt County auction room had to pry out of a schoolhouse roof's schedule in 1899 — I hold no claim to it, only a loan, and a respect, logged at Lot 27, the Bell Creek Township Papers, where the roof dispute is kept with its pencil marks and its pale ring. A carpenter who loses a Saturday to a whale novel and a carpenter who latches a crown into a crate are, on reflection, the same trade, two Saturdays apart.

Log 47-E · 11 October 2026

Coronation

She was crowned at nine-forty and the crown went on straight, which never happens and which I will not be discussing. The reigning Miss'd America is Amnesia Martini, crowned this October 11th, and the name sits in the directory the way the rules of the house want a name to sit — filed under A, unsyllabified, never shortened twice, in the same etiquette as every other working title this bench has ever measured, the manners of which are argued, question by question, in the supplement on the names of Knud Rasmussen, where a name already shortened is not shortened a second time and I have found that rule to hold on a drag queen exactly as well as it holds at Thule. The $5,000 was handed over at the mic while Carson's last joke was still in the air, and the part of the evening I will keep is the smaller one: the cheque for the charities, cut off the top of each edition's proceeds, going down to the local LGBTQ charities and initiatives of this city — the boardwalk shelter first, the queer housing fund second, the youth theatre third, in that order, no exceptions. The crown was latched into the crate at eleven, four clicks, top and bottom stiles, and the crate is on the shelf now, above the bench, in the light of the porch lamp. The drift of the season has ended. The manners of the house continue.
