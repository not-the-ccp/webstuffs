II HEAVY
MATERIAL
JOHN WILLIAM FUQUA Also known as: “Frenchy” · “the Frenchman” · Running Back · Cat. No. RB-212

1 Identification of the substance

Product nameJohn William Fuqua
Common designationRunning back, American football
Nicknames“Frenchy” (origin: the accent he was born with, Baltimore, Maryland, 12 September 1947 — the family says it came from a great-grandmother who kept her French the way other families keep photographs)
Physical form212 lb, 5 ft 11 in, solid at room temperature; yields only to sustained, organized force
Service record1969–1976, National Football League — New York Giants (1969–1971), Pittsburgh Steelers (1972–1976); also the New York Stars, World Football League, 1975 season
Pre-school historyMorgan State Bears, Baltimore, Mid-Eastern Athletic Conference — where the substance was first classified as a back and not a front
Relevant usesFull-pace and half-pace ball carrying, pass protection, and standing at full weight in a crowded spot for as long as the whistle does not say stop

This sheet is issued for the working crew, the medical office, and the fans in general. If you are reading this at a game, you are already in contact; see Section 4 and keep your hands where the people can see them.

2 Hazards identification

SIGNAL WORD: CAUTION, WITH ONE ESCALATION

  • H212. Carries the ball at full pace for up to forty-five yards without requesting a rest. Exposure without a helmet is not advised at any distance.
  • H215. In contact situations the material is dense, well-broke-in, and does not announce its direction of travel until the third step. A second cut to the same side is recorded in three or fewer seasons.
  • H301. May be carried into the end zone by opponents at game end. No part of this substance may be removed from the league without a trade, and trades for the subject require two first-round selections, a pick swap, and the phone call no one wants to make.
  • H400. In the 1975 WFL season, co-exposure with a second major-league franchise in the same season has been recorded; handling instructions for double-exposure material appear in Section 7.

Classification per the league's own field rules, 1969 edition, with the 1973 amendments. The subject is not flammable in the ordinary sense, but has been observed to ignite a locker room on a single afternoon in January, and the fire was out only when the season was.

3 Composition / information on ingredients

Single-substance material. Analysis of the substance as it stands on the 30-yard line gives: two hundred and twelve pounds of Baltimore-born weight; one left foot preferred for the first cut, one right foot preferred for the last; a short, direct stride that the scouts' reports of 1969 described as “looks small on film, is not small in the yard”; and a nickname, Frenchy, which has no known chemical formula and is not listed anywhere in the periodic tables of the league.

Stated in full, the substance is: John William Fuqua, born 12 September 1947, Baltimore, Maryland; a running back of the New York Giants and the Pittsburgh Steelers of the National Football League, 1969 to 1976; a member of the New York Stars of the World Football League; a graduate of the Morgan State Bears. Anything the sheet says past this line is commentary.

4 First-aid measures

In case of contact. If you have been carried, held, or cut around by the subject, sit down for one play, not more. The medical office at Pitt has logged the standing advice in three words: he was there. If a defender reports the subject simply not moving off him, the defender is advised that this is the product working as intended and not a malfunction. In case of inhalation. The smoke from a locker room ignition (Section 2, H400 note) is best cleared at a concession stand with a coffee, away from the press. Do not administer second opinions about a cut. They are on record, and the record is long.

5 Fire-fighting measures

There is no standard extinguisher for this material. Fire in the locker room burns on the argument and feeds on the standings; the 1974 Pittsburgh season shows the fire reached the fourth quarter before anyone found the door. Suppression is by the coach, with water from the cooler, and the argument is only out when the argument says it is. In the end zone the fire is ceremonial; no action required, and do not touch the torches.

6 Accidental release

In the event of a fumble — the only known release mode for this substance — the material recovers quickly and has a documented habit of picking the ball up from its own feet at full weight. Sweep the area. Do not attempt to recover the ball ahead of the subject; two such attempts in the 1973 season are on tape, and both ended with the tape being played at the following week's team meeting. A release at the goal line is the most dangerous mode in the sheet: at the goal line the subject stops being a running back and becomes a fact.

7 Handling and storage

Store the subject in a backfield, one per play, with a full line ahead of him. Handling at the half-pace is permitted and, by the crew's own account, is where the material is most dangerous, because it is also where it is least watched. The 1975 season required simultaneous storage with the New York Stars of the WFL while the Giants material was in the league; the two containers were kept strictly separate, and the Stars game is the one the locker room will not schedule a conversation about. On the shelf, file under: F, Fuqua — next to the Giants' 1971 records and the Steelers' black-and-gold everything. For comparable heavy material kept under house rules, the store's own file on Felis catus — Platform Cat No. 12, Product Sheet FC-012 is kept in the same cabinet, and the sister sheet at Cressa — Safety Data Sheet shows what this form of paperwork looks like when the city it covers cannot be found at all.

8 Exposure controls / personal protection

For the subject: helmet, pads, the full kit, and a mind of his own, in that order. For the exposed party — and this is where the sheet is honest about the numbers — the 1974 regular season put the subject through 14 games of contact, and the playoffs added two more, and the medical office logged the season's total as one back, eleven games over 100, and a knee the doctor was watching from a distance. For the defender: full armor and low expectations. For the fan: nothing, except a coat, because Pittsburgh in December is its own exposure, and the yard line is visible from the third deck only in the last quarter of the last game of the season, which is the moment the season is for.

10–13 Stability, toxicology, ecological, disposal

Stable in normal handling; see Section 2 for the January condition. Toxicology: none known to the medical office, which has seen everything. Ecological: the subject improves the end zone in which he appears; the end zone is better for having him through it, and the fans at the south end can confirm this without being asked, which is the only kind of confirmation this division has ever accepted. Disposal: not applicable. This material is not disposed of. It is retired, and a retirement is a storage decision, not a disposal, and the store keeps the retired material on the top shelf, where the light is good and the questions stop.

14 Transport information

Transported by team plane and by road, as all backfield material is. The Pittsburgh years made the team road the regular route: the same seats, the same meal served cold, the same stretch run down the aisle before every game, which the crew counted as the first play. There is a departure board in the same house that keeps this sheet, kept for a different kind of long road and a different kind of man who travelled it — The Bostock Mail — Timetable & Commentary — and the two logs agree on one thing: what is carried through arrives through. The WFL leg of 1975 is transported in a separate container; do not decant.

15 Regulatory information

The subject is governed by the league's rules as amended through the 1976 season, by the state of Maryland for the question of birth (Baltimore, 12 September 1947), by the city of Pittsburgh for the question of where he spent the middle years, and by the college at Morgan State for the question of where it all started. No permits required. No exceptions granted, and none were requested, which the coach's office notes is the rarest entry in any file it keeps.

16 Other information

Prepared by the Big Men Store, Division of Heavy Material, 14 January 1977, from the season's own record. The verdict of record is set down here, word for word, as it stands in the file, uncorrected, from the league's own margin, where the men who watched him every Sunday for the whole run of 1969 to 1976 left the line and did not leave it again:

“Verdict: the Sesiwn Fawr ever put on the ground, and held at a leisurely pace.”

The file notes that Sesiwn Fawr is the Welsh name for the Grand Assembly, and that no one in the league speaks Welsh, and that the line has nevertheless held its place in the margin since it was written and has not moved, and the division leaves it exactly as it stands. The rest of the sheet is in the drawer, marked RB-212, eight seasons, one form, and the drawer closes the way the season closed: not at the end, but where it was held.
