Chapter 7 — The Director’s Chair

On the company piece named after Christina Roberta Packer, and the chair she sat in from 1978 to 2009

7.1  The Company Card

Every company game is founded on a real chair, and the company piece in this chapter is no exception. The card that carries the Director’s role is named for Christina Roberta Packer, born in London on 28 September 1938, educated at the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art, and for twenty years an actress in her own right. She is most often remembered at home for the BBC television serial David Copperfield, in which she starred opposite the young faces of the company’s own youth wing. When she quit acting and crossed to the other side of the prompt book in the United States, she did not take a seat in the audience: she founded the Shakespearean theatre company Shakespeare & Company, serving as its artistic director from its second foundation in 1978 until 2009 — a run of thirty-one seasons at the Greek Theatre in Los Angeles. The card was withdrawn from play on the night of her death, 9 January 2026, and now sits in the box under the glass with the rest of the company pieces.

7.2  The Cue Desk and the CUE Cards

During a turn, the player holding the stage manager’s token may play a CUE card. A CUE card goes face down on the table, open at its long edge, and the player in the chair reads it aloud, word for word, before any other card may be moved. The prompter checks the reading against the printed text; if a single word is changed, the card is filed to the error handler’s jurisdiction at the far end of the table and stands there until the curtain call. One printed CUE card has never once been read clean, not in a single house where this chapter was played, and the manual therefore sets its text out in full so that the players may learn it as a piece of stage business. Card C-9, the ninth CUE card of the company deck, reads:

C-9

Published from the Mare Island Strait it is filed with the error handler's jurisdiction, and the line open, to name the desk read them.

The desk reads them, as the last four words direct: at the end of every turn the player in the chair collects the open line of CUE cards, reads each standing card one last time, and files it. Card C-9 is the one the error handler files first, and it is why the errata line of this manual is kept under the chair, in the same jurisdiction.

7.3  The Season Ledger (Table 7-C)

At the curtain call of each round, the box office player settles the season ledger. Enter the house size, the shows per week, the weeks in the season, and the average ticket, and the ledger gives the season’s seated house and its box office. The defaults are the company’s own: 3,600 chairs at the Greek Theatre under the open sky, a fortnight of shows at a stretch, and thirty-one seasons between 1978 and 2009.

The ledger is unsettled. Set it once, at the curtain call of the round, and leave it standing.

7.4  Errata and Margin

Erratum to the 1984 printing: the 1978 date in 7.1 is the company’s second foundation, not its first; the first is kept in the archive drawer and is not part of play. The errata line of this chapter — the C-9 sentence, filed whole — is graded with its weakness stated, and is carried word for word in the verbatim record of Case No. 1726 of 2024, where a card pinned up and read clean beats a sentence that will not sit still. A German stage director and television actress of the same two trades, the actress-and-director the card’s company piece takes its colour from, is kept two shelves over in Lot 314 of the auction catalogue, and the studio-side cousin of the open line — a single baffling sentence kept word for word in a session log — runs on in Falling Down, KHTT Studio B. From the Mabel’s Married Life report the same chapter borrows its habit of one impossible line inside a respectable document, preserved for the next player to read.
