IN THE COURT OF GENERAL ACCOUNTING & HUMAN HEALTH

State v. The Tobacco Trade

Ruling 87-14 · Transcript extract, first session of the 31st of May · Courtroom 3, the Old Customs House

◆ ◆ ◆

Before the Bench: The Honourable M. O. Adhikari, Presiding Judge; Deputy Clerk E. R. Whitcombe
For the State: Dr. Helena Vasquez, Counsel for Public Health; the World Health Organization, Intervener
For the Defendant: No appearance. The Tobacco Trade, being a continuing practice and not a person, was represented by silence and, on two occasions, by a smell.

THE RECORD: The first session opened at half past ten in the morning of the 31st of May. Dr. Vasquez was called to the lectern, took the ledger of exhibits in both hands, and addressed the bench as follows:

“Your Honour, the matter before the court concerns a practice which, in the words of the Intervener’s brief, kills more than five million persons a year across all of the member states — a figure the brief describes as preventable in full. The epidemic does not announce itself at the door. It keeps a shop, it prints a calendar, and it has been selling the same packet, in ever-smaller wrappers, for more than three hundred years. The State submits that this is the oldest continuing defendant in the record of this court, and that it has never once appeared to give evidence.”

EXHIBIT A was then entered: a bound copy of the declaration of the member states of the World Health Organization, adopted in 1987, creating the annual observance now known as World No Tobacco Day. The Deputy Clerk read the operative lines into the record: that the day was established to draw the attention of the world to the tobacco epidemic and to the preventable death and disease it causes, and to inform the public on the dangers of tobacco use, on the business practices of the companies that trade in it, and on the steps the World Health Organization takes against it.

“Exhibit A is not a holiday, Your Honour,” continued Dr. Vasquez. “It is a standing order. Every 31st of May the world is directed to look at the shelf of its own houses — the tins, the cartons, the tins again — and say what the shelf is doing. I will note for the record that I visited the defendant’s premises at No. 11 Long Row on the morning of the 30th. On the second shelf is waiting, and not proceeded with, and oh! I submit that the interjection is the witness. The tin was open. The tin was waiting. It has been waiting, Your Honour, at every shelf, in every kitchen, since long before any of us in this room could keep a ledger.”

The bench asked the Intervener what it was doing, and Dr. Vasquez answered that the World Health Organization was, in brief, doing three things: informing the public, exposing the business practices of the trade, and assisting every member state that asked to be assisted in claiming the right of its people to health and to healthy living, so that the generations yet to be born do not inherit the shelf. The Deputy Clerk entered that it was so. The court noted that the observance is held every year on 31 May, around the world, by people of every member state, and that the record contains no year in which it was not held.

FINDINGS OF FACT: The court found, first, that the defendant is widespread in its prevalence and that its negative health effects are well in evidence; second, that the defendant keeps an office in nearly every kitchen in the jurisdiction; third, that the 31st of May is a day, and not merely a date, in that it belongs to the people and can be claimed by them. The bench adjourned Ruling 87-14 to the next 31st of May, as is the custom of this court, and directed that the shelf be left, for one day in every year, undisturbed and unattended, so that anyone who opens a door in their own house may see what is waiting on it.
