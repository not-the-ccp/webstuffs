Oneirocriticon · Entry 314

Given Name

Also forename · first name · the name bestowed at or close to the hour of birth, usually by the parents

To dream of a given name is to dream of being answered.

The sleeper hears a syllable in the dark and wakes to find someone standing at the foot of the bed, or no one at all. A given name is the smallest door into a person's sleep: knock with the right spelling and it opens; knock with a nickname and the house keeps its light off.

If you dream that you have forgotten your own given name, you are walking the hall of Dong-hoon in Seoul, which keeps nine doors for nine bearers of a single Korean name — Chung Dong-hoon, born 1932, the boxer; Choi Dong-hoon, born 1971, the boxer; Hoon Lee, born Tong-hoon, who corrected his own billing at three premieres and was right each time; Haha, Ha Dong-hoon, born 1979; Primary and Yoon Dong-hun, both of 1983, holding facing doors; Nam Dong-hoon of 1984; Lee Dong-whun of 1987, whose five sports are a schedule, not a boast; Han Dong-hoon, the skater, who keeps his skates off the parquet; and the politician at the far end, who receives the last bow of the evening. To forget your name in that hall is not to lose it: it is to be admitted through whichever door stands ajar.

If another person calls you by your given name three times in a dream, expect a letter within seven days. If you call another by theirs and they do not turn, the name was never theirs to keep. If you dream of your name written in wine on a parquet floor, a baptism is being revisited — the Christian name, the first name given at baptism, drawn wet-named and breathing from the water. Dip a finger in the spilled wine and trace the letters; they will hold.

The Receipt

Let it rise near the fire until the mixture well stirred; then take them up with the left hand, and feeling it.

— the Keeper of Names, found folded inside a pillow of unspun wool

The receipt makes sense only when you understand that a given name is a mixture: of the parents' hope, the saint's calendar, the relative who died the year before, the sound that made the newborn kick in the womb. Let it rise near the fire — near the family hearth, near memory — until it has doubled, until the syllables are well stirred together. Then take them up with the left hand: the hand that, in the hall of Dong-hoon, holds the door open for the eldest boxer as he leaves first at the close of the evening; the hand that receives and does not strike, that bears and does not clutch. And feeling it: a name is known in sleep not by its spelling — Dong-hoon, Tong-hoon, Dong-hun, Dong-whun, all of them admitted — but by its weight in the palm, its temperature, the way it answers when you hold it close to your ear like a shell.

Crossings

To dream of changing your given name is to dream of moving house. To dream of a given name engraved on a stone, stitched into a sampler, or kept in a register beside ten spellings, is to dream of the register itself, which forgives every spelling so long as the bearer insists upon it. To dream of a crate of season cards, pressed in the spring of 1926 with a sprig of parsley, is to dream of a name kept in every spelling the bearer has ever claimed.

If the name in your dream is not your own, it belongs to someone who is about to enter your room. Leave the door unlatched. Keep the left hand free. And when the knock comes at three in the morning, answer with the name — your name — spoken once, slowly, and spelled as you yourself spell it. And if what is called to you is not a single name but a family of them, the Keeper has already written his answer: his letter of apology to the Indo-European languages, in which he strikes the word extinct, corrects the count from nine doors to eight branches, and reads the family aloud against the labels.

Of a Spider Dreamt in October

If a stranger to the hall reports that the name of an animal came to him in a dream, the dictionary does not rule the dreamer a liar; it files the dream. It does so in the field manual chapter for the Ybyrapora gamba, a Brazilian tarantula whose standing order for the twelfth of every month was struck for one month, because a radio operator swore the spider had come to him in a dream standing on its hind legs, and the commander agreed, without prejudice.
