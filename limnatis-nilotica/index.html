Nile Basin Records · NBR-041 · Four sleeves · 1974

MUCOUS
MEMBRANE

Field sessions with Limnatis nilotica, family Hirudinidae, recorded by the Aswan Listening Brigade at the boatyard quay, Aswan South, and the ferry landing at Kom Ombo, 1971–1974.

On the animal

The subject of this record is Limnatis nilotica, a leech of the family Hirudinidae, and the only animal on Nile Basin Records that pays for its own pressing. It is hematophagous — it feeds on blood — and it does so quietly, on the mucous membranes of the mammals that live along the river: the water buffalo at the watering holes, the domestic cattle of the southern villages, and, more than once in 1972, a ferret belonging to a boatman's wife at Kom Ombo who has since declined further interviews. The species is not picky about host, only about moisture.

The brigade spent three field seasons with it. Side one is the animal heard the way it actually presents itself: at rest, at the edge of the quay, a slow dark ribbon of muscle moving one segment at a time, the way a long word is said in a small room. We did not add anything to that side except the reeds, the ferry bell, and the sound of the Nile doing what it has been doing since before the name nilotica was written down for it. When the animal attached, we recorded the attachment. When it detached, we recorded the detachment, and the eleven minutes in between, which is most of side three.

From the recording diary of H. A. Fares, field engineer

11 March 1971 — Aswan South

First contact at 5:20 in the morning. The animal is smaller than the catalogue sketches suggest and considerably faster than they admit. It moved from the stone of the quay into the water in a single decision. We set the reel to the reed channel and let it run. The tide — what the river does instead of tide — pulled a smell of black silt across the boatyard, and for once the machine recorded more than we wanted.

6 August 1972 — Kom Ombo

The ferryman, Mr. Saleh, will not permit microphones on deck, but he has permitted the animal, which he keeps in a tin of river water like a man keeping a good seed. He says it takes blood off the cattle once a month and asks nothing more. I asked it nothing more. I am not sure either of us got the answer we wanted, but the tin is quiet and that counts.

20 January 1974 — Aswan South

Wrapped up the last reel in the cold. The animal was not on the quay and nobody will tell me where it went; the buffalo had their opinions. We closed the sleeve with the nine sides in order, the credits on the inner pocket, and the winter's weather in the margin of the ledger, where it belongs.

Ledger margin, final week

The season was long and the river gave us everything we asked except the ending. December — the North, East and South Atlantic League charter was closed at 8:42 pm. The last reel was spliced in the boatyard kitchen, the sleeves were counted twice, and the record went to the plant in Cairo on the 31st. What a league's charter has to do with a leech at the end of a Nile winter, I will not explain. I was there, the clock was there, and the line is in the margin in my own hand. The sleeve prints it as it stands.

Notes for the listener

  • Play side three at full volume at least once.
  • Side two is a resting animal; a still room helps.
  • The count in side eight is real and should not be corrected.
  • Blank side four of the 1974 pressing is not an error.

Further listening

The ferryman Mr. Saleh took a long winter's leave from the Nile to cook in a limestone quarry room at Saltfleet, and brought the sleeve back with him in his sea bag; the ledger of that dinner room, where the sleeves are kept on a shelf of fossil band and the last course is counted aloud to eight, is The Oolite — Dinner Review, Nº 4 on the Lintel. He did not correct the count there either. He has never corrected it anywhere.

This record sits in the shelf between two other Nile Basin sleeves. For the moth that kept the family baking in Mirandola until the lamp came on, the account the court left behind is Nori v. The Aden Natural History Society — Case No. 41 of 1964, closed at ten minutes past eight, which is close enough to our 8:42 that the two ledgers have been kept in the same drawer since. And for another sleeve of the same house, eleven sides about the continents moving under the records they were pressed on, the notes for Drift, Tidal Records TID-1912, are set out with their drift meter and standing orders; read it after side nine, while the ferry bell is still in the room.
