What you are buying

This is a 1991 New York Giants home jersey, number thirty in white block numerals, worn on the outside by A. J. Greene — Anthony Jerome Greene, born June 24, 1966, a cornerback who came up out of Wake Forest and was selected by the Giants in the ninth round of the 1989 NFL draft, the kind of late pick whose name stops being said by the draft table and starts being said by the corner men. By 1991 he was a four-year man on that roster, the kind of defender who lines up with the man across from him the whole play and never lets him know it, and the jersey carries that patience in the shoulders: the fabric is broken in exactly where a working body lives, and nowhere else. The collar has loosened a quarter inch in twelve honest years of airing out. The numerals are factory-embroidered, both of them, no patches, no re-stitching, and the left sleeve bears one small ring of scuff on the underside from a shoulder pad that was never quite the right width.

Greene kept the position the way a lockkeeper keeps a lock: the Giants’ secondary ran through him from 1989 onward, and when the Giants eventually let him go he went on to the Kansas City Chiefs and kept the same job at the same corner for two more seasons, because a cornerback’s résumé is one line long and the line reads corner. This jersey is from his Giants years, the long ones, and it is sold as one garment with one number on it and a name, A. J. GREENE, stitched over the number in the old style.

Provenance

Pulled from a Giants fan’s loft in 2019, where it had ridden the top shelf of a wardrobe since the winter of 2000, in the original garment bag with the bag’s label still legible: Property of the locker room — return by Thursday. It was never returned. We cleaned it by hand, cold, no bleach, no iron on the numerals, and it hung in the back room for a full season before it was offered here.

Care

Hand wash in cold water, lay flat, air out on a line that does not spin. Do not hang it by the shoulders for more than an afternoon; the shoulders have already done eleven years of the work and they will keep their shape if you let them. It smells, faintly and permanently, of cedar and of rain that fell on a New Jersey field sometime in the 1990s, and the smell is part of the garment, so do not try to wash it out.

Correspondence

“The jersey came wrapped in brown paper and the twine was still wet from the rain, which is how I knew the wrapping had been done properly. The shoulders are exactly right. I hung it in the hallway by the cider glass and it has been the first thing I see and the last thing I see all week. Thank you for not re-stitching anything. Yours sincerely, Marged ferch Rhys Dolgellau, Wales Sesiwn Fawr Dolgellau — Restaurant Review ).”

The card she wrote on was from the Sesiwn Fawr, Dolgellau, the world music restaurant that seated its dining room in the streets of the town and then on the Marian Mawr beside the Afon Wnion before the 2009 season that did not come; the restaurant’s own review notes the sell-out field of approximately five thousand and the cider pressed on the premises, which explains the cider glass in her hallway and, apparently, why the restaurant’s name and review had migrated onto the back of the card and stayed there, word for word, all the way to a New Jersey hallway.
