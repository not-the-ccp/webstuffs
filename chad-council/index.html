Chad

Officially the Republic of Chad

Landlocked · Central Africa · Session I, Wednesday 12 February 2025, 10:00 · The lower room, riverside building, N'Djamena

Present Adama Faki, chair · Salou Moundoul, secretary-general · Abdelkerim Osman, commissioner of borders · Bello Ngou, commissioner of the lake · Aïcha Drouin, commissioner of languages and the register · the interpreter · the clerk of the lower room, formerly of the Para River Entomological Stores, No. 14 Kruiskade, Paramaribo
Absent the boatman, whose reason is the boat
Quorum Seven of eight; the boatman's seat is kept warm by a lamp that is not the lamp (see item 8)

1Opening

The chair opens the first session of the council at 10:00, the date being chosen by the council's predecessor, the river, which floods when it pleases and keeps its own calendar. The interpreter is confirmed in her post, working between French and Arabic, which are the two official languages of the Republic of Chad, with French the lingua franca and the language of government and education. The clerk is confirmed with a reservation: the clerk arrives from across two seas and one obituary, and the lower room must be made to understand him, or he it. [Res. 25/1]

2Minutes of the preceding session

There is no preceding session. This is the first. The chair reads the minutes of the predecessor body, the river, and finds them acceptable: they are all water, they are all true, and they are impossible to amend. They are adopted. [Res. 25/2]

3The standing rules, Article 7

The clerk is directed to read the standing rules of the council as found in the old register, which the clerk brought in the valise. Articles 1 through 6 are adopted without amendment. Article 7 is read aloud, once, and then a second time for the interpreter, and then a third time because the room is quiet in the way that a room is quiet when a rule is not going to be argued:

Where any name happens to be ready for use, pour over the table.

Kept word for word, as found. The clerk reports that the same line has been found in every register in the lower room from No. 14 Kruiskade to this table, and that the practice is to pour the tea over the table when the name is ready, and the name, if it is a name, survives the pour. Commissioner Ngou asks what happens to the table. The chair rules that the rule is not for argument, and the table is entered into the minutes under the name table, which it is said to have survived.

The rules are adopted. [Res. 25/3]

4Entry of the territory

The commissioner of borders is directed to enter the territory of the Republic of Chad into the register of the lower room, and does so as follows. Chad, officially the Republic of Chad, is a landlocked country in Central Africa. It is bordered by Libya to the north, Sudan to the east, the Central African Republic to the south, Cameroon to the southwest, Nigeria to the southwest, at Lake Chad, and Niger to the west. Six neighbours; the register is drawn as a hexagon with the door on no side.

There is a dispute over the word southwest, which the register shows twice. The commissioner explains that the lake comes between the two: Cameroon to the southwest, and Nigeria to the southwest at the lake, which is a different southwest, closer to the water. The chair examines the register and rules that the doubling is a fault of the map and not of the border, and both southwests stand. The secretary-general notes that the council has thus registered the only country she knows of that touches two countries to the same bearing, and that this fact is entered under Chad and not under southwesterly weather. [Res. 25/4]

The dimensions are entered as reported by the clerk's valise and not disputed: a total area of around 1,300,000 square kilometres (500,000 square miles). The clerk observes that the country in the obituary, the one filed last year beside the cedar boxes, was 165,940 square kilometres, and that the room therefore holds a country about eight times the size of the dead one. The chair rules that the room is the room, that the rain is the rain, and that size does not change the filing. The register adds, in the margin, that Chad is the fifth-largest country in Africa and the twentieth largest nation by area, which is to say it has stood in line behind nineteen and been passed by none of them since. [Res. 25/5]

5Population, and the capital

The register shows a population of twenty million. Of these, 1.6 million live in N'Djamena, the capital and largest city, which is to say that the city the council is sitting in holds a little more than one soul in twelve. Commissioner Moundoul moves that the council's own presence be struck from the count, being temporary. The chair refuses: a census, like a lamp, is not moved to make room. [Res. 25/6]

6Of the languages

The commissioner of languages reports that the Republic of Chad holds over two hundred ethnic and linguistic groups, and that the register of languages has two hundred columns of which two are in official use. She proposes, and the council agrees, that the register be kept in two columns, French in one and Arabic in the other, and that the remaining hundred and ninety-eight be kept in the lower room the way the cedar boxes keep the moths: fitted, patient, and on the brick. The interpreter is reminded that her post is not a language of the Republic, it is the hinge, and that hinges are not numbered. [Res. 25/7]

7Of the lake

The commissioner of the lake reports on Lake Chad, which belongs to four countries and to the wind. He reports that the lake is smaller than it was at the last flood, and smaller than the register's last measurement, and that he will not say by how much, because the number has been smaller every time he has said it. He asks the boatman for a sounding. The boatman, absent, sends word that the boat is not in the water, and that a lake not sounded is a lake that has said nothing, which is what the lake has said all year. The measurement is deferred to the second session, which is deferred to the flood, which is not for argument. [Res. 25/8]

8Matters from abroad: the valise

The clerk is invited to open the valise, and does. It contains three things. A lamp, at forty watts, which the Para River Entomological Stores at No. 14 Kruiskade, Paramaribo, Suriname, left burning at the window on the long evening, and which the clerk carried home in a cedar box because it is the only lamp he has ever carried home. A clipping, filed beside the stores' safety data sheet: the obituary for Suriname, the Republic of Suriname, which died at home in Paramaribo, 14 May 2024 — the obituary in the Paramaribo Weekly, with its kapok, its howler chorus, and its interment beneath the canopy with no flowers. And a card on which the stores keep the lamp's oil count, beside which is pinned a moth of the family Erebidae, described by Caspar Stoll in 1781 from Surinamese material, whole, pinned, patient: Cosmosoma evadnes, entered at the stores' desk as Safety Data Sheet No. CE-1781.

The council takes note that the neighbouring register, Suriname, was filed in obituary form, and that this council holds no form for the living, which the clerk observes is the true test of a country: not whether it will die, but whether the clerk has a form. The chair rules that the lamp be kept burning in the lower room, at forty watts, and that the moth remain where it is, on the card, reading the dark the way other animals read the light. The secretary-general adds that the lamp and the obituary and the pinned specimen are entered under the same rule that governs this council's records: a record is a record, and the rule is not for argument. [Res. 25/9]

At 16:42 the clerk interrupts the reading to report that something has come in at the window and is sitting on the register. It is brown, it is folded, and it is keeping to itself. The clerk does not call it in, for fear it is a record. The council observes the visit, enters it under Others, and does not move the register, because a thing on the register is the register's business and the business of nothing else. [Res. 25/10, unanimous, no discussion, the visitor having departed at 16:51 on its own schedule, which was quick]

9Filing and adjournment

The same week arrives, folded into the register, a small-ads page from the Paramaribo Weekly grown out of the obituary clipping: one tropical rainforest biome for sale by descent, one ecosystem to let, a biotope in German measure, a marlingspike lost, and a public notice that the council has re-entered Lake Chad in its register as a wetland biome, the measurement deferred to the flood — the Small Ads & Notices, Page B7. The clerk reads the lake's notice once, finds it says nothing new, and files the page in this drawer, where it sits with the lamp.

The clerk files these minutes in the drawer of the lower room that already holds the minutes of the Machinery Restaurant Trust, No. 47, on the naming of the North Hall, and Newsletter No. 47 of the Krummhörn Ditch-Watch & Pump Society, which keeps, like this council, one unfinished line of its ledger verbatim. The drawer has no policy about the verbatim lines, and the clerk does not propose one. The two papers and these minutes sit together with the lamp, and the lamp keeps the drawer at the temperature of a room that is listening.

The chair closes the first session at 17:02. The second session is fixed for the flood, date to be given by the river, at 10:00, in this room, with the window shut on the far side of the water and the lamp at forty watts. Nothing else being entered, the council rises, the register is closed, and the tea — the last of it, because of Article 7 — is poured over the table, and the table, which is a name, survives the pour.
