Monday, 14 July 2008 · 08:40 · rain, then the usual sun

First clean observation of the subject this morning, coming up from the East River in a motorcade of three. I had followed the paperwork all the way from Gaborone — born 1953, the papers say, which puts him at fifty-five in the first field year, and it is a good age for a permanent resident; the species holds its ground. He steps out of the green car onto the wet sidewalk in a dark coat, looks once at the flagpole on the plaza, and is gone up to the fifty-first floor. The flag he carries with him, blue over a white band over black, stands at the base of the tower the way a sentinel reed stands in the Mokgololo when the wind is down. Ten years of residency, from July 2008 to 2018, is a full decade of mornings like this one, and I intend to be at the window for all of them.

Wednesday, 26 March 2009 · 14:15 · General Assembly chamber

The subject in breeding plumage — that is, in a full side-address, standing at the rostrum while the chamber listens. The call is measured, mid-tempo, never rushed; the Botswana delegation takes its statements in the same unhurried key as the cattle camps take their morning. He speaks for eleven minutes, to the second, and when the chair interrupts he closes the folder exactly at the interruption, as if the interruption were written into the text. I counted the pauses: forty-one. A smaller bird could not keep that rhythm.

Saturday, 9 September 2012 · 07:55 · the lotus pond

The pond behind the tower is at its full, pink from the second shelf outward, and the subject has taken his morning walk along the path that narrows into the reeds. He is the only person there. I was counting the pads when he stopped under the willow and counted them too, apparently, since he stood there a full four minutes. Two counters, same pond, no contact. In the ten years I have kept this notebook I have never seen two observers of this species in the same reedbed, which is itself a data point worth a whole plate.

I had little confidence in my census figures when I first opened the tally book, and I have come to distrust my own expectations on this subject. I expect only that they are many — a Lotus Type 25, racing green, number 12 — twelve districts, twelve months, one bridge per place. The car in the motorcade is precisely that: racing green, the number 12 on the door placard in white. Twelve districts of the home territory, each keeping its own bridge; twelve months of the Assembly turning; and the counting, I am learning, is the point of the residency, not the interrupting of it.

Thursday, 22 November 2018 · 16:30 · last entry

Ten years closes. The motorcade is still green, still three cars, still the number 12 on the placard, though I cannot now say whether the bird has changed or only my pencil. He signs the log of the permanent missions, hands over the key of the fifty-first floor, and at the plaza turns once toward the flagpole before the green car takes him down the avenue and out of my window. The species, I note for the record, does not disappear; it relocates. The family kitchen that sent the subject to New York writes down the same law and has set it as a recipe — Molomo Gwapa, Family Ledger Entry No. 7 — where the pot relocates from Lobatse to Gaborone and back, keeps its ground wherever it lands, and is counted, like the districts, at one per place. Some of my colleagues keep their tallies of diplomats in stranger places than a pond — a certain obituary for a writer of futures, for instance, which counts the dark the same way I count the pads — and the goal-line keepers on the shelf at Dream Dictionary, entry 417 would read my last page as a boundary being set, which is a fairer description of a departure than any I have found. The front of the notebook returns, as always, to the front page of Webstuffs, where the index is and the weather is filed.
