Jubilant Sykes

Jubilant Roberto Sykes · American baritone, 17 September 1954 – 8 December 2025 · Los Angeles, California, to the touring circuit and back

Signal wordCAUTION
Form of supplyLive voice; concert, opera house, church, and recording medium
Prepared14 December 2025, by the Archivist of the Stage & Studio Register
SupersedesNothing. First and final issue.

1 Identification of the Substance

Substance nameJubilant Roberto Sykes
Common descriptorsBaritone; the Celebrant; the man who sang the Mass at two in the afternoon and the spirituals after midnight
OriginLos Angeles, California; raised between the conservatory and the church choir loft
Period of activity1954–2025, of which the singing career ran roughly 1970–2024 across five continents
Cas / registry no.None. The register lists him once, under Sykes, Jubilant R., filed beside the lion-class and California sheets because a sheet is a sheet.

The substance is a human baritone voice at its most complete: a low register with weight without heaviness, a mid-range that sits in a hall the way warm light sits on a wall, and a top that, when needed, cracked open into tenor brightness on a single sustained note. He was active internationally on the opera stage, in the concert hall, in musicals, and in the sanctuary, and he moved between the four without changing clothes. Contemporary worship music, African-American spirituals, gospel, funk, and pop were all in the working inventory, and he kept them in the same register as the Wagner.

2 Hazard Identification

Hazard classSustained tonal exposure; primary hazard is involuntary audience emotion at ranges up to the full house and, in recordings, indefinitely
Route of entryAuditory, direct. No skin, ingestion, or inhalation route has ever been recorded.
Hazard statement H-201May cause tears at close range, standing ovations at mid-range, and a desire to return to church on a Thursday.
Hazard statement H-204Recorded samples (see Section 8) remain hazardous after the performer has ceased; playback in a quiet room is a controlled operation.
Prevention statement P-210Keep one's handkerchief within reach. Do not sit in the front five rows without having slept.

3 Composition / Information on Ingredients

The substance is single-component. Its working mixture, however, contains the following genres in approximate proportions, by concert season: opera and art song, 35%; gospel and spirituals, 30%; contemporary worship, 15%; musical theatre, 10%; funk and pop, 10%. The proportions were not fixed. On a given Friday the worship set could swallow the evening, and on a given Sunday the opera recital could come down through the choir loft in a coat and tie.

4 First-Aid Measures

In case of exposure. If an audience member is overcome, seat them, dim the house lights to the half-plate, and allow the phrase to finish. Do not interrupt a sustained note to administer comfort; the phrase will end, and the ending is where the medicine is. If the exposure occurred in a recording, lower the volume by half before stopping the playback — stopping in the middle of a spiritual leaves the residue in the room. For exposure during the Celebrant's part of a Mass, see Section 8; the text itself is the antidote, and it should be permitted to run to the last note.

5 Firefighting Measures

Not applicable under normal conditions. The substance does not burn, does not flash, and has never once refused to sing a note that was in front of it. In the event of a stage emergency during a performance, the standard account is that the singing stopped a full bar before the alarms were acknowledged, and that the pause — exactly one bar, the conductor's count held in the air — was the safest thing in the building.

6 Accidental Release Measures

An uninvited release of the voice — a sound check that ran long, an evening song for a colleague in the wings, a funeral hymn taken up in the back of a taxi — is not an incident to be contained. Containment instructions on file at the house manager's desk are: none. The releases recorded in the register averaged four minutes, were never reported as damage, and were noted in the wing ledgers as received. The register's own practice, since the December of 2025, is to enter a single word in the margin of this sheet on any night the voice is heard again on tape: received.

7 Handling and Precautions in Use

  • Handle in halls of at least 1,200 seats; in smaller rooms the concentration of the effect is above the recommended limit.
  • Do not program the funk set immediately after the funeral hymns without an intermission; the two do not share a mood and the audience is not the buffer.
  • In musical theatre, keep the actor's entrance on the downbeat. The substance did not forgive a quarter-beat of lateness and neither, it is reported, did he.
  • Store concert recordings separately from working tapes. Playback of the working tapes was for the singer alone and is now restricted to the archive room, one playback per quarter.
  • Wash hands after handling the 2008 session tapes (see Section 8); they were handled a great deal in the weeks before the nomination.

8 Exposure Controls / Personal Protection

Occupational exposure for the substance himself was unlimited by regulation and self-governed: two recitals a night was recorded once, at the opera house, and the next morning the voice in the interview was described by the press as unbothered. For audiences and bystanders, the controls are the ones in Section 4, plus the house rule of the archive: no ear protection is ever worn for the Mass sessions. The 2008 recording of Leonard Bernstein's Mass, in which Sykes sang the part of the Celebrant, was nominated for the Grammy Award for Best Classical Album; the nomination was read on the wire at 10:41 in the morning, and the archive's standing order is that the Celebrant's section is played through, in full, whenever the wire is read. That is the only exposure limit the register has ever enforced, and it is a limit of the hearing, not of the music.

9 Physical and Chemical Properties

RegisterBaritone, with the notes described in Section 1; pitch range on file at the house tuner's bench
AppearanceSee photographs in the register; the substance was as legible as any in the catalogue
StabilityStable in use from the 1970s to the last documented performance; no degradation on file before the date of death, 8 December 2025
OdourNone. Audiences report that the room smells of nothing until the second movement of the Mass.

10 Stability and Reactivity

Stable under all conditions of performance known to the register. Incompatible with: timed ovations during a phrase, house lights raised before the last chord, and anyone who calls a baritone just low. No hazardous decomposition products are known. The voice, under stress, did not break; it got bigger, and the house manager's logbooks from three decades agree on this to the line.

11 Toxicological Information

No acute toxicity. Chronic exposure is reported to produce: improved taste, a slower pace in ordinary argument, and the habit of humming the gospel set while cooking dinner. The last has been observed in every city on the touring circuit and is not listed as a condition. Long-term exposure (decades) was the substance's own condition; no adverse effect is on file, and the register keeps the question open with the courtesy of one empty line, which is where the family has asked that nothing further be entered.

12 Environmental Information

Persistent in recording medium indefinitely. Mobile by radio. Does not bioaccumulate except in the memory of anyone in the front rows. Removal from the environment is not recommended and has not been attempted; the register treats all known copies as preserved material, and the one tape from the Los Angeles church sessions, 1998, is kept sealed and played, on request, once a year on the Sunday nearest the seventeenth of September.

13 Disposal Considerations

The substance passed on 8 December 2025. Disposal is not a word the register uses of the singer; it uses released, in the sense of Section 6, at the end of the final phrase. The remains of the working life are retained: session tapes, tour logs, the house-manager's ledger, and the nomination card, filed under Sykes, Jubilant R. Nothing is to be deaccessioned. The card itself carries the single annotation in pencil: received.

14 Transport Information

Transported by air between cities in three continents' worth of seasons, in ordinary luggage, one suitcase and the songbook. The songbook is the only document the substance carried on every leg, and it travels now, unaccompanied, to any engagement the estate accepts. No special transport class applies. The airline's manifest line, on the last flight, read the same as on every other: one passenger, one case, and a voice in the hold that the ground crew later described to the register as the loudest quiet they had ever met.

15 Regulatory Information

One nomination on file: Grammy Award for Best Classical Album, presented on the strength of the 2008 recording of Leonard Bernstein's Mass (see Section 8). No recall, no restriction, no advisory. The substance's name appears in the register's compliance column as conformed, in the sense that everything the voice undertook to do, it did.

16 Other Information

This sheet was closed on the fourteenth of December, 2025, the first Thursday after the date in Section 13. Before closing, the archivist entered one line from the intake slip, read into the sheet verbatim and left, as the register's practice requires, neither explained nor amended: "It is on the ballot box by the level of military equipment." The line came to the slip from the house manager's desk, in a hand that was not the archivist's, and the archivist will not attempt to construe it. It is part of the sheet, and the sheet is closed with it. The register keeps two sheets in the same drawer as this one, on the principle that a record is kept slowly and read twice: the safety data sheet for the Lion-class battleship, SDS-LN-1938, which is also built at speed, also revised once, and also ended in a single entry in the margin, and the brochure of the Bath Assembly Rooms, where the voice was last documented in the public calendar, on the ballroom floor, in the spring of the final season. The front of the whole is at Webstuffs.

End of sheet. Revision 0 is the final revision. Nothing further will be entered except the word received, once a year, on the Sunday nearest the seventeenth of September.
