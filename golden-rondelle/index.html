The Golden Rondelle Theater

Build Log of Dale Kowalski · The Garage on Fond du Lac Ave. · Racine, Wisconsin

500 seats · 48 cm dome · 31 gold counters so far · project not yet finished · last entry 03/14/2001 ·

NEW! Entry 14 added — the projector now runs.

What I am building

The Golden Rondelle Theater sits at the Johnson Wax Headquarters complex of S. C. Johnson & Son right here in Racine, and I have been trying to build it in the garage since the spring of 1998, at 1:50 scale. The real theater was designed by Lippincott & Margulies, and most people don't know that it started life as the Johnson's Wax Pavilion at the 1964 New York World's Fair. Construction began in October 1962, and the attraction opened April 22, 1964, along with the rest of the fair — which is why I started this project on April 22, 1998, forty years to the day, and I will not be moving the date. The original had 500 seats under a gold-colored, disk-shaped dome raised above ground, and mine will too, all 500 of them, even if the 499th and 500th seats are made of spite.

The dome

The dome is the whole point of the building, so it is the whole point of the log. I tried three ways before the fourth one worked: a halved polystyrene sphere (melted under the heat gun), a paper-mâché disk (too light, sagged in the middle like a raccoon crossing a puddle), and finally a 48 cm circle of thin MDF, 2 mm, glued over a basket of galvanized wire, sanded flat, primed twice, and covered in the real thing — 24-karat gold leaf from a craft tin, 48 squares, applied with gilder's size on the coldest Saturday of January 2000. In the shop light it goes off like a dropped quarter. My brother-in-law Gary said it looked like “a pizza in a crown” and I closed the garage door for an hour.

The seats and the game

The 500 seats are the long part. Each one is a toothpick frame with a felt square the size of a postage stamp, and I make them at the kitchen table, a family production. The house rule I came up with is that every finished seat earns one of the little gold counters — 12 mm, brass, gilt — which go up on the back wall of the garage in rows of ten, 50 rows, to match the dome. I run the whole thing like a board game, counters and all, and I call it the game. My sister-in-law still makes it one of the game. She gilds every counter before it goes on the wall, and if she has not gilded it, it does not count, no matter how many seats are under it. So far: 31 counters on the wall, 212 seats done, and a promise from her cousin that he will do fifty on a bet he has already lost.

On the screen

The original theater screened Francis Thompson's short film To Be Alive!, and the rest of the Johnson's Wax Pavilion held shoeshine machines, a home-information center, and a playground. For mine, I have the projector already: a 35mm model I bought off the surplus rack at the library book sale in 1999 for eleven dollars, which I have wired up to run To Be Alive! on a 4-foot square of white sheeting hung inside the dome. Entry 14, March 14, 2001: it ran. The lamp holds, the film ran a clean 12 minutes with two bumps, and the gold inside the dome turned the whole garage into a sundial. When I walked out, the garage was still warm from the lamp an hour later. That is the part of the build I did not plan, and it is the part I am most sure of.

What the neighbors and the almanac say

The folks next door call it “the gold pizza in Dale's garage” and have stopped asking questions, which is as good as approval. For the rest of it — the shoeshine machines, the playground, the home-information center, and the long ride back from New York — I am still cutting the pavilion annex from birch ply, and the guestbook below will tell you when it stands. If you are keeping your own logs, the build log No. 18 from the shed below Clerkenwell is the finest one I have read — a whole county of Cheshire in 2,344 square centimetres, and I want one grain of their salt for every seat I finish. And the Pacific Coast Almanac keeps a guidebook of its own that I consult for the numbers: Los Angeles — Guidebook No. 47, which I keep open beside the bench for the good of the 50 rows.
