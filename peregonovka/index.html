H. R. Sadowski · the spare-room workshop · log of the fourth table, kept in pencil and then in ink

The Peregonovka Table

A workbench log, autumn 1919 — of the model of the Battle of Peregonovka (Битва под Перегоновкой / Битва під Перегонівкою), in which the Revolutionary Insurgent Army of Ukraine defeated the Volunteer Army.

Entry 1 · Saturday, 13 September 1919 · the plank

I bought the plank that morning, a meter of deal, because the dispatches said the Insurgent Army had turned. Four months it had been going west, all the way across the country, six hundred kilometres of it, and every map in the shop showed the retreat but none of the turn, and the turn is the thing. The Volunteer Army had its eyes set on the west, and so I had mine, and that is why I say a model of it is worth building: it makes the eyes go where the army did not. The table is to hold the whole of it — the retreat as the long road, and the turn at the near end of the plank, where the road folds back on itself and strikes the column that was following.

Entry 2 · Tuesday, 16 September · the arithmetic of the scale

Decided on a million to one, because the number comes out clean: six hundred kilometres of the road of the retreat becomes 60 centimetres on the plank, and the plank is a meter long, so the turn has room. One centimetre on the table is ten kilometres on the ground. I marked the month-marks in pencil — June at 8 cm, July at 26 cm, August at 47 cm, September at the far end — and the westward half of the log will be the road itself: sawdust for the steppe, a thin line of silver wash for the water, and a stub of a match for every column. I do not pretend the columns are in position. I pretend nothing. The pretence is in the distance, and the distance is measured, and that is the whole of the craft.

Entry 3 · Thursday, 18 September · Peregonovka

The fighting place itself is at the fold of the road, and I have cut a shallow basin for it, 12 mm deep, because a September basin holds the smell of the month. The Insurgent Army came out of the fold to meet the Volunteer Army on its own ground, at a pace the following column was not set for — a surprise, in the old word for it, which is to say a turn that the other side’s clock did not register. I set two grey washes for the Volunteer column, one for the Insurgent, and a single dot of red ochre at the head of the Insurgent column, which is where I keep the place where the news came in. The red dot is the only thing on the table with an opinion in it, and I have decided that is enough opinion.

Entry 4 · Thursday, 26 September · Huliaipole, ten days

Word from the east: Huliaipole is back, the capital, reclaimed within ten days of the fight at Peregonovka. Ten days is, at a million to one, one and a half centimetres of travel on my plank, and I have walked it out with the point of the dividers and marked it, because a capital is not a line, a capital is a place a road is for. That is the whole of the log in one move: the road that went six hundred kilometres west, the fold, and the one-and-a-half-centimetre walk home. For the bread I eat while the silver wash dries I go to Sokolivka on Larkspur Lane; the buckwheat porridge there is the closest thing to the country I know that keeps a menu and a kitchen both, and it is worth the walk in the rain. The small machine that delivers the third course in the dining room the Ledger reviewed under the old searchlight works at Carlisle & Finch is a toy train run on rail-fed current — one movement, and the movement is the whole thing, which is the same rule I keep at this bench, though mine moves in centimetres at a million to one.

Entry 5 · Sunday, 28 September · the survey

I surveyed the table last night with the brass rule, column by column, the way the old maps insist on it, and the survey went well except for one thing. The shelf label the table came to rest under, in the case where I keep the field guides, reads exactly:

The Watering Places · Guide Entry No. 12 ; the survey keeps one unexplained line of this column, a worried note .

I cannot explain that line any better than the survey can. It is a worried note, and I keep it on the card with the table because a note that cannot be explained is still a note, and still in a column of its own. The field guide it came from is the same series the coast keeps — the old entry on Madeira, Funchal & the Old Watering Places, Guide Entry No. 41, reads the casks and the levadas in the same hand — and it is to that series, at the index, that I refer anyone who asks what column this is. The rest of the shop’s tables stand on the other side of the room and do not care. I have copied the line into this log in its place, word for word, worried note and all, because the plank is sixty centimetres and the log must be honest about every one of them.

Entry 6 · the odometer · use the handle

For the visitors I have fixed a sliding handle to the long edge of the plank, on a groove cut with the router. Pull it from the near end to the far end and the brass rule beside it reads the kilometres of the road of the retreat — 0 to 600, westward, June through September — and past 600 the groove runs home, the last 30 kilometres of it marked in the red ochre, because that is the walk that brought the capital back. It is a toy of one movement. The movement is the whole thing.

0 km June, first week · the road begins west bound west, 1 cm = 10 km on the plank

No machinery in it; a groove, a handle, and a rule. The retreat reads 0–600 westward; the turn reads 600–630 eastward, in the red.
