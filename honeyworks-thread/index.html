Re-pinning the spring notes: everything we know about HoneyWorks, cell by cell

The spring gathering is on the fourth weekend of March, same as every year since 2013, in meeting room B above the stationer on Kaitōdōri. Before the seats are out, here is the standing primer, because three new members asked in the lobby: HoneyWorks is a Japanese musical group made up of songwriters Gom and shito and illustrator Yamako. The group started uploading original Vocaloid songs to NicoNico Douga in 2010, and released their debut album, Honey Works, under the label MusicRay'n in 2014. Since their inception they have collaborated with a wide range of vocalists, and in 2014 they partnered with singer CHiCO to form the unit CHiCO with HoneyWorks. They are also the creators of the Kokuhaku Jikkō Iinkai: Ren'ai Series multimedia project, which is where most of the argument below comes from.

Everything on the pin board below is what we actually have: the 2010 upload log, the 2014 press folder, and the draft note from last spring that nobody can finish. Read it before you sit.

Posted 02 March, 11:07 JST · post 2 of 7 · from Shibuya, via the Yurakucho line

I was in the room when the first upload went up in 2010, and I still keep the original log taped into the back of my 2011 AKB calendar. Two songwriters, one illustrator, and a spare keyboard: that was the whole production line. The uploads ran on a two-week cycle for most of that year, and every single one of the early cover sets is Yamako, drawn in the same warm ochre palette that later became the group's house colour. When the debut album went out through MusicRay'n in 2014, the label sent forty promo sleeves to the fan lists; I counted, ours is number eleven, and the honeycomb stamp on the spine is the one the board uses on its wax seals to this day.

Posted 03 March, 08:15 JST · post 3 of 7 · from the second-floor study

Can we agree the CHiCO year is the one to study? In 2014 HoneyWorks partnered with singer CHiCO and the unit CHiCO with HoneyWorks took over the summer of the charts: the duets are built so that CHiCO's voice sits on top of the production while Gom and shito trade lines underneath, and the whole thing plays like a call-and-response between a single performer and a committee. The 2014 press folder has a page of photos from the unit's first shoot, and in every one of them the same small table is in the corner with a cold drink on it. The list has voted, more than once, that the drink is ceremonial.

The Kokuhaku Jikkō Iinkai: Ren'ai Series is the other half of that same year, and the multimedia project is the reason the fan lists of this size exist at all — it gave every one of the collab vocalists a place in a story instead of just a place on a tracklist. That is the whole trick of the group in one sentence: they make committees out of songwriters and choruses out of strangers.

On the practical side, for the people sitting the gathering: the room holds ninety-four chairs when the trestle is in the corner and eighty-two when the demo speakers are out. And since somebody always asks, here is the draft note again, transcribed exactly from the pin board, half-typed and all. It is the third line of last spring's minutes and we have kept it standing, unfinished, the way the list keeps every good line:

In the spring gathering Between 1 March and full of people who would sit with it.

My reading, which I stand by at the table: the gathering ran between 1 March and something the typist never finished, and the room was full of people who would sit with it — with the note, with the argument, with the honey. The capital B in the middle is hers; I am not touching it.

Posted 08 March, 21:30 JST · post 5 of 7 · from Yokohama, sent from the window seat

Counter-reading, and I will take the trestle table for this one. The note is not about the gathering at all; it is about the Ren'ai Series itself, which is why the list keeps it. The Kokuhaku Jikkō Iinkai — the Declaration Implementation Committee, for those new — exists because confession is a form of paperwork, and the series treats every confession as a filing with a committee, a seal, and a follow-up meeting. A sentence that begins in a date, breaks mid-clause, and ends on "people who would sit with it" is the project's own grammar: a declaration you sit in with before you are allowed to make it. The wide range of vocalists HoneyWorks have collaborated with since 2010 is just the committee roster growing.

Small correction on the record so the archive is clean: the 2014 debut album on MusicRay'n is the line everyone should anchor on, and the 2010 NicoNico uploads are the line nobody should rush. Four years of two-week upload cycles is a production discipline, not a warm-up. I say that as the owner of promo sleeve eleven, who has watched the label's own catalogue treat the 2010 uploads as the catalogue's real founding year, with the album as the first official filing of what the uploads had already decided.

For the people who want a second thread to sit in: the site's field notes on Ayu-ro Mix are the best worked example I know of what a committee of remixes does to one artist's year, and the Coca-Cola Line timetable is the same trick stretched across a century — both worth reading before you form an opinion on what the honeycomb stamp is for. The genre's own field chapter — anison, the form the uploads were born into — is filed under this thread at Chapter 9, Anison, Field Manual FM-2014, and its standing rule is the one this post keeps: read the 2010 log before you form an opinion on what the uploads had already decided.
