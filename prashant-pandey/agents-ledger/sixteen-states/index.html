Sixteen States in One Morning

A walking tour of Germany — sixteen constituent states, more than 83 million people, the Baltic Sea and the North Sea to the north, the Alps to the south, and a capital on the Spree.

The country on foot

Germany is a country in Western and Central Europe, lying between the Baltic Sea and the North Sea to the north with the Alps to the south. Its sixteen constituent states have a total population of over 83 million, making it the most populous member state of the European Union. It borders Denmark to the north; Poland and the Czech Republic to the east; Austria and Switzerland to the south; and France, Luxembourg, Belgium, and the Netherlands to the west. The nation's capital and most populous city is Berlin and its main financial centre is Frankfurt; the largest urban area is the Ruhr.

The tour walks sixteen states in sixteen days, in short walks linked by regional rail — about ninety kilometres on foot in all, with the rest done by train and by looking. Settlement in the territory of modern Germany began in the river valleys north of the Alps, and the order below follows that arc: north to the Baltic, then down through the riverlands to the mountains. The party assembles at 06:15, the same hour the bus leaves the char for Bhopal.

Stops

1 · Schleswig-Holstein · List → Hörnum

Day 1 · 08:00–11:40 · 6.2 km

Walk the foot of the Red Cliffs from the Danish-border ferry to the lighthouse at the island's tip — the North Sea on one side, the Baltic on the other, Denmark two kilometres north along the beach. Two lions and a nettle leaf fly above the harbour.

2 · Mecklenburg-Vorpommern · Rostock → Warnemünde

Day 2 · 08:30–12:00 · 5.4 km

Along the Strom to the Baltic: the old Hanseatic gables, the lighthouse shaped like a teapot, the ferry across the Warnow. Seats on the pier are numbered like a theatre.

3 · Hamburg · Speicherstadt → Landungsbrücken

Day 3 · 09:00–13:30 · 7.1 km

The warehouse district on oak piles, the Elbe, the landing bridges, and the grain elevators. This was the state of the 1892 cholera, and the port health office kept its rules on the wall.

PORT HEALTH OFFICE — HAMBURG — 1892 — FRAGMENT
“The proper place for cholera; where windows and admit fresh air.”

4 · Bremen · Schlachte → Markt

Day 4 · 08:45–11:15 · 4.8 km

The Weser embankment, the Roland, the town hall, and the smallest state in the federation. The market step measures exactly one Bremen ell — or so the custodians say.

5 · Lower Saxony · Hannover

Day 5 · 09:10–12:30 · 5.9 km

Georgstraße to the Maschsee: Leibniz's umbrella, the lake dug by hand in the thirties, and the beer gardens along the far bank. The train to Quedlinburg leaves from the hall beneath the clock.

6 · Saxony-Anhalt · Quedlinburg

Day 6 · 08:20–10:45 · 3.9 km

More than a thousand half-timbered houses climb the castle hill to the abbey church, the Bode running below. The lanes are cobbled and the walk is short; take it slowly.

7 · Berlin · Brandenburg Gate → Reichstag

Day 7 · 09:00–11:00 · 1.9 km

The capital and most populous city: the Gate, the Spree, the dome, the government district. Nineteen hundred metres and the better part of a country's history.

8 · Brandenburg · Potsdam

Day 8 · 08:50–13:00 · 6.3 km

From the Stadtschloss across the Havel to Sanssouci: the Dutch Quarter on one side, the New Garden on the other, and the palace on the hill where the wind keeps the count.

9 · Saxony · Dresden

Day 9 · 09:30–14:15 · 7.7 km

The Zwinger and the Neumarkt along the Elbe: the Frauenkirche rebuilt stone by stone, the procession of princes on the wall, and the funicular climbing the hill above the river.

10 · Thuringia · Erfurt

Day 10 · 08:40–11:50 · 5.2 km

The Dom steps, the Gera, and the Krämerbrücke — the merchants' bridge with houses built on the bridge itself. Buy a Thuringian rostbratwurst at the far end and eat it walking back.

11 · Hesse · Frankfurt

Day 11 · 09:15–13:05 · 6.8 km

The Römer and the Main bridge, the skyline across the water, the central bank in its slot. This is the main financial centre; the trains here run to the second, not the first, decimal place.

12 · Rhineland-Palatinate · Bacharach

Day 12 · 08:30–11:45 · 4.3 km

The river ferry across the Rhine and the climb to Burg Stahleck: castles above the vineyards, the Lorelei rock downstream, and the wine list written on a slate by the landing.

13 · Saarland · Saarbrücken

Day 13 · 09:20–11:40 · 4.6 km

Ludwigskirche and the Saar bridge: the river loops like a letter, and the Franco-German border is close enough to read on the menu. The stop is short; the train waits.

14 · Baden-Württemberg · Stuttgart

Day 14 · 09:00–12:25 · 6.1 km

Schlossplatz to the Königstraße: vineyards rising behind the department stores, Swabian precision in the station clock, and Spätzle where a vermicelli might be. The V-mark brass gauge travels with the party — its one instruction reads: “This may be used to come within 2 inches of the best vermicelli.”

15 · North Rhine-Westphalia · Cologne

Day 15 · 09:00–13:30 · 8.2 km

The Dom and the Rheinauhafen, the Rhine wide and slow, and the Ruhr beyond — the largest urban area in the country. The bridge locks outnumber the days of the tour.

16 · Bavaria · Munich → Garmisch-Partenkirchen

Day 16 · 08:45–13:15 · 7.4 km

Marienplatz and the Isar bridges, then rail south to the Alps: Zugspitze at 2,962 metres, the country's highest point, where the count ends and the walking tour folds itself into a map.

Kit and custody

Carry the Agent's Ledger No. 007 — 210 pages, sewn split-cane cover — whose chain-of-custody column is ruled into the right margin of every page, with space for hand, date, and a witness's initials. The Vidisha ferry stub (valid once, either direction, in any fog) is accepted nowhere in Germany; keep it anyway, since the river charges no tariff. The master copy of this tour is filed by the nail by the Napa-side door, and the records are grass: grass is pulled up, and grows again.
