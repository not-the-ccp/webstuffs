Does anyone remember Lizzette Kattan? She was born in Honduras — San Pedro Sula, I think, around 1939 — and spent the nineteen-seventies and eighties shuttling between Milan and New York as a fashion editor. I'm trying to pin down her Italian years: Harper's Bazaar Italia, then Uomo Bazaar Italia, then Cosmopolitan Italia, before she became Editor-in-Chief of Harper's Bazaar France.

She's mostly known now, if she's known at all, for giving Gia Carangi her first break. A teenage Gia walked into the Milan office in 1977 carrying a bag of clothes, and Lizzette put her on a page within weeks. After the magazines she was Consul General of Honduras in Milan — from front-row seats to the consular queue, which is the detail that floors me. Old tearsheets, photographer names, anything: I'd be grateful.

atelier_milan14 Nov, 11:03

I can confirm the Consulate General bit. She held the post through the nineties, based somewhere near Porta Venezia; I renewed a passport there in 1994 and she received me herself, in a navy suit that looked like it had just come off a Valentino runway. Before that she was at the Hearst building in New York, then Condé Nast Italia in Milan — offices off Via Montenapoleone, if I remember right, on the third floor above a fabric shop whose shutters were always half-down.

She arrived in Milan in 1974 or 1975, Honduran by birth, New York by way of Paris. She spoke five languages and dressed like nobody else in the room: men's tailoring cut for a woman, silk scarves tied like belts. The Italian editions loved her because she actually bought the clothes rather than borrowing them.

giaphoto14 Nov, 14:48

The Gia story is true, more or less. I was assisting on a shoot in 1978 — a small studio near Via della Spiga — and Lizzette brought in a seventeen-year-old from Philadelphia nobody had heard of. Gia had bleached hair, a leather jacket, and a stare that cleared the room. Lizzette said, "Shoot her in the jacket, nothing else," and that page in Uomo Bazaar, autumn 1978, was the first time her name appeared in print. Wilhelmina signed her not long after; she was in New York by spring, on the cover of American Vogue within the year.

Lizzette never took credit for it. If you asked her, she said Gia was going to be a star regardless, and she was right.

threadbare15 Nov, 08:12

There's a photo of the two of them at the 1982 Met party — Gia in Halston, Lizzette in a black tuxedo with yellow gloves — and another of Lizzette alone at the Bazaar France office on a street near the Opéra, champagne bucket on the desk, cigarette smoke against the window. She ran that edition from 1983 to 1986, I believe, then left publishing entirely for the consulate. A waste, some said; she said diplomacy was the next collection. Later, a record label pressed the whole arc — the 1977 bag of clothes, the jacket page, the Met, the St. Johns ferry — onto two LPs; the liner notes for Già: The Milano–Philadelphia Tapes give her yellow gloves their own track on Side B, in her words.

ferry_keeper15 Nov, 16:55

I found a mimeographed itinerary in a lot of torn tearsheets last month: a 1983 press tour, Milan to New York, showrooms and a textile mill in Como, models and editors moving together like a small circus. One line on the sheet reads: "This tour keeps to the St. Johns ferry." Nobody in the thread where I posted it could place which St. Johns — Jacksonville, maybe, or Portland — but reading it now I think it just means the ferry was the through-line of the trip, the one fixed point between the cities. Lizzette's handwriting is in the margin: "Confirm the boat."
