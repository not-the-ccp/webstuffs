Welcome, everyone, to the pinned thread for the new expansion piece. The board in play is the oldest institution of higher education in New York and the fifth-oldest in the United States: a private Ivy League research university in New York City, established in 1754 as King’s College by royal charter under George II of Great Britain, on the grounds of Trinity Church in Manhattan. The university was renamed Columbia College in 1784 following the American Revolution, and in 1787 was placed under a private board of trustees headed by former students Alexander Hamilton and John Jay. In 1896 the campus was moved to its current location on Morningside Heights and renamed Columbia University.

The piece carries one card, K-1754, the Charter Card. It is played exactly like a CUE card: face down on the table, open at its long edge, read aloud word for word by the player in the chair, and checked against the printed text by the prompter. I will say it once here, the way Chapter 7 says it of its own card: K-1754 has never been read clean, in no house where it has been played. This thread is the place for clarifications, and the final line of the card is the whole of it. Bring a magnifier.

This is the same trick as card C-9, and the manual already warned us. Chapter 7 — the Director’s Chair chapter, The Director’s Chair (Tina Packer) — sets the precedent in plain print: one printed CUE card has never once been read clean in a single house where that chapter was played, which is why its text is set out in full. K-1754 inherits the record. The line in question, the card’s final line, reads:

K-1754

“The duty or function of recording true time only so long as those made without Meat_.)”

My reading after eleven attempts: the clock in the board room keeps true time only for so long as those made without the meat of the charter stand at the desk — and the Meat is a misprint for Met, and the period-and-parenthesis is the printer shrugging. I have changed one word on all eleven readings. The error handler has eleven copies. I am not sorry.

A small archival footnote before the arguing starts: the green ribbon of this expansion’s binder is a deliberate nod to the script drawer of Lot 314 in the auction catalogue, the personal archive of Monika Baumgartner, where the leaf of the first printing still carried its green ribbon when the nephew of the consignor displayed it at the sale of 14 October 2025. Whoever was in that room remembers the running joke about the tea-stained page corners in her scripts. The King’s College binder therefore arrives with exactly four stained corners on page one, none of them from coffee, and the estate lawyers have been informed. Hamilton’s handwriting on the 1787 trust minutes was never this legible either, so we are ahead of the original.

For those who have not walked it: the old campus sat on the grounds of the very church that gave the charter its setting, and a visitor’s board of that era is a small, steeple-shadowed thing. The 1896 move to Morningside Heights is where the expansion earns its name — a whole second board, reached after nineteen years of argument and forty-two hires of the moving company, which the box office player may enter in the season ledger as a one-off expense. The defaults of the new ledger are the college’s own: charter year 1754, trustees seated 1787, campus seated 1896, and a house of four thousand chairs in the new city, of which Hamilton’s ghost holds the season ticket. Set the ledger once and leave it standing; that rule has outlived both colleges.

Clarification, for the record, in the wording of section 7.2 of the manual: if a single word of the Charter Card is changed in reading, the line is filed to the error handler’s jurisdiction at the far end of the table and stands there until the curtain call. As of the curtain call of 9 March 2026, twenty-seven copies of the final line of K-1754 stand here, each with a different word changed. The line is kept word for word, stray marks and all:

“The duty or function of recording true time only so long as those made without Meat_.)”

Do not “fix” the Meat. Do not “explain” the closing parenthesis. It is the line that is kept, in the same jurisdiction as card C-9, under the same chair, with the same weakness stated. Gus is welcome to his twelfth attempt; the jurisdiction is spacious.

One more rule before Marta pins the summary, because the true time half of the sentence is doing real work: the board clock in the King’s College room is wound by the reading of the charter, and it runs only between the first and the last reading of the card in a round. Read the card twice, as the house is supposed to, and the clock keeps true time for exactly the interval between your two voices. That is the whole mechanics of the line, as far as I can make out of it. The twenty-seven filed copies at the far end of the table are worth twenty-seven minutes of that clock, which is to say the board in Morningside Heights is now, canonically, a quarter of an hour behind the one on the church grounds. Nobody play it on the hour.
