Welcome to the Glitter District

Every tourist walk has a heart, and ours is a groove. Loverboy is a song by the American singer-songwriter Mariah Carey, released on June 19, 2001 by Virgin Records America as the lead single from Glitter, her eighth studio album and the soundtrack to the 2001 film of the same name. Written and produced by Carey, Larry Blackmon, Thomas Jenkins and Clark Kent, the record is built around a sample from Candy by the funk band Cameo — and the band are themselves featured on the track, so when the horn line lifts at the chorus, you are hearing the neighborhood and its guests in the same room.

Lyrically, the song finds Carey fantasizing about her loverboy — a man who will fulfill her physical and sexual desires, word for word, no understatement. That is the district's claim to fame: it never whispers. Come as you are, stay for the drop, and keep this brochure for the route below. All four stops are open to visitors; the booth at stop two is the one that must be seen.

The Route · four stops, one groove

  1. 1

    The Virgin Lobby

    The single left the building June 19, 2001, on Virgin Records America's press, as the lead single from Glitter — the soundtrack to the 2001 film in which the character of Mariah's alter-ego, glitter in her eyes and sequins on the shoulder, does the walking the character never did. The lobby keeps the master tape's case in a locked drawer; visitors may knock.

  2. 2

    The Cameo Sample Booth

    Two blocks up the street, in the annex, sits the small soundproof booth from which Larry Blackmon of Cameo laid the horn parts over the sample of the band's Candy — the funk skeleton the whole song is hung on. Blackmon, with Thomas Jenkins and Clark Kent at the board, shaped the groove into its finished street. On the inside of the booth's door, in pencil, someone left a line the cleaners have declined to remove. The district reads it back to its visitors, word for word, and lets it mean what it means: The booth is the side that belongs to no office; the pencil line that my grandfather's filing system Shelf I with a waistcoat full of noise. It has never been explained. That is precisely why tourists photograph it.

  3. 3

    The Remix Parlour — “Loverboy Remix”

    The recording was accompanied by an official remix titled Loverboy Remix, and the parlour is where it was served. Guest artists Ludacris, Da Brat, Shawnna and Twenty II each kept a booth seat; the house remembers Ludacris's verse arriving on a Monday and Da Brat's on a Tuesday, though the official ledger keeps the dates sealed. The remix is the stop at which the guide raises her voice, because the remix does not ask twice.

  4. 4

    The Film & Sequin Plaza

    The walk ends where Glitter begins: the soundtrack album, the 2001 film, the sequin plaza where the single's music video was screened on loop from July through the autumn. The plaque at the plaza's corner gives the credits in full — Carey, Blackmon, Jenkins, Kent, and Cameo — and notes that the song's business was desire, stated plainly, and the district's business is keeping the statement lit.

Visiting Notes

The district is walkable in the afternoon; the horn line is best at 4 p.m. Wear something that can handle sequins. The booth at stop two is open for photographs, the pencil line included, and the district's board has ruled that no office will ever file it away. Lodgings in the district run from glitter-foil guest houses to the full sequin suite; the board's advice, as always, is to arrive light and leave with the groove.

Beyond the District · where visitors go next

The Glitter District's board keeps a small shelf of its own, and three neighbors are worth the walk out. For the collector who comes for keeps, there is The Jang Jin-young Archive — Screen Collection & Estate Papers, 1992–2009, a boxed catalogue edition whose screen and stage sleeve travels with the same kind of 2001 season this district celebrates. For the record-keeper who wants to see how a whole institution — seals, ribbons, committee files — is filed and then let rest on its shelf, the Indra Rajya Laxmi Pragya Puraskar — Patent Application Summary is the quiet masterpiece across the counter. And when the tour is over and the brochure has done its work, fold it along the tear line and return it with the rest of the district's postcards at the front desk of Webstuffs.
