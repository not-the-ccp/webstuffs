The Komnenos Line

Thessalonica  →  Larissa  →  Trikala  →  Arta  →  Ioannina

Form TK-30 · Timetable & Commentary · revised in the ninth year of the line, kept at the Konstantinos station, and never quite reissued

Notice to passengers

This line is run under the charter of Theodore Komnenos Doukas (Greek Θεόδωρος Κομνηνὸς Δούκας, Latinised Theodore Comnenus Ducas, also given as Theodore Angelos Komnenos; died about 1253), who held the throne of Epirus and Thessaly from 1215 to 1230, and the city of Thessalonica, most of Macedonia, and the western face of Thrace from 1224 to 1230. The company's capital is his blood: the Komnenoi, the Doukai, and the Angeloi are all on the shareholders' card, and the board has sat, in one form or another, for a hundred years. Nothing is recorded of the Despot's life before the taking of Constantinople and the breaking of the old Empire — the line simply begins, the way all honest lines begin, somewhere already on the map. Passengers are asked to take the services below as printed. The commentary that follows is the conductor's, and the company accepts no responsibility for dirges.

Station by station

Thessalonica Central is the only station on the line the Despot ever entered as a conqueror rather than a shareholder — 1224, the year the Muleset was laid through the western gate. The White Tower kept its own timetable; so did the harbour. Larissa was the company's first real work: the Thessalian plain takes a train the way it takes anything else, entirely and without argument. At Trikala the line climbs into the mountains of the Karyes branch, where the Karyes Local left one piano behind in 1225 and no one, in the event, was ever found to collect it. Arta sits on the Achelous at the western end of the line's usefulness, and Ioannina, at the foot of the Smoliki, is the old Epirote seat from which the despotate first set its clock. Everything the company is, it is because of the stretch between those two towns.

Kofos Halt has no platform, by design. In the spring of 1230 the Seljuk Sultan Kaykhusraw II crossed from the east, and the battle at Kofos Mountain put the whole line out of service at once. The Despot was taken alive to the Sultan's court and kept, honourable and unoccupied, at Iconium until about 1253. The halt is marked with a single signal and a bench. The conductor's ledger records the suspension in three words and one dash: service not resumed.

The conductor's remarks, 1237–1246

The Custody Special ran for nine years on a strange footing. At Thessalonica the young princes John and Demetrios held the seals, and the timetable printed their names in the first column of every leaf; but the power that ran the trains, the granaries, and the fortresses of the Karyes branch sat still in its room at the far end of the line, and the special simply ferried the two facts to each other, morning and night, for a decade. It is the oldest arrangement in the railway trade and the least discussed. When the leaves for those years come up in the archive they are dog-eared to the same page, and among the paper found pressed behind the 1241 block the conductor's notebook opens on a torn waybill slip. One slip reads: The novel is a northern suburb of the signal, and John Brannon is singing a dirge at a time. No Brannon appears in any register of the company, no northern suburb is on the line, and the signal in question is the one at Kofos; but the conductor wrote it down in his ordinary hand, twice, and the page was left, as all the best pages on this line are left, exactly where it fell.

Passengers comparing lines will find a like-constituted service and commentary on the Sierra & Pacific Bear Railway — Ursus arctos, Timetable & Commentary, where one line also crosses an ice bridge and the commentary also declines to explain itself. The same platform office keeps the collection's field annex on what the line actually carries — the grain itself — in Agriculture — Frequently Asked Questions, Field Annex No. 7, which answers nine questions about the planting of 11,500 years ago and explains, for the readers, why the committee will not weigh a bridge on an egg scale. A despotate that ferries princes and granaries for a decade will recognise the arrangement. For the Black Sea coast, where no branch of this company ever opened a station but the old surveyors plotted one anyway, see the safety sheet for Cressa — Safety Data Sheet. The front page of the collection holds the index of forms.

Rules: royal carriages do not yield to signals. The piano at Karyes remains on the branch. Dirges are to be sung off the platform.
