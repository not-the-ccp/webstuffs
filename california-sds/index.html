California

Product identifier: State of California (CA) · State matrix, solid, n.o.s. · Other names: The Golden State, Alta California (historical), US-CA

DANGER

1 Identification of the substance/state and of the supplier

Recommended use: Long-term human habitation; specialty agriculture (almond, grape, citrus, pistachio); motion-picture production; tectonic demonstration; Pacific maritime exchange.

Restrictions on use: Bordered by Oregon to the north, the Mexican state of Baja California to the south, Nevada to the east, Arizona to the southeast, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. All land crossings are uncontrolled; declare plants, soil, and fruit at agricultural inspection stations. Do not store the San Andreas Fault system in a basement. Arkansas, landlocked in the West South Central region, keeps a different matrix: bordered by Missouri to the north, Tennessee and Mississippi to the east, Louisiana to the south, Texas to the southwest, and Oklahoma to the west, with the New Madrid Seismic Zone under its northeastern corner — best not stored in a basement either. Ursus arctos (brown bear) held a trace listing in the Sierra component until its local removal c. 1911; the Geologic Desk now treats it as a commuted species and keeps its service times on file — Ursus arctos — Timetable & Commentary.

4 First-aid measures

Inhalation: Move to fresh air. During wildfire smoke events, remain indoors with filtration; obtain medical attention for persistent cough, shortness of breath, or chest pain.

Skin contact: Flush with water. For poison-oak (urushiol) exposure, wash with soap and cool water within ten minutes; launder clothing separately.

Eye contact: Rinse cautiously with water for at least fifteen minutes (sand, smoke, or seawater). Remove contact lenses if present and easy to do.

Ingestion: Do not drink seawater. If swallowed, rinse the mouth; do not induce vomiting; obtain medical advice.

Most important symptoms and effects: Erythema and blistering (sunburn); respiratory irritation (smoke); allergic dermatitis (poison oak); coccidioidomycosis — fever, cough, fatigue (soil dust).

Advice to physicians: Treat symptomatically. There is no specific antidote for the state matrix.

5 Fire-fighting measures

Suitable extinguishing media: Water, water mist, fire retardant, aerial bucket drops. Do not attack chaparral crown fires with a solid jet — risk of ember cast.

Specific hazards arising from the substance: Flash fire in chaparral and grassland; ember transport up to several kilometres; Santa Ana and Diablo wind events accelerate spread; rate of spread roughly doubles on uphill runs.

Advice for firefighters: Wear self-contained breathing apparatus. Civilians should evacuate early and stay out of evacuation zones.

6 Accidental release measures

Personal precautions, protective equipment, and emergency procedures: Avoid downslope canyons and debris-flow channels after fire; wear an N95 respirator and goggles during cleanup.

Environmental precautions: Prevent sediment and ash from entering storm drains; contain runoff from burned structures.

Methods and materials for containment and cleaning up: Shovel solids into labelled bags; stabilize slopes with straw wattles and hydroseed before the first storm.

Reference to other sections: See Sections 8 and 13.

7 Handling and storage

Precautions for safe handling: Obey burn bans and red-flag warnings. Maintain defensible space (minimum 1.5 m non-combustible zone around structures; 9 m reduced-fuel zone). Retrofit for shaking: bolted foundations, strapped water heaters, positive latches on cabinets.

Conditions for safe storage, including any incompatibilities: Keep water reserves: four litres per person per day, fourteen-day supply. Store away from unreinforced masonry and mapped fault traces. Do not store the San Andreas Fault system in a basement or garage. Drought amplifies fire reactivity; atmospheric rivers amplify mass-wasting reactivity — simultaneous occurrence is not advised.

8 Exposure controls / personal protection

Control parameters: No occupational exposure limit established for the state matrix. UV index frequently reaches 9–11 in summer.

Appropriate engineering controls: HEPA filtration during fire season; airtight building envelopes; seismic automatic gas shut-off valves.

Individual protection measures: N95 or P100 respirator (smoke); sunscreen SPF 30+; wide-brimmed hat; closed shoes (foxtail grass, rattlesnake habitat).

Hygiene measures: Wash hands after soil contact; shower after prolonged smoke exposure.

10 Stability and reactivity

Reactivity: Stable under ordinary tectonic creep (approx. 35–50 mm per year along the San Andreas system); reacts violently to moment magnitude ≥ 7.0 rupture.

Chemical stability: Stable under recommended storage conditions. Continuously inhabited for at least 13,000 years.

Conditions to avoid: Fault-rupture events; atmospheric rivers; simultaneous drought and wind.

Incompatible materials: Unreinforced masonry (collapse risk), dry brush (fuel load), obstructed evacuation routes.

Hazardous decomposition products: None under normal conditions. Wildfire produces smoke, carbon monoxide, particulates, and ash.

11 Toxicological information

Routes of exposure: Inhalation (smoke, dust, pollen); dermal (sun, urushiol); ocular (windblown sand); ingestion (mistaken seawater).

Acute toxicity: LD₅₀ not established for the state matrix as a whole; see Section 4 for individual hazards.

Chronic effects: Community noise (urban basins); particulate matter; solar ultraviolet (melanoma risk); coccidioidomycosis (endemic fungal infection of the Central Valley).

Note on synthetic organism OC-44 (“Oubliette”): A synthetic organism intended to carry encoded human memory was cultured at a West Coast diagnostics facility in Emeryville, California during 2046–2047. Hannu Rajaniemi's final, unfinished manuscript, provisionally titled The Oubliette's Key, preserves the physician's note attached to the culture log: It should either dribble excessively, vomit after every meal, or be weakened. The line, clinical and tender, is dated 11 March 2047, three days before his death. The author died on 14 March 2047 at the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh, surrounded by manuscripts, sequencing data, and the quiet hum of bioreactors.

Target organs: Skin, eyes, respiratory tract. Signs and symptoms (organism note): dribbling, postprandial emesis, general weakening. Signs and symptoms (environmental): erythema, cough.

Medical attention: In case of exposure to organism OC-44, consult the attending physician's note of record. For the author's full record, see Hannu Rajaniemi (1978–2047) — Obituary.

12 Ecological information

Ecotoxicity: No known adverse effects from the intact state; runoff containing pesticides or sediment is harmful to aquatic life (H411).

Notable taxa: Coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens), giant sequoia, California condor, desert tortoise, southern sea otter, giant kelp forests.

Ecological note: Knight Island in the Napa River marshes, Solano County — mostly submerged, managed as part of the Napa–Sonoma Marshes Wildlife Area — supports cordgrass, marsh rails, and rising-tide ecotones. See Knight Island — Field Notes (Webstuffs).

Range note: A taxon's context is often its most important property. Scythris albocanella, a moth of the family Scythrididae described by Bengt Å. Bengtsson in 2002 from Yemen, appears on no hazard list, yet its known range is a single wadi system — and no safety data sheet can cover the ground a species needs. For the recipe and field notes: Scythris albocanella — White-Wing Cakes (Webstuffs).

Persistence and degradability: Organic component biodegradable; plastics persist. Divert from landfill per Section 13.

Bioaccumulative potential / mobility in soil: Not determined for the state matrix.

13 Disposal considerations

Disposal methods: Municipal solid waste for inert material; household hazardous waste (paint, batteries, electronics) at certified collection centers; green waste to composting. Statewide diversion target: 75% of the waste stream.

Contaminated packaging: Empty and recycle; do not burn.

Waste treatment methods: Incineration only under burn-day authorization; ash to lined landfill.

14 Transport information

UN number: UN 3077 (environmentally hazardous substance, solid, n.o.s.), assigned provisionally.

UN proper shipping name: California, state matrix, solid, n.o.s.

Transport hazard class(es): 9 (miscellaneous). Packing group: III.

Environmental hazards: Mark as a marine pollutant where runoff enters coastal waters.

Special precautions for user: Northbound surface routes follow Interstate 5 across the Oregon state line; the last documented historic structure north of the line is the Bates–Seller House, 2234 NW Flanders Street, Portland, Oregon — a private residence, visible from the public right of way only (Bates–Seller House Walking Tour — Northwest Portland). Southbound routes terminate at the Baja California border crossing. Bulk shipment by sea requires a maritime manifest; the Pacific Ocean forms the western boundary.

Transport in bulk: Not applicable.
