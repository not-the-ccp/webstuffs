Hibiya Ward Community Office · Station Annex, third floor

Lost & Found

Notice No. 216/2016 · Filed 26 January 2016 · 04:12 · Status: UNCLAIMED

KEPTWard Annex · Bin C, shelf 3

Item One pen name, male form, Japanese, printed on a white script label in blue pencil: Mitsurō Kubo (久保 ミツロウ, Kubo Mitsurō).

Reported missing from The title block of a television script, a radio schedule for a Tuesday morning, and a stack of rough pages in which the name stood in for an artist who draws manga, writes copy, and holds a microphone on the same morning.

Last seen Pinned above the door of the sound lab by the Steward’s bench, 25 January, at the hour the animators’ first coffee lands. The lab belongs to a 2016 series — the one about ice, Yuri on Ice — for which the missing name is on the co-creator’s line, beside the animators, the composers, and the men who cut the skates’ lines into motion.

Account of the Finding

At 04:12 on the morning of filing, the office was notified that a white label of the description above had come off its pin and been found on the third-floor corridor, half under a fan heater and half over a schedule that no one had re-pinned since the winter recording week. The label had traveled with the draft: a small pencil note in the corner of the schedule read return the name to the lab, and beneath it a fragment of a longer sentence, in the hand that keeps the benches, which the office reproduces in full and in its damaged condition, the sentence ending where the pencil gave out:

The sound lab by the Steward (the same man, unappointed, who keeps the standing one.

Transcribed from the schedule’s corner, 26 January 2016; no further copy is known to exist.

The office notes, for the record, that a Steward is not a post of this office: no such office has been appointed, and the man in question keeps the standing one — the bench at the head of the lab, the one that has never moved — by habit only. The fragment is therefore held, as the office holds all ungrammatical finds, as a fact rather than a sentence.

The True Name, Found Inside

Open the label and a smaller card lies inside it, in a neater hand: Kubo Mitsuko (久保 美津子, Kubo Mitsuko). The office records that Mitsuko is the name the pen name carries, that she is a manga artist, a writer, and a radio personality, and that she and the missing Mitsurō are one artist in two voices — the male name on the label, the true name in the fold. Both are hereby joined in this notice and may be claimed together.

Claim Instructions

To claim the name (either voice, or both), present the claim form at the annex desk with any two of the following: a pencil sketch of a figure on ice wearing a jacket; a Tuesday radio sign-off, word for word; the co-creator’s line of a 2016 credit block; or the fan heater’s third-floor position, described. A reward of one kettle of green tea, kept at the strength of a Tuesday, is attached to the claim. Unclaimed names are transferred each December to the drawer that holds the other loose names of the ward; the last such transfer took a whole afternoon, and this office would like to spare the artist another.

Also kept in this office’s drawer: the matter of the Mayers of Irwell Bank, whose festival the court found ancient and the greenery of which one witness mistook for a name worn out loud, read into the record at In re the Mayers of Irwell Bank — Salford Hundred Court, 6 May 2009; the ledger that refuses to write off a name once it has been entered, kept on The General Ledger — Liner Notes, Sessions 1969–1974; and the larger lost article that this office refers to the school, Lost — Earth, Third Planet from the Sun, Coopers’ Lane School, Notice Nº 47.
