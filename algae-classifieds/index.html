Station No. 9 · Trabzon, Pontic coast · week 19–24 October · year 13 of the binder

The Pontic Ledger

Classifieds column · notices posted at the wharf wall · sixpence the line, seaweed by the metre, the sea by its own account

WET-POSTED
BRINE-PROOF

Entered by R. Vale, field naturalist, at the stone tiers of the Trabzon wharves, with the Pontic rising directly behind the town and the last ferry going south through the channel. The sea keeps its own reckoning, and this column transcribes it at the going rate. The measures in the Field Manual FM-13, Chapter 4 are set in metres; the measures below are set in sixpence. Both are correct. Both are transcriptions.

For Sale

Kelp, live, with holdfast — lot K-114 11s. the metre, holdfast included free

Forty metres of it, harvested this week off the Trabzon reef, the longest the wall has taken in. It is a macroalga in the proper sense, multicellular and patient, and it is sold with its holdfast still in the root, for the holdfast is the whole point: the plant anchors the way the sea anchors its own dead, and a buyer who cuts the holdfast to save a shilling buys forty metres of something that will not stay put. Mooring advice, on request, is the manual's: record the water, keep the hours, and let the land be what the land will.

Bottled bloom, Danube silt water — lot B-07 2s. 6d. the bottle, six to a crate

Six glass bottles, corked, of the surface bloom taken where the Danube's silt lays down its fresh-water skin over the salt. Phytoplankton in the pour, exactly as the river brings it: the river brings what the basin forgets, and twenty-four countries' forgetting, bottled. The bottles turn green at the window and dark in the cellar, which the seller notes is the whole trade of the sea in miniature, and which the manual's axiom would call, without comment, an imperfect fermentation. It is not a drink.

Herbs of the shore, prepared — lot H-1 price suspended

One stallholder on the lower quay sells a preparation of shore herbs, ground with the kelp's own salt, against what he will not say. A buyer came up the tiers one morning, poured a little into a glass, tasted, and left the following sentence on the counter in ink, unsigned, which the stallholder has since framed under the price board: “Now I don’t much care; for it not appear to have the herbs be as fine as silk shag.” The stallholder reads it as a verdict and holds the price accordingly. The station does not, and the column prints the line verbatim because the ink has not yet come out of the wood, and because the ledger's standing rule is that a line left on a counter at the wharf is a notice, and notices are the only record the wall makes for itself.

Wanted

Filamentous spirogyra, freshwater — reward 15s. a length

The station's river tank wants spirogyra, of the Charophyta, in lengths that will take the glass without tangling, for the tank that sits beside the salt water to keep the difference honest. The stoneworts, grasslike, are taken as well, by the bunch, from anyone who has pulled them out of a delta channel and does not know what to do with them, which is the correct state. Fresh water only; the salt water is the sea's and the sea is the claimant's, as was conceded in open court, and the column will not carry a crossing of that line.

For Hire

Divers, top water only — two wanted daily rate, plus the ferry

Two divers for the working column, the oxygenated sea from the surface to somewhere between 150 and 200 metres, to count, not to collect. Below the oxygen line the shelf is a sealed cellar, and the station does not employ anyone for it, and does not print an advertisement that claims otherwise; an ad that did would be the sea's own joke, and the sea is on the record as keeping everything. The chemocline is a few metres thick and tastes, says the manual, faintly of struck matches, if you take a sip you should not take. Do not take a sip. The shelf has swallowed more ships than storms, and it keeps them whole at 2,000 metres with their shapes still sharp, amphorae and all. There is no work in a cellar that keeps.

Lost & Found

One cohesive colony, last seen in the Bosphorus

Lost: one cohesive colony of the kind that holds together, drifting out through the strait on the morning of the 21st, heading for the Marmara and possibly already through the Dardanelles, where the current has turned against fleets and will not stop at a colony. The gulls were watching the crossing — yellow-legged, the same birds that quartered the gulf at Güllük and that have sat through every sitting of the coastal bench without ever being found out. Reward: a bottle of lot B-07, and whatever the sea returns, which is, at present, everything. The finder is asked to post the colony, not the sea.

From the Bench

Standing notice of the court — docket kept open

The Coastal Court of the Aegean, which sat at the head of the gulf on the length of the peace and on one skull found beneath the fish-market wall at Iasos, kept a parallel docket on a wreck drawn up at the Bosphorus, and that docket is still open, which the column notes the way it notes the tide: as a standing item. The full record of that case, including the finding that the bone is not the bone of the man in the chair, is kept beside the Aegean sheets (The Mediterranean v. Octavianus — Transcript Excerpt, Case No. 14 of AD 14). The stallholder of lot H-1 is among those who sell from the shadow of that wall, and his framed line is copied here by the clerk's standing rule for lines left on counters. The court has not said which sea won. The water below the theatre is, for this week, the colour of the bloom in lot B-07.

Notices

Pier readings, 3 a.m. — Dr. Halcyone

Dr. Halcyone, reader of bone and of deep water, takes readings at the pier at 3 a.m., beside the bitter-juice man, whose duty and habit it is to keep the 3 a.m. platforms company. Isotope dates for holdfasts, for bone, for anything the sea has kept and is willing to give back a number for. Appointments by notice posted at this wall. The readings are transcribed the same night, before the hand goes bad, and are held to be the court's own until the court says otherwise.

Where the column keeps itself

Readers who have followed the method upstream — the binder, the bays, the bank-watching, the marsh notes that taught the station the economy of preservation on a smaller scale — will find the rest of the station's record at the front page, and the elder chapter, with the sea's measures in their own figures, at FM-13, Chapter 4. The column posts weekly, wet or otherwise. The sea is not obliged to post at all; it keeps its own.
