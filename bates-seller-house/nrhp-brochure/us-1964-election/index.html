01 Territory of Origin

The invention has its place of origin in the United States of America, a country of some 9.8 million square kilometres (3.8 million square miles) reaching from the Atlantic to the Pacific, with its capital at Washington, D.C. On the territory's civic calendar, Tuesday falls on 3 November 1964, and that day is the filing date of the present application: the presidential election held that day, the forty-fifth such contest since 1789. The incumbent President, Lyndon B. Johnson of Texas, took office on 22 November 1963 following the assassination of John F. Kennedy, who had won the previous election, and Johnson generally continued Kennedy's policies with greater emphasis on civil rights. The election was the first in which the District of Columbia cast electoral votes — three of them, under the Twenty-Third Amendment — and the first in which all fifty states took part.

02 Problem Addressed

The known art provides elections decided by the width of a single state: 1876 turned on one electoral vote, 1888 awarded the presidency to the candidate with the fewer popular votes, and 1960 turned on a few precincts in Illinois and Texas. The problem to which the invention is directed is therefore twofold: to conduct an election whose outcome is unambiguous on the night it is held, and to tabulate it so that the name of every state and every candidate survives the count in the order the returns kept them. A further difficulty attends an incumbent who reached the Oval Office by succession: he must face the voters in his own right, less than a year after taking the oath, with a record that is partly his predecessor's and partly his own.

03 Statement of the Applicant

The applicant files two tickets. The Democratic ticket: Lyndon B. Johnson, incumbent President, and Hubert H. Humphrey, Senator from Minnesota, nominated at Atlantic City in August 1964 on the record of the Civil Rights Act and of the Kennedy years. The Republican ticket: Barry Goldwater, Senator from Arizona, and William E. Miller, Congressman from New York, nominated at San Francisco in July 1964. The election itself is held on 3 November 1964, every state voting on the same day, the returns travelling by wire and by telephone to the state capitals and to Washington, where they are added up. The names below are taken from the certified state returns.

04 The Invention, in Brief

There is provided a method of conducting a presidential election comprising: a single election day, the Tuesday after the first Monday in November; a popular-vote count in each of the fifty states and in the District of Columbia; and an electoral-college tabulation in which each jurisdiction awards its electoral votes to the candidate carrying its popular vote, the whole summed to a majority of two hundred and seventy out of five hundred and thirty-eight. The certified return records 43,127,000 popular votes and 61.1 percent for Johnson and Humphrey — the highest popular-vote percentage in American presidential election history since 1820 — against 27,175,000 and 38.5 percent for Goldwater and Miller. Johnson and Humphrey carry forty-four states and the District of Columbia, including Minnesota, the Senator's home state, and New York, the Congressman's home state; Goldwater and Miller carry six states — Arizona, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia and South Carolina. The electoral vote is 486 to 52, a landslide by any reckoning, and the names are preserved.

05 Claims

  1. 1. A method of conducting a presidential election in the United States, comprising a single election day — Tuesday, 3 November 1964 — a popular-vote count in each state and in the District of Columbia, and an electoral-college tabulation completed on the election night on which it is held.
  2. 2. The method of claim 1, wherein each state and the District of Columbia awards all of its electoral votes to the candidate receiving the largest popular vote within its borders.
  3. 3. The method of claim 1, further comprising the Democratic ticket of Lyndon B. Johnson, incumbent President, and Hubert H. Humphrey, Senator from Minnesota, and the Republican ticket of Barry Goldwater, Senator from Arizona, and William E. Miller, Congressman from New York.
  4. 4. The method of claim 3, wherein Johnson and Humphrey receive 61.1 percent of the popular vote and carry forty-four states and the District of Columbia, including Minnesota and New York.
  5. 5. The method of claim 4, wherein the electoral vote is tabulated as 486 for Johnson and Humphrey and 52 for Goldwater and Miller, out of 538, with two hundred and seventy required to elect.
  6. 6. The method of claim 1, wherein the District of Columbia casts three electoral votes under the Twenty-Third Amendment, the first time the district is so counted.
  7. 7. The method of claim 1, wherein Goldwater and Miller carry six states — Arizona, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia and South Carolina — and wherein each jurisdiction named on the return is named on every sheet, in the order the returns kept them.
