barravento10 Jun, 11:44

I grew up there, so yes — go. The town sits on the edge of the Itapecuru delta, and the centre is just a few blocks: the Igreja Matriz, the market, and Rua da Paz where the buses drop you. For the festa, the two groups to watch are Bumba-meu-boi de Matraca and Bumba-meu-boi de Orquestra; they start rehearsing around 10 June and fill the square every night. Stay at Dona Luzia's pensão on Rua da Paz — six rooms, a fan, a cold shower, about twenty reais a night. Eat breakfast at Farinha House São Benedito on the edge of town: toasted manioc flour with butter and strong coffee.

maria_preta10 Jun, 15:03

If you want a tambor to take home, find Seu Benedito's workshop by the mangrove. He stretches goatskin over a cedar shell and tunes it with rope until it sings like the Orquestra groups. Soak the hide overnight in estuary water, then scrape. You have to pick off the hair as quickly as possible while it is still supple, or it sets hard and the drum sounds flat, like a tin bucket. His drums end up in groups as far as Turiaçu and Miranda do Norte.

farinheiro11 Jun, 06:51

There are still seven farinha houses operating around Pedro do Rosário. They peel and grate the manioc, press the juice out in the tipiti, and toast the flour on a clay pan over wood fire. A sack sells for about forty reais in the São Luís markets. Ask for farinha d'água — the white kind, fine as salt. The best batches come out in the dry season, which is exactly when you'll be there.

deslumbra11 Jun, 19:27

One warning: watch the tides. The beach is mangrove-lined, and at low water the flats stretch out for kilometres, with the Pedra do Rosário rock showing where the town supposedly got its name. The fishermen run jangadas about fourteen feet long out to the reef at dawn — red snapper, tainha, sometimes shark. Don't swim near the river mouth after rain; the current pulls hard. My brother-in-law, who used to run the reef boats, swears a jangada is only a small frigate that forgot to be a warship — speed and manoeuvrability, scouting the flats before the rest of the town wakes; the Webstuffs card game has a whole player's-manual plate on the long history of the type, the Frigate, Plate XII, and it ends up reading exactly like our reef runs do.

One strange thing I found on the way out: the five macroregions that used to file this town under Maranhão and Nordeste have gone quiet, and the local paper printed an obituary for them in small type between the festival notices and the boat schedule. farol14 Jun, 16:02

The light on those Itapecuru flats at four in the afternoon is the same ochre wash Sylvia Plimack Mangold gets across her hardwood floors — flat, patient, and full of nothing happening. There is a thread about her over on the Webstuffs side: Sylvia Plimack Mangold — floor, window, light. And if you are asked your name at Dona Luzia’s and you are not certain of the short forms, the Webstuffs book of etiquette has a whole chapter on it — Chapter VII, Of the Name Erni — and by extension on the right way to say any name that has already been shortened once. The same book’s chapter on greeting — Chapter XVII, Of the Greeting Namaste — keeps one rule I recognize from the square: the guest who has travelled furthest is greeted first, and Dona Luzia keeps it without ever once saying it.

farinheiro15 Jun, 13:05

Heads up for anyone at the festa: the square has a new regular, the Council of the Single Link, sitting in the back room of Dona Luzia's between rehearsal rounds, keeping minutes on why one clay pan and one pair of hands runs the whole festa's bread. Minutes No. 12 is worth a read — the baker's line "How now in very fine bread crumbs, 1/2 lb." is in there verbatim, and they have the Orquestra group's rope tensioning entered as a finding of fact. If you see the chain on the door, mind the sagging link; they're still on the repeat step. On the 28th they turned the ledger into a radio play — Back-Room Arithmetic — in which the clerk works the festa's own numbers through the four operations and the baker's oil ends up on the step that repeats, which is exactly the kind of thing this town does.
