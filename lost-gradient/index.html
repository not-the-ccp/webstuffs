Notice 4172 — Unclaimed Property

Lost one gradient descent

Item
One (1) gradient descent, a first-order iterative optimizer for unconstrained minimization of a differentiable multivariate function.
Last seen
Basement server room, 3400 N. Willamette, Portland, following the weights of the spoken-language routing model Corvina (Willamette Basin Transit Cooperative). Last observed stepping in the opposite direction of the gradient—the direction of steepest descent—at a learning rate of roughly 0.01.
Identifying marks
Responds to "step," "learning rate," and "converge." May be stalled on a flat ridge or caught in a saddle point. Not a local search: though both iterate, this one follows the gradient. Run in reverse—stepping with the gradient—it becomes gradient ascent and maximizes instead; this desk has had enough of ascent.
Reward
One cup of coffee from the coaches' room machine at AAMI Park (it never quite heats properly), and the thanks of the night porter, Dale, who compares the server's hum to a cat in the boiler room.

If found, please return to Petra L. Vance, Data Stewardship Officer, Basement 2, Division Street, extension 114. Keep the receipt.

Found slip of paper

Folded ruled slip, blue ink, no name and no date. Turned in at the desk on the morning after the quarter-final. It reads, in full:

Lunch was chicken and rice in the eighty-ninth.

The words make no sense to us. The eighty-ninth of what—a minute, a page, a room—we cannot say. It may belong to the coaches' room at AAMI Park, or to nothing at all. The night porter, Dale, has pinned the slip beside a photocopy of the liner notes for Aromaten, the Botlek sessions, on the theory that both were logged by someone standing in a place that does not intend to be remembered — one in a server room, the other on a quay where the satellites come in to feed. Claim it at the desk, or leave it; some notices stay unclaimed because the thing they describe has already walked out on its own feet.

Unclaimed property is held thirty days. The desk also keeps the ledger for the editor's letter on machine unlearning, which describes what happens when an optimizer is made to forget: the facts remain—the brick, the bay windows, the tour—but they are no longer offered. And it keeps, beside the blue folder, an assistant coach's diary, 2023–24, in which the same coffee machine appears and the eighty-ninth minute is already a landmark.
