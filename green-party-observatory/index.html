1 · Nature of the system

The Green System is the oldest standing pressure mass on the domestic chart and, by its own standing order, the only one with a colour. It first organised itself in 1984 from a loose cluster of cells — the Society for Environmental Defence and the People & Environment Party among the seed weather — and on the present range, the party of England and Wales, it has kept its seat at the Park Street station ever since. For its first four decades the system drew little notice from the barometric press: it moved slowly, it rarely broke, and its isobars ran close and patient the way the water runs in a long, narrow creek that everyone has agreed not to dredge. The Committee of Keepers has noted, for the file, that the system's own barometer has always read a touch high in winter and a touch low in August, a quirk it blames on the kettles.

2 · The 2023–25 disturbance

The present entry exists because of a disturbance the station has not seen since the range was first drawn. With the outbreak of the Gaza war in late 2023 and the wider Middle Eastern crisis that followed, the weather around the system sharpened quickly, and with it the complaints — incidents, allegations and debate of antisemitism regarding members of the Green Party of England and Wales drew increased attention by the media during the 2020s, reaching their peak pressure in the autumn of 2023, when the station's log ran two pages a day for eleven days. The party's officials, questioned at the Park Street door, returned the same reading every time: antisemitism has no place in Green politics. That line has since been kept word for word in this almanac, in the margin of Entry 14, where the Keepers hold that a party's barometer is only as good as its worst forecast.

Action followed the statement. Through 2024 the system issued antisemitism training initiatives to its local federations, from the Cornwall Cell in the southwest to the Leeds & Bradford front in the north, with a two-hour course, a register of attendance, and a standing item at the annual conference at the NEC, Birmingham, in February. Complaints received through the party's own channels are now logged at the Park Street station in the same book that logs the barometer, which the Keepers consider a fair arrangement: both are instruments, and both must be read honestly or not at all.

4 · Companion entries

Readers who wish to follow a long, slow water the way this system has followed its own weather should turn to the liner notes of The Creek, Northbound (Meadows & Corona Records, MCR-114), where a septet recorded three October nights along the Flushing River and the last track insists, at eight and a half minutes, that the water was here first and is not finished going. The Keepers have filed that record beside this entry on the theory that a creek that will not stop and a party that has not stopped are read at the same station. As for the world the system sits on — blue-green, round, exactly where the receivers say it is, and missing only from the shelf — see Notice Nº 47 at Coopers’ Lane School, where the office has entered the planet under OCEAN and otherwise said nothing, which is the school's way of agreeing with the chart. Both entries return, at the end of the day, to the front page of the almanac, where the committee keeps the master map and the kettle is, for once, off.

5 · Addendum — air-quality advisory (standard plate)

Printed at every station on this chart, under the heading STORM & AIR, and kept here word for word: "During wildfire smoke events, remain indoors with filtration; obtain medical attention for persistent cough, shortness of breath, or chest pain."

The Committee of Keepers reminds readers that a party, like a weather system, is measured by how it treats the air inside it. The green mass on this chart has been asked, lately, to clear its own. It is doing so, one training register at a time, at 46 Park Street, where the pressure is, at the hour of writing, slowly rising.
