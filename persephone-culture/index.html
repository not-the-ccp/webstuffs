Persephone in Popular Culture

Eight questions about the daughter of Zeus and Demeter, answered from the surface down: the films, the novels, the name Proserpina, the pomegranate, and the trademark files. The page descends as you read; so, in the myth, does she.

1 · Who is Persephone, precisely?

Persephone is the daughter of Zeus and Demeter, the goddess of the harvest. She was carried off by Hades, the god of the underworld, and became its queen; having eaten pomegranate seeds while below, she is bound to spend part of every year with him and part with her mother. That rhythm is why she carries a dual function — chthonic and vegetation goddess at once — as the personification of vegetation, which shoots forth in Spring and withdraws into the earth after harvest. Her Roman equivalent is Proserpina, and a great deal of what the modern world borrows arrives under that older, harder spelling.

2 · Why does the story keep working?

Six months above, six months below, a return that is never quite a rescue. That structure maps onto exile and homecoming, on marriage understood as a kind of burial, on any season — or any person — that comes back wearing different clothes. She can be used as a character, which is most of the work, or purely as a symbol, which is the rest. Even the railway companies have taken to her arithmetic: the Willamette Valley Line prints its northward service on the same six months up, six months down, and keeps one night train that works only on the nights it does — see the NW-4 timetable and commentary, where the margin line above it is preserved word for word. The name itself is short, unambiguous in most languages, and legible in a credits sequence, which no amount of symbolism can replace.

3 · Which films have carried her name or her story?

More than the register below can hold, but the standing list begins with Proserpina (1969), Kenneth Tynan's film in which a girl's descent is staged as the myth in the round, and ends, for the moment, with The Pomegranate (2021), the eight-part series that moved the whole story into a shipping office in Piraeus. Between them: H Persefoni (2001, Thessaloniki, 18 minutes), a short about a florist whose daughter stops answering the shop phone in February and starts answering it in April; Spring-Back (2013, dir. Elena Markou, 104 minutes, shot on Crete), about a sister who comes home in September and is not recognized at the door of the bakery; and What the Earth Gives Back (2019), a one-hour documentary made in the orchards of Euboea that spends its final fourteen minutes on the seed. The name also rides out of the theatre entirely on asteroid (458) Proserpina, discovered in 1900, which the film credits of that decade sometimes thanked for good light.

4 · What about the literature?

Enough to fill a shelf in any city with a spring. Spring-Back began life as a novella in 2008 before the film; the novel that is usually cited first is C. Farrow's Six Months (1989, 214 pages), in which a woman keeps two sets of keys and a house in each; and the standing poetry entry is What the Earth Gives Back, the 2016 collection from the Saffron Press in Nicosia (96 pages, 40 poems, all of them in even months). The 2022 novella The Underworld Season, published in Athens by the house of N. Kalogeropoulou, retells the whole myth in the present tense from the point of view of the soil, and it is the book most often handed to visitors at the depot archives with the sentence, read it slowly, it is the earth speaking.

5 · Is she used more as a character or as a symbol?

Both, and the split is old. In the theatre and on screen she is a character first: a queen, a daughter, a woman with keys. In trade and design she is a symbol second: the mark of a seasonal return, a house name, a line of perfume. The Athens band Kermes closed their 2019 record Anthos with a seven-minute track called “Six Months” that names her nowhere and uses only the number, and it has been played at harvests in at least four prefectures since. The name does the character's work even when the character is not on stage. Each of the three — the mark, the house name, the line of perfume — has somewhere it keeps itself: the Jarrow–Manchester Correspondents, a small club that signs its door Mars and its register Magrs, carry all three in a single line of their own annual, the Mars Post, Newsletter No. 17, which issues on the twelfth of November and holds the season exactly as found. The same engine of descent and return, with a price tag, drives the quarter's own dark season: John Steakley's Vampire$ (1990) and John Carpenter's Vampires (1998), the film it became, whose release-date poster still runs in the Cinema Palace case and whose whole route — eight stops, 2.1 miles, the record shop on the corner included — is kept at The Steakley Walk.

6 · Why does everyone borrow the pomegranate?

Because it is the one concrete detail in an otherwise large and shadowy story: seeds, a number, a bargain. In the older versions she eats six seeds and is bound for six months; in the later ones three seeds and three months. Designers keep the fruit and drop the arithmetic, which is why it appears on the labels of the fashion house Maison Proserpina in Lyon, on the stamp of the spring catalogue of the Athenian paperer at 14 Agiou Konstantinou, and on the crest of a rowing club in Corfu whose season, like the goddess's, runs on a fixed return whether the boats like it or not.

7 · What do the trademark files say?

That no one owns the name, and that the files keep their oddities exactly. The myth is older than any claim, and “Persephone” has been registered, opposed, and abandoned at least three times in the last twenty years, each time without the myth itself moving. The entry that the register still prints for the 2019 application of Persephone Labs of Athens, Class 9, is the examiner's line, kept exactly as filed: “The company is obliged to cite, for it since.” The handwriting is the examiner's; the meaning was never recovered, and the opposition proceeded on other grounds and was allowed. Readers of the Transmission error files will recognize the register's treatment of an unrecoverable minute — one sentence preserved whole, meaning not restored — and the same principle runs the Redlands bulletin board, thread No. 41-M, which keeps its pencilled margin line with the identical patience. The principle reaches its furthest extent one season past the last planet: the Flagstaff station logbook, whose 1915 carbon line — the crow, the voided plate, the unspent hours — is preserved whole, meaning likewise not restored, in the field notes beyond Neptune.

8 · Where should a reader begin?

With the myth as a rhythm rather than a plot, and then with the smallest borrowed detail — the seed, the number of months, the door that is not recognized — and let the name do the rest. If the register above reads short, the archive is open on Thursdays, the orchards of Euboea are open always, and the front page of this miscellany keeps the rest of the field notes. And for readers who would like one long, fixed object to walk round while the season turns, the 1905 pre-dreadnought at the bottom of Gotenhafen Bay runs on a fixed return of its own — three crossings a day, whether the hull likes it or not — charted in the SMS Schleswig-Holstein harbour brochure.
