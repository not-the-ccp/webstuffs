61. Offered is the surviving paper record of the Danish state's 1951 experiment known as eksperimentet — the re-education of twenty-two Greenlandic Inuit children, the so-called eksperimentbørn, who were sent from Godthåb to Denmark to be raised as "little Danes," Danish in tongue and manner, and who never went home again as children might have hoped.

The children were presented as orphans; most were not. Twenty-two left Greenland in 1951, ranging in age from six to nine. They were received at a Red Cross children's home near Copenhagen and then placed with Danish foster families in Jutland and on Zealand. Six were adopted while in Denmark. Sixteen returned to Greenland in 1952, only to be placed in Danish-speaking institutions in Godthåb, and none lived with their families again. Half died in young adulthood; the survivors carried mental health disturbances for life. The government of Denmark officially apologised in 2020.

The lot comprises eight items, housed in the original navy-blue folio. First, the bound admissions register of the children's home, autumn 1951 to spring 1952, in a single clerk's hand, listing all twenty-two names with dates of arrival, home parishes, and foster assignments. Second, fourteen foster-placement agreements, signed by the families and by the home's superintendent, six of them marked in red ink adoption. Third, fourteen letters written by the children in Danish during 1951–52 — most never posted — with the office's translations filed beside them. Fourth, the return papers of July 1952: embarkation at Copenhagen, the passage north, and the placement orders that sent the sixteen to the Danish-speaking orphanage in Godthåb rather than to their own settlements. Fifth, the school register of the Danish-language school, 1952–1956, every lesson in Danish, with the Greenlandic columns left blank. Sixth, a sheaf of photographs: the children disembarking at Copenhagen in October 1951, the foster-class portrait of 1952, the six adopted children posed in a garden in 1955, and the sixteen stepping ashore at Godthåb that same summer with small Danish flags in their hands. Seventh, a bundle of testimony notes gathered in the 1990s and 2000s, and the copy of the 2020 apology as read in the Folketing, with the press clippings of that week folded inside.

And, bound in with the folio by a hand no one at the auction house can now identify, one stray leaf from the same week's sale — a natural-history lot offered two rooms away — whose heading stands unexplained on the page: Description of item One (1) Amazon River, whole, in approximately 6,400 kilometres of channel, rising in the same week. The river and the children have nothing to do with each other, except that both were described in the same days, and both turn up here, whole, measured in kilometres and in lives.

Condition. The register shows foxing to the first leaves and one repaired gutter; sound. The foster agreements are complete, with all seals intact. The photographs are silver gelatin prints, some faded at the edges, none mounted. The folio is navy buckram, lettered in gold Grønland 1951, corners rubbed, one corner softened by damp. The Amazon leaf is a single sheet, cream paper, letterpress, creased once down the centre; offered as found, not to be removed.

Terms. The lot may not be divided. A buyer's premium of twenty percent applies. Payment at the fall of the gavel, in Danish kroner; the folio will be wrapped on the spot, and export licences, where required, are the buyer's responsibility.
