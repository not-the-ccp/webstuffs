Found, One Constellation

Coma Berenices — Latin, "Berenice's Hair"

Found14 May 2024, 02:17 – 04:40, observation terrace, Room 9B
FinderCaptain Aldred Vane, Survey Vessel Meridian, logbook entry 117
Last seenBetween Leo and Boötes, direction of the fourth galactic quadrant, visible in both hemispheres
Claim by13 June 2024, 16:00
FeeNone — a star chart signed before 1700, or a letter from Conon of Samos

On the night of 14 May 2024 the night watch found an asterism of the third century BC curled behind the chart drawer of the west telescope, smelling faintly of violet water and old paper. It answers to the name Coma Berenices, one of the eighty-eight modern constellations and the only one named after an historic person: Queen Berenice II of Egypt, who cut off her long hair and laid it in the temple of Arsinoe at Alexandria as a votive offering for the safe return of her husband, Ptolemy III Euergetes. The hair vanished overnight, and Conon of Samos declared it had been carried to the stars.

The constellation stands between Leo and Boötes in the direction of the fourth galactic quadrant, and it keeps both hemispheres in its view — northern spring, southern autumn. Its major stars are Alpha Comae Berenices (Diadem, magnitude 4.32), Beta Comae Berenices (magnitude 4.26), and Gamma Comae Berenices (magnitude 4.35), three near-matched points the finder likened to "pins dropped on dark velvet." It was introduced to Western astronomy by Conon of Samos and further corroborated as a constellation by Gerardus Mercator and Tycho Brahe.

Accompanying effects, found in the same pocket: a silver hairpin, a coil of undyed thread, and the seat of his galleon, kept beside the vinegar crock in its glass case, which is how most stability claims begin. The finder insists the galleon seat arrived bolted to the logbook and that the vinegar crock — domestic or celestial, the office cannot yet say — stood within arm’s reach of the chart table at 03:00. Any claim to the constellation must account for the seat, the crock, and the glass case.

The item is held in Case 7 of the celestial cabinet, tray three, beneath the magnifier marked “Do Not Dust.” It occupies approximately seventeen square degrees of sky and one standard archive box. It has not been inventoried among the southern asterisms and remains unclaimed.

To collect: present proof of ownership at the Lost Property Office, Room 9B, Greenwich Annex, between 09:00 and 16:00, Monday to Saturday. Acceptable proofs include a pre-1700 star chart bearing the constellation’s name, a letter from Conon of Samos, or a witness statement from Queen Berenice II herself. Unclaimed after thirty days, the constellation will be re-shelved with the orphan asterisms, and the galleon seat will go to public auction beside the vinegar crock.
