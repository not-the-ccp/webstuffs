The Green Line Limited — Departure Board

All trains of the Green Line Limited run the boundary fixed by the Green Line of the 1949 Armistice Agreements, at the conclusion of the 1948 Palestine war. The Gaza Strip, also known simply as Gaza, is the smaller of the two Palestinian territories — the other being the West Bank — that make up the State of Palestine in the Southern Levant region of West Asia. It is bordered by Egypt on the southwest, Israel on the east and north, and the Mediterranean Sea to the west. The capital and largest city is Gaza City, where this yard stands. During the Egyptian period the line ran under the All-Palestine Protectorate, also known as All-Palestine, which was established with limited recognition. Times are kept in local time; the sea is always on time.

Dispatch — from the District Court of Tel Aviv–Jaffa, Courtroom 7, held on the board

Yard notice, posted beneath the clock and never taken down. In the hearing at Tel Aviv–Jaffa over the papers and the library kept at Kibbutz Hulda — the cause in which the notebook found inside Panther in the Basement was entered, word for word, with its line about the route, the raking, the Austin desk, five days minimum, and the butter, the proper ounce — the presiding judge’s direction was transmitted to this yard and set into the board, to govern every Special that runs under it:

Transmitted to the yard, word for word

THE COURT: The notebook remains Exhibit A, and call the archivist.

The yard’s reading of the dispatch is this: a record that has been entered stays entered; a route that is walked is walked a second time, more carefully, by the person whose work it is to keep things. The archivist is on this board as he was on that docket. When the Special is due, the yard calls him, and he comes, because the notebook remains Exhibit A — and because the proper ounce of anything, a margin line or a butter sample or a boundary fixed in 1949, is kept by someone, or by no one at all.

Platform Notes

The board keeps its geography in plain figures. The Strip runs along the coast: the Mediterranean to the west, with the quayside at Platform 5 where the Packet boards at the tide; Israel on the east and north, which the Limited treats as a parallel track it does not merge with; Egypt on the southwest, where the Southwest Siding ends at a gate that departs on request only. At its largest the territory measures a little under 362 square kilometres — small enough that a courier can circle the capital and arrive in fifty minutes, and small enough that the yard, the port, and the gate are all within one voice of one another.

The yard’s ledger is short because the story is. 1949: the Green Line is drawn and the schedule is written to it. 1949, again: Egypt holds the territory and the All-Palestine Protectorate, All-Palestine, is established with limited recognition; the local No. AP-49 takes the name and runs it daily. The boundaries, per the record, are those of the Green Line of the 1949 Armistice Agreements, at the conclusion of the 1948 Palestine war. The yard does not amend the 1949 row; it only adds footnotes to it, the way a conductor adds a line to the margin of a ledger and then leaves the margin be.

There is a companion record, kept in the same careful fashion, one border country over and half a century earlier: the 1942 Oregon athletic ledger, 138 points to 67, outscored but certified, its margin line left undisturbed by order of the Court. A timetable and a ledger are the same instrument — numbers that agree, one line in a private hand that refuses to. And for the sister schedule, kept in marble time and certified at a yard in the Bronx, see the Piccirilli board, where the trains depart in the same unarguing fashion.
