Description of the Lot

Offered in one crate, stencilled in two colours and packed to survive a drop: one complete Blitzkrieg, the field edition. The lot is not a machine but a method, and the house has had to make a machine's inventory of it. Blitzkrieg is the German term used to describe a combined arms surprise attack, using a rapid, overwhelming force concentration that may consist of armoured and motorized or mechanized infantry formations, together with artillery, air assault, and close air support. The intent is to break through an opponent's lines of defense, dislocate the defenders, confuse the enemy by making it difficult to respond to the continuously changing front, and defeat them in a decisive Vernichtungsschlacht: a battle of annihilation. What the crate holds is the working of that intent, paper by paper, from the interwar period in which aircraft and tank technologies matured and were combined with the systematic application of the traditional German tactic of the envelopment — the Kessel, the pot, which the lot demonstrates closed, more than once.

The Contents

(a) One field map, Ardennes sector, 1940, with the pencil route from Sedan to the Meuse in a hand the house will not name. The route was worked in thirty hours on 12 June, and the map carries the pencil correction river is not in the key in three places. (b) One fuel register, Rees depot, January 1945, in the hand of Cpl. R. A. Farrow of the RASC: 2,140 gallons of petrol and 38,000 rounds of 25-pounder drawn by one division, every field gun with its full motor convoy. The same clerk left stores line 912 in the Arnhem ledger — a clean breast of 912 fillet of real, boiled bacon-cheek, peas, potatoes — and the house has ruled, after long argument, that the line is part of the lot and goes to the hammer as sold. (c) One 25-pounder barrel, 87.6 mm, field gun-howitzer, the regiment's voice, complete with sight, quadrant, and a brass telescope that still points at the wall of the Ordnance Room, which the house has stopped moving it from. (d) One elm cutting, Constan˜ta, from the village that moves its elm a hundred yards east every seventh year; the cutting is in its fourth year and has not, yet, moved, which is as it should be for a thing sold in a crate.

Provenance, Briefly

The method in the crate was worked, in the form that gave it its name, in the twenty-six days from Liège to the sea in the spring of 1940: the thrust through the Ardennes, the Meuse crossed at Sedan before the defenders had finished being surprised, the point driven to the coast, and the pot closed behind the army it had passed. Some hundred thousand of them found out what a changing front does to an answer at Abbeville, and the air that did the confusing worked the causeways with a patience the lot's Stuka whistle can still demonstrate, on request, at ten feet and downward. The lightning has not struck since, in the house's experience, and the house will not warrant that it will.

Condition Report

The lot is complete as crated and is offered with the papers it carried, which is the whole point of the business: a Vernichtungsschlacht is either decided or it is only weather, and the papers decide it. Pasted into the lot file, between the lines for “petrol” and “powder”, is one clip from the encyclopedia article on the arm that kept the lines open for the crate, heading and first sentence cut together as a single line and kept word for word, unamended:

The field gun When the armies left the siege lines for open country, artillery became lighter, more mobile.

The clip is the article's own reckoning of the difference between a siege train that moves at the pace of its oxen and a battery that moves at the pace of the argument. The house considers it the lot's best document and prices it in the estimate rather than outside it. Farrow's elm, for its part, is vigorous, and the village rule about every seventh year is understood to run from the sale, not the cutting.

Where the Lot Sits

The article the clip was cut from stands in the binder beside this sale and is the lot's elder piece: Artillery — Encyclopedia Article, from the siege bombards that waited at Constantinople to the self-propelled regiments that no longer wait at all. Farrow's road out of the gun positions ran east through Constan˜ta to the water that kept him three months at a convalescent house on the road thither, and that water is measured in the same binder, chapter by chapter, at The Black Sea — Field Manual FM-13, Chapter 4; the anoxic shelf below 200 metres is the only place on the lot's route the house will not ship the elm to, and the house will not say why beyond “the water will”. Collectors of the house's earlier sales will note that Lot 41 of Catalogue No. 37 — one deep-level ‘tube’ railway, entire, thirteen stations, a two-pence fare — went to the same anonymous purchaser as Lot 63: Lot 41 — The Central London Railway — Catalogue No. 37. The house records the parallel without comment, on the ground that two ledgers in one hand is a doctrine, and the house is not in the business of doctrines, only in the business of lots. For the whole of the house's earlier work and its returns, the index stands at Webstuffs.

The House Note

The lightning is sold as lightning: rapid, overwhelming, and gone before the buyer can say the number. The hammer falls at the pace of the argument.
