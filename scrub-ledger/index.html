Found

Found — Double Helix, Freshly Spun

Found below the ford at Vacy on the morning of 14 October, in the lomandra clump within two metres of the Paterson’s edge: one double helix, freshly spun, composed of two polynucleotide strands coiled around one another, carrying the genetic instructions for the development, functioning, growth and reproduction of one red-backed fairywren in full breeding dress — black head, back the colour of burnt scarlet, eleven centimetres, about eight grams. Deoxyribonucleic acid, in good order, answering to Malurus melanocephalus melanocephalus. To its owner on production of a thermos and a clear description. (cl. 418)

Found — Polynucleotides, a Pair

Two strands, each longer than an 11-cm bird, of the simpler monomeric units called nucleotides. Sold singly they will unravel and be missed; offered coiled, as found. The strands are known as polynucleotides, which we think is the truest thing anyone has called anything this side of the river. (312/2)

Lost

Lost — One Schoolbook Caption, 1923

Misbound between a wren plate and the index of a school-prize volume in 1923 cloth, Maitland origin: “Tucked between the head and the majority of the children, amen.” Last seen at the library history hour, where no one could place it. The library declines to explain it; this page declines to explain it, and will keep it filed beside the wren plate. Reward: one red pen, Halls Creek issue. (h. w.)

For Sale — Four Nitrogenous Nucleobases, in Pairs

The set that makes a nucleotide of itself: one of each of the four nitrogen-containing nucleobases, supplied in the pairs the strand keeps — adenine with thymine, guanine with cytosine, no substitutions, no loose thymine. Offered by a reader who has studied the plate. Cash at the bridge, or two grams of seed by weight. (cl. 418)

For Sale — Genetic Instructions, All Known Organisms & Many Viruses

Complete set: development, functioning, growth and reproduction, packaged in the double helix and held in every known form of life and many viruses. Readable by any party that knows the four letters. Full complement included at no extra charge; the viruses are what they are. (312/5)

To Let

To Let — Nest, Domed, Low

Domed nest in grass and spiderweb, low and hard to see, holds three eggs, pale pink with freckles, about fourteen days’ incubation, let within five metres of water — all twenty-two nest cards in our reserve since 2016 agree on the water. Brown goshawk not included; the arithmetic of this business is the tenant’s own. No nest above waist height, and no exceptions. (s. k.)

Wanted

Wanted — Male, Cruentatus, Kimberley Run

Black from bill to tail with a red back like a coal pulled from the fire, brown wings. Last seen working the mangrove edges near Cable Beach, October. Parties of four to eight around the damp patches after the wet. Whether he carries more crimson because he is cruentatus or only because of the dawn light, we will not swear to, but we would like to see him anyway. Information to the keeper of the Year 8 geography report, Halls Creek. (d. r.)

Wanted — A Cat, or Its Explanation

Boyne Valley, near Gladstone. One third egg, one lignum clump, three feet off the ground. The cat is known to the neighbourhood and to the ledger. We would like a word, or a word to the contrary, before next season. (b. o.)

Notices

Notice — Four Major Types of Macromolecule

Nucleic acids, alongside proteins, lipids and complex carbohydrates (polysaccharides), are one of the four major types of macromolecules that are essential for all known forms of life. Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and ribonucleic acid (RNA) are nucleic acids, and they are announced here for the record. This notice is published without charge; it is the kind of thing that has to be said. (The Ledger, on behalf of the four)

Notice — The Delta’s Line, Kept Word for Word

A correspondent on the edge of the Itapecuru delta, Maranhão, writes of the three branches of the federal republic and says that judicial power sits in the courts, independent, beginning at two o’clock sharp in Brasília, four hours ahead of the delta. A page of the same correspondence has come through misbound into a recipe column, and the line is kept on this page, word for word, exactly as it stands:

Judicial power sits in the flour and lumps and crumbs, and pour over the fowl, 8d.

The club has voted 4–3 to keep it unexplained, in the same manner as the surveyor’s page. We print it anyway, and note for the record that 8d is the price of the fowl. Filed beside the Federative Letter, No. 14. (The Delta Ledger, to The Scrub Ledger)

Personals

B. O. to C. G.

Twelve at a trough is a party and a half. A nest in the lignum three feet off the ground is a negotiation. The arithmetic holds; the third egg was the arithmetic. Reply through the Board, No. 418. (b. o.)

D. R. to C. G.

Coal from the fire — I have it, and it is exactly your bird. The northern bounds are Indian Ocean north and west, and the wrens hold the creeks through all of it. I will not swear to the crimson; I stand by the image. (d. r.)

Dead Notice

Third Egg, Lignum Clump, Boyne Valley

Gone to a cat, January of last season, from a laying of three. May the two that fledged on 12 January hold their brown through the dry, and may the clump be planted with more lignum than with grief. (b. o.)
