PRAIRIE BEND STATION · DEPARTMENT OF FOUND PROPERTY · BEND RAILWAY, DIVISION OF THE NIGHT

LOST & & FOUND

Notice No. 9 · Filed 14 October 1934 · Clerk of the Office: J. M. Cowper · Vial register number 118

FOUND

I. The Item

Found on the evening of 13 October 1934, at 02:40, on seat 9 of van 4 of the 21:04 Nightly Bend: one (1) small glass vial, four centimetres across and eleven centimetres tall, with a cork stopper and a film of fine, clear residue inside the neck. The contents are clear, colorless, odorless, viscous, and sticky; they cling to the glass in a thin, slow sheet, and one drop, placed by the clerk on the intake card, had not yet stopped running when the card was filed. No label. No weight recorded. The vial was dry on the outside except at the mouth, which held a single bead the size of a pinhead.

II. Note of the Station Chemist, E. V. Lumen

"Submitted for description, not for sale. The fluid is similar in appearance to a certain thick cream, but the two are not the same thing, and the difference is in the chemistry, not the colour. Count of live matter: low to absent, and varying. It is issued, on its owner's behalf, as a natural lubricant and an acid neutralizer, and it does both offices quietly. I have tasted nothing. I recommend the office should not either. Filed under miscellaneous secretions, male, benign."

III. Circumstances of Finding

The Nightly Bend is the Division of the Night's one train, which leaves Prairie Bend at 21:04, keeps its schedule against the weather, the prairie, and any request for a stop, and arrives nowhere the timetable will print. It has not once been seen to hand a passenger in or out: the doors are opened from the other side, or not at all. On the morning of 13 October the guard, clearing van 4 for the daylight line, found the vial on seat 9, a man's coat folded over the armrest, and a small black notebook open beside them. The coat was claimed within the hour, by a gentleman the guard describes only as "the regular," who gave no name, took his coat, declined to take his vial, and walked out onto the platform into the rain. The notebook was not claimed. It is kept in the office with the vial, in the same drawer, under the same key.

IV. The Margin Line, Kept Word for Word

The last page of the notebook held one line, in a hand that leaned to the left, as if the writer were watching something at the window:

"He was the king of the train that never had to hand."

The clerk's note reads: Who "he" is, the notebook does not say. The train, the clerk adds, does not hand, and therefore has no hand to hand with, and the line is filed as written, not as corrected. The vial, the clerk adds further, was found in the coat pocket nearest the train, which the clerk takes as its only confession.

V. Conditions of Claim

The vial will be returned, corked and unopened, to the person who satisfies the office on all three points: first, that they can name the fluid's two offices — the lubricant, and the neutralizer of the acid — without naming the fluid itself; second, that they can produce a stub for the 21:04, any year, legible or not; third, that they can say, in one sentence, what the man on the platform was on his way to before the rain took him. Claims are received at the office window, between 02:00 and 04:00, when the office is properly awake. A claim fee of one (1) drop, matching, is required and will be examined by the chemist under the green lamp. Should the vial go unclaimed within six months — i.e. by 14 April 1935 — it will be corked a second time, registered against vial number 118, and shelved in the depot archive among the other clear things that came in on the Nightly Bend and asked for nothing.

VI. Cross-Filed by the Office

Notices of this kind, in which the item found is smaller than its description, are not new to the office. The notice of the same class kept on the Pembrokeshire side — Lost & Found Notice No. 47, Tufton Parish, for a Welsh hymn tune last seen near Blaenwern Farm and first registered in 1915 — shows the correct method: a thing that cannot be pinned down is described, registered, and left to answer to its name. Readers wanting the register itself, and the rest of the archive, will find it at the front page, where the vial's number, 118, is entered under clear, unclaimed, and keeping.
