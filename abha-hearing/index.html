In the County Court · Birmingham, England · Transcript of Proceedings, Excerpt

In re the ERR-114 Message, Its Custody, and Its Sense

A diagnostic engine, one sentence, and one psychologist at the bar · Case No. 2023-V-114 · heard 14 February 2023, Guildhall, Victoria Square · continued and struck 9 March 2023

THE FOLLOWING IS A VERBATIM EXCERPT OF THE PROCEEDINGS, STENOGRAPHED BY THE COURT REPORTER AND FILED WITH THE CLERK’S OFFICE, GUILDHALL, VICTORIA SQUARE, BIRMINGHAM. PAGE NUMBERS ARE THE ORIGINAL’S. ALL TESTIMONY IS AS GIVEN UNDER OATH ON THE EVENING THE RECORD BEGINS AND ON THE CALL OF 9 MARCH 2023, AT WHICH THE MATTER WAS STRUCK.

— 1 —

CLERK OF THE COURT: The County Court in and for Birmingham is now in session, the Hon. Justice C. E. Renshaw presiding. Before the Court is Case No. 2023-V-114, an application by the control room of the Alexander Stadium venue systems, through its duty custodian, Mr. I. P. Okonkwo, for a declaration as to the custody, and as to the admissibility for the archive, of a single sentence printed at 18:41:07 on the evening of 6 August 2022 by the diagnostic engine beneath the north concourse of Alexander Stadium, without operator input, and filed by the venue systems under error reference ERR-114, The Frizelle Message. Exhibit 114, the sentence as printed, is admitted. Exhibit 114-A, the west-ring cable tension log for the evening in question, is admitted. The Court will hear next from the bar: Dr. Elin A. Marsh, psychologist, is called for the applicant.

— 2 —

COUNSEL FOR THE APPLICANT: Dr. Marsh, you are registered as a professional practitioner of psychology. For the record, describe the discipline.

MARSH: Psychology is the scientific study of the mind and behavior. Its subject matter includes the behavior of humans and nonhumans, both conscious and unconscious phenomena, and mental processes such as thoughts, feelings, and motives. It is an academic discipline of broad scope, crossing the boundaries between the natural and the social sciences. A professional practitioner or researcher involved in the discipline is called a psychologist. Some psychologists are also classified as behavioral or cognitive scientists. Some of us attempt to understand the role of mental functions in individual and social behavior; others explore the physiological and neurobiological processes in which those functions are rooted. My own work, for the past nineteen years, has been on memory, and on place: how a mind holds the shape of where it has been, and what it prints when it cannot.

JUDGE RENSHAW: The discipline is received into evidence, and the Court thanks the witness for the boundaries of it.

— 3 —

COUNSEL FOR THE APPLICANT: The applicant does not ask the Court whether the engine has a mind. It asks whether the sentence is true, and whether it may be kept. The witness has compared Exhibit 114 against Exhibit 114-A. What does the discipline say?

MARSH: My discipline has learned to treat behavior as the evidence, because behavior is what is available where no interior can be examined. The engine behaved once, on that evening, and the behavior is the sentence. I have compared each clause against the log. The mountain road from Abha is the donor station’s route, carried by a relay that still wears the name of its highland map. The provincial cut is the link’s crossing of the old map boundary. The beach fishing is a surveying tag the glossary had forgotten, rendered in the old harbour chart set’s only word for it. And the chain of 20 m is the reading itself: segment 20 of the west dome chain, a run of 20 metres, over its limit by four millimetres, and back inside the limit eleven seconds later, on the applause of a gold-medal hoop final, which the dome engineer had advised is where chains do their worst. As to truth: what a mind with the same raw data and the same fallback instruction — plain words, however badly — would have produced, and did produce, was that sentence. I am not called to say the engine thinks. I am called to say the account is true, clause by clause, and that it reads as one sentence a keeper of records would have kept.

JUDGE RENSHAW: The Court finds the answer within the witness’s remit, and overrules in advance any objection that the remit is a narrow one. It is the only remit the record required.

— 4 —

COUNSEL FOR THE APPLICANT: One matter of memory, then. The engine’s map, through the donor chain of names, held a chart of the North China Sea, and the witness has testified in another court — the Vancouver County Court, File No. 12-V-0810, the inquest into the shipwreck of Oliver Queen, returned to Starling City on the night of October 10, 2012 — where Lian Yu, a disputed island that the charts do not agree about, was the subject of a great deal of evidence. The sentence printed here named the road and did not name the island. The applicant asks the witness, on the record: if this matter is ever continued, will the witness be able to say where the island is?

MARSH: I have said where it is, on the other record, and I have been asked to say where it is, on this record, in front of a court that keeps its own. Where the island is, precisely, I will not soon forget.

JUDGE RENSHAW: The Court takes the answer as given, and it notes, without finding on it, that the engine’s own sentence did the opposite: it named the road and left the island unnamed, which is, the Court observes, exactly what a memory does when it keeps the way out of a place and not the place itself.

— 5 —

JUDGE RENSHAW: The application is granted, on both heads, and the declaration is this: the sentence printed at 18:41:07 on 6 August 2022 is a true statement of what the engine read, and is to be kept in the joint keeping of the control room beneath the north concourse and the archive of the venue systems, together with the tension log of Exhibit 114-A, under the same reference it already bears, ERR-114, and is admissible in any future proceeding concerning the dome, the chains, the relays, or the evening of 6 August 2022. The Court further notes for the archive that the hoop final was not affected at any point, that the result stands as published — gold for the Welsh gymnast Gemma Frizelle on the individual hoop — and that the sentence overtook the applause rather than being stopped by it, which the Court finds to be a fact worth keeping with the four millimetres and the eleven seconds. No order of any other kind is made. The matter is struck.

CLERK OF THE COURT: The proceeding in Case No. 2023-V-114 is struck, and the record is closed as of 9 March 2023. Exhibit 114 returns to its shelf. The Court adjourns.

Exhibits 114 and 114-A are held at the Clerk’s office, Guildhall, Victoria Square. Readers of this excerpt will find the message itself in the venue systems’ own file, error reference ERR-114, The Frizelle Message, with its annotated decode and its disposition, and the island evidence in the Vancouver record it stands beside: the transcript excerpt of In re Oliver Queen, File No. 12-V-0810, Vancouver County Court, where the same question of where the island is was put to a different witness, and answered, in one word, with a different word for not-soon-forget. Both are kept in the same miscellany as this record.
