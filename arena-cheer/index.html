Hobbyist’s build log · one bench, one benchlight, no witnesses

Squadwork

The Los Angeles Memorial Sports Arena cheer squad’s exhibition routine of August 1984, remounted at 1:24 in balsa, wire, and cotton poms — ninety seconds of floor, one three-high stunt, and a final pose.

Why this build

My sister Marta was on the arena squad in 1984, pom position 4, row C, and she has the photographs to prove it. The squad gave its exhibition in the arena’s main bowl the same week the weightlifting hall was running, and Marta swears the whole floor counted down — five, six, seven, eight — and then somebody in the crowd below started screaming, which was the 196.0 kg snatch of athlete KOR-090 from the bar at position 2, row B. The squad kept the routine going. The desk, as ever, did not confirm. I have wanted to build the moment the crowd turned for forty-two years. This is remount No. 4; the first three were a weightlifter, a barbell that is now catalogued as Item 107.3, the 1984 competition bar, and a pier at Santa Monica that I will not discuss.

The homework, set for myself and done properly: cheerleading is an activity in which the participants, cheerleaders, cheer for their team as a form of encouragement, ranging from chanting slogans to intense physical activity, performed to motivate sports teams, to entertain an audience, or for competition. Routines typically run one to three minutes and hold tumbling, dance, jumps, cheers, and stunting. The activity originated at Princeton University in 1884, where it has become a tradition, and it is less prevalent in the rest of the world except via its association with American sports or organized contests. I wrote all of that in the margin of page 1 of the log before I cut a single piece of balsa, because a build without homework is just wood.

The log

Sat 3 October 2026

Committed to the plan. Nineteen figures on a 2.4 m mat, 1:24, so the squad reads as a squad and not as a single tall figureine. Routine timed at 90 seconds, inside the one-to-three-minute band, with the crowd-turn at the 54-second mark. Marta reviewed the plan over coffee, corrected one jump, and said the count had to start on the four, not the one. The count starts on the four.

Sat 17 October 2026

First six figures done. Heads in foam, 9 mm, faces left to the benchlight. Legs in balsa with a half-millimetre hinge at the knee, which is the whole of the art: a figureine without a hinge will do a jump and land standing, which is a lie. Poms are cotton on a brad, 8 mm crown, red and gold. I made the poms out of proportion on purpose. At 1:24 a proportional pom is invisible, and the squad is a pom activity first.

Tue 3 November 2026

The stunt. A three-high pyramid, base of three, mid of two, top of one, armatures in 0.5 mm wire through the torsos, tops seated on the base pair’s wrists by way of a 3 mm tenon. It stands. I have stood it up forty times and knocked it down thirty-seven. Marta says the top flyer never looked down, and I believe her, and the model top flyer has no eyes in the first place, which helps.

Sun 9 November 2026

Tumbling pass: a 6 m run of 3 mm foam mat in a chalked lane, front handspring to layout for figureine 12. The layout is a 30-degree wedge under the feet held by a thread of 0.2 mm wire; the wire is released by a dab of solvent. On the ninth run the wedge held and the figureine stayed down where the layout should end. Two of three poms stayed in the hand, which I have decided is the best score this bench has ever posted. The desk would not confirm it, but the desk keeps its own records now.

Sat 16 November 2026

The crowd-turn. At 54 seconds a small card on a pin goes up in the stand behind the mat: 196.0, bar 2, one signal. The squad in the model does not stop; the count goes on to the eight. I built the pin because the photographs show nobody on the floor looking down, and a remount that looks down is a different building.

And the music. The final pose holds on the last chorus of “Everywhere,” which Marta had on the squad’s mixtape since 2001 — the one the obituary at Everywhere — An Obituary just filed under E. It entered the Billboard Hot 100 in the country. It peaked at twelve, and it died on November 4th, four days before I cut the final balsa. The model can play dead music as well as living, and the desk’s answer is the same as its answer to everything else: accurate.

Wed 25 November 2026

Full run, 90 seconds, benchlight only, one witness (Marta, from the sofa). The pyramid stood for the count, the layout landed, the card went up at 54, and the final pose held to the last chorus with all nineteen figures and fourteen of nineteen poms accounted for. The log closes with the line Marta wrote on the mat in her own hand: the floor does not look down.

Terms of remount

  • One squad, one mat, one count. The count starts on the four.
  • The build includes the pyramid, the lane, the card, and the song, played once, at low volume, with the doors open.
  • The build excludes the witnesses’ comments, which are accurate and are not filed.
  • Confirmation of the final run requires two of three signals: the builder, the witness, and one pom retained in the hand.

Signal the run

Press a signal. Two of three and the desk — hypothetically — confirms.
