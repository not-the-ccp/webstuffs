Joint Council on Constitutional Reform · Rome · Second Session

Folio No. 2/2014 · Registered at the Chancery, Via della Lungara

Minutes of the Meeting of 18 January 2014

Sala del Nazareno, the small council room off the corridor of the Palazzo, Rome · commenced 10:40 in the morning, closed 17:15

I. Present and in Quorum

The council met in the Sala del Nazareno at 10:40. Present: Matteo Renzi, Secretary of the Democratic Party; Silvio Berlusconi, President of Forza Italia; Signora Adele Fontana, Clerk of the Council, who kept these minutes; and three assessors each produced by the two delegations, of whom the clerk names only Mr. Gianni Cuperlo, who was present and took the floor twice, and notes the other five without ceremony. The quorum of sixteen of twenty-two was found wanting at the call; the clerk thereupon read into the record, from the margin of the standing-entries book kept by the chamber since the old days, a line she was obliged to transcribe as it stood, without emendation:

“The club’s quarterly letter on what a standing entry: a certain number do they act together, a practice in 1908.”

Mr. Cuperlo asked that the line be struck as unintelligible. It was not struck. The council resolved that a line kept since before either delegation was born stands as entered, that the quorum is met by the sixteen present, and that the session may proceed. The resolution passed without division, which is to say that both delegations were agreed on the one point the day would actually test: that a quorum is what a certain number do, acting together.

II. Title V of Part Two

The first article of the proposed agreement concerns Title V of Part Two of the Constitution, which arranges the division of powers between the State and the regions. Mr. Renzi stated that the reform would fix the competences once and for all, hand the regions their budgets with their laws, and end what he called the quarrel of the competences, in which the Court of Constitutional Arbitration has decided more cases than the council has held sessions in a decade. Mr. Berlusconi agreed, and added that his party wished the reform to be visible at the ballot, that is, to be put to a referendum of confirmation in the summer of 2014. It was resolved: Title V is amended; the reform is to be submitted to a referendum of confirmation.

III. The Chamber of Autonomies

The second article transforms the Senate of the Republic into a chamber of the regions, the two parties agreeing on the name Camera delle Autonomie, the Chamber of Autonomies. The clerk set the arithmetic on the board: one hundred members in all; twenty of them the presidents of the twenty regions, and the other eighty elected from the regional councils by the councillors themselves. Mr. Renzi observed that a senator so chosen could no longer be a member of the regional executive, and Mr. Berlusconi observed that a member of the regional executive could no longer sit in the chamber, and the clerk noted the two observations as agreeing with one another, which happened once.

Mr. Cuperlo moved that the clause be reconsidered; the motion failed. He recorded, and the clerk set down, that the Democratic Party has not been unanimous since the coffee at 11:20, and that the party's left flank will have to carry its own bags to the vote. From the Forza Italia delegation the abstention of one assessor was noted without name, the clerk observing that the name was supplied but did not read aloud.

IV. The Electoral Law

The third article approves a new electoral law for the Chamber of Deputies: a majority premium for the list above a threshold set at thirty-seven percent, the balance of seats allocated proportionally, and a referendum of confirmation to be held together with that of the second article. Mr. Berlusconi proposed the premium at one hundred and sixty seats and was permitted to keep it. Mr. Renzi proposed the thirty-seven percent threshold and was permitted to keep it also. The two propositions, the clerk noted, had been in the room since 9:00 and had waited politely to be asked.

V. Dissents on Record

The clerk reported that the Five Star Movement, the main opposition group, has called the agreement a bargain of the old classes made in a small room with the doors shut, and that its leader has announced the opposition will collect signatures for a counter-referendum before the summer. Mr. Cuperlo reported the discontent within the Democratic Party in a sentence that the clerk transcribed without ornament: that the left of the party will not sign what it cannot explain in a kitchen. From Forza Italia, the clerk reported the same discontent in the same shape, differing only in which kitchen. Both reports were accepted as accurate, which is the whole of the council's finding on the matter.

VI. The Signature and the Date

It was resolved that the agreement so drafted, in its three articles, be signed by both delegations this day, and that its entry into effect be tied to the formation of the government, Mr. Renzi understanding that he stands to be named Prime Minister of Italy on 22 February 2014, Mr. Berlusconi understanding that his party will support the government against a confidence vote. The document was signed at 17:10 in the afternoon, the clerk initialled the copies at 17:15, and the session closed with the bell.

The minutes were read through and found to keep the day's shape. For a council's minutes kept in the same spirit — another chamber, another country, another small room — the minutes of the Curriculum Council of Wales, 14 March 2025 stand in the same folio; and the winter that carried this agreement also carried the storm that took the pine from the hill above Llanfyllin, recorded at the manual for The Lonely Tree, first Powys edition.
