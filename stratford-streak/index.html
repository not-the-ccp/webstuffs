Winter Auction · Sale No. 92 · Ice & Winter Sport Memorabilia

Lot 47

The Stratford Streak — a centreman’s estate, in one box

21 September 1937, at three in the afternoon · the Winter Room, Stratford Hotel, Stratford, Ontario · catalogued and sold by R. & W. Pemberton, Auctioneers

Description

Lot 47 offers, as a single consignment in one oak box, the playing estate of Howard William Morenz (Stratford, Ontario, 21 September 1902 – 8 March 1937), centre for the Montreal Canadiens in two stints, the Chicago Black Hawks, and the New York Rangers, and for years one of the most dominant skaters in the National Hockey League. Before the league found him, the Stratford Streak had already won a Memorial Cup in the junior Ontario Hockey Association, where his team played for the championship of junior ice hockey in Canada. In the league he set several scoring records that stood for a decade or more; a strong skater from the first season, he carried two names the newspapers could not agree on — the “Stratford Streak” after the town that raised him, and the “Mitchell Meteor” after the Mitchell arena in Winnipeg where, it is said, the fans measured him against the clock. The box contains the following, as catalogued below.

  • 47a One hockey sweater, Montreal Canadiens, red and white, nameless, size medium, worn 1935–36. Stitch repairs at the left cuff in black thread. Slight pilling on the chest from the boards, two seasons of use. The number position is blank: the Canadiens did not yet wear them.
  • 47b One pair of steel skate blades, sharpened, with a leather guard, marked by the Stratford skatesmith. The guard carries the initials H.W.M. tooled into the leather, and a date, 1919.
  • 47c One ticket stub, Memorial Cup final, Stratford, 1922, junior Ontario Hockey Association: Morenz’s own seat, front row, kept in the same box ever since. Price on the stub: one dollar and a quarter.
  • 47d Forty-one game cards, NHL, various issues, 1924–1937, in the original wax sleeves; three bear pencil scores on the back in a hand the family identifies as his father’s, who marked the centre’s goals and nothing else.
  • 47e One card, plain cream, hand-lettered, found at the bottom of the box under the blades. It reads, in full, and is reproduced here exactly as it was found: “You are hearing the storm, because the clerk who wrote it and does not require it.” The family cannot account for it, and neither can we. The night steward at the Montrose Arena, who took charge of the box the day after the accident, wrote to the auction house that the line matched a phrase he kept in the crowd ledger for storm nights — the storm being the crowd, the clerk being himself, and the account being one nobody ever asked to be required. We print the card’s wording as found, and we price the card for what it is, which is a sentence.

Provenance & condition

From the family of the player, Stratford, Ontario, to the estate of the late player, of the accident on the King’s Highway near Sherbrooke on 8 March 1937, by which the centreman, three days short of his thirty-fifth birthday, was taken from a league that had already begun to put his records in a case. The box reached this house by the Montreal post of 24 August 1937, addressed simply Pemberton & W., Winter Room. Condition of the box is sound; the contents are as catalogued. The sweater has been pressed once and not since. The blades ring true on the marble of the auction table, a fact the room heard and the steward wrote down.

Estimate & terms

Estimate $150 – $250 · sold as one lot, the box to travel with it · the card (47e) cannot be separated, being a sentence and not an object · payment within ten days · the auctioneer reminds bidders that the Streak has already won four times, twice with the red sweater’s club and twice with the Rangers, and that the lot is offered in that memory.

Bidding opens at $75. The hammer is at three o’clock sharp, after Lot 46 (a pair of figure skates, junior size) and before Lot 48 (one puck, 1926, Chicago, the league’s first year of the current era). Bidders from out of town are asked to wire in advance; the Winter Room does not hold checks, and it does not hold storms.
