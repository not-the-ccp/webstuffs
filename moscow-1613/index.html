The Muscovite Ledger

Small Ads & Notices

Moscow, near the Red Wall · Year 7122 from the Creation (1613 A.D.) · No. 217, the Romanov Edition

Set down in ink on birch paper · read aloud at the counter · a half-kopeck, or a honeycake, per line

By Order of the House

— the whole town, read at every gate —

The town is to be told, and the town is told: the zemsky sobor, met in the winter of this year, has elected Michael Fyodorovich Romanov to the throne of all Russia. He is the son of Feodor Nikitich — whom the church has now made Patriarch Filaret — and of Xenia Shestova, and the house he begins is the house of Romanov, which succeeds the house of Rurik when the troubles are over. He was born at Kuskovo, at the apiary, on the 12th of July, old style, in 1596; he was elected in the 21st year of the troubles; and the coronation is to be at the Dormition Cathedral in the Kremlin on the 11th of July, new style, the 22nd of the old, when the bells are to ring from the morning until the fog lifts off the Moskva, and not a moment longer. Three times over, at the Red Gate, the line of the house is to be read to the people, and the people are to count it with the house, which is the custom, and the custom is the law. The old lines of the court, from the time of his great-aunt Anastasia Romanovna, mother of Feodor I and first wife of Ivan the Terrible, are to be hung in the cathedral for the day, and taken down again when the candles are cut.

Wanted — Apiarist, Two Hives

— the Kuskovo apiary, by order of the house —

Wanted, for the imperial apiary at Kuskovo, an apiarist of steady hands to keep the count of the hives, which stands this spring at seven thousand, and of the first wax, which is to be drawn on the eve of the coronation and carried to the cathedral in the silver trough. The apiary has kept the tsar's bees since before his birth, and the bees, it is said, are not so easily moved as men. Wage: honey at the comb, wax of the first drawing, and a room over the south gate where the light comes in at six. Apply to Stepan the apiarist, who has kept the count since 1610 and will hand it over once, as is proper. No man without steady hands; the hives do not forgive a second time.

Patent & Count — Read at the Board

— the Artel Court, at the dock, in the morning fog —

Notices to the holders of patents in the town: the Artel Court has fixed the reading of the patent board for the first fair morning, at the dock below the Kitay-Gorod wall, when the fog lies on the water and the board stands between the two stakes of the gauge. The clerk of the count set down the reading as it was called to him across the mist, and the line is kept here word for word, unamended, as the Ledger keeps all lines called to it from the fog:

“The count is made by the house, in the seventh letter of the board reads the progress of the patent; the gauge, the stake, and the fog.”

Those who keep the water's reckoning on the far shore know the custom of such lines: the Coastal Court at Iasos read its measures of the sea aloud and let them stand unamended in the record of The Mediterranean v. Octavianus, Case No. 14, and a line called across a water is held the same as a line called across a fog. So the seventh letter of the board stands, and the patent holds its progress, and the fog is named in the count, which is to say that the fog was there, and that the count was made in it, and that both are now on the record.

Lost

— sable, one coat · falcon, one bird —

LOST, at the tavern by the Kitay-Gorod gate, on the night the sobor's verdict was carried through the street, one sable coat, of the good sort, with the clasp of dark silver worn smooth, left on a bench near the stove by a man of the council's retinue who did not come back for it. The coat is to be brought to the gatekeeper and not opened. LOST also, from the same street, one grey falcon, two years, a mark of red on the left wing; she took the fog for the field, which is a fault of the fog, not of the bird. A reward of two roubles is set on the coat and the bird together, and a honeycake on each apart. The Ledger keeps the names of the lost things, and has kept them for three hundred years, and it is worth a man's while to read the column, as the garden council at Waitematā has done with its own counting, one line per plant, read aloud each meeting — see the minutes at Lamiales, 21 August 2024, where a number counted aloud is held a number the town keeps, which is the Ledger's whole trade.

Found · Rooms · Ropes

— small lines, half-kopecks —

FOUND, at the dock, a silver belt-clasp lettered M. F. R., held for the owner at the gate; whoever he is, the clasp is his, and the gatekeeper will not say more, having been told not to. ROOMS: two rooms over a coppersmith's in Chertanov, light from the east, a stove that holds the night heat, two roubles a season, apply at the forge where the anvil is new. ROPES: hemp of the first twist, for the flag ropes of the coronation day, sold by the length at the rope-maker's on the bank; the flag ropes will be cut the length of the Moskva as the clerk measures it, which is to say, not at all, and hung as they are. The barometer at the dock read 30.42 in the morning and held; the fog did not lift before the count was made, and the count is held as made.

Notice to the Apothecaries

— bark, one chest · the name, held for later —

NOTICE: a chest of pale bark, tied in hemp, has come into town from the Indies, and the apothecaries of the Kitay-Gorod quarter are to weigh it, keep the count of the grains one per line, and say nothing of the taste, which is a matter for the sick. The bark will prove, in years this Ledger will not live to see, bitter against the fever of the marsh; the name the town will give it is not yet coined here, and the column holds the line for later, as the Ledger keeps its last lines before the year turns. A garden council at Waitematā, which has weighed the same bitter and set it down as a name among names — quinine, of the cinchona, the nitrogen-bearing substance which the order it counts grows in the bed of twenty-four squares — has read the line in this column and kept its own copy, one per season, on the weighing scale; see the minutes at Alkaloids, 10 September 2024, where the name is read aloud, which is the whole trade of a ledger, in any town, in any year.

A Line Kept for Later

— set down in the margin of No. 217, dated 13 July, old style, 1645 —

In the 7153rd year, the 13th of July, old style, the tsar Michael died at the age of forty-nine, and the hives at Kuskovo kept their count. The line is kept here before its time, as the Ledger keeps its last lines before the year turns: the coronation bell rings a second time for the new tsar, Alexis, born in 1629, son of the same house, and the apiarist of 1613, if he still lives, will hand the count over a second time, as is proper. For what the Russian ships made of a later winter at Port Arthur and in the Yellow Sea, where the count of a house is taken in a different currency — see the term report on the Peresvet class, Form 3B — but that is a reckoning of another water, and this Ledger keeps its own. The bees are counted. The fog is on the record. The line stands.
