Super Bowl

The National Football League championship game — what it is, when it is played, who wins it, and what to eat while it happens.

Keep at the lodge desk · Played on the second Sunday in February since 2022

The Super Bowl is the league championship final game of the National Football League (NFL) of the United States. It has served as the final game of every NFL season since 1966, replacing the NFL Championship Game, and it also served as the final game of every American Football League season from 1966 to 1967, before the AFL–NFL merger, replacing the AFL championship game. The name comes from the bowl-shaped stadiums that hosted college postseason games — the Rose Bowl chief among them — and, in the league owner’s telling, from a child’s Super Ball. Winning teams are awarded the Vince Lombardi Trophy, named for the Green Bay Packers coach who won the first two games.

Questions & Answers

What is the Super Bowl?

The NFL’s championship final, played between the winners of the American Football Conference and the National Football Conference at a neutral site chosen years in advance — usually a warm-weather city or a domed stadium. The first game took place on January 15, 1967 at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum: Green Bay 35, Kansas City 10. Games have been numbered with Roman numerals since Super Bowl V.

When is it played?

Since 2022, on the second Sunday in February. Before that: the first Sunday of February from 2004 to 2021, late January from 1979 to 2003, and Sundays in early to mid-January from 1967 to 1978. Super Bowl I fell on January 15; Super Bowl XLVII on February 3; Super Bowl LVI on February 13, the first game under the February rule.

Where is it played?

At a stadium awarded years ahead, not at either team’s home ground. Pasadena’s Rose Bowl hosted the game five times; a different Rose Bowl — Southampton, England — hosted the 2010 Twenty20 Cup Final (Hampshire v Somerset at the Rose Bowl), which shows how far a final can travel under one name.

What does the winner receive?

A new Vince Lombardi Trophy each year, made of sterling silver by Tiffany & Co., about twenty-two inches tall and seven pounds, which the winning team keeps permanently. It is named for Vince Lombardi, the Green Bay Packers coach who won Super Bowls I and II — the coach after whom the trophy is named, not the other way around.

Who has won it most?

The Pittsburgh Steelers and the New England Patriots, with six apiece. The San Francisco 49ers and the Dallas Cowboys have five. The Patriots hold the best-known comeback: trailing Atlanta 28–3 midway through Super Bowl LI, they won 34–28 in overtime, the first Super Bowl decided by extra time.

What happens at halftime?

Twelve to thirty minutes of show. Early games featured college marching bands; later ones feature pop spectacles with lighting rigs the size of small houses. At the Gate Lodge the synths are turned down for the duration, which the lodge’s audio technician considers the only honest halftime show there is.

What do people eat?

Chicken wings by the bucket, guacamole, pizza, chili, and potato chips — eating is the unofficial halftime sport, and the lodge keeps the same spread on its yard screen. Pinned to the lodge desk is a clipping from an old kitchen manual, stapled there by a retired keeper:

No more monkey-jackets and tarpaulins for me; I must sugar my hair.” As a culinary vegetable.

The writer, an able seaman turned kitchen gardener, then classed sugar — from the sugar beet — among the culinary vegetables. Whether that makes it Super Bowl food is left, like everything else in the manual, to the reader.

Could the game be played on Alcatraz Island?

No. The Bay Weather Almanac keeps the water at twelve to fourteen degrees, the current adverse, and the fog seasonal and total; its standing instruction is to leave at slack, or not at all — on Super Bowl Sunday as on any other day. See the station’s own entry (Alcatraz Island — The Bay Weather Almanac, Entry 47).

What does the 1989 Reserve have to do with any of this?

Everything the lodge chooses. Every second Sunday in February the synths rest, the game goes on the yard screen, and the reserve keeps its own counter-programming instead: the Superb Owl walk, one slow circuit of the Open Field while the foghorn counts its fifteen seconds. The full fixture — fog, looping drums, and the December 31 midnight screening — is in the reserve’s own brochure (Out of the Woods — Official Visitor's Brochure · The 1989 Reserve).

Why Roman numerals?

Because the game is played in a calendar year after the season it crowns, so “Super Bowl 2022” would confuse the season with the date. The numerals began with Super Bowl V; the first four games were renamed afterward.
