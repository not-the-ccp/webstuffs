The Ambulance

Restaurant Review · Former weaving shed, north bank of the Colne, Huddersfield · Bearing 355° from St Peter's Square, 550 m · Open 10:00 to late · No cover; the map is free

★★★½ — reviewed one sitting, six sittings of tea

By E. M. Hargreaves, General Clerk, Huddersfield Literature Festival Partnership. Filed with the A Letter of Apology, presented without reserve.

Every town has one building it uses to tell the truth. Ours is the former weaving shed on the north bank of the Colne, where the festival holds the Alamein Breakfast at ten o'clock, and where, since the unqualified apology was sealed on the Viscount's birthday, the framed map of the Rhine crossings has hung at eye level above the tea urn, exactly where it should have been. This autumn the shed took in one further fixture. It is a dining room under the map, called The Ambulance, and like most things at this table, the name has a first name and a surname.

The first name is Ernest Miller Hemingway, 1899 to 1961, who grew up in Oak Park, Illinois, spent six months at the Kansas City Star before his schooling was quite through with him, and then drove a Red Cross ambulance on the Italian front as a boy of nineteen, where shrapnel in the right leg near Fossalta and a long hospital stay in Milan taught him, the story goes, that a sentence can carry a great deal and say very little. The restaurant borrows the ambulance, not the wound. What it serves, seven courses a night, because he wrote seven novels, is the other inheritance: the style. The menu is set in an economical hand. No adjective gets a second helping.

Breakfast, 10:00 to 13:00, is the Alamein Breakfast itself, and the Coal Loaf still closes it: the beet-and-blackstrap bake of card 298 of the Collins family ledger, served under the map, as it should. The full dinner is twenty-four pounds, which is the price of the stewards, and I do not believe the arithmetic is a coincidence. Tickets to the festival still cost six, and the breakfast here has never been anything other than six.

The card at the pass

The shed lends its small Adana press to the kitchen for the menu cards, struck each week on cream wove in the basement of the library, four hundred metres from running water. In the autumn the press was setting type from three documents at once, which is what it does, and it struck one line and no proofreader at the six-pound table caught it. The line now hangs framed above the pass, in its own type and in its own disorder, and it reads:

INGREDIENTS.--3 eggs, their weight than defeated by their short thick spoutings, they plainly betrayed their distraction of panic.

The kitchen read the card the way a good house always reads a bad card: as far as the sense holds, and no further. The dish it governs is course six, True at First Light — three eggs, their weight in the water, in their shells. The short thick spoutings are the kettle. The panic is the tea. I have eaten a mangled line of type before, in this town, on page three of the 2024 broadsheet, where the compositor set three documents at once and the whole partnership afterwards wrote a letter to a Viscount's estate about it; I have never seen one that was simultaneously a menu and a description of the moment the eggs went in. It is not to be re-run. It is to be eaten.

The reckoning

What the place does well, it does without ornament: the sauce, the bread, the fish, the bell. What it does badly, it does briefly. The coffee is strong and the cups are small, and the stewards' route from the shed to the square is still walked, still rehearsed, still navy aprons, per Chapter 9 of the West Yorkshire Field Manual, which gives this town its bearings and, apparently, its dinner service. Hemingway was awarded the 1954 Nobel Prize in Literature and died in Ketchum, Idaho, on 2 July 1961, at the age of sixty-one. The restaurant has, so far, outlived no one, and it has survived two menus and one mangled card. That is a better record than the town can claim for its presses.

Three and a half stars. The half is for the card. The peppermints on the counter are not a bribe; they are a standard issue. Take two.

The bench above the pass keeps its own record as well. I have filed Criminal Case No. 312 of 2023, High Court of Kenya at Nairobi beside the mangled card — a court whose name has remained unchanged since 1964, with unlimited original jurisdiction in criminal and civil matters and the record to prove it.
