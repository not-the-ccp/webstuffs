HILL COUNTRY
STATION No. 9

The Natural History of Machines · Field Series · Vol. III

Chupacabra

The Inverted Machine of Fiesta Texas — Bolliger & Mabillard type, track over, riders under

Observer: M. E. Calloway, naturalist of machines (license 9, third class, machines of the lifting kind)
Station: Six Flags Fiesta Texas, at the rim of San Antonio, Texas, United States
Season opened: 14 March 2025 · Book kept in the field, in pencil where the rain got it

SPECIMEN No. FC-1995 · INVERTED COASTER · LIVE

14 March, 08.40 — the machine at rest

The hill behind the gate at Fiesta Texas is limestone, pale and cut in steps, and the machine sits on its crest exactly where the ground has already gone over onto its back, so that from the queue the whole structure reads as a fold of the terrain that the park simply fenced off and charged for. It is an inverted coaster: the track is the spine and the train hangs beneath it, each row of five riders suspended under the rail with their shins on the outside of every turn, which is to say, the animal flies upside down, or rather it flies right side up and drags you beneath its belly. The designer was Werner Stengel; the manufacturer is Bolliger & Mabillard, out of Switzerland, and the Stengel hand is unmistakable — the lift hill is a clean diagonal, no cheating, no pretence of a scene, the whole argument made in one long held note. I have counted the cars: twelve, sixty seats, and on a quiet morning the train goes through the line like a single slow sentence.

14 March, 10.15 — biometrics, taken with the tape

Heights are the only honest language at this station, and here are this animal’s. Crest of the lift: 105 feet (32 metres) above the footpath — I verified it three times against the survey post the park left standing, and against my own pulse, which disagreed. Maximum speed on the first drop, by the clock I carry and by the scream, is 63 kilometres an hour, which is fast enough to matter and slow enough to think, and the animal knows it. The ride lasts a minute and fifty-odd seconds and turns four inversions, including one back roll in the second half that the manual of the park calls a “vertical roll” and I call the turn where the sky is filed away and the limestone comes down to take its place. The lift chain makes its sound at 10.12: a low ratchet, one link, two links, like a jaw working. The naturalist in me is not certain whether the machine breathes or whether I do.

14 March, 13.50 — the specimen’s own biography, as the park’s board gives it

No specimen at this station has been so much a migrant as this one, and the board by the gate is generous with its dates. It was born in 1995 at Odakyu Gotemba, in the Family Land there in Shizuoka, Japan — the hill resort that later ran under the name Thrill Valley — and it opened there under its first name, the one it now wears again. In 2002 it was taken apart, crated, and carried to the Gulf, to Six Flags New Orleans, where it reopened in 2003 as Batman: The Ride and ran, they say, with a full house in the dark seasons. Hurricane Katrina came in August 2005, the water took the park, and the park was abandoned with its machinery standing in the salt, which is the one page of the biography I keep returning to: an inverted animal, left facing the sky in a drowned room. It was moved a third time and reopened at Fiesta Texas in 2008 as Goliath, and in 2024 the park announced the name you see on the sign above the queue: Chupacabra. Three names, three harbours, two oceans crossed, one hurricane survived — the creature outlived every name except the one it was first given, which is, of all the things it has done, the strangest.

14 March, 17.10 — on the keeping of the numbers

I work stations in three countries, and this book is only the page that belongs to Texas. The other stations have their own folios — the small-ads column of the Central Division, kept where the red ledger is wanted and answered at the Small Advertisements column of 11 November 1931, and the folio of nine numbered chairs, the minute of 3 October 2007, which the Hall prices and refuses to parse at Lot 42 of the sale of 14 June 2024 — and the series is cross-checked in full against the front of the collection at the cover of the Miscellany, where every specimen is counted, in order, faults and all. Between the stations, the numbers do the waiting. We fly Miami–São Paulo regularly; winter headwinds make you respect the numbers. An aircraft crossing the tropic in December is a machine that knows exactly what 105 feet is not, and the respect carries back to the limestone hill: the lift is 32 metres, the drop is a fixed thing, the four inversions will come in their order and no other, and the one number that never changes on the board is the year of first birth — 1995 — which the machine, like the ledger men and like the chair that was sold separately as a reservation, carries under all its other names.

14 March, 19.05 — last observation

At dusk the track goes dark first and the hills behind it go dark second, and for one minute the structure is only a black rib against the last of the orange, and the next train ratchets up beneath it, sixty seats, suspended, going over the fold of the world and coming back the right way up. I have logged it live, and I will log it again next season. Names, at this station, are a matter of customs only. The animal keeps its own. — M. E. C.
