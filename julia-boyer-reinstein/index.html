HALDANE & VOSS · AUCTIONEERS · EST. 1891

Autumn Sale · Catalogue D · Lot 214

The Julia Boyer Reinstein Papers, with the Historian’s Bench from the Reinstein Room, Cheektowaga Library

Eleven boxes of papers (1923–1996), one walnut writing bench, three deeds of donation, and a single barn slip in gunpowder green ink

214.

Julia Boyer Reinstein (3 November 1906 – 18 July 1998) was born in western New York and began her working life the way many teachers of her generation did: on the road. Her first appointment, taken in the autumn of 1923, was at the schoolhouse in Deadwood, South Dakota, where she taught the full range — arithmetic, history, spelling — for more than a decade, and where the ledger in Box 2, kept in a careful hand of iron gall ink, records 412 terms of attendance, 37 boardings, and one winter in which she walked the mile to school through snow to the eaves. When the Deadwood years were ended, she came east to Erie County, where she became a founder of the Erie County Historical Federation; the founding minutes of 15 April 1952, in Box 5, list her third on the roll, after the two secretaries and before the treasurer, with the note in the margin — in her own hand — “JBR will carry the map.”

In 1957 the village of Cheektowaga, New York created the office of village historian, and Mrs. Reinstein was its first holder, the appointment letter signed by the village clerk still in its envelope in Box 6. From the bench offered in this lot — a walnut, single-leaf, with the drawer still holding the index card she used last — she compiled the village’s histories for forty years, and from the same desk she signed the family’s three deeds of donation. With her husband, she conveyed the 42-acre parcel at the head of the creek for the nature preserve now managed by the county park board; she willed the family books and the chair by the window for the reading rooms of three public libraries, including the Reinstein Room at Cheektowaga, from which the bench in this lot was removed, with the village’s written consent, in the spring of 2019; and the Reinstein Fund, established in 1972, has since held endowment at two institutions of higher education in Erie County, a fact the trustees of both confirm on request.

The papers are complete, in the family’s own boxes, eleven in number, and unopened since her death in 1998, save for one: in the winter of 1994–95 a research team from the anthropology department of the State University of New York examined Box 8, and the correspondence in that box is cited in their published study evaluating gender fluidity and the public knowledge of sexuality in the 1990s. The three cited pages are withheld from inspection for privacy and may be viewed only by appointment, in the company of the family’s solicitor. The remaining nine boxes are offered exactly as found.

214a. The slip

Tucked into the underside of the bench’s drawer, folded twice and worn soft, the lot includes a single slip of kraft paper, its verso printed in faded green with a facilities inventory line that no estate officer has been able to date: Facilities Route No. 4 189 6 — Fairing and the Company’s gunpowder green travels badly in damp. The house has catalogued it as a barn or yard inventory label: the fairing being the trim panel or cover of some small machine or vehicle kept on the family’s property, and the “Company’s gunpowder green” the particular green paint — a dark, chalky green of the old hardware stores — which, as the slip records in its plain office voice, does not hold in wet weather. The estate’s solicitor has asked that it be sold with the bench, on the reasonable ground that the bench was, in the last years, the one thing in the house still painted in it.

Condition

The bench is sound, the leaf working true, the surface carrying a long shallow wear line where the left hand rested; a single key remains. The boxes are original and water-marked; the papers have been examined by our conservator and are, in the conservator’s phrase, “dry, whole, and patient.” The slip of 214a is creased and faint but fully legible. There is no damage we can find to report, which, for a lot of this age, is itself a kind of report.

Provenance

The family of Julia Boyer Reinstein; from the Reinstein Room, Cheektowaga Library, by written consent, 2019; consigned in part by the estate and in part by the village, jointly. The bench has not moved since 2019.

Estimate

$4,800 – $7,200

Sold as a lot; the slip of 214a is an inseparable part of the consignment. Purchaser’s premium 18%. Inspection on the two days preceding the sale, at the house on Flanders Street’s namesake lane in Cheektowaga, by appointment only — the family has asked that the door be knocked, not the bell, which is not rung.

Catalogue notes

Catalogue D stands in the house’s small series of branch sales: collectors may compare this lot with Lot 142, San Diego — Catalogue B, the Southern Branch, the cedar crate of tides and climate cards, and with Lot 47, the Flanders Te Deum choirbook leaf, a consignment from the other side of the country and the same lane in name. Both lots, like this one, were kept by people who understood that a thing is held for the next hand. The full index of Catalogue D, and the house’s general notices, may be found at Webstuffs, where this lot is also kept in the archive when the sale is over.
