One Song, Baby Boy

Notice No. BB-0308 · Claim window: open · Reward: one glass of sweet tea

DESCRIPTION OF THE ITEM

The item is a song, one in length, title Baby Boy. American singer Beyoncé, the first person named, sings it; Jamaican dancehall musician Sean Paul enters at the back door, the second person named. The song carries Caribbean and Indian marks and moves on a dancehall-and-R&B beat, and its lyrics detail, in the clerk’s phrase, a woman’s fantasies, which the clerk has logged as aspirational content, no injury reported. The item contains, folded inside it, a lyrical interpolation of No Fear by the hip-hop group O.G.C., and it should be searched for the splice before it is lent out.

PARTIES ASSOCIATED WITH THE ITEM

The song was co-written by Beyoncé, Sean Paul, Robert Waller, Jay-Z, and Scott Storch, the last of whom produced it together with Beyoncé. All five names appear on the back of the sleeve card; the office keeps the sleeve card in the ledger, so a claimant should expect to be asked which of the five wrote the hook and will be forgiven for guessing.

WHERE AND WHEN FOUND

The item left its last known home on 3 August 2003, the day it was released as the second single from Beyoncé’s debut solo studio album, Dangerously in Love (2003), by Columbia Records and Music World Entertainment, and it was next reported playing, unattended, in the kitchen of the 404 Club, Houston, above a pot that the night cook left on the back burner. The item also belongs, the office notes, on the reissue of Sean Paul’s second studio album, Dutty Rock (2002), which is why two people at two different addresses both believed they had lost it. The office holds one copy, and it is not being split.

FOUND IN THE SAME POUCH

Taped to the back of the sleeve card was one recipe slip for callaloo, in a hand the office has not identified, with a pencilled margin line that the cook who left it has never once returned to collect. The line reads, and the office prints it exactly as written: Add salt if it needs only the water. The night cook, asked what it means, turned the fire down and left the room, and the office records that the pot, when checked, was fine, and that the question remains open. The slip is part of the claim.

TERMS OF CLAIMING

To claim the item, a person must come to the office between 6:00 and 9:00 p.m., state which of the five writers they most admire, hum the chorus once, and sign the ledger in the column marked vocal, not written. The office will release the song with the sleeve card and the recipe slip together, sealed in one pouch, because the margin line is part of the item and the cook has made that point in writing. The office has also cross-shelved this notice against the two other open cases it is related to: the Pamplona Football Society’s own lost-and-found board, where a club president went missing from the El Sadar tunnel and left a folio behind, is kept at Lost & Found Notice No. 402, and the four-hour simmer the night cook was running under the music is written up, down to the six grams of salt and the 190 kr torte, on Safety Data Sheet No. HE-786. The master ledger of the office, and the front of the whole shelf this drawer hangs off, is the front page.

Status: open, one copy held, hum before signing.
