Lost & Found

Lost Ref. MH-1916L · Reward in marble time

One walnut chisel box, seven tools, last used in the Mott Haven yard of the Piccirilli Brothers, the yard that cut the seated Lincoln from Daniel Chester French’s model in twenty-eight blocks of Georgia marble. The yard’s ledger logs the box under two names — the run number of the yard and the line of the commission sheet — and the entry, set here as preserved, runs: No. 1916L Lincoln Memorial from Daniel Chester French; their chisels stand on Washington Square Arch, the New Style; both kept. Whoever holds the box may keep one chisel; the register insists on the other six. The full yard board, with run No. 1916L on Platform 2, is kept at Mott Haven Yard — The Marble Limited.

Found Ref. ENC-2M · Under review

Two million works, counted in the census of the year 2000, found in the basement stores, the 22nd floor, and one hallway in the Greek and Roman galleries where no one admits to having put them. The register now reports a total of 1.5 million. The registrar calls it encyclopedic loss, not theft: a museum of this size grows out of its own count the way a city grows out of its street numbers. No claim expected; the works have not missed the attention.

Found Ref. VIS-5727258

One visitor, numbered 5,727,258 — the last of the fiscal year 2025 count, which made this the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum in the world — found leaving at closing by the east door, still arguing with the 22nd floor about what a department means. Returned in good order, coat and all. The museum, being the largest art museum in the Americas, keeps its visitors briefly and returns them whole.

For Sale

For Sale Price: the view is included free

One window seat, facing east, in the building at 1000 Fifth Avenue, along the Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park. By floor area the building is one of the largest museums in the world, and the window is a small thing that costs as much as the rest. The seat looks on the park; the park does not object. Non-transferable after dark.

For Sale Ref. PED-1921A · Chisels as advertised

One pediment, in marble, for the front of an exchange; the New York Stock Exchange piece among the commissions from the Bronx yard is named here for reference only — that one is not for sale, that one is for the ledger. This one is. It cuts like the lot named in the Mott Haven departure board, run 1921A, Arch & Pediment, Platform 3. Buyer should bring a cradle and an apology.

For Sale One lot only

Floor area, by the tenth of a square metre, from a museum that is fourth-largest in the world by the measure. Cut to order, cured in the east corridor, guaranteed to hold the weight of two million works or, failing that, 1.5 million. Suits collectors of encyclopedic institutions and anyone with a wall that has never met a museum.

Wanted · To Let · Exchange

Wanted Answer in any of 17 departments

One thing per department, being the honest want of the collection: Arms and Armor wants one gauntlet, Asian Art one lacquer moon, the Egyptian department one sandhill, Greek and Roman one argument about a pediment, the Medieval Arts one hinge, the Islamic Art one mirror, the Robert Lehman Collection one glass, the American Wing one porch, the Cloisters one window (see above; not this one), and the rest of the seventeen what they each will not name out loud. Pay in floor area. The collection, divided into 17 curatorial departments, is the whole of the want.

Wanted Ref. WORLD-5

A fourth place, by visitors. This museum sits fifth among the world’s art museums and most-visited in the United States, which is a fine row to hold and a poor row to die in. The register will pay in window seats, in floor area, in one honest argument per visitor. Fifth is a starting position; the ticker at the top of this sheet has the arithmetic.

To Let Top floor · full daylight

One department, the American Wing, top floor, full daylight off the park, no subletting to encyclopedics. References: the whole building. Rent: what the building already is. Tenant must accept that the wing is named for the country in which it stands and that this is not a complaint, only a fact set in small type.

Exchange Both kept

One seated Lincoln, Georgia marble, cut from the model of Daniel Chester French by the Mott Haven yard on run No. 1916L, offered in exchange for one arch, Washington Square, likewise of the yard’s chisels. The ledger says both kept, and this exchange agrees with the ledger. Terms as struck on the board the yard keeps at Mott Haven: twenty-eight blocks, one platform, no delay except in marble time.

In Memoriam The census line, 2000

The permanent collection of 2,000,000 works, 2000. It was more than the living collection, which it survived by the census count and then quietly stopped being. Survived by 1.5 million, who hold the register and decline to be called a loss. A flower may be left at the East Door. The museum, being encyclopedic, will keep it.

Persons Missing Recounted Tuesdays

One curatorial department, the count running 17 and then running 16 between the Tuesday recount and the morning call. Returned in full at 9 a.m. with nothing to say and everything to show. The other sixteen kept their posts; none of them is naming the seventeenth. If the missing department calls, the answer is that its work is in the basement stores, which is where all the answers go.

Notice from the Registrar’s Office

This sheet is set by the registrar’s office in the building at 1000 Fifth Avenue, along the Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park on Manhattan’s Upper East Side, and read by anyone who has come down the line from the Bronx yard where the Marble Limited keeps its departures in marble time — run No. 1916L is logged here exactly as it is logged on that board — and by anyone who has carried the error ledger in which Old Style and New Style dates were set side by side and both kept, the Hancock — Field Error Reference, Revision 1793, whose language the ledger-line above borrows word for word. Claims on any ad in this sheet are to be made in writing, in a signature large enough to be read from the back of the hall; the registrar will accept a name or a signature, and where the two disagree the signature wins, as it has always won, and the name is filed under the signature, both kept.

The museum takes no order in this sheet, offers none, and answers none. It is encyclopedic, and the classifieds are its one department that invents its own contents. Visitors numbered 1 through 5,727,258, fiscal year 2025, are each entitled to one reading of this page, free of charge, at the East Door, before the ticker wears through.
