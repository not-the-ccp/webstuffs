WANTED — ONE AUTO DOLLAR, GENUINE

Wanted, the silver one yuan coin struck in 1928 at the new mint in this province, known to the road crews and the money changers of Guiyang as the Auto Dollar (汽車錢, "automobile coin"; also sold as the Guizhou car coin, 贵州汽车币). It was struck by Zhou Xicheng, who took the province in 1927 and has spent the last year laying road where the province had stone paths, to commemorate the construction of the roadways of Guizhou. The obverse shows an automobile driving along a road; on either side of the road the field grass is worked into strokes that spell the proprietor's personal name, Xicheng (西成). A square hole sits at the top, as the province has always wanted its money to.

The coin is fine silver, one tael heavy by the mint's own weighing, and is the only piece in the province that carries a motor car. It passed through the hands of the road supervisors at milestone 47, and it is wanted at a figure the office will name in person and not in print. Bring the coin, bring the box it came in if you are still sentimental, and do not bring a copy from a catalogue: the office has seen three, and the engraver's pencil test distinguishes a struck car from a drawn one. Payment in bank notes, or in road work, at the claimant's preference.

REWARD: 120 yuan · answer under the sign of the automobile, at the money desk of the leafet office, 11 Miao Road, Guiyang. No letters. The desk opens when the automobile passes.

CLEANING & GRADING — HAND-CHASED, SUNDAYS

Base Patina, the cleaning and grading house of the mint quarter, takes in the new silver for a hand chase: the grime of the roadway lifted, the automobile sharpened, the grass of 西成 brought up stroke by stroke, and the edge teeth counted for the owner's record. No machine touches the field. The price is eight cents per side, plus a cent when the owner asks to see the work, which is most of them.

The house keeps one ledger at the register room of the mint office, and the register has one entry the office has not yet finished arguing with. It reads, word for word:

“For 101 weeks, one week number, hand-chased Base Patina worked with old prompt books, and the room from the register does not have.”

Which is to say, in the register's own account: for 101 weeks, one week number's worth of work, the hand-chase done by Base Patina was carried out against the old prompt books of the mint — the pre-1927 strike sheets, the ones the new proprietor kept in a tin box — and the room that holds the register does not have that week number written in it at all. One week is unnumbered. The coins chased that week therefore have no register line of their own and must be proved by the hand that worked them: look under the car for the small chaser's mark, a tick in the grass, and if the tick is there the coin is Base Patina's, and Base Patina will stand behind it, in person, on Sundays.

FOR SALE — ONE AUTOMOBILE, THE PROVENANCE OF WHICH IS THE PROVINCE

For sale by private treaty, one automobile, American-built, 1925, dark red, the first known automobile in Guizhou, acquired by Zhou Xicheng in 1927 and since driven on the new roadways, including the drive to the mint and arsenal ground and the ceremonial drive from which the coin's obverse is copied. Runs on the new fuel drums; the radiator was re-riveted at milestone 31; the horn is a bicycle horn, which the driver insists is a feature. With the car: the driver's log, one tin of the old prompt books, and a set of road markers. It is for sale because its owner is now, as the ticker says, abroad. Serious bidders only; the province needs the car on the roads more than in a barn, and the office will say so to you to your face.

NOTICE — NEW ROADWAY, MILESTONES 1 TO 47

Notice to drivers, coolies, and officials: the roadway from the city gate to the mint ground is open in full, milestones 1 to 47, surfaced, and marked. The surveyors' figures: fourteen li of road, nine culverts, and one stretch of the line that crosses what the surveyors call, in their report, "a small conviction," which is the bridge at the third crossing, built in eleven days. Cattle are to be kept off; the automobile that carries the mint's weekly silver is to have the road. Complaints to the road office, or to the grass, whichever answers first.

SITUATION WANTED — ONE MINT GRADUATOR, THE ARSENAL WILL DO

Situation wanted by one man of thirty-two, a graduate of the northern school at Nanjing, good with a micrometer, good with an argument about tolerances, and able to keep a ledger so clean the register room would adopt it. Seeks employment at the mint in the grading of the new silver one yuan, or failing that at the arsenal, where he has heard the rifling gauges are older and kinder. References: the road supervisors at milestone 47, who employed him all last month to count the edge teeth of the trial strikes, of which the count was 150, and of which he has since counted 4,312 in the office of this leafet. Wages will be named at interview, in the register room, where one week number is still missing and he suspects he could find it.

LOST — ONE REGISTER, THE MISSING WEEK

Lost, on or about the fourth month of 1928, from the register room of the mint office, one register, red cloth, in which the work of the hand-chase house is entered week by week. The missing portion is precisely one week number: the week in which Base Patina worked from the old prompt books. The person who has it will notice, when they open it, that one page is softer than the rest, the way a page is softer that has been turned more often. Return it, or the page, to the money desk at 11 Miao Road. Reward: eight cents per side, forever, on one coin of the owner's choosing. That is the house's whole fortune, and the office will not haggle, because the office, like the room from the register, does not have the week, and would very much like to.

Classified columns of this leafet are read in the same room where the one-yuan is weighed, and the office keeps the pages of the columns where they are wanted. Travellers who collect such things will find our Classified Notices of the Hall of Names set in the same tradition — a notice board in which even a hyphen is sold by the piece — and, further off, the Auction Catalogue, Lot 1861, where a whole republic goes on the block with all faults, which is a starker column than any this leafet would dare to run. Beside that lot sits the Dream Dictionary's Entry 1861, on Conscription, where the three-hundred-dollar substitute buys the same exemption a coin struck for a province would have bought in 1862 — money, in either province, doing the work of the roll.
