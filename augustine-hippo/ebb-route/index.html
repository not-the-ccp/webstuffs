The Ebb Route A Walking Tour in Nine Stops · Itinerary No. 58½

Walked this 29th, the day after the bay went quiet, from the Thagaste Gate to the harbour and back · 4.1 km, nine stops, one ebb · weather: falling tide, wind off the Numidia, door standing open as the book requires · guide: the grey bronzeback, in person, keeping his shelf by the parcel post · rates: twelve shillings, water included, the midwife's figs at stop 4, and whatever the sea has left.

The matter of this walk, the Clerk was told, is not a name but a word — and the Hall, which has argued a hyphen to its end in Minutes No. 57 and ruled that a vowel, being soft, is not to be argued at all in Minutes No. 58, proposes to walk it instead. The word is abortion: the termination of a pregnancy by removal or expulsion of an embryo or fetus. The unmodified word, the guide is to say at the gate and nowhere else, generally refers to induced abortion, or the deliberate actions to end a pregnancy; and the ending that happens without any intervention is known as spontaneous abortion, or “miscarriage,” in the house's mouth, and it occurs in roughly 30 to 40 of every 100 pregnancies. Mrs. Monica of Thagaste objected to the second figure at the planning table and was overruled on the substance and thanked on the manner, which is the way of the house.

Stop 1 The Thagaste Gate 7:00 · 0.0 km

Gathering under the gate where the road in from Thagaste meets the coast, the gate standing exactly where the book keeps its doors: on its side. Name cards for the walkers — spell them as the bearers spell them, and only as the bearers spell them, the rule being the rule — and a short word on the word, once, as the guide is bound to say it once and the hall is bound to hear it once. Abortion: the termination of a pregnancy by removal or expulsion of an embryo or fetus. The bronzeback is to be reminded that he is a guide, not a minute book, and that red ink belongs to the Clerk. Common reasons, the Clerk reads from the ledger, are birth-timing and limiting family size; the rest of the ledger waits at the later stops, which is why the walk has nine.

Stop 2 The Grove of Numidia Cirtensis 7:25 · 0.6 km

The olive grove where the household of Thagaste keeps its counting, in the province of Numidia Cirtensis, and where, on the 13th of November 354, one Augustine was set down among the first-fruits, seventy-six years before the bay took him. Birth-timing is timed here by the olives, the way the house times everything by fruit: a tree does not argue with the season, the guide says, and stops. Limiting family size is limited here by the wall of the grove, and the wall is not argued. The walkers are given ten seconds to count the trees on the north row; no two walkers count the same number, and the Clerk rules that the grove, like the number of refusals in Milan, is one of the figures that drift the way all figures do in a chancery, and that a drifting figure is not a false one. Mrs. Monica counts forty-one, and is thanked on the manner.

Stop 3 The Well under the Tamarisk 7:55 · 1.2 km

Here the walk slows, because here the ledger is read, and it is the heaviest page in the book. Roughly 30 to 40 of every 100 pregnancies end as a spontaneous abortion — a miscarriage, the word the house is entitled to use — without any hand, plan, or argument in the matter at all. The well gives up its water and the well takes it back; the water is not consulted. The guide is to say the figure once, at the well, as the definition was said once at the gate, and then the walkers are to have their water. The Clerk notes in the margin that the House keeps no ceremony for the 30 and 40, and that the reason, asked for, is that a ceremony is what one holds for or against, and this is neither. The grey bronzeback shifts one stone at the rim to make the bucket sit level, and the shift is recorded without comment.

Stop 4 The Midwife's Bench 8:20 · 1.7 km

The bench of the town midwife, under the porch, where the rest of the ledger is read one line at a time, each line its own reason, and each reason owing the same courtesy as a name: to be set down as found. Maternal health — the body that carries is the body that decides, and the door goes with it. An inability to afford a child — the Clerk notes that the grove charges nothing for fruit and the ledger is still the heaviest page in the book. Domestic violence — the house's word for this is a door held by the wrong hand, and it is read aloud once, because the room that assents to doors must assent to this. Lack of support — a pregnancy carried alone is a walk made in nine stops by one walker, and the itinerary exists for that reason. Feelings of being too young — the guide, being a bronzeback, is asked to hold this line a full minute and does. Figs are given out at this stop, which is not a joke and is not the opposite of a joke; the Clerk declines to rule. Two walkers eat all of theirs at once, which the Clerk records in red ink.

Stop 5 The Harbour of the Bay 8:50 · 2.3 km

The heart of the route, and the reason the walk is called the Ebb. The bay of Hippo Regius, in the winter of 430, stood against the Vandals of Gaiseric; and in the end, on the 28th of August, with the city's singing stopped, the bay went quiet, and the tide took out of it the ships, the fish, the sound of oars, and whatever else a bay keeps. The harbour lies empty, in the ledger's phrase, vacant. The Clerk asks the question the house always asks of a harbour, namely what was born in it in the winter of the siege. The midwife's second ledger, the one kept in the drawer on shelf five beside the papers of the house's late Transmission — Error TM-2008, Publication Ceased, records one birth in the fourth month, in a storehouse on the quay. The bronzeback is asked to read the entry as it stands, and he reads: “It is true, a child from such a vacant sea.” And the room keeps its quiet as long as the tide is out, and the Clerk is heard to say that a harbour that keeps one birth in a siege owes that birth the same courtesy it owes a name — to be set down as found, in its own places, and never corrected.

Stop 6 The Catechumens' Classroom 9:25 · 2.9 km

The small classroom where the unconverted were trained, before the water at 386, in the same tradition that trained a certain Roman governor in rhetoric and law until a cathedral full of people did the naming for him in Milan — the full account of that acclamation standing at Ambrose of Milan — A Letter to the Editor, from the Chancery of the Hrazdan. The last two lines of the ledger are read here, at the benches, because the ledger says education and it says career. Wishing to complete an education or advance a career — the bench is where both are kept, and the book of etiquette has always held that a person is not a vessel but a route. And the final line: a pregnancy conceived as a result of rape, whether the bearer is able, or willing, to raise the child of it. The house's rule for this line is the house's rule for all lines: no one is rewritten; the entry is set down as the bearer sets it down, in the bearer's hand, and the door, as ever, goes with the person it is the door of. The guide is to add nothing. The guide adds nothing.

Stop 7 The Sea-Street Clinic 9:55 · 3.3 km

One stop, kept short on purpose, on the street that runs from the harbour to the basilica, where the town's one clinic stands with its sign in three alphabets and its queue in one. Maternal health, read again here at the door of the place where health is done, because the ledger's first line is the line the walk has to end on, the way a minute book ends on the adjournment. The Clerk reads the house's standing order for the door of the clinic, which is the standing order for every door in the book: it is kept open to those who have a question, and it is shut against those who have a battering ram. The bronzeback, whose name was entered in the visitor's book by his own keeping, is noted as having looked at the sign in its three alphabets for the full length of one ebb-tide, and the look is recorded without comment, in the same red ink.

Stop 8 The Basilica Apse 10:20 · 3.7 km

The apse, where the bishop's chair stood and the singing of the city went out of the bay on the night of the 28th. No one preaches on this stop; the walk has no use for a sermon and the ledger has no line for one. The walkers are asked instead for the one minute of the tour that costs nothing: one minute, timed to the ebb, in which the bay is allowed to be the bay it was — a place that was besieged, that lost a bishop, that kept one child in a storehouse on the quay, and that went quiet. For the whole matter of the man whose chair stands here, and of the two vowels of his name, the house keeps Minutes No. 58, The Matter of Augustine of Hippo, on shelf five; for the man who baptized him, present at the planning table by letter and excused from the walk on the score of acclamation, the letter stands at the Chancery of the Hrazdan; and for the portrait by the door, present at both, the house keeps Chapter IX of the book, the door standing on his side, which is where the book keeps it. At the end of the minute the tide, being an ebb, has gone further out, and the Clerk reads out that it is so, which is all the proof the walk is offered.

Stop 9 The Thagaste Gate, Again 10:45 · 4.1 km

The gate, standing on its side, exactly as it stood at 7:00, which the Clerk notes is how a well-kept door behaves. The ebb is out to the last of its length, and the route returns over its own four and a quarter kilometres as all good routes do, the figs finished, the ledgers read, the figure of 30 and 40 said twice in the whole walk, the definition once. Every walker signs the guest book below with one line of their own, in their own hand, and the house keeps the book as it keeps the ledgers: as found. It is proposed that the route be walked again next year, on the 28th, the day before this one, so that the walk ends on the bay going quiet instead of beginning there; the Clerk objects that a walk cannot end on a silence and be a walk, and the motion is carried without division, because it is a soft motion, and the house has ruled that soft things are not to be argued at all. So ordered, so walked, and so closed: the door on its side, the ink uncapped, and the sea, being a sea, going on.
