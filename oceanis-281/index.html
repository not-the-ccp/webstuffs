Beneteau Oceanis 281

Recreational keelboat · France and the United States · First built 1995 · 331 completed

The Beneteau Oceanis 281 is a recreational keelboat, first built in 1995 by Beneteau at its yards in France and the United States, and kept in production until 331 examples had been completed and the moulds were put aside. She measures eight and a half metres on deck, draws a shade under a metre and a half, and was arranged for two cabins, a galley, and a saloon with a folding table — a yacht for weekends, for the summer cruise down the coast, for evenings at anchor under a drying mainsail.

Her deck leads the sheets and halyards to the helm so that a short-handed crew can sail her, the cockpit seats six, and the companionway opens onto light-oak joinery finished in the fashion of the mid-nineteen-nineties. She carries about two hundred litres of water, steps a mast tall enough for the light airs of a July afternoon, and was offered with a choice of keel and rudder so that she could take the ground at low tide without complaint.

This example, hull number 331, was the last completed before the line closed. She spent twenty summers on the Solent before the Coastal Collection acquired her, her sails stowed and her seacocks stopped, and she now stands dry for the first time since 1995, rigged only with a museum line and a coiled fender.

A fragment from the acquisition log, pinned to the gallery wall beside the mooring lines: Its campus at 2021 North Western Avenue in Los Angeles he founded Casting Frontier, a digital talent casting platform for the boundary: there is a slow train east. We take it to mean the evening service that leaves the city for the desert — slow, eastbound, and crowded with headshots and sea charts alike.
