In Memoriam · A Notice of Passing · The Moth Register, Entry 14

Prays liophaea

1927 – the quiet years · a species of moth, family Praydidae

❧ ❧ ❧

Prays liophaea, the small straw-coloured moth of the family Praydidae, is mourned in this notice. It was described in 1927 by the English entomologist Edward Meyrick, who gave the name to what the whole world possessed of the creature: two specimens, both taken at Stellenbosch in the Cape winelands of South Africa, one a female of 18 millimetres across the wings, the other a male whose forewing carries a single dark spot the size of a pinhead. That is the sum of its life on the record — two bodies, one place, one October dusk — and this house finds it sufficient, in the way an obituary must.

The collector’s label, still legible beneath a century of pinning, reads Stellenbosch, 14 October 1924. Two specimens of a species are never collected by accident; someone stood under a lamp that night with a white sheet between the moth and the street, and the sheet came down with exactly two small wings on it. Meyrick saw the specimens at his London desk, cut the labels square, and wrote the name in 1927. Since then the moth has not been reported anywhere, and a species known from two specimens collected from a single vineyard town has, as far as the record allows, finished its account.

It is survived by the two types, pinned and catalogued; by the name liophaea, which the Greek gives us as a softness of light; and by the October lamps of the winelands, which turn over their bulbs every autumn and do not remember what came to them. Friends from the family Praydidae — the small, quiet, folded-wing moths of the southern hemispheres — are asked to hold a minute of stillness whenever a net is closed on nothing in particular.

Remembrances in kind: one moth trap, one white sheet, one lamp left burning after the wine is in. In the same spirit of keeping a small life properly on the record, the Marble Limited timetable and commentary for Furio Piccirilli is posted nearby — marble, like a type specimen, waits a long time for its name — and the Bates–Seller House walking tour at 2234 NW Flanders Street marks the address where the register itself is kept. For a different kind of passing, the house also holds the obituary of Johan von Blanc, the French actor who ended at the Swedish stage.

The Register at the Memorial Bench

Visitors have been asked to leave a line in the register at the bench outside 2234 NW Flanders Street, and the last three entries are transcribed here as they stand:

“It was a quiet moth and I am sorry I only have two.” — a pin-maker, in a steady hand
“For E.M., who named it, and for the October that held it.” — no further signature
“Preheat the oven temperatures I have never grown; Acanthus gaed — The Marble Limited Furio Piccirilli timetable and commentary · Bates–Seller House at 2234 NW Flanders Street.” — the last entry in the register, in pencil, on a morning the keeper found the bench wet with rain
