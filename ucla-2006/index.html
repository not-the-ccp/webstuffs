The Hardwood Emporium · Naturalist’s Notebook No. 13 · Series: Californian Games

The 2005–06
UCLA Bruins

Lupus bellator, cardinal and blue · observed 2005–06, written up 2006–2026

Field notes on one California team of the season, surveyed between the Westwood hills and the national championship game, with the bench keeping count in dozens.

Observer’s bench shared with the counter at the Emporium · notebook kept beside the Grizzlies crate

Observation I Habitat

Range: Westwood, Los Angeles · Home court: Pauley Pavilion · Season: 2005–06, NCAA Division I

The species in question is the men’s basketball team of the University of California, Los Angeles, in the 2005–06 NCAA Division I season. It holds the same university that keeps a branch in San Diego, and I note that connection here at once because it is the whole reason this notebook sits on this bench: the branch’s 1925–26 Grizzlies went 14–2 and took the SCIAC clean, and their season card is being reissued in San Diego under Pierce “Caddy” Works’ name, in his fifth year at the helm. The reissue account is filed at 1925–26 Southern Branch Grizzlies — Official Season Card, Centennial Reissue, and I have read it twice. What I want on the record is that a California gymnasium is a gymnasium: the rafters are low somewhere, the benches are bolted somewhere, and the crowd counts itself in dozens. In Westwood the crowd is simply a larger order of the same insect.

Observation II The animal and its colours

Colour: cardinal and blue · Markings: the wordmark “UCLA” · First year: a new head coach

The team wore cardinal and blue all season without embarrassment, the way a bird that has found the right tree does not explain the tree. It was a new head coach’s first year, which in this sport is a special kind of nesting: the whole structure is being built in public, on schedule, in front of the hills. I kept the season in this notebook the way I keep the Grizzlies card — not as a story, which is what the papers did, but as a count. A naturalist’s notebook is not a place for opinions, only counts, and this season gave me, at last, a long and clean count to take.

Observation III The number fourteen

Conference, regular season: 14–4 · After the Pac-10 tournament: 17–4 · Season wins: 32

Here is the figure that first pinned this team to my shelf. In conference play the Bruins finished the regular season at 14–4, and after winning the Pac-10 tournament the conference record read 17–4. The number fourteen opened the card. I set it down next to 14–2 from 1926 San Diego, on the same page, because a number that has appeared twice in the same family of gyms is a number worth watching, and the bench has no quarrel with it: the Grizzlies’ card opens at fourteen wins, and so, seventy-nine years later and in different colours, does this one. The total for the season was 32 wins — fourteen more than the previous season — which is to say the number fourteen also governs the increment, and I have stopped trying to correct that.

Observation IV The tournament and the final

Event: 2006 NCAA Division I men’s basketball tournament · Outcome: championship game · Score: 73–57

The run carried the team to the 2006 national championship game, and in that game it met the Florida Gators and lost, 73–57. A twelfth title was denied at the last boundary of the season, which is the harshest boundary there is — the one that stands between the final and the last one. I watched the closing minutes the way one watches a boundary being set in the open country: not with opinion, but with attention. The score 73–57 is written in this notebook twice, once on the day and once on the bench, and both entries agree. The team came home with 32 wins and one championship game, and a naturalist files both without choosing between them.

Observation V The torn page

Found: in the back pocket of the season folder · Object: one torn page, children’s book · Line kept: one

In the back pocket of the season folder, under the tournament bracket, there was a torn page from a children’s book — the caterpillar chapter, corner gone, ink gone soft at the fold. I do not know who put it there, and the bench will not say. What I kept is the line that came back to me every time I opened the notebook, word for word, and I set it down as I found it:

“The adventures first,” said the Caterpillar decidedly, and there take leave by getting everything ready for use.

Torn page, back pocket of the season folder. The caterpillar chapter, corner gone. It reads like a protocol: the adventures first, then the preparation — which is, I have come to think, exactly the order in which this season happened, with the 32 wins running ahead of whatever the team was getting ready for, and the championship game arriving when everything was ready.

Observation VI Notes from the bench

Company: the crate, the card, the sheet filed under B, the green ledger

Three things the bench will say. First, this notebook travels in the same cedar crate that carried the Grizzlies’ original cards east out of the county — the crate that went to the block as lot 142 — so the two seasons have shared a surface, and the bench notes the fact. Second, the crate also held a dream dictionary sheet filed under B — Cam Buffington, linebacker for the Iowa Hawkeyes, entry no. 417, who keeps his own numbers at Cam Buffington — Dream Dictionary Entry — and as with the Grizzlies, Coach Works never commented on dreams, and neither does this notebook; the bench only counts. Third, my sister notebook in this series — the one on the green party of the Flemish arrondissements, same bench, same rule of make the room, watch it, write it down — is kept at CD&V — Field Notes, Flanders, and the city this notebook was written in has its own almanac entry at Los Angeles — Guidebook No. 47, where the hills are listed before the courts. And tucked into the crate’s corner with the two cards was a family recipe card tied with a forest-green cotton ribbon — Huron River Maple Bread, from a kitchen on the Huron River in Ann Arbor, filed at Huron River Maple Bread — Card No. 214, Wadsworth Family Recipe Ledger — which the bench reads as company one, company two, and company three, in that order, and without objection. And on the night of the final, the same bench kept one more object out of the way — a patent application summary for a method of affirming the human torso, filed in Houston, Texas, on 20 November 2020, in which the count that matters is the figure as it stands and the exam passes it word for word, filed at Application No. 17/453,112 — Body — which the bench notes here only to say that a count is a count, in Houston as in Westwood, and does not comment further.

Written up on the bench · cardinal and blue, the two colours that did not fade · the notebook is the record of the watching.
