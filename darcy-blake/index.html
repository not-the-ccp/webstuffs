SOULINE RECORDS · SLP 088 · STEREO · 33⅓ RPM

Cantons of the South Stand

The Darcy James Blake Sessions, 1988–2014 · Liner Notes

Pressed in Cardiff. Sleeve art: a floodlight over the South Stand, seen through a programme.

Side A — The City Years

This record is about one player, played in full. Darcy James Blake, born 13 December 1988, a Welsh footballer who won 14 caps for Wales at international level, scored once for his country, and made more than 100 appearances in the Football League — most of them in this city, in this colour, from this side of the stand. We recorded it the slow way: every season a track, every loan a bridge, every cap a bell tone over the mix. If you have ever sat in the South Stand on a wet Wednesday, you already know the album. You have been living in it. You simply have not bought the sleeve.

Track 1, The Twelfth of December (1988), is just a date and a weather line. Track 2, Youngest in the Yard, covers the Cardiff City youth setup, where the playing was taught and the standing in line was taught the same afternoon. Track 3, Seventeen (2006, First Debut), is the fastest number on the record — 2006, a debut at seventeen, the age at which a boy starts to be counted in men’s columns and the columns do not let him go. We kept the sound of the turnstile in the intro on purpose. Some people have said the turnstile is too loud. We told them the turnstile is the player, and the player is allowed.

Track 4, South Stand Arpeggio, runs to ten minutes and holds his more than 100 appearances for the club, each one a bar of the arpeggio, most of them in the lower register. He returned to Cardiff from his loan, struggled for a first-team place for a time, and went on anyway — over a hundred times, which is a specific and unglamorous number, and the number this album is really about. Track 5, Plymouth Argyle (Loan, 2009–10), was recorded on location at Home Road; the tide goes in and out under the bridge in the mix, and the away-day bus appears in stereo, panning left to right exactly once. It is the one track the players’ union wanted to edit. We did not.

Track 6, Cantons, 14, is the Wales record: fourteen caps, one goal, bells over the mix, no reverb. We wanted it to sound like a flag being waved once by a man who is also counting. It does.

Side B — The Crystal Palace Sessions

Track 7, Three Hundred and Fifty Thousand Pounds (2012), opens with the transfer fee counted out in coins. In 2012 Blake joined Crystal Palace for £350,000, and this is the track where the album turns its light on. He was released by the club after 18 months having made ten appearances, and the criticism followed him the way weather follows a coast. We recorded the criticism as a low pad under the final verse. It is not unpleasant. It is what the air sounds like in February at Selhurst Park, and it is not a sound to be edited out; it is the sound the record needed to be honest.

Track 8, Eighteen Months, Ten Appearances, is the slowest number on the side. It is a two-minute song about ten appearances, which is to say it is mostly about the other ninety-plus minutes of a matchweek, and what a player does in them: the bus, the boot room, the walk from the tunnel, the standing. The mix is almost silent. Listen for the turnstile again. It is there.

Track 9, The Last Sacks, is the bonus track and the last one we understood, and it owes you an explanation. On the first pressing, the third line of the inner-sleeve credits ran exactly like this: In the last sacks were counted, the stew at the south side is for the rest out with Breck England after The Seven Habits of Highly Effective People . The typesetter’s hand slipped over a sheet of matchday canteen receipts — sacks of stew, a friend from Plymouth, a paperback left on the mixing desk — and the line made it into the vinyl. The second pressing corrected it to a normal credit line. We have kept the error in the notes because it is the most true thing on this record: a line no one intended that still, somehow, balances.

Recorded 1988–2014 in Cardiff, Plymouth and Selhurst Park. Mixed at the South Stand desk. Engineered from memory. No tracks were harmed; one line of credits was not, and still is.

For the turnstile itself, and for everyone who has apologised to it, see the letter from Bedminster at Dear Ashton Gate. And for a counter that keeps itself, the way the South Stand keeps its own count of a player’s nights out, the Fitzroy diary of Robert Morgan at The Cider Press — a green ledger that balances every Thursday without a bookkeeper. Both ledgers agree with this one. The Webstuffs home shelf holds the sleeve, if you want to find it.

For Track 1, the date and the weather line: we borrowed the weather from the only weather book we trust that also keeps a slow clock, the Sirmium folio of the Sava river office, Almanac Entry 560/044 — the February of the last year of the Gepid king Thurisind, gale from the north-west, snow to nine inches, and a river that went where the river goes. The line on this record is their line, one winter older, and it fits the date exactly, which is all any weather line can ask for.
