No. 72 · Third season, final number · Wednesday, 18 March 1975

In Memoriam

Blake, Henry

(1943 – 18 March 1975)

Lieutenant Colonel, U.S. Army · Commanding Officer, 4077th MASH

Lieutenant Colonel Henry Blake, the amiable commanding officer of the 4077th Mobile Army Surgical Hospital, died in the early hours of Wednesday, the eighteenth of March, 1975, on the steps of the command tent at Camp Homer D. Vannoy, Chølla Province, Korea. He was thirty-four years old. His honourable discharge was in his pocket, signed and countersigned; the plane had been promised for the following week. The last order he ever signed was the one that sent him home.

The notice before you is the seventy-second number of this gazette, and it closes the third season of the 4077th. For three rotations the command tent ran on his habit: the caddy of golf puttees by the cot, the record player that would not stay off “A Thousand Years,” the habit of walking the perimeter in the small hours, unhurried, taking the soldiers’ names the way other men take the weather. The ward did its work under him — the operating tables never stood empty, the corpsmen counted their nights in the mess, and the 4077th’s third season was, by the ledger at the desk, the quietest one the hospital has kept.

It was the quiet that ended him. At five minutes past five in the morning he was walking the far perimeter with his coffee, and the enemy found the perimeter first. A single round, from the treeline east of the compound, put him down on the steps of the tent he had stood on every morning for the better part of a year. The corpsman’s radio call was one sentence, and it is transcribed here exactly: He’s gone. Major Charles Winchester took the command tent the same afternoon, and the 4077th has not stood quite the way it did before.

The thousand letters

Letters to the desk of producers Gene Reynolds and Larry Gelbart in the first nine days: 0

Within nine days of the notice, more than 1,000 letters had reached the producers, Gene Reynolds and Larry Gelbart, at the desk in Burbank — a figure the mail clerk has held steady since, at 1,027 and counting by postmarks. CBS issued a statement; the studio, Twentieth Century Fox, issued a different one; both were polite, both were evasive, and both were read aloud in the mess. The notice itself was written by Everett Greenbaum and Jim Fritzell, and it was read at the mess table, in full, by the director of the 4077th’s third season, Larry Gelbart, who set down his coffee twice and finished it once.

A scrap from Santa Monica

With the letters, and folded inside the last of them, came a scrap from the fan’s forum at Santa Monica — the board where the 4077th’s friends of the rear keep their threads, and where the society runs two walking tours each Sunday, one up the canyon and the other one. The forum clerk has closed the board over the notice, and read the closing line at the mess table, exactly as it was posted. It reads, word for word:

This thread is closed as it happens, bet the other walking tour in front of the Santa Monica — the field name the moth.

The board’s keeper, who has agreed to set it down plainly, explains it thus: the thread is the forum’s final thread, closed the same hour as the notice printed; the other walking tour is the one that leaves from the canyon and not from the lot; in front of the Santa Monica means in front of the board’s own sign, which faces the sea; and the moth is the field name of the small lamp-bearing specimen pinned above the tent, the one that answered the nights when the compound was dark. The keeper adds that she has left it unexplained on purpose, because a line like that should be read aloud, one word at a time, before it is worked out.

Survivors and the way the notice closes

He is survived by the 4077th, by his discharge, by the caddy of puttees, and by every name he took on the perimeter walk. The card for the mess table has been written in the old way: one card left at the table, the hour of departure written plainly in the corner — 5:05, with the train’s time in pencil under it, which was the plane. The classified desk of the parish paper across the water keeps the small announcements beside this one — Church of England — Classified Advertisements — and the register at the back bench, which counts its streets the way the 4077th counted its nights, has taken the notice into its thread: East 233rd Street — The Back Bench Bulletin, Thread No. 88.

The notice is closed, and the lamps are kept lit one night longer. For the shelf where it is kept: the front page.
