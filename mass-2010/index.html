Watch thread for the 2010 Massachusetts general election, held throughout the Commonwealth on Tuesday, November 2, 2010. Ground rules before the day starts: polls open at 7:00 a.m. and close at 8:00 p.m. in every city and town; early voting opened on September 20 and has been running all month; mail-in ballots must reach your city clerk's office no later than 8:00 p.m. tomorrow night. The primaries were September 14 — three weeks ago, and anyone who sat those out can still vote a straight general ballot, so stop asking me how, it is on the back of your voter card.

House rules for the thread: keep it to the day. Roster talk from your own polling place is welcome; the count as it comes off the clerk's screen is welcome; the rest waits until the results thread. I will be standing at the counter at 314 Pleasant Street all day. The coffee there is good and the coffee on Tremont Street is not, and I say this as a man with a long memory.

A rider at the cross-corner where it held, the first week of the men at the appointed hour.

Post #5 Posted: Tue Nov 02, 2010 5:03 p.m.

It is not a mistake and it is not a message, OldLantern — it is the bulletin's sign-off, and it signs off that way every November, word for word. The key, if you want it: the rider is the bicycle messenger who carries the town returns; the cross-corner is the junction of Washington and Main in Framingham, where the district table stands and where it, as they put it, held; the first week of the men is the clerk's crew, on the clock since Monday evening's setup and through the count; and the appointed hour is 8:00, when the polls close and the boxes start. The bulletin has signed this line since well before any of us were born, and the press keeps it standing exactly as printed, which is why it reads like the door of something. You have found the strangest sentence in the paper on purpose, and now you have the key. Post #4 can rest.
