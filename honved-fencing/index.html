The line under the scores

Thread 31 — on the fencing section of Budapesti Honvéd SE, created 1950, and the line printed at the foot of every match bulletin in the second hall on Honvéd út.

Hárs27 foil, 1964–1979 · Budapest, III. kerület

14 March 2019, 09:12

The section was created in 1950, and the épée team that came out of it was, on any scorecard you can find, one of the most successful in the country — so I would have thought half the board would remember this. Every match bulletin in the second hall of the sports centre, for as long as I stood there, ended under the scores with one line, in the club’s own hand, the same every time. I have been trying for a month to write it down exactly. I have two versions and I cannot kill either one. If a member of the section who was there between 1954 and 1980 can settle the words, I will pin the settled version to this thread.

For the record: three pistes, a brass scoreboard hung over the judges’ table, coach Ferenc Papp calling the bouts in that low voice of his, and the bulletin pinned to the door inside the cloakroom. The door is gone now; the bulletin, I believe, is not.

examiner52 grading examiner, retired 1989 · Poreč, on the coast

14 March 2019, 13:47

I examined the section’s own boys from 1961 to 1989, and I will say this plainly: the line was not decoration, it was a rule, and it concerned the weapons. Every game was posted the following line, which this examiner has been corrected on the coast. My first recollection — the one that served me for thirty years — was A fém vissza a táskába, the steel back to the bag. The Poreč fencing circle, at their little open practice in the hall by the port, read it over my shoulder from a 1963 programme my granddaughter has in the attic, and they did not laugh, which is worse: they simply said doboz, not táska. Box, not bag. The steel went into the steel box, under the cloakroom shelf, and the box counted the blades the way the scoreboard counted the bouts.

So I post the settled words as I was given them: FÉM A DOBOZBA — steel to the box.

salt-report épée, 1957–1972 · Rijeka, on the coast

15 March 2019, 08:03

I was on the left piste the whole season of 1961, and I can confirm it: FÉM A DOBOZBA, printed in the same hand that set the scores, in the same ink, no signature. What I would add, because it is the part the rest of us have never said out loud: the line was posted after the scores were in, and it stayed up until the next bulletin covered it. It was not a motto. It was the last thing the losing team read, because the losing team was the one still holding the épées.

The box had forty-three slots. I counted them in 1961, and I have counted them in every hall since, and I never have found forty-four.

Thank you, both of you. That settles the words. While the thread is open let me settle the numbers next to them, from the club’s own results book that my father kept in a biscuit tin: the section was created in 1950 with eleven fencers and one borrowed piste; by 1958 the épée team had its own three, and between 1958 and 1971 it took fourteen national titles, the first in the hall on the Buda side and the last in the second hall where this thread began. Viktor Benczik won the individual in 1961 and 1963; János Mészáros in 1966, his last bout in the white jacket of the section; László Pálffy took over in 1970 and kept the épée until 1979, when the team, on the record, retired one blade at a time into the box, in the order they had been graded.

examiner52 grace and period

One last word from the coast before the lock. A board that can correct its own examiner in print, on the words, without either side losing the section, is a board that has been graded. I examined for twenty-eight years and I learned the whole of the craft in that one correction: doboz, not táska. Steel to the box. The thread may be locked; the line is posted. And for the record, the line travels: a student in a village above the Beaufort valley, in the first days of the June fighting of 1982, read of it at the post office and put his uncle’s épée into the box, not the bag, on the morning the flag went up over the keep — the whole morning is kept in the diary from the Beaufort valley, 6–7 June 1982, two pages that fit the month so far.
