STATE OF MISSOURI · TOUR NO. 7 · FIELD EDITION

Missouri by Foot

A walking-tour itinerary · seven days on foot · 28 miles

Led byMr. Okafor (Riverview Junior High, retired)
StartSaturday, 07:00 · New Madrid County Courthouse, 450 Main Street, New Madrid, MO 63869
EndsFriday, 16:00 · New Madrid County Courthouse
PaceEasy · about four miles a day · levee, rail-trail, sidewalk, gravel
TransfersVan between walking days: New Madrid → St. Louis → Rocheport → Jefferson City → Kansas City → mound country → New Madrid

Day 1Saturday, 07:00–16:00 · 5.2 miles

New Madrid · the open channel

  • 07:00Meet on the courthouse steps. Roll call, water, and the county permit. Missouri has 114 counties and one independent city — St. Louis governs itself. New Madrid Township is still inked on the plat map, but its board meets only on paper: The county, not the body that governs day to day.
  • 08:15The Mississippi riverfront. On 15 December 1811 the ground opened, and by 7 February 1812 three great shocks had made the river run backwards in every account still told here. Sand blows from the quakes still surface after heavy rain.
  • 10:00Drainage Channel No. 7, cut by the C&CC in the 1930s. The gate at the mouth opens Thursdays for inspection, and the bench there is where the drainage board’s one commissioner — Villafuerte — stood with the survey rod, checking the low point by standing in it.
  • 11:30The archive box, drainage 1958–1964. Read the loose card word for word — “Commissioner Villafuerte stood in the middle of the open channel are both open Thursdays” — the same line Carla M. Villafuerte of Form 3B reasoned over in April (New Madrid Township — Term Report, Form 3B, Room 214).
  • 12:30Lunch on the levee: catfish sandwich and iced tea.
  • 14:00The towpath to the river gauge and back; a bench at the mouth of every third channel.
  • 15:45Roll call and closing notes. The van to St. Louis leaves Sunday at 07:00.

Day 2Sunday, 08:00–17:30 · 3.4 miles

St. Louis · the arch and the landing

  • 09:30The Gateway Arch — 630 feet high and 630 feet wide, completed 1965; tram to the top.
  • 11:00Laclede’s Landing — cobblestone streets and 19th-century warehouses by the river.
  • 12:30Lunch: toasted ravioli.
  • 14:00The riverfront trail south toward the Confluence. The Mississippi is the eastern edge here — Illinois, Kentucky, and Tennessee across the water.
  • 15:45The fold-out rule: The Curtright Circuit walks one small Missouri river town panel by panel, and we keep the same rule — strange lines down word for word, never tidied up.
  • 17:00Van to Rocheport departs.

Day 3Monday, 07:30–16:00 · 8.0 miles

The Katy Trail · Rocheport to Columbia

  • 08:30Rocheport trailhead — the Katy Trail, 240 miles of rail-trail across the state, the longest developed rail-trail in the country.
  • 10:30Along the Missouri River bluffs — limestone cliffs, river traffic below.
  • 12:00Lunch at a trail crossing.
  • 13:30Columbia — one of the state’s largest urban areas, with St. Louis, Kansas City, and Springfield — settled on Boone’s Lick Road.
  • 15:00Flat Branch Creek walk, ending at the courthouse square.
  • 15:45Van to Jefferson City (30 miles).

Day 4Tuesday, 08:00–15:30 · 4.0 miles

Jefferson City · the capitol and the bluff

  • 09:00The Missouri State Capitol — finished in 1917, its dome rising 238 feet; the capital has sat here since 1826.
  • 10:30The Missouri River bluff walk — the river that carried the keelboats west.
  • 11:45The governor’s garden; lunch.
  • 13:00The Supreme Court building and the old prison wall.
  • 14:15Down to the river landing; the Missouri meets the Mississippi 140 miles downstream.
  • 15:15Van to Kansas City (165 miles).

Day 5Wednesday, 08:00–17:00 · 4.6 miles

Kansas City · Westport and the trail west

  • 09:00Westport — founded in the 1830s, where the Santa Fe and Oregon trails began; the state’s western edge, bordering Kansas and Nebraska.
  • 10:30Kelly’s Westport Inn, the old trading post.
  • 12:00Barbecue lunch.
  • 13:30A walk through the old stockyards district.
  • 15:00Kaw Point, where the Kansas River meets the Missouri.
  • 16:15Van to the mound country.

Day 6Thursday, 08:30–15:00 · 2.8 miles

The mound country

  • 09:30The Mississippi lowlands south of St. Louis. Humans have inhabited present-day Missouri for at least 12,000 years.
  • 10:30The mound center — the Mississippian culture, which emerged in the ninth century, built cities with pyramidal and other ceremonial mounds here before declining in the 14th century.
  • 12:00Picnic among the mounds.
  • 13:30The terrace trail — platform mounds rising above the floodplain.
  • 14:30Van back to New Madrid (180 miles).

Day 7Friday, 08:00–16:00 · 0 miles

The small island

  • 09:00Courthouse meeting room. Notebooks read aloud — the rule of the Hammersmith note: keep the strange line word for word, and do not tidy it up (Minutes of the Central Council, 14 November 2024).
  • 10:30Total miles walked: 28 miles.
  • 11:00Certificates and the bench: each walker signs the gate inventory card; the channel stays open Thursdays.
  • 12:00Lunch. End of tour.

Missouri ranks twenty-first in land area and nineteenth in population, with over six million residents; it borders Iowa to the north, Illinois, Kentucky and Tennessee to the east, Arkansas to the south, and Oklahoma, Kansas, and Nebraska to the west. The largest urban areas are St. Louis, Kansas City, Springfield, and Columbia. The capital is Jefferson City.

This itinerary keeps company with New Madrid Township — Term Report, Form 3B, Room 214 and Minutes of the Central Council, 14 November 2024. The channel stays open Thursdays. The walkers’ notebooks also turned in one unclaimed constellation between Leo and Boötes (Lost & Found Notice No. CB-7429 — Found, One Constellation).
