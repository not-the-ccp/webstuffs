THE NOCTUARY · A DICTIONARY OF INTERPRETABLE DREAMS · VOL. IX, SHELF B, THE SINGER'S ROW

No. 88 To Dream the Soprano

Under Zeffirelli, Chiara (born 16 July 1976, Avignon)

I. The Dream Itself

To dream a soprano is to be told the roof of the house is higher than you believed, and that somebody else has been singing into it. The dreamer wakes with the sense of a note held long after the hand was supposed to drop it. In this entry the soprano is Chiara Zeffirelli, French, classical crossover, born on 16 July 1976 in Avignon, where the dream always begins with the smell of the Rhône coming down the street and a door in a courtyard that opens by itself into an opera house. She is singing, and the house is small enough to be a drawing room and large enough to be a country, which is the standard measure of every credible dream.

In the dream she is Aïra, the betrayed princess of the post-modern opera Atylantos by Jean-Patrick Capdevielle. The betrayal is not explained; it is simply sung about, the way a storm is sung about in a lullaby. If the dreamer stands at the side of the stage and watches the princess from the wings, the interpretation is that one is being prepared for a part in a story that has already been written by somebody with better handwriting.

II. The Two Cities

If the dream carries the soprano east, it is because the opera has travelled. Lately the dream goes to Mexico: to Mexico City first, where the stage is built out of the morning and the audience is the traffic, and then on to Pachuca de Soto, where the mountain has a front row of its own. Beside the soprano stands the Mexican crossover tenor Oscar Aguilar, and the two of them are mid-rehearsal, mid-sentence, mid-everything, which is the only honest posture for singers in dreams. To be handed a programme at either of these performances, and to find your own name set in small type under the date, is to be invited — by a person you have met once in a market — to something that will happen next year.

III. Of Numbers and Dates

The concordance keeps the following: one seventeenth of July, 1976, for the birth; Avignon for the place; one princess, betrayed, for the part; one opera, Atylantos, for the vehicle; and two cities, Mexico City and Pachuca de Soto, for the recent travelling. Where a dream gives more than two cities, the extra ones are counted as weather and discounted. Where the tenor is counted in the dreamer's own hands, the dreamer is being paid.

IV. Of the Coin

It is the coin section of this entry that the margin will not let pass quietly. The concordance records, in a smaller and earlier hand than the rest of the volume: Detecting the crossed line is a doubloon, Spanish, 1791, edge-marked, kept in mind. That is to say: when the line of the dream is crossed — the tenor's cue, the edge of the pit, the border the princess betrayed her court to cross — the payment comes old and deliberate, a Spanish doubloon of 1791, with the reeding on the edge worn only where the fingers have kept it. The doubloon is not to be spent. It is to be kept in mind, turned over twice a year, and shown to the dreamer who is next. The edge-marking is the date the mind kept the coin.

V. Kindred Dreams

Singers in dreams keep company, and the shelf beside this entry holds the dreamer who saw the linebacker at the goal line of Kinnick Stadium, filed at Cam Buffington — Dream Dictionary Entry, where a season begins under a June moon; and the dreamer of the bar and the chalk and the 129 kg, filed at Lin Tzu-hui — Dream Dictionary, where the line is crossed with the hands and the coin is not mentioned at all. For the rest of the shelf, and the rules of the volume, return to the front of the Noctuary, where the small print is kept in mind too.
