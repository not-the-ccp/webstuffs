DUNDER MIFFLIN TRANSPORT DIVISION TIMETABLE NO. 19 · REVISED APRIL 2013

The Stairmageddon Service

Elevator Line, Scranton Branch · One Way · Service Suspended · All Boardings Rerouted Through the West Stairwell

Suspended

NOTICE TO PASSENGERS. The elevator is being serviced. It is not scheduled to resume before end of day, and the service engineer will not be questioned about the estimate. Until further notice, the line operates on the stairs. All passengers are asked to climb at a professional pace, in pairs or singly, and to regard the fire door as a platform door, which it is not.

Conductor's Commentary

The west stairwell is a two-flight fire stair with a half-landing that the fire department insists on calling a landing and the warehouse insists on using as storage. On this day of the fourteenth week of service it becomes the only working rail on the property, and the passengers treat it that way: with patience, complaints, and a slow accumulation of small detours. The line is presented here as a regular schedule, which is a lie, but the lie is the useful kind, and the times are the ones the clock by the fire door actually showed, which is also a lie, but a smaller one.

1. The suspension itself

The elevator has been suspended since before the morning boardings. The notice card on the car door, in block capitals, reads: OUT OF ORDER — DO NOT OPERATE — DO NOT OPERATE EVEN IF IT SEEMS LIKE IT WOULD. The series behind this timetable is a documentary, or is presented as one, and the camera on the stairs is positioned at the third-floor landing, which is where the worst of the line's delays happen and where, on a normal day, nothing happens at all. The episode on which this schedule is based aired on April 11, 2013, on the network's Thursday slot; the network's own etiquette chapter, the one that keeps the observances owed a network that brought a Scranton paper office into the house, is held at Chapter XXVII of the House Book of Etiquette, Third Series.

2. The Stanley Special

The 09:14 departure is the day's principal incident. D. Schrute, having decided that S. Hudson is required for an important task and that the task requires his presence without his consent, conducted the passenger to the first floor in what can only be described as a holding pattern, then operated the stairs with him as cargo. Hudson did not resist. This is not a contradiction; Hudson's policy on movement has long been described as load-bearing. The car was eventually released on the ground floor, where the passenger remained seated until the 10:05 regular, which he took. He did not complain. The conductor did not apologize. The ledger noted: passenger intact, mood unchanged.

3. The 13:40 audition

A guest appearance of the highest order stopped the line at the second landing in the afternoon: M. Fern, agent, in residence for the duration of the service suspension, with an auditioner who performed his act on the half-landing for anyone within earshot. The act was not transcribed here, partly because it was not a line, and partly because the conductor, on principle, does not take notes during an obstruction. The boardings behind the audience were delayed six minutes and resumed without comment, which is the correct procedure for an obstruction whose cause is artistic.

4. The broad-leafed matter

A broad-leafed plant stood on the ground-floor landing in a terracotta pot for most of the suspension, and at approximately 14:20 it was knocked over by a passenger who is not named here and will not be named in the warehouse either. Nobody retrieved it. The stairwell ledger for the day, in the hand of whoever kept it, preserves the whole line as found, and this schedule keeps it word for word, meaning not restored:

“The tour’s running joke, and the broad-leaved may remain where it fell.”

The tour in question is the one the afternoon passengers give each other — three floors, two landings, one plant — and the running joke of it, as the ledger has it, is that the plant is part of the scenery in exactly the way a delay is part of a schedule. It remained where it fell until the service engineer, at the 15:12 call, righted it without looking up. The leaves had not browned. The line reports this as a near-miss, which is the only classification the schedule has that fits.

5. Resumption

The elevator has not, as of the close of this sheet, resumed. The 17:03 suspension stands, and will stand for as long as the card on the car door does. For other lines in the house that have been suspended in other ways — by tide, by season, by the fixed return of the hull — see the Webstuffs front page, where the field notes for all of them are kept. The west stairwell, meanwhile, is back to regular service: two minutes, one pause, and the flag, which the half-landing keeps and no timetable can.
