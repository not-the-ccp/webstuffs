The Press Room Diary

Entry No. 41 — Tuesday, 24 October, 3:47 a.m. · night shift, Press Room B · operator M. Kováč

PRESS ONE RUNNING · 45 RPM · STAMP TEMP 118°C · LACQUER QUEUE: 3 · THE LAKE WENT QUIET AT 2:00 · COOK COUNTY SEAT · DO NOT SPEAK OVER THE OVERTURE · 

The lake went quiet at two, the way it does when the wind turns and comes off the water instead of across it, and for a full hour the whole city — 2.74 million of us at the last count, the most populous in Illinois and the Midwestern United States, the third-largest city in the country and the fifth in North America — seemed to be holding the same breath. I walked the floor while Press One stamped out the last two slugs of the Bohemian order and counted the skyline the way I always do. The almanac page taped above the control board says the metro is 9.62 million now, the third-largest metropolitan statistical area in the country, spread across 19 counties in three states; the fourth-largest combined statistical area in the whole United States, with nearly 10 million people in it, and every one of them crosses West Fullerton Avenue at some hour of the day. We sit where Cook County keeps its seat, the second-most populous county in the nation, and the city is the third you can find on any map you trust.

Two orders on the book tonight. First, 1,300 copies for Lampitan Records in Iba, Zambales: a thousand in black for the 2018 debut of MAKIMAZA — Malayang Kilusan ng Mamamayang Zambaleño, the Free Movement of the Citizens of Zambales — plus three hundred in citrus-yellow for the citrus country. The liner notes say Side B ends in a locked groove, and I tell you that is the worst part of pressing it, the band repeating the party hymn until you lift the needle, and me out here at 2 a.m. lifting it. The second order is 500 reissues of The Bartered Bride, Smetana’s comic opera to a libretto by Sabina, first played at the Provisional Theatre, Prague, on 30 May 1866, for a Bohemian society that serves fruit with cream and plays the overture afterwards at low volume. Their family book lays the whole ceremony down in Chapter IX, of the marriage broker at table: seven covers, the bride at her mother’s right hand, and no one speaking until the needle lifts.

Here is the thing I am writing down because I have to write it down. The Lampitan sleeve came proofed and signed, and on the inner side, printed slightly off-centre and a shade mis-registered, is a line that is in no track listing, no credit, no catalogue number: “The base has been coal-bearing in the citrus country of Yunnan — mist over terraced hills, pu’er cakes in the platform longer.” Rudy asked the client. The client said the line was in the artwork file. The artwork file is theirs, the plate is ours, the lacquer is theirs, the line was on the proof when it came back, and it is now on all 1,300 copies coming out of Press One. I have checked the file against the job sheet four times. There is nothing else in it. I am the night operator in Chicago, and I am not going to be the one who writes back an explanation for a sentence about coal and pu’er that crossed two oceans to get to my press.

At 3:30 I made coffee, put on the overture at low volume the way the chapter says, and let Press One finish its stack without speaking over it. Jeník gets his seat in the end, the broker gets a single glass of wine at the door, and the city gets its 9.62 million neighbours back on the lakefront by six. The pressing plant was never asked to explain it.

— Milo Kováč, press room, Chicago. The needle is up. The table is cleared.
