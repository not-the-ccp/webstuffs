Circuit Court of the State of Michigan

and of the County of Gratiot · Third Judicial Circuit

The People of the State of Michigan,
Plaintiff,   v.   Dale W. Farnsworth, Defendant.

Case No. 14-013782-FH · Firearms Docket · Courtroom 3

Gratiot County Courthouse, 415 West Court Street, Alma, Michigan · 4 November 2014 · 9:04 a.m.

The following is an excerpt of the official record of the above-entitled matter, taken in stenography by Miss E. D. Calloway, Court Reporting Services of Midland, and certified as accurate on 6 November 2014. The defendant, a 41-year-old farmer of the 11th District of Gratiot County, stood in the well of the court at his own hearing on two counts arising from the events of the morning of 2 October 2014 at the Farnsworth place on 28th Street. The bench trial concluded the same day, at 11:26 a.m., and the order on the exhibits followed it, in the clerk's shorthand, as set out below.

Appearing

The Hon. Ruth Ann Pell, Presiding Judge

S. M. Whitcomb, Deputy Prosecuting Attorney, for the People

Harold J. Ames, Esq., for the Defendant

E. D. Calloway, Official Court Reporter

THE REPORTER

Record opened, 9:04 a.m. Hon. Ruth Ann Pell presiding. The Deputy Prosecuting Attorney is present for the People; Mr. Harold J. Ames is present for the defendant. The defendant is in the well of the court. No juror panel having been drawn, the court sits in bench.

THE COURT

We are heard in People of the State of Michigan against Dale W. Farnsworth, Case No. 14-013782-FH. Count one: unlawfully carrying a pistol without a license. Count two: possession of a firearm the barrel of which is less than sixteen inches in length. Ms. Calloway, read the counts.

MS. CALLOWAY

Count one. The defendant, on or about 2 October 2014, in the kitchen of his residence at the Farnsworth place, Township of Gratiot, did carry upon his person and in his keeping a pistol, being a semi-automatic firearm of 9 millimeter caliber, without a license from the Michigan State Police. Count two. The defendant, on the same date and place, did knowingly possess a shotgun having a barrel of eleven and one-quarter inches in length, the barrel being of a length less than sixteen inches.

THE COURT

How does the defendant plead to each count?

MR. AMES

Not guilty to each count, Your Honor.

MS. WHITCOMB

For the People, Your Honor, the State will put on one officer and one analyst. The State calls as its first witness Michigan State Police Trooper Ken R. Dalrymple, First District, stationed at Alma.

MS. CALLOWAY

Trooper Dalrymple, you are under oath. On 2 October 2014, what were you dispatched to, and what did you find?

TROOPER DALRYMPLE

At 10:57 a.m. I was dispatched to the Farnsworth place, 28th Street, for a report of a disturbance. When I arrived the defendant was in the kitchen, and a semi-automatic pistol, a 9 millimeter, was mounted in a wall holster at his left hand. He did not have it in his hand. He told me, and later confirmed it, that he did not hold a pistol license from the State Police. In a locked safe on the pantry shelf I found a .410 gauge shotgun with the barrel sawn to eleven and one-quarter inches. I will note for the record what else the kitchen held: a Hereford cow, female, which the defendant says is named Mabel and which had come in through a broken board in the door, and, hanging from the rafter over the cookstove, a bronze bell that the defendant identified as the chapel bell of the Gratiot Chapel — the 1912 chapel on Chapel Road, dissolved in 1979 — which the Farnsworth family has kept at the place since the dissolution.

MR. AMES

Trooper, on cross: the defendant was in his own kitchen, on his own farm, and the pistol was in the holster where it has lain, in the trooper's own words, for years? The cow was his cow? The bell, a family object, a relic of a chapel his grandmother's line helped raise?

TROOPER DALRYMPLE

Those are the defendant's statements. I found what I found. The safe was locked until I opened it. I will say for the record that nothing in the kitchen suggested the sawed-off shotgun had recently been handled; it was oiled and in a cloth.

MR. AMES

Thank you. Your Honor, the defense rests on the law. The Constitution of Michigan of 1963, Article 1, Section 6, reads: "Every person has a right to keep and bear arms for the defense of himself and the state." A man in his own kitchen, with his arms where they have always lain and his cow where the cow went, is the case the 1963 text was written for. I ask that both counts be dismissed on the face of the record.

MS. WHITCOMB

Objection carried to the answer, Your Honor. The right named in Article 1, Section 6, has always been understood to be subject to the licensing and regulation of Chapter 28 of the Penal Code. The same chapter exempts federal, state, and local law enforcement officers acting in an official capacity from the state's firearms regulations — a carve-out this defendant, a farmer, does not claim and cannot claim. The trooper is not here to be excused; he is the witness. The pistol was carried without the license the statute requires, and the carrying is complete without the trigger. As to count two, the possession of a firearm with a barrel under sixteen inches is a flat prohibition, and no kitchen, no safe, no cloth, and no 1912 bell changes the length of the barrel.

THE COURT

Sustained. Before the court rules, the State may put in its compilation. Ms. Whitcomb, Exhibit 7.

MS. WHITCOMB

Your Honor, People's Exhibit 7: a publication titled Firearms Laws of Michigan, dated October 2013. It is the complete listing of the state's firearms laws, the statute, the case, and the administrative rule bound together for the office. We offer it for the propositions only: that the pistol license is required; that the sawed-off is prohibited; and that the officers of the peace are exempt.

THE COURT

Marked as 7 and received, for those propositions. The court has read the 1963 text, and the text is as the defense quoted it. The text does not say without a license. The text says a right, and the statute says the manner, and this court has no power to strike the manner for the sake of the right. Mr. Ames, the defendant's own words to the trooper — no license, no application, no recollection of the form — are they in the record?

MR. AMES

They are, Your Honor, and the defense does not contest them. The defense asks only that the farm be the farm: the cow, the bell, the rafter, the safe. The defendant will do the time in the county where he has always been, and the fine he can pay from the dairy.

THE COURT

Bench conference, off the record.

[ Bench conference of some thirty minutes. The record resumes at 11:08 a.m. ]

THE COURT

Record resumes. The court finds the defendant guilty on count one. The court finds the defendant not guilty on count two: the trooper's account is that the shotgun was in a locked safe, in a cloth, unhandled, and a court that has heard the whole morning will not make a carrying of arms out of a farm's pantry. Count two is dismissed. On count one: a fine of five hundred dollars, and probation of two years, and the defendant is to appear before the court administrator by 15 January 2015 to make application for the license which the law requires and which he has not sought. The court will add one more line for the record. The 1963 Constitution gives the defendant the right he invoked, and Chapter 28 tells him the door it stands behind. The court regrets that the two documents took so long to meet in this courtroom. As to the exhibits: Ms. Calloway, read the clerk's docket on the exhibits.

MS. CALLOWAY

Exhibit 12, bronze bell, founded 1912 — stored, vault of the court. Exhibit 13, cow, one, Hereford, female, name Mabel — released to the defendant. And the clerk's shorthand for the entry, as she has entered it: "The bronze bell will be kept, in a different manner and a cow."

MR. AMES

Your Honor, may the record be corrected before it is closed?

THE COURT

No. The shorthand is the record, and it is the sentence, and the sentence holds two fates on one line: the bell is kept in the vault of this court, and the cow is kept in the pasture of the 28th Street place, and they are kept in a different manner, and that is the whole of the law of this morning as to them. Let it stand as read. The record is closed at 11:26 a.m. The defendant may retrieve his hat from the bench.
