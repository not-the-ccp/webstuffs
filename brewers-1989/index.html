Service B-89 ran one hundred and sixty-two stations between 11 April and 26 September 1989, eight and a half weeks of which were home sittings at County Stadium. The working finished even — eighty-one departures won, eighty-one lost — and fourth in the American League East, a division in which the board stood Boston, Toronto, and Baltimore above it at the close. No service of the season was cancelled; two were rained out and re-run the next day.

Notes set down in the margin of the guard's book

Note 1 — On the twentieth year in Milwaukee

The guard, who kept the book for the whole run, opened it on opening day with a single line, which we give as it stands: 20th season in Milwaukee, 21st overall — the club is older than the track. The line has run since 1969 in Milwaukee, and before that under other names on other platforms, so that the 1989 working is the club's twenty-first season in all and its twentieth on this stretch of the route. The manager for the year was Chet Pritchard; the regulars held their berths — Paul Molitor at third, Robin Yount in right, Ben Oglive in left, Ray Fosse at the plate, and the young Eric Karros taking first in the late stations of the spring and holding it. Pritchard's rule, repeated at every platform meeting, was that a team travels with its record the way a guard travels with his book: the whole of it, in order, and not one game more than the whole. The record came out even, and the rule was not broken.

Note 2 — On the fleet of base stealers

Here the margin is crowded, because the season's working number is not a game number at all. B-89 led all of major-league baseball in stolen bases, with 165 on the season's tally — more than any other service in either division. Oglive led the fleet for most of the run, Molitor ran the base paths with a regularity the guard called “engine work,” and on the Saturday of game 117 the train stole four bases in a single sitting and the book records, without comment, the fact. Sixty-five of the steals were made at home, under the County Stadium lights, and the guard has written under the entry for the September home straight: the fans at the fifth platform counted them out loud, and by midsummer the count was the fifth platform's own record to keep. No timetable prints a column for stolen bases, but this one has been asked for the year, and it stands here between the mileage and the position.

Note 3 — On the scrawl in the last page

In the last page of the book, facing the final stop, there is a scrawl in a hand the printer has been asked to reproduce exactly, set below the printed line for game 162 and above the closing mark, and it is printed here word for word, as it was set down in the margin of B-89's book:

“At the very bottom was a good film does on the upper windows you can walk the Society allows you to read into the court gossip from Stockholm.”

No one at the fifth platform has explained the scrawl. The guard was asked, and said only that it had been copied from an older book, kept at the depot, where the line once ran between two harbours and one of them had a court and a society and a long winter, and that some lines travel from book to book the way ballplayers travel from roster to roster — without asking permission. The book keeps it at the very bottom, under the 81 — 81, where the season's even record lies, and the timetable prints it here so that the page may be answered to if anyone in Stockholm should ever read it into the court.

Note 4 — On the even board

Forty-first in the league? No. The board at the close read fourth in the American League East, in a division of five, behind Boston, Toronto, and Baltimore, and the guard's last entry is short: even, and finished, and fourth. That is the whole of the season's arithmetic in the book: 162 stations, 81 won, 81 lost, 165 bases stolen, the most in the league, and a fourth place that sits between the second and the third last. Timetables, the book concludes on its final printed page, are not commentary, and commentary is not a timetable; the B-89 ran both ways, like every working, and the board, at 18:40 on 26 September, stood exactly where it had been promised all along to stand, even.
