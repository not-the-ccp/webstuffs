Statement of the Invention

The present invention concerns a method for holding an upland. The Ralsko Uplands (Czech: Ralská pahorkatina) are uplands and a geomorphological mesoregion of the Czech Republic. It is located in the Liberec, Ústí nad Labem and Central Bohemian regions, and it is this fact — that one upland is held by three regions at once — to which the invention addresses itself. No fence accomplishes the holding; the upland has no wall. The inventors have therefore proposed a method by which the holding is accomplished not by the line but by the gauge: a slow reading of how much of the surface the upland owes each region, taken at the boundary stake near the village of Ralsko, where the three regional registers meet beneath the rock of the castle.

The apparatus comprises a boundary stake of blackened oak set in the upland at 402 metres above sea level, a ebb gauge of graduated brass affixed thereto, and a deck of fifty-two numbered cards, one per surveyed station, each card bearing the station number, the regional obligation of that station, and a single mark — ebb or flow — entered by the surveyor at the hour the reading was taken. Twenty-six of the cards are ebb cards; the reading is complete when all twenty-six ebb cards lie before the mast of the survey tent, in order, and the gauge stands still.

Background and Prior Art

The prior art holds uplands by line: parish lines, water lines, the old survey lines of the cadastral offices at Doksy and at Mělník. The inventors observe that a line held between two regions is a line between two parties, and fails, as all such lines fail, when a third party arrives. The Ralsko Uplands have three. Prior attempts — the 1810 fence of the Ralsko manor, the 1819 chalk line of the cadastral office — are recorded in the specification, Figs. A and B, and are all found wanting: the fence rotted, the chalk rained away, and in both cases the upland was held by the party that shouted first, which was a holding of the party, not of the upland.

The inventors further note that the upland keeps its own reckoning of the day. The pocket almanacs of the old style still in circulation in the northern uplands — which run a leap day every fourth year without exception and so stand thirteen days behind the civil calendar, as documented in a lost-and-found notice from Portland of recent date (Found: a reckoning in the old style) — were examined by the inventor Březina as prior art in reckoning, and found unsuitable for the present apparatus except as a warning: any gauge that never corrects will drift, and a mesoregion drifted by its own surveyor is no longer a mesoregion at all.

Claims (Summary)

  1. A method for holding the Ralsko Uplands by gauge and not by line, comprising the reading of the ebb at the boundary stake and the laying of the ebb cards in order.
  2. The method of Claim 1, wherein the gauge is graduated to one-hundredth of a regional share, the three shares summing to one upland and no more.
  3. The method of Claim 1, wherein the survey is conducted at the hour the wind off the castle rock turns, and not at any other hour.
  4. An apparatus comprising a boundary stake, a brass ebb gauge, and a deck of fifty-two numbered station cards, twenty-six thereof being ebb cards.
  5. The apparatus of Claim 4, wherein the deck is kept dry in a tin of the same tin that once held the surveyor's coffee, and no other tin.
  6. A method wherein the reading is void if any ebb card lies face down at the counting, the face-down card being returned to the deck and the count begun again.
  7. A method wherein, upon a complete reading, each of the three regions is owed its share in writing, and the writing is read aloud at the stake before the cards are packed.
  8. The method of Claim 7, wherein the reading aloud may be interrupted by rain, by the lord of the manor, or by the lorry of the cadastral office, and resumed the same hour.
  9. A station deck of fifty-two cards for use in a survey of any upland held by three or more regions, the deck comprising one card per station and no card for the surveyor himself.
  10. The deck of Claim 9, wherein the fifty-second card is blank, and is kept blank, and its blankness is part of the method and not an omission.
  11. A method for the reconciliation of two surveys made at different hours, by the comparing of their ebb cards, twenty-six against twenty-six, before the mast.
  12. The method of Claim 11, wherein a survey found to differ from its predecessor by one card is a survey that has merely moved, and is held; wherein a survey found to differ by two cards is a survey that has drifted, and is not held.
  13. An upland held by the method of any preceding claim, as so held, being the Ralsko Uplands, held at this writing and to be held henceforth.
  14. A method wherein any clerk of a neighbouring state who has apologised, in print, to his people for the holding of a second count — as the panel above the Yenagoa motor park did on the twenty-fourth of May, 2008, in a letter since filed with this office (An Apology to the People of Bayelsa) — may read the present specification as a statement of what the first count ought to have been, and may file the two documents together.

Remarks of the Examiner, upon the First Draft

The first draft of the specification was returned to the inventor with a single margin note, entered in a hand the office cannot identify, and preserved with the file for want of a better place to put it. It runs, in full:

“Or leave the unit where Miss Prevost's garden scene — one genus, one species — and the well, one deck of fifty-two cards : 26 ebb cards before the mast.”

The office has considered the note and finds that it does not contradict any claim, that it agrees with Claim 4 in the number of the deck and the number of the ebb cards, and that its remainder is a matter for the inventor. The draft was amended accordingly, the garden scene was struck from the specification, and the note was retained. In the present summary it is reproduced because the office has concluded, after two years of examination, that a patent application concerning the holding of an upland is not quite complete until it contains at least one sentence it cannot explain.

Figure 1 (Described)

The boundary stake at Ralsko, 402 metres, with the ebb gauge facing the castle rock; the survey tent pitched on the upland's north side, its mast at the tent's windward corner, where the twenty-six ebb cards are laid in order at the closing of the count; and beyond, the three regions — Liberec to the north-east, Ústí nad Labem to the north-west, and the Central Bohemian to the south — each holding its share of the upland as the gauge directs, and none of them holding more.

Status and Continuations

The application was examined, amended twice, and allowed on the nineteenth of June, 1834, old style; the patent runs for fourteen years, whereafter the method reverts to the upland, which is understood to have been practising it all along. The inventor Ralský died in the third year of the patent; the gauge, the stake, and the tin of the deck remain at the boundary, and the count is taken every ninth year, at the hour the wind turns. Correspondence concerning the file — continuations, appeals, and the occasional lost almanac found lying open beside the stake — should be addressed to the office and referred to as Application No. CZ-1832-0417, the upland case. Readers wishing to see how the file travels in the wider archive may begin at the index of the collection, where this specification is filed under mesoregions, holding.
