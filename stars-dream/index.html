The Dream Dictionary · Nightly Register · Entry S-104

S T A R S

(a noun, plural; also the title of a record, in all caps, as it is stylized)

Dreamt the night of the 24th, over the sound of a horn that would not repeat its figure, in the room above the old church.

i. The Dream

You stand in a long black hall lit only from the ceiling, where small bright things are pinned like flies, and you understand, without being told, that you are in a registry. Behind the glass there is a choir, and the choir is singing a gospel that has been turned inside out — the same bright hymn, pitched down through water, so that the melody keeps its shape but its hope has sunk. Two men stand at the front of the hall, in caps worn backwards, and they are not singing; they are reading the list, one name per line, each name one of their own accomplishments, and every line they read, one of the pinned bright things above them lights up and stays lit. The list is longer than the ceiling. You know the two men, though you cannot name them: one of them is loud even when he is not talking, and the other wears his voice the way the first wears his cap, backwards and on purpose.

ii. The Reading

To dream of stars this way is to dream of a catalogue. The dictionary has kept the entry since the record arrived — the opening track of Vultures 1, the debut studio album of the American hip-hop superduo ¥$, Kanye West and Ty Dolla Sign, released in 2024. What you are hearing in the dream is the track itself: a dark gospel, built on a sample of “Good Luck” by Dijon, so that the luck the dream is about is borrowed, and everyone in the hall knows it is borrowed, which is what makes the bright things on the ceiling stay lit. The choir that will not repeat its figure is the uncredited voice — Camper sang on the record and the credit line does not carry the name, and the dream, which is a registry, knows exactly who is missing from its own register and keeps the seat open.

The reading at the front of the hall is the lyric work of the two of them, boasting of their accomplishments, line by line, the way the producers laid the track: the duo alongside Aver Ray, Camper, FnZ, JPEGMafia, Shdøw, the Legendary Traxster, and Timbaland, who all also served as co-writers, with Lucien Parker and Quentin Miller. Nine names on the wall, nine bright things on the ceiling, and the two men in the caps reading the list aloud so that nobody in the registry gets to claim a line without hearing it read back.

iii. The Registry

The rule the hall is kept by is written on a slip of paper taped above the door, and the slip reads, word for word: The prototype of the registry does not replace the stones it watches. The dictionary has held this line in the entry since the night it was first dreamt, because the dreamer understood it at the time. The registry is a thing made to watch the stars — a first, rough instrument, a prototype — and whatever else it does with the names it keeps, it does not stand in for the sky. The pinned bright things are not the sky. The album is not the choir. The list read aloud in the hall is not the accomplishments, only the record of them, and the record is what the critics kept, mostly, when they praised the composition: they kept the pinned bright things, and they let the sky go out the door. The stones in the slip are the actual stars, and the registry, whatever its drafter intended, was never built to take their place.

iv. Occurrence

The dream occurs most often in the third week after a record you love is finished with you, and it occurs reliably to those who keep some kind of register — ledgers, almanacs, club newsletters, station timetables — because a registry is what a dream of this kind is asking to be built. It occurs less often to those who have never heard a gospel song at 3 a.m. in a car, because the dream is one-eighth that, exactly, and it will not form without the sample. When it occurs, keep the slip above the door, and do not mistake the watch for the watched. The club on Magsaysay Avenue, which keeps a ledger of whatever the record refuses, has asked to borrow the slip. The Stratum, Vol. 7 No. 11 — The Ediacaran Number files it beside the soft-bodied entries, under the heading things that keep what they bear.

v. Prognosis

Good, if the list is longer than the ceiling and the light stays on the last line. The two men will not stop reading; that is the whole of the omen, and the critics were right to praise the composition, which is to say they were right to praise the hall and its pinned sky, and wrong, gently, in the way the registry is wrong — by never once taking the place of the stones. If you dream it again, bring the timetable: the line from Mammoth Whistle Stop to Eurasia Terminus crosses the Bering ice bridge at 14:35, and the dream is best read from a moving vehicle, where the ceiling passes overhead slowly enough to count. Do not count it. That is the registry's work, not yours. At Mammoth Whistle Stop the registry is kept on a counter where the small living stock of the platform shop sits with its paws — the front of the hands, as the shop's 1926 ledger has it, looking further off from the tremendous apparition — and the shop's own sheet for it, Felis catus, Platform Cat No. 12, is filed beside the slip above the door, under the heading the watch.
