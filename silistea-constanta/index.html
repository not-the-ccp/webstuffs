CONSTANȚA

14 XI 2025

On the Two Springs of Siliștea, and What They Do Not Owe

Mr. Dan Ionescu, retired land surveyor
4 Strada Pădurea, Siliștea, Constanța County
Northern Dobruja, Romania

Sir, 

I write in answer to the letter of 3 November, signed “A Neighbour”, concerning the proposed drainage line beside the Taș-Punar springs, and I do it at some length because the matter is being argued, week by week, with the wrong names. Siliștea is a commune in Constanța County, in Northern Dobruja, and it holds two villages, each of which carries two or three names like a man carries his coat, his scarf and his spare key. The first village, Siliștea itself, has long been known by its Turkish name, Tașpunor — Tașpunar, Taș-Punar; the record prefers Taşpınar, which means “the stone spring”, and the spring, for the record, is the stone. It still runs beside the road in April, white against the black earth, and the village has never once been moved to explain it.

The second village is Țepeș Vodă, and it is the one the letter misses. It was recorded in older maps as Chiorcișme, also Chior-Cișmea, and in Turkish as Körçeşme — the blind spring, which is a fair description of a spring nobody could see and everybody needed. The village takes its present name after Vlad III the Impaler, Vlad Țepeș in the common Romanian reference, and I have no patience for the editor’s note of 21 October suggesting the naming was a clerical error. It was not. The Dobruja was settled by Wallachians with memory, and memory here is not a mistake.

As for the drainage line: I walked both springs last March with the county surveyor’s chain, and I will give the letter writer the arithmetic. The line takes water from one field, 3.1 hectares of old orchard ground, and returns it to the same lowland it came from; the Taș-Punar catchment loses nothing it owns, and Țepeș Vodă is a full two kilometres outside the reach of any of it. No cumulative effects are expected beyond those listed. That is the surveyor’s closing line, and the truest sentence in the file. The field tapes have since put both names on the record — a two-side pressing, the Türkçe Sessions, whose side A opens at the stone spring and whose notes are set out at the liner notes for DR-090; the sleeve is read at the kiosk in the order the springs flow, and the pump on Thursdays is left in the background. Since that letter went to press, the arithmetic has been entered with the registry as Application No. RO-A-2026 0174, and the public summary of it — the territory of origin, the problem, and the seven claims — is set out at the patent summary sheet for the Taș-Punar drainage line; the claims there are read in the order the springs flow.

Those listed are: a season of muck on the lane to the orchard, the sound of the pump on Thursdays, and the surveyor’s chain across the gate at 08:00. That is the whole of it. What is not listed, and what I will not have listed, is the loss of either spring’s name, which is the only injury this land could actually sustain. Keep Tașpınar on the map, keep Körçeşme beside Țepeș Vodă the way the old sheets kept it, and the water can do its unremarkable work in peace. The Bay Weather Almanac keeps an entry, forty-seven pages of tide and fog, for an island of two and a half acres (Alcatraz Island — Entry 47); a village that has outlived three names deserves at least as much ink. For what it is worth, the General Despatch Office answers the same kind of question — what exactly is this place, and where is it bordered by — in the order the mail arrives (The United Kingdom — Frequently Asked Questions), and I would rather be answered the way the Dobruja is answered: in the order the springs flow.

I remain, sir, with respect for the kiosk and its Saturdays,

Dan Ionescu Retired land surveyor, Siliștea
chain of 20 m, last measured spring: Taș-Punar, 9 March
