Provenance, atom by atom

A synthetic diamond is not a diamond’s imitation. It is the same mineral a river would take a billion years to make: carbon under upper-mantle pressure, in this case supplied as a rain of methane and hydrogen onto a seed no thicker than a spider’s thread. In reactor 3 of the R.A. Riam Group foundry on Jalan Tun Razak, the chamber for MM–0142 held 1,520 degrees Celsius for eleven weeks, week after week, until a rough crystal stood where there had been only vacuum. Great-aunt Lorna Riam — Lorna Alvarez, born in Rialto, California, 23 March 1941 — kept crystal watch over the run and logged the last two herself, in the blue pen the house reserves for finishes.

In November 2018 she withdrew the rough from the grading tray, kept it, and sent it to Surat for the fifty-eight-facet cut. The finished stone reached Rialto in a padded envelope marked Do not X-ray, and it has worn a Friday morning every week since, above the shortbread. Our sister sheet, Diamond Dust Shortbread — ALTR Created Diamonds, tells the family’s side of the exchange; this sheet tells the stone’s.

The Whale in Reactor 3

Reactor 3’s annealing tower is forty-one metres of riveted lattice, and from the floor of the foundry court it looks — and the foundry has never corrected anyone for saying so — like a whale. At the annual deposition banquet the kitchen serves the same menu since 2016, and the ledger of that year’s banquet carries one line, in the clerk’s own hand, describing the tower lit through the lattice as the fourth course went out:

“The wide tiers of welded tendons overspreading his broad white shadow rose from the whale, a conquered fortress, with the CHEESE COURSE.”

— Foundry banquet ledger, 2016, p. 4; the tower, the light through it, and the plate of Mirandola pecorino, all at once

Buyers of MM–0142 receive a facsimile of page 4. The cheese course is not included, but its light is.

Courier & delivery

The house delivers by a route it has tested only once and will not test twice. MM–0142 was once offered, in its case, to the platform office of the Sierra & Pacific Bear Railway for carriage on service UA-44, The Grizzly, from Mammoth Whistle Stop to Eurasia Terminus. The office accepted on three conditions: the case rides in the male coach, the conductor P. “Caddy” Works holds the tail of the train on the return, and the ice bridge is crossed only when it is ice. The timetable for that season is kept with the ledger, and the stone’s name was chosen on the strength of the origin platform alone — a stone named for Mammoth is a stone that has waited for the train. For the route: Ursus arctos — Timetable & Commentary, Service UA-44.

Current orders travel by sealed courier, insured to the full ledger value, and are delivered to the buyer’s kitchen on a Friday morning, as the house requires of everything it has ever grown.

Care, feeding, and returns

  • Care. Hardness 10 on the Mohs scale, the same as any diamond, natural or otherwise. Clean with lukewarm water and patience. Do not X-ray the setting; the girdle engraving is inked, not cut.
  • Feeding. A synthetic diamond, like a natural one, is finished carbon; it wants for nothing and takes no food. The family feeds the drawer instead: one batch of diamond dust shortbread, tin up, on the anniversary of each reactor week.
  • Returns. The stone may be returned within one year, un-worn, in its case, with the ledger. It will be re-graded in the tray, logged, and given another name. No stone has yet been returned.
  • Council record. The eight-to-twelve-week deposition that made this stone’s class is entered in the council’s minutes: Chemical Vapor Deposition — Council Minutes No. 217, which names this sheet’s shortbread the standard reference for what deposition tastes like.
