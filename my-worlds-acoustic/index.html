Gallery of Recorded Sound · Case 3, The Retail Shelf · Placard No. 88

My Worlds Acoustic

Justin Bieber (b. Stratford, Ontario) · acoustic reissue, 2010 · pressed for one cold November and kept

Provenance

Release
26 November 2010, through the artist’s own label and the parent house, RBC/Island
Channel
Walmart retail stores and Sam’s Club only; elsewhere it exists inside the compilation My Worlds: The Collection (2010)
Contents
Nine acoustic settings of songs from the 2009 extended play My World and the 2010 album My World 2.0, and one new song, “Pray”
Makers of the setting
Dan Kanter, the artist’s music director; Kuk Harrell, vocal producer; and Rob Wells, producer
Object on the shelf
Cardboard sleeve, ten tracks, running approximately thirty-six minutes; the sleeve is the object, and the sleeve is what this case keeps

Label

The case holds the debut remix album of a Canadian singer who, at nineteen, had already moved a great many people’s attention and now wished to move their instruments. The idea was simple and the discipline total: strip the nine songs the public knew by heart down to wood, string and voice. Kanter sat in as the music’s architect, Harrell handled the vocal production with the care of a restorer at a panel painting, and Wells oversaw the cuts in the studio, so that a record of pop became, track by track, a record of the same songs remembered in a smaller room. “Sorry” arrives as an apology performed rather than announced; “Baby” loses its beat and gains a chair; “Mistletoe”, the year’s own small invention, is kept exactly as it sounds when a singer does not intend to be played on a speaker in a car at all. And where the nine known songs close the set, the new song “Pray” stands at the end like a benediction the others had been waiting for.

The distribution is part of the object’s history and must be stated plainly. The album was initially sold exclusively at Walmart retail stores and Sam’s Club, which means that for one season it belonged to a particular kind of shelf, under a particular kind of fluorescent light, in the record racks of the American middle where a paper price card would have been clipped to the bin. International listeners, in the meantime, met the same songs inside My Worlds: The Collection, the compilation that gathered the two previous releases and let the acoustic settings travel with the box set. The gallery has always treated such a sale as a form of architecture: the building defines the visitor, and the visitor, once inside, arranges the record into a winter.

From the Sleeve

Behind the jacket of the accessioned copy — the one that arrived at the gallery in a plastic mailer from a liquidator’s warehouse outside Winnipeg, corner cracked, price card still on — a single line is written in a hand the conservators have not been able to attribute. The desk has no theory of it, and the placard does not require one. It is read into the catalog word for word, exactly as it stands in the paper:

“The town’s own thread — I only wish your yard smelling of pitch and mostly used his feet, has died at Heinola Terminus, in the sleeve.”

— the marginal line, back of the jacket, ballpoint

Heinola Terminus the catalog has checked against two rail maps and found once; the pitch the reader may take as tar, as a yard, or as a smell. That is the whole of the entry, and the whole is enough. The object remains in Case 3, the fluorescent light above it is now a gallery lamp, and the ten songs still play, in the smaller room, in the order they were given.
