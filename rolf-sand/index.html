In re Sand

The Estate of Rolf Sand, c/o administrator Einar Vik, Markens gate 33, Oslo, — v. — The Norwegian Theater (Det Norske Teatret), c/o counsel Astrid Halvorsen, Brattørkaia 17, Oslo.

Third day of hearing · 13 March 2019 · 09:05. Present: Judge Håkon Berg presiding; counsel for the Estate, Mr. P. Dahl; counsel for the Theater, Ms. Astrid Halvorsen; shorthand clerk Ms. I. Solberg, sheet no. 7. Called as witness: Karl Nygaard, former stage director, Trøndelag Theater.

Recital of the matter before the Court

The dispute concerns the estate of Rolf Sand (21 February 1920 – 20 May 2018), Norwegian actor, born in Kristiania, the half-brother of Bjørn Sand. The witness record is not in contest: Mr. Sand made his stage debut in 1949 in the title role in Holberg's comedy Den pantsatte bondedreng; performed at the Trøndelag Theater from 1952 to 1954; served with the National Traveling Theater from 1954 to 1958, which company worked the county circuit by train out of Trondheim; and held an engagement at the Norwegian Theater from 1962 until his retirement in 1987, with a large number of minor characters in Norwegian film productions between. The estate alleges that a letter of engagement dated 3 October 1958, in the hand of the Traveling Theater's stage manager, purporting to extend Mr. Sand's touring season by one week, is missing from the contract box recovered from the theater's cellar, and that the box was stored for eleven years beside a vinegar crock. The estate claims 47,800 kroner. The Theater admits the crock; it denies the letter.

Examination of the witness

JUDGE BERG: You served the 1958 season with the National Traveling Theater. Confirm your name and position for the record.

WITNESS NYGAARD: Karl Nygaard. Stage director. We ran the season in a four-wagon carriage and a flat, from 4 March to 27 December, forty-one venues, the timetable pinned above the wardrobe trunk. Mr. Sand was the senior man on the board that year.

MR. DAHL: Is it your evidence that on 3 October 1958 you wrote Mr. Sand a letter extending the season to 10 January 1959?

WITNESS NYGAARD: It is my evidence that I drafted one. The winter stretch, Orkanger to Steinkjer, took four days and we were short a man for the second performance at the Røros mill. Whether it was signed, posted, or left in the flat car at Oppdal, I cannot swear. Men's letters went on the train as often as the post.

MS. HALVORSEN: The contract box came from the cellar at Markens gate 33. What can you say of that cellar?

WITNESS NYGAARD: It was cold and it was damp and it smelled of a crock. The keepers pickled there for the canteen — fish, beets, and one large crock of vinegar that outlived three directors. Papers that went to that cellar came back with a sour margin. That is the whole of what I can say.

MS. HALVORSEN: And the letter itself?

WITNESS NYGAARD: If it went to the cellar, it went to the cellar with everything else. I have not seen it in sixty-one years.

Exhibit C — the shorthand sheet, line 44

Ms. Solberg, you prepared sheet no. 7 from the shorthand of the original proceedings taken at Trondheim in 1959. Read the note you have appended to line 44.

CLERK SOLBERG: The shorthand is partly undecipherable at that line; the writer had retired in 1979 and the carbon is failing. I have reproduced it verbatim and have not corrected it.

"The train assembles here in a cool archive; the sheet so the vinegar crock, and nothing else to do: it was phased out by how much."

JUDGE BERG: Counsel for the Estate, does this line assist?

MR. DAHL: It is, in my submission, the only contemporary note that puts the traveling train, the contract sheets, and the crock in one place. What it asserts, I am not in a position to say. The Committee for the Record declined to emend it, and I decline to paraphrase it. The line stands as written, and the Court may make of it what it can.

MR. DAHL: The practice of keeping such a line unemended is not new to this court. A century before the present proceedings, the minute book of the Leicester Temperance Society — the book that recorded the 17 May 1841 excursion to Loughborough at one shilling per head — carried a line of its own, which its committee likewise declined to correct, and which has stood in its clerk’s hand ever since; the committee, the minute book, and nine surviving ticket stubs are kept under glass on the gallery placard for Thomas Cook, Case No. 47.

MS. HALVORSEN: The line has not been explained. It has not been explained for sixty years. We note that for the record as well.

Adjournment

At 12:41 the Court heard the estate's account of Mr. Sand's later years: the engagement at the Norwegian Theater from 1962, the retirement in 1987, the minor characters in film, and the death in May 2018 at the age of 98, none of which is disputed. The claim for the missing letter is adjourned to 2 April 2019, when the estate is to produce the 1959 Trondheim season book, if it exists. The Court observes that the record of this case is kept in the manner of all records of this court — sheet by sheet, in the cool of the archive — and that one garbled line among seven sheets is a small loss, unless it is the line. The record is closed for this day.

HÅKON BERG
District Judge, Oslo Tingrett
