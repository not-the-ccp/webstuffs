Bearclaw Ridge Station · Lost & Found Office

Season 1974–75 · notices kept by the day clerk · no fee on claim

Lost & Found
Notice No. 221

“Found, not picked. It was standing where the light went down.”

Found on the morning of 9 October, in the spruce hollow below Kettle Brook at about 7:40, by E. M. Carrow, game warden, walking the north fence line in a rain that had stopped ten minutes earlier: one fruiting body of Chroogomphus vinicolor, the wine-cap Chroogomphus, also kept on this station’s books as the pine spike, of the family Gomphidiaceae. The finder did not touch it. He stood with his back to a white spruce and counted his breaths until the cap looked the same colour as the weather, and then he came in and wrote this notice.

I. Description

The specimen, taken as a party to this notice, presents a reddish-brown, shiny cap of some six and a half centimetres across, still carrying a bead or two of the October rain, set atop a tapered stem that runs the length of a small pencil and holds itself as upright as the fence post it was found beside. Beneath the cap, the gills are thick, initially pale orange before turning blackish, and they extend a short way down the length of the stem, a little past halfway, in the manner of a cuff turned down rather than rolled. The ground it stands in is dark loam under a tangle of fir needles, in North America, and a sister population of the species keeps itself in the conifer woods of Europe, where the same name is used over a different argument about cystidial walls.

II. Of Confusion with Others

Notice is hereby given that the pine spike is difficult to separate from certain other similar Chroogomphus species, for their morphology is close, and the cap colouration is too variable to be a reliable characteristic; a reddish-brown cap will not settle the question any more than a coat settles the question of its owner. The species in hand is differentiated from the European C. rutilus and the North American C. ochraceus by the thickness of its cystidial walls, which the finder cannot measure and the office therefore declines to measure. Anyone claiming this notice on the strength of a photograph is asked to bring a microscope, not an opinion.

III. Claim Conditions

Claims are to be made at this office in person, on any weekday before the light does what it did at 7:40, and are to be answered, in the clerk’s words, with the following standing orders, entered in the station ledger without correction:

“The owner of the pine spike shall prove acquaintance by naming the hollow, the fence line, and the colour of the gills at the hour of finding.”

“Do not flip the record line seven of the table, the small ads that feed the drawer.”

The clerk has been asked to explain the second order. The clerk has said that line seven of the table was entered by a hand the station has never traced, that the small ads are paid for in the way the drawer is fed, and that no one at Bearclaw Ridge has flipped that line since 1961, when it was flipped once, on purpose, and the ledger never sat flat again. The office stands by the order. For the manner in which a similar uncorrected line is kept alive in a court record, see the excerpt from the verbatim record, Case No. 1726 of 2024, where the golden rule is heard in both its positive and negative forms and the table is not to be handled. And for the small ads themselves, the full column as it runs — the linen, the red shirt, the witnesses to the eighty-ninth minute — keep open the classified columns of the Lagan Ledger, which the drawer, on the evidence of this notice, reads on its way down.

IV. Disposition

Should the pine spike be unclaimed by the first frost, the office will not bury it, press it, or publish it. It will be left in the spruce hollow where the light goes down, under the standing order of this notice, which is the oldest order in the book: a thing found in the woods belongs to the woods; the office is only the record of the finding.

— Entered on 11 October in the station ledger, page 31, in ink that dried before the question about the microscope was finished. —
